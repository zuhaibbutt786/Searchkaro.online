India edges Pakistan 3-2 in SAFF Championship opener

India opened the SAFF Championship 2026 with a 3-2 win over Pakistan, thanks to a brace from Suhail Bhat. Pakistan fought back late but fell short despite a reduced squad.

Latest developments

The opening match of the SAFF Championship 2026 in Goa saw the defending champions India defeat their long‑time rivals Pakistan by a narrow 3-2 margin. The game unfolded with India taking an early lead, and forward Suhail Bhat adding two goals to secure the victory. The match, broadcast across South Asia, attracted significant viewership as fans anticipated a tightly contested encounter between the two neighbours.

India entered the game with a depleted roster, fielding only 17 players after injuries and administrative issues forced several squad members out. Despite the shortage, the team displayed cohesion, with midfielders linking play effectively and Bhat exploiting gaps in the Pakistani defence. The limited squad size was highlighted in pre‑match coverage, noting the challenge of maintaining intensity for the full 90 minutes.

Pakistan, aware of the numerical disadvantage, launched a sustained late onslaught after falling behind. Their forwards pressed high, creating several scoring chances in the final quarter of the match. However, missed opportunities and a resilient Indian defence prevented a comeback. The Pakistani side managed to pull one goal back, narrowing the deficit but ultimately could not equalise before the final whistle.

The result places India at the top of Group A with three points, while Pakistan remains winless but retains a goal‑difference that could keep them in contention for the second qualifying spot. Coaches from both camps praised their players' effort, and analysts noted that the narrow margin reflects the growing competitiveness of South Asian football.

Background

The South Asian Football Federation (SAFF) Championship is a biennial tournament that brings together eight nations from the region. Historically, India has dominated the competition, winning multiple titles, while Pakistan has struggled to reach the latter stages. The 2026 edition marks the first time the tournament is hosted in Goa, offering modern facilities and a neutral venue for both teams.

Previous encounters between India and Pakistan in SAFF tournaments have often been closely contested, with the most recent meeting in 2022 ending in a 2-1 win for India. The rivalry extends beyond sport, reflecting broader cultural and political ties that heighten public interest each time the teams meet.

Both federations have faced administrative hurdles in recent years. Pakistan's football association has dealt with funding shortfalls and player eligibility disputes, leading to a reduced squad for this match. India, meanwhile, has invested in youth development programs that are beginning to bear fruit, as evidenced by the emergence of players like Bhat who are now key contributors at the senior level.

What happens next

India's next fixture in Group A is against the Maldives, a match that will test the depth of their squad after the demanding opener. Pakistan will face Sri Lanka, aiming to secure a win that could keep their qualification hopes alive. Both teams must manage player fatigue and injuries as the group stage progresses.

The tournament format allows the top two teams from each group to advance to the semi‑finals. With three points already secured, India will look to maintain its unbeaten run, while Pakistan must win its remaining games and rely on favorable results elsewhere to move forward.
