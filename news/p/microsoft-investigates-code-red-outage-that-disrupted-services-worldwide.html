Microsoft investigates 'Code Red' outage that disrupted services worldwide

A global outage that took down Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft services has been linked to an internal "Code Red" event. The company is now explaining the chain of failures that led to the disruption.

Latest developments

Microsoft released a technical briefing on Thursday describing the cascade of failures that triggered the "Code Red" incident on November 22. The briefing said a mis‑configured network switch in an Azure data centre caused a loss of connectivity for several core services, including Outlook, Teams and SharePoint. The problem was compounded by a monitoring alert that failed to reach the on‑call engineers because an internal email routing rule was broken. In a video address, CEO Satya Nadella apologized for the outage, noted that the issue was resolved after several hours, and promised a full post‑mortem report. The company also said it is reviewing its incident‑response procedures to prevent similar events.

Why it matters

The outage affected millions of U.S. businesses, schools and government agencies that rely on Microsoft 365 for daily communication and collaboration. Many reported lost productivity as employees could not send or receive email, join virtual meetings or access shared documents. Financial services firms said the disruption delayed transaction processing, while health‑care providers faced challenges coordinating patient care. The incident also raised concerns about the resilience of cloud platforms that dominate the enterprise market. For consumers, the outage highlighted the risk of over‑dependence on a single provider for essential digital tools.

Background

Microsoft's cloud infrastructure spans dozens of data centres worldwide, and its services are built on a layered architecture that includes networking, storage and compute components. Internal code names such as "Code Red" are used to classify high‑severity incidents that require immediate escalation. The Register's investigative report revealed that the testing environment for the affected switch had not been fully exercised after a recent software update, creating a blind spot in the change‑management process. Additionally, the report noted that a batch of automated emails intended to notify engineers of the alert never arrived, a failure traced to a misrouted email address in the monitoring system.

What happens next

Microsoft has pledged to publish a detailed post‑mortem within the next two weeks, outlining the root causes and corrective actions. The company said it will implement stricter testing protocols for network changes and overhaul its alert‑delivery mechanisms to ensure critical notifications reach the right teams. Industry analysts expect the incident to prompt other cloud providers to review their own incident‑response frameworks, especially around alerting and change validation.

Summary

- A mis‑configured network switch and a broken email alert triggered the "Code Red" outage on November 22. - Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and other Microsoft 365 services were unavailable for several hours, impacting millions of users in the United States. - Satya Nadella addressed the issue publicly and committed to a full investigation. - The Register identified testing gaps and missed alerts as key contributors to the failure. - Microsoft plans to tighten testing procedures and improve alert routing to avoid repeat incidents.
