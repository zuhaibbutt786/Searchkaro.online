SMBC to Consolidate India Operations Under Yes Bank by FY29

SMBC has informed the RBI that its Indian operations will be merged into Yes Bank by fiscal year 2029. Bondholders of Yes Bank are urging the bank to comply with the Supreme Court's AT‑1 order.

Latest developments

In a recent filing with the Reserve Bank of India, SMBC Holdings Ltd. announced that its Indian banking arm will be fully integrated into Yes Bank by the end of fiscal year 2029. The move is part of a broader strategy to streamline operations and reduce regulatory costs across the group. The consolidation will involve the transfer of branch networks, customer accounts and loan portfolios to Yes Bank’s existing infrastructure.

SMBC’s decision follows a series of regulatory reviews that have highlighted the need for greater capital adequacy in the Indian market. By merging with Yes Bank, the group aims to create a more resilient entity that can better absorb market shocks. The RBI has been briefed on the plan and will assess the proposal against prudential norms before granting approval.

Meanwhile, Yes Bank’s bondholders are pressing the bank to comply with a Supreme Court order that requires the institution to issue AT‑1 bonds to strengthen its capital base. The court’s directive, issued earlier this year, mandates that the bank raise additional capital to meet regulatory thresholds. Bondholders argue that the AT‑1 issuance is critical for restoring confidence among investors.

The convergence of SMBC’s consolidation plan and the AT‑1 compliance issue has drawn attention from market analysts and regulators alike. Both developments could reshape the competitive dynamics of India’s banking sector and influence the availability of credit to small and medium enterprises.

Why it matters

For retail customers, the consolidation could mean a smoother experience as Yes Bank expands its branch footprint and digital services. Existing SMBC customers will be migrated to Yes Bank’s account systems, with promises of no disruption to deposits or loan repayments.

From a regulatory standpoint, the merger will test the RBI’s ability to manage cross-border banking operations and ensure that capital adequacy standards are upheld. The AT‑1 bond issue, meanwhile, will affect the cost of capital for Yes Bank and could influence interest rates offered to borrowers.

Bondholders, many of whom are institutional investors, are keen to see the court’s order enforced. Their compliance demands could accelerate the capital raising process, potentially stabilising the bank’s balance sheet and protecting depositor interests.

Background

Yes Bank, once a high‑growth challenger bank, faced a liquidity crisis in 2020 that forced a government‑led recapitalisation. The bank was placed under special resolution and later emerged with a new ownership structure and a capital infusion from the government.

In 2022, the Supreme Court issued an AT‑1 order that required Yes Bank to raise additional capital through a bond issuance. The order was aimed at ensuring the bank met the Basel III capital requirements and safeguarding the banking system.

SMBC, a Japanese financial conglomerate, entered India in 2011 and has grown its retail and corporate banking operations. However, the bank has faced challenges in scaling its services amid intense competition from domestic players.

What happens next

SMBC will submit a formal application to the RBI detailing the integration roadmap and capital allocation plan. The regulator will review the proposal for compliance with prudential norms and may seek additional information before granting approval.

Yes Bank is expected to launch the AT‑1 bond issuance within the next few months, subject to regulatory clearance. Bondholders will monitor the process closely, and the bank will need to demonstrate that the capital raise will meet the court’s requirements.
