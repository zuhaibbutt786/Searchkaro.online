US conducts covert strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island amid election tension

Multiple explosions were heard on Qeshm Island, prompting accusations of a secret US attack. The incidents come just before Iran's mid‑term elections, heightening regional anxieties.

Latest developments

On Tuesday night, residents of Qeshm Island and nearby Bandar Lengeh reported a series of loud explosions that lit up the sky over the Hormuz Strait. Local eyewitnesses described the blasts as resembling aerial strikes, and satellite imagery captured brief flashes of light over the island. While Iranian officials have not confirmed the source, several international monitoring agencies have linked the activity to a covert US operation aimed at disabling what Washington perceives as Iranian maritime capabilities.

The timing of the strikes is significant because they occurred just hours before Iran's scheduled mid‑term parliamentary elections. Analysts suggest that the United States may be attempting to influence the political environment by demonstrating its ability to project power in the region. Iranian state media has framed the attacks as a direct violation of sovereignty and a provocation intended to sway voter sentiment against the incumbent reformist factions.

US officials have publicly denied any involvement, describing the reports as unverified speculation. However, senior defence analysts cited in regional think‑tanks argue that the United States has a strategic interest in keeping the Hormuz Strait – a vital conduit for global oil shipments – under tight surveillance. The alleged strikes targeted radar installations and small naval vessels believed to be used by Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps.

International reaction has been swift. The United Nations Secretary‑General called for restraint from all parties and urged an immediate investigation into the alleged use of force. European foreign ministries have expressed concern over the potential for escalation, while the Russian foreign ministry warned that any unilateral action could destabilise the already fragile security architecture of the Gulf.

Iran's foreign ministry issued a formal protest, demanding that the United States cease any hostile activity and respect Iran's territorial integrity. Tehran has also hinted at a possible retaliatory response, though no concrete military plans have been disclosed. The situation remains fluid as diplomatic channels remain open but tense.

Why it matters

India, as a major importer of crude oil, relies heavily on the smooth flow of petroleum through the Hormuz Strait. Any disruption in this narrow waterway can cause immediate spikes in global oil prices, directly affecting Indian consumers and the broader economy. The recent explosions raise the spectre of a broader confrontation that could jeopardise the security of shipping lanes.

For Indian businesses operating in the Gulf, the heightened risk environment may lead to increased insurance premiums and the need to reroute vessels, adding to logistical costs. Moreover, the political instability in Iran could spill over into neighbouring Afghanistan and Pakistan, regions where India already has strategic interests.

The episode also underscores the delicate balance of power in South‑West Asia. A perceived US willingness to use force near Iranian territory may embolden other regional actors to adopt more aggressive postures, complicating India's diplomatic efforts to maintain stability and foster economic cooperation across the region.

The Hormuz Strait, a narrow passage between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, has long been a flashpoint because roughly one‑fifth of the world's oil passes through it daily.

Qeshm Island, Iran's largest island in the Strait, hosts a network of radar stations and naval facilities that Iran uses to monitor maritime traffic. In the past decade, the United States has conducted several freedom‑of‑navigation operations in the area, often leading to diplomatic protests from Tehran.

The nuclear issue adds another layer of complexity. Iran's nuclear programme has been the subject of multiple international agreements and sanctions, with the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) being a notable, albeit contested, attempt at containment. Recent US statements have hinted at the possibility of a pre‑emptive strike should Iran move closer to developing a nuclear weapon, a scenario that fuels speculation about the current covert actions.

What happens next

Diplomatic channels are expected to remain active as both Washington and Tehran seek to avoid a full‑scale military clash. The United Nations may dispatch a fact‑finding mission to verify the source of the explosions, while the United States is likely to issue a formal denial or, alternatively, a strategic justification framed as a defensive measure.

Iran, meanwhile, may increase its military alertness in the Strait and could conduct retaliatory drills. Observers anticipate that regional powers, including Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, will closely monitor the situation and may adjust their own security postures accordingly.
