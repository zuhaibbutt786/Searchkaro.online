Rana Sanaullah warns PTI against holding state hostage amid stalled talks

Law Minister Rana Sanaullah cautioned PTI that using the state as a bargaining chip will not work, while Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif warned that protests could damage economic progress. The political deadlock deepens as PTI insists on Imr

Latest developments

Rana Sanaullah, Pakistan's law minister, told reporters that the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) cannot compel the government to meet its demands by holding the state hostage. His remarks came after a series of meetings between PTI leaders and the federal government failed to produce a consensus on key policy issues. Sanaullah emphasized that democratic institutions must not be used as leverage, and that any attempt to disrupt governance would be counterproductive. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif issued a public warning that continued protests could erode recent economic gains, urging PTI to reconsider its confrontational stance. He highlighted that inflation has been falling and foreign investment is returning, but political instability could reverse these trends. In a separate statement, PTI officials declared that no meaningful decision could be reached without the direct involvement of former Prime Minister Imran Khan, whose absence they say has stalled negotiations. The party argued that Khan's leadership is essential for any agreement on the disputed issues, and they threatened to suspend talks if his participation is not secured. The three statements together illustrate a widening gap between the ruling coalition and the main opposition, with each side accusing the other of undermining national stability.

Why it matters

The standoff has immediate implications for Pakistan's fragile economy. Shehbaz Sharif's warning reflects concerns that large‑scale protests could deter investors, increase borrowing costs, and slow down the recovery from a recent recession. If PTI continues to mobilise its supporters, the risk of disruptions to transport, energy supply, and market operations rises, potentially inflating prices for ordinary citizens. Politically, the insistence on Imran Khan's involvement signals that PTI is unwilling to compromise without its charismatic leader, whose legal battles and exile have already shaped the country's political narrative. This dynamic could influence upcoming local elections and affect the balance of power in the parliament, where the ruling alliance holds a slim majority.

Background

Rana Sanaullah, a senior member of the ruling Pakistan Democratic Movement, has served as law minister since 2022 and is known for his firm stance on rule of law. PTI, founded by Imran Khan in 1996, rose to power in 2018 before being ousted in a parliamentary vote of no confidence in 2022. Since then, the party has organised nationwide rallies demanding Khan's release from legal challenges and a return to power. The current political crisis began when PTI submitted a set of demands, including the reinstatement of certain officials and a review of recent judicial decisions, which the government deemed unacceptable. Negotiations have been mediated by senior officials, but progress stalled after Khan's legal team refused to attend without assurances of immunity.

What happens next

Analysts expect that the government will maintain pressure on PTI to drop its demand for Imran Khan's direct involvement, while seeking to keep essential services running. If PTI escalates protests, security forces may be deployed to prevent disruptions, which could lead to arrests and further tension. International observers are watching closely, as prolonged instability could affect Pakistan's debt restructuring talks with the IMF and other lenders. The next week is likely to see a series of back‑channel meetings aimed at finding a compromise before the political calendar fills with election campaigns.
