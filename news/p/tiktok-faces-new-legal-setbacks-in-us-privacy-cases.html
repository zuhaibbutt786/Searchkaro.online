TikTok faces new legal setbacks in US privacy cases

Recent court rulings have limited TikTok's proposed settlements over privacy violations. The decisions are prompting users and regulators worldwide to watch the platform closely.

What is happening

A federal judge in Washington, D.C., has signaled that a key component of the proposed TikTok privacy settlement will not be approved. The judge expressed concerns that the settlement does not adequately address alleged violations of the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and may not provide sufficient restitution for affected users. The decision leaves the broader settlement in limbo and could force TikTok to renegotiate terms with the Federal Trade Commission (FTC).

In California, a state court has blocked a portion of a $400 million settlement that TikTok offered to resolve claims that it mishandled user data. The court found that the settlement’s consumer‑class‑action provisions were overly broad and could limit the ability of individual users to seek redress. As a result, TikTok must return to the negotiation table with California regulators to craft a more narrowly tailored agreement.

Separately, the Department of Justice attempted to spend $100 million to lift a previous order that restricted TikTok’s collection of data from children under 13. A federal panel rejected the request, citing ongoing concerns about the platform’s compliance with child‑privacy rules. The rejection maintains the status quo, keeping the protective order in place while the DOJ evaluates alternative enforcement strategies.

Together, these rulings represent a coordinated push by multiple jurisdictions to tighten oversight of TikTok’s data‑handling practices. Legal analysts note that the fragmented outcomes could compel the company to adopt a unified compliance framework that satisfies both federal and state requirements, rather than relying on separate settlements that may conflict with each other.

Why people are searching this now

The timing of the court decisions coincides with heightened public awareness of digital privacy, especially after high‑profile data breaches and the rollout of new privacy legislation in several countries. Users are eager to understand whether their personal information, and that of their children, remains at risk on a platform that commands billions of daily interactions.

In South Africa, where TikTok usage has surged among younger demographics, the news has sparked particular interest. Local regulators are monitoring the US outcomes to gauge whether similar enforcement actions might be considered under South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA). Consequently, South African users are searching for updates to gauge how the rulings could affect the platform’s availability and data‑privacy safeguards in their region.

Background and context

TikTok, owned by Chinese company ByteDance, has faced scrutiny since 2019 for its data‑collection practices. Early investigations focused on whether the app harvested location data, device identifiers, and browsing habits without clear user consent. In 2020, the FTC opened a formal inquiry into the platform’s compliance with COPPA, leading to a proposed settlement that would have required TikTok to implement a comprehensive privacy program and pay a civil penalty.

The settlement that emerged in 2022 was intended to resolve multiple state and federal claims, including a $92 million fine and a $400 million consumer‑class‑action component. However, critics argued that the agreement allowed TikTok to continue certain data‑processing activities with limited oversight. Subsequent state‑level actions, such as the California case, have sought to tighten those provisions, reflecting a broader trend of regulators demanding more transparent and enforceable privacy commitments from large tech firms.

What to know next

TikTok is expected to file appeals against the recent rulings, particularly the federal judge’s refusal to endorse the settlement’s COPPA component. If the appeals succeed, the company may be able to negotiate a revised agreement that satisfies both the FTC and state authorities. In the meantime, regulators in other jurisdictions may use the US decisions as a template for their own enforcement actions.

For users, the immediate implication is that existing privacy controls on the app remain largely unchanged. However, the continued legal pressure suggests that TikTok could introduce stricter age‑verification mechanisms, clearer consent prompts, and more robust data‑deletion options in forthcoming updates. Users concerned about privacy should review the app’s settings, limit the amount of personal information shared, and stay informed about any policy changes announced by the platform.

Key takeaways
