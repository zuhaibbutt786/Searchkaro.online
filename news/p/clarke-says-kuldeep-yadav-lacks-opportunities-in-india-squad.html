Clarke says Kuldeep Yadav lacks opportunities in India squad

Michael Clarke, the ex‑Australia captain, has voiced concerns that India are not using left‑arm spinner Kuldeep Yadav enough. The comment comes as the team finishes a tightly contested series against the West Indies.

Latest developments

Former Australian skipper Michael Clarke took to social media on Monday to argue that India’s current spin attack is under‑utilising left‑arm wrist‑spinner Kuldeep Yadav. Clarke, who led Australia to the 2015 World Cup final, wrote that Kuldeep has "the skill set to trouble any batting line‑up" but is "not getting enough opportunities" in the recent series against the West Indies.

India completed a three‑match One Day International (ODI) series against the West Indies last week, winning the series 2-1. Throughout the series, Kuldeep featured in only two matches and bowled a total of 12 overs, a stark contrast to his usual workload in previous tours. In the decisive third ODI, he was omitted from the playing XI despite a strong domestic season.

The Indian Cricket Board (BCCI) did not issue an official response to Clarke’s remarks, but senior coach Rahul Dravid was quoted in the Idaho Press report as saying the team is "evaluating all options" and that selection decisions are based on current form and match conditions.

Cricket analysts in India and Pakistan have picked up Clarke’s comments, with several Pakistani sports websites noting that the criticism could fuel discussions ahead of the upcoming Asia Cup, where spin bowling is expected to play a pivotal role.

Fans on Pakistani platforms expressed mixed reactions, some agreeing with Clarke that Kuldeep deserves a longer spell, while others defended the selectors, pointing to the emergence of other spin options such as Axar Patel and Yuzvendra Chahal.

Why it matters

Spin bowling has traditionally been a decisive factor in sub‑continental cricket, and any perceived mismanagement of a specialist can influence team balance. For Pakistani readers, the debate touches on a long‑standing rivalry with India, where both sides closely monitor each other's selection policies.

If Kuldeep Yadav is indeed being under‑used, it could affect India’s ability to vary its attack in conditions that favour turn, especially in upcoming tournaments hosted in the sub‑continent. Pakistani teams, which also rely heavily on spin, may see an opening to exploit any weakness in India’s spin depth.

Beyond the immediate tactical implications, Clarke’s public criticism highlights the growing trend of former players influencing current selection narratives. This can shape fan expectations and put additional pressure on the BCCI ahead of high‑profile fixtures.

Background

Kuldeep Yadav made his international debut in 2017 and quickly established himself as a left‑arm unorthodox spinner capable of delivering sharp turn and deceptive flight. He played a key role in India’s 2018 Asia Cup victory and was instrumental in the 2020 T20 World Cup win, where his variations earned him the Player of the Match award against Australia.

Michael Clarke, meanwhile, captained Australia from 2011 to 2015 and is widely respected for his tactical acumen. Since retiring, he has remained vocal on international cricket matters, often commenting on selection policies and player development across major cricketing nations.

The India‑West Indies ODI series in June 2024 was the first bilateral series for both teams after a brief hiatus caused by scheduling conflicts. India fielded a relatively experimental side, giving opportunities to several younger players while rotating senior members to manage workload.

What happens next

India’s next major commitment is the Asia Cup, scheduled to begin in September 2024. The selection committee is expected to announce a 15‑member squad in early August, and Kuldeep’s inclusion will be closely watched after Clarke’s comments.

Additionally, India will host a bilateral series against Bangladesh in October, providing another platform for spin bowlers to prove their worth. Observers will be looking for any shift in Kuldeep’s playing time and whether the BCCI adjusts its spin strategy based on the feedback.
