Qualcomm India to cut 1,000 jobs, offers 15‑month severance to some staff

Qualcomm has announced a significant reduction in its Indian workforce, citing global restructuring. The move has left many employees scrambling for new opportunities.

Latest developments

Qualcomm announced on Tuesday that it will reduce its presence in India by more than 1,000 positions as part of a global restructuring effort that began earlier this year. The company said the cuts would affect teams across engineering, sales and support, with the majority of affected staff located in Bangalore and Hyderabad. The decision follows a broader trend of cost‑cutting in the semiconductor and technology sectors.

In a statement, Qualcomm offered a severance package that includes up to 15 months of salary for certain employees. The package, reported by NDTV, was described as a “significant” benefit that would help laid‑off workers transition to new roles. The offer is not universal; only a subset of employees, primarily those with senior technical or managerial experience, received the extended payment.

One employee, who chose to remain anonymous, shared that he was informed of his layoff a few days before his wedding. The Times of India reported that the manager confirmed the termination just before the ceremony, leaving the employee to juggle personal commitments and the sudden loss of income. The story highlights the human cost of corporate restructuring.

The layoffs have created uncertainty across the tech ecosystem in India. Many of the affected workers are highly skilled professionals with experience in chip design and software development. Their departure could slow the growth of local start‑ups that rely on talent from established firms. Meanwhile, the severance packages may provide a temporary cushion, but the long‑term impact on the workforce remains unclear.

Why it matters

India’s technology sector is a key driver of the country’s economic growth, and Qualcomm’s workforce is a significant contributor to that ecosystem. The loss of more than 1,000 jobs could reduce the supply of experienced engineers, making it harder for new companies to scale.

For the employees, the sudden termination and the variability of severance benefits add to the uncertainty. Those who received the 15‑month package may find it easier to search for new roles, but others without such support may face immediate financial strain. The situation underscores the need for robust career transition programs in the industry.

Background

Qualcomm, a global leader in semiconductor and telecommunications equipment, has faced declining revenues in recent years due to competition and market saturation. In 2023, the company announced a worldwide cost‑cutting initiative that targeted several regions, including India.

India has become a major hub for Qualcomm’s research and development, with several offices dedicated to chip design and software engineering. The company’s decision to reduce its workforce there reflects a shift in strategy toward more automated processes and outsourcing of certain functions.

What happens next

Qualcomm is expected to finalize the list of affected employees by the end of the month. The company has indicated that it will continue to support those who receive severance with career counseling and job placement services. Industry analysts predict that the layoffs may prompt other firms in the region to reassess their staffing models.
