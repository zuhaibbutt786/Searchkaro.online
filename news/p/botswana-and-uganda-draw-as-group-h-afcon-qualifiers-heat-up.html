Botswana and Uganda Draw as Group H AFCON Qualifiers Heat Up

Botswana and Uganda share points in a tightly contested Group H match, leaving the qualification picture uncertain. Fans and officials from Zambia and Uganda are urging a win in the upcoming games to keep hopes alive.

Latest developments

In the latest round of the TotalEnergies CAF AFCON 2027 qualifiers, Botswana managed to hold co-hosts Uganda to a goalless draw at the Enyimba International Stadium, tightening the competition in Group H. The match, played under humid conditions, saw both sides create chances but fail to convert, leaving the scoreboard unchanged at the final whistle. The point earned by Botswana moves them level on points with Uganda, while also keeping Zambia, known as the Zebras, within striking distance of the top two spots that guarantee qualification.

Uganda, as one of the tournament's co-hosts, entered the fixture with the expectation of securing a win to cement their position. Coach Milutin Sredojević emphasized the need for the Cranes to sharpen up ahead of the return leg in Gaborone, noting that the narrow margin against Botswana could prove costly if the team does not improve its defensive organization. Analysts highlighted that Uganda's midfield struggled to dominate possession, allowing Botswana to press high and force errors.

Meanwhile, Zambia's head coach, George 'Miso' Orebonye, appealed directly to Zebras supporters, urging them to rally behind the team in the upcoming clash with Uganda. In an interview with Pan Africa Football, Orebonye said that a victory over the co-hosts would not only boost morale but also serve as a decisive factor in the group standings. He reminded fans that the Zebras have a strong record in away matches and that their backing could provide the extra impetus needed to secure three points.

The Cranes, aware of the mounting pressure, have been urged by former players and pundits to tighten their defensive shape and capitalize on set‑piece opportunities. ChimpReports highlighted that Uganda's recent training sessions have focused on quick transitions and aerial duels, aiming to exploit any lapses in Botswana's back line during the second leg. The upcoming fixture is expected to be a high‑intensity encounter, with both sides aware that a single goal could swing the group dynamics dramatically.

As the deadline for the group stage approaches, the remaining fixtures will determine which two teams from Group H advance to the final tournament. With Botswana, Uganda, and Zambia all within a point of each other, the next round of matches promises to be decisive, and the stakes are particularly high for Uganda, given its role as a co‑host nation.

For South African football fans, the developments in Group H carry several implications. First, the performance of neighboring nations such as Botswana and Zambia provides a benchmark for South Africa's own qualification campaign, which is currently underway in a different group. Observers note that the tight margins in Botswana's draw illustrate how defensive solidity can be as valuable as attacking flair in the qualification process.

Background

The Africa Cup of Nations, now in its 2027 edition, features a qualification phase that began in early 2025. Twenty‑four teams will ultimately compete in the finals, with the host nations automatically qualifying. Uganda and Kenya were selected as co‑hosts for the 2027 tournament, granting them a direct slot but still requiring participation in the qualifiers to maintain competitive rhythm.

Group H comprises Uganda, Botswana, Zambia, and the Central African Republic. Historically, Uganda has been a regular participant, while Botswana and Zambia have experienced fluctuating fortunes. Zambia, nicknamed the Zebras, won the AFCON title in 2012 and have since been rebuilding under a new generation of players. Botswana, often considered underdogs, have shown steady improvement in recent years, highlighted by their disciplined defensive performances.

The qualification format pits each team against the others in a home‑and‑away round‑robin system. Points are awarded using the standard three‑point win, one‑point draw system, with goal difference serving as the primary tiebreaker. As the group stage progresses, the top two teams secure a place in the finals, while the third‑placed side may still have a chance through a playoff, depending on CAF's regulations for the edition.

What happens next

The next set of matches will see Uganda travel to Gaborone for the return leg against Botswana, while Zambia hosts the Central African Republic. Both fixtures are scheduled for early December, leaving little time for teams to recover and adjust tactics. A win for Uganda would likely secure their qualification, whereas a loss could open the door for Botswana or Zambia to claim the second spot.

Stakeholders, including national federations and broadcasters, will be monitoring the outcomes closely to plan logistics, marketing campaigns, and fan engagement activities ahead of the tournament's opening ceremony in Kampala and Nairobi.
