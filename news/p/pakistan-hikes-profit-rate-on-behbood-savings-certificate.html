Pakistan hikes profit rate on Behbood Savings Certificate

The government has raised the profit rate on the Behbood Savings Certificate, offering higher returns to small savers. The move aligns with recent hikes across other National Savings instruments.

Latest developments

The Central Directorate of National Savings (CDNS) announced on October 21 that the profit rate for the Behbood Savings Certificate will rise to 9.5 percent per annum, up from the previous 8.5 percent. The increase is effective from November 1 and applies to new certificates issued after that date. The decision was part of a broader adjustment of profit rates across the CDNS portfolio, as reported by Dawn and The News Pakistan. The Behbood scheme, designed for low‑income individuals, now offers a ten‑percent higher return compared with the rate that applied during the first half of 2024. The CDNS said the change aims to keep the savings instruments competitive amid rising inflation and to attract more small‑scale deposits.

In addition to the Behbood certificate, CDNS raised the profit rates for several other key instruments, including the Regular Income Certificate, the Defense Savings Certificate, and the Special Savings Certificate. The Regular Income Certificate now yields 10.5 percent, while the Defense Savings Certificate moves to 11.0 percent. These adjustments were highlighted in a release by Mettis Global, which noted that the overall portfolio now offers some of the highest returns among government‑backed savings products in the region. The rate hikes are expected to increase the total amount of funds mobilised through the National Savings scheme, which traditionally serves as a stable source of financing for the federal budget.

Market observers noted that the higher rates could shift investor preference away from bank deposits, which currently offer lower yields due to the central bank’s policy stance. Small investors, who form the core constituency of the Behbood Certificate, are likely to benefit from the enhanced returns, especially those who rely on regular income from savings. Financial analysts cited in local media suggested that the move may also help curb the outflow of funds to informal saving channels, thereby strengthening the formal financial system.

Officials from the Ministry of Finance, speaking to the press, indicated that the rate increase is a response to persistent price pressures and the need to protect the purchasing power of low‑income savers. While no direct quotes were provided, the statements reflected a policy intent to align the profitability of government savings schemes with the prevailing inflation rate, which has hovered around 15 percent in recent months. The CDNS also reaffirmed its commitment to periodic reviews of profit rates to ensure that the schemes remain attractive and financially sustainable.

Why it matters

For ordinary Pakistanis, especially those in rural areas and low‑income brackets, the Behbood Savings Certificate is a trusted vehicle for preserving capital and earning modest returns. The rate increase translates into an additional 1 percent of profit per year, which can make a tangible difference for families relying on savings for education, health, or emergency expenses. By offering a higher yield, the government hopes to encourage more people to place their money in a regulated, low‑risk instrument rather than informal saving methods that lack legal protection.

From a fiscal perspective, the National Savings scheme provides a reliable source of non‑tax revenue for the state. Higher rates may boost subscription volumes, thereby expanding the pool of domestic financing available for public projects without increasing external borrowing. However, the increased profit payouts also raise the cost of the scheme for the treasury, requiring careful balancing against budgetary constraints.

The adjustment also has implications for the broader financial sector. Banks that have struggled to compete on interest rates may see a slowdown in deposit inflows as savers gravitate toward the more attractive government‑backed certificates. This dynamic could prompt banks to reassess their product offerings and consider alternative incentives to retain customers.

Background

The Behbood Savings Certificate, launched by the CDNS in 2019, is part of Pakistan's National Savings portfolio aimed at encouraging savings among low‑income earners. The word "Behbood" means "welfare" or "well‑being" in Urdu, reflecting the scheme's social objective. The certificate has a ten‑year maturity, with the option of early withdrawal after a specified lock‑in period, and it traditionally offers a fixed profit rate that is reviewed periodically.

National Savings schemes have long been a cornerstone of Pakistan's domestic financing strategy. They provide a safe, government‑guaranteed alternative to commercial bank deposits and have historically attracted a large segment of the population due to their simplicity and perceived security. The CDNS, operating under the Ministry of Finance, periodically adjusts profit rates to align with macro‑economic conditions, particularly inflation and the prevailing market interest rates.

Prior to the latest revision, the Behbood Certificate's profit rate of 8.5 percent was already above the average return on bank fixed deposits, but it still lagged behind the inflation rate, eroding real returns for savers. The recent hike to 9.5 percent narrows that gap, though real returns remain modest given the high inflation environment.

What happens next

The CDNS has indicated that profit rates will continue to be reviewed on a quarterly basis, taking into account inflation trends, fiscal considerations, and market demand. Investors are expected to monitor further announcements, especially as the government seeks to balance the need for attractive savings rates with the cost implications for the treasury. Analysts suggest that additional adjustments may be announced before the end of the fiscal year if inflation remains elevated.

Summary

- The Behbood Savings Certificate profit rate rises to 9.5% per annum, effective November 1. - The increase is part of a broader rate hike across National Savings instruments, including the Regular Income and Defense Savings Certificates. - Small investors benefit from higher returns, helping protect purchasing power amid high inflation. - The move aims to attract more deposits to the government’s savings scheme, providing a stable financing source for the budget. - CDNS will continue periodic reviews of profit rates, with further changes possible later in the year.
