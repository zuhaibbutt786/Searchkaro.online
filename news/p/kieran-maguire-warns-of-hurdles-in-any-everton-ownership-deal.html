Kieran Maguire warns of hurdles in any Everton ownership deal

Kieran Maguire, known for his detailed breakdowns of club finances, says selling Everton will be far from straightforward. He points to recent owner negotiations and market sentiment as key factors.

Latest developments

In a recent episode of The Football Boardroom podcast, analyst Kieran Maguire examined the practicalities of The Football Group (TFG) attempting to sell Everton. He noted that while the club's valuation has risen, the pool of qualified buyers remains limited. The discussion referenced an article on ToffeeWeb that asked how difficult a sale would be, and Maguire agreed that any transaction would need to satisfy both Premier League fit‑and‑proper tests and the expectations of a fan base that has endured years of financial uncertainty.

Meanwhile, the Liverpool Echo reported that a former Everton player, now worth an estimated £4 billion, described the Blues as a "great investment opportunity". Maguire used this comment to illustrate how high‑net‑worth individuals are beginning to view Premier League clubs as long‑term assets rather than short‑term ventures. He cautioned, however, that the presence of wealthy suitors does not automatically translate into a smooth sale process.

The i Paper highlighted that, despite speculation, there have been "no talks" over a Friedkin family sale at this stage. Maguire interpreted the lack of formal negotiations as a sign that Everton has, for now, avoided a rushed deal that could have destabilised the club's finances. He emphasized that the club's recent performance on the pitch, combined with a cautious approach from potential investors, has bought the owners additional time to consider strategic options.

Why it matters

Everton's ownership saga is more than a boardroom story; it directly impacts the club's ability to compete in the Premier League. A change in ownership could unlock fresh capital for player recruitment, stadium upgrades and debt reduction, all of which affect the team's on‑field performance and the league's overall competitiveness. Maguire stressed that fans are acutely aware of how ownership stability influences ticket prices, community projects and local employment.

Financially, a sale could reshape the balance sheet of a club that has struggled with cash flow constraints. Maguire pointed out that a well‑structured deal might allow Everton to refinance existing loans, potentially lowering interest costs and freeing up resources for strategic investment. Conversely, a poorly timed or undervalued transaction could leave the club with lingering liabilities, jeopardising its long‑term sustainability.

Background

Everton was purchased by American billionaire Shahid Khan and his family in 2016, after a period of ownership uncertainty that saw the club flirting with administration. The Friedkin family, who own a minority stake, entered the picture in 2022, adding a new layer of complexity to the ownership structure. Since then, The Football Group, a consortium of investors, has expressed interest in acquiring a controlling share, prompting speculation about a possible sale.

Kieran Maguire, a former accountant turned football finance commentator, has built a reputation for dissecting club accounts on his popular YouTube channel and in podcasts. His analysis often informs public debate on issues such as wage structures, transfer spending and ownership models. In recent weeks, Maguire has focused on Everton's financial statements, highlighting the club's modest revenue growth relative to its wage bill and the need for a capital injection to meet UEFA licensing requirements.

What happens next

The next few months are likely to see intensified due diligence from interested parties, as well as continued scrutiny from the Premier League's owners' and directors' test. Maguire expects that any credible offer will need to address the club's existing debt, provide a clear investment plan and demonstrate a commitment to the local community. He also warned that external factors, such as broader economic conditions and changes in broadcasting revenue, could influence the timing and valuation of any deal.
