Istanbul hit by heaviest rainfall in 84 years, two dead

Two people have died in Istanbul following the heaviest rainfall recorded in 84 years, according to the governor. The city is currently shut down with traffic congestion reaching 89 percent and schools closed.

Latest developments

Istanbul has been brought to a standstill by torrential downpours that officials describe as the most significant weather event in over eight decades. The governor of Istanbul confirmed that two individuals lost their lives in the aftermath of the extreme weather conditions. The intensity of the rain has overwhelmed the city’s drainage systems, leading to widespread flash flooding in low-lying areas and major thoroughfares.

The disruption has been immediate and severe. Daily Sabah reports that traffic congestion in the metropolis has reached 89 percent, effectively paralyzing the city’s transport network. Commuters are trapped in vehicles, and public transit systems have faced significant delays or suspensions due to water accumulation on tracks and roads. The scale of the disruption has forced local authorities to take decisive action to ensure public safety.

In response to the hazardous conditions, schools across Istanbul have been ordered to close. This measure aims to prevent students from navigating the flooded streets and to allow parents to remain with their children during the crisis. The closure highlights the severity of the situation, as educational institutions are rarely shut down for weather events unless the risk to life is deemed high.

The weather event has triggered a state of emergency in many districts. Rescue teams are working around the clock to evacuate residents from submerged buildings and to manage the surge in emergency calls. The sheer volume of rain has turned streets into rivers, making it difficult for emergency vehicles to reach those in need in some of the hardest-hit neighborhoods.

Background

Istanbul, a city of nearly 16 million people, is situated on the Bosphorus Strait and is prone to seasonal heavy rains. However, the current event is distinguished by its unprecedented intensity, with rainfall totals exceeding any recorded in the past 84 years. Historically, the city has experienced significant flooding, but the scale of this event surpasses previous records, raising questions about the changing patterns of precipitation in the region.

The city’s infrastructure, while extensive, has struggled to keep pace with rapid urbanization and the increasing frequency of extreme weather. Drainage systems that were adequate for historical rainfall patterns are now overwhelmed by the higher volumes of water. This event is part of a broader trend of extreme weather events affecting Europe and the Middle East, driven by rising global temperatures and shifting weather patterns.

Previous incidents of flooding in Istanbul have highlighted vulnerabilities in urban planning, particularly in older districts where drainage infrastructure is outdated. This event marks a significant escalation in the severity of these challenges, prompting a re-evaluation of the city’s long-term climate adaptation strategies.

What happens next

Authorities in Istanbul are expected to maintain emergency protocols until the weather conditions stabilize. The focus will shift from immediate rescue operations to damage assessment and cleanup efforts. Local governments will likely coordinate with national agencies to provide financial aid to affected residents and businesses, ensuring that the city can recover swiftly from the disruption.

In the coming days, meteorological services will monitor the region for any residual weather systems that could exacerbate the situation. Schools and public institutions will remain closed until safety assessments are completed. The event is likely to spark a broader national conversation in Turkey about climate resilience and infrastructure investment, potentially leading to policy changes aimed at preventing similar disasters in the future.
