Heavy Fog and Winds Trigger Red Weather Alert for Mecca

A red weather alert has been issued for Mecca following reports of dense fog and strong winds. Residents and visitors are advised to take precautionary measures.

What is happening

The Saudi Meteorological Authority released a red alert for the Mecca region after detecting unusually dense fog and sustained winds exceeding 60 km/h. The alert, the highest level in the national warning system, is active from 7:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. local time. Visibility in the city center has dropped below 500 meters, and gusts have reached 80 km/h in some areas. The warning also highlights the risk of reduced visibility for vehicular traffic and potential disruptions to the flow of pilgrims during the holy month.

What is happening

Meteorological stations across the Eastern Province reported a sudden drop in temperature, which contributed to the formation of the fog. The temperature fell from 25°C to 18°C within a few hours, creating a temperature inversion that traps moisture close to the ground. The high winds are a result of a low‑pressure system moving across the Arabian Gulf. Local authorities have instructed traffic controllers to reduce speed limits in key routes and to suspend non‑essential public transport services. Airports in the region have increased monitoring of runway conditions to ensure safe arrivals and departures.

What is happening

The red alert also advises that the combination of fog and wind may lead to sudden drops in visibility, especially near the Grand Mosque and the surrounding streets. Pilgrims are encouraged to wear reflective clothing and to avoid walking alone during peak fog hours. The Ministry of Interior has deployed additional security personnel to key checkpoints to manage crowd flow and to respond quickly to any incidents. Emergency services have been put on standby, and medical centers have increased their readiness to handle potential accidents related to the weather.

What is happening

The alert is expected to last until the evening of the same day, when temperatures are projected to rise and the wind speed to decrease. Residents are urged to stay indoors if possible, and to keep windows closed to prevent moisture buildup in homes. The authorities have also issued a public advisory through radio, television, and social media channels, reminding people to check official updates before traveling. The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah has specifically requested that pilgrimage organizers coordinate with local authorities to adjust schedules and routes in response to the weather.

Background and context

Mecca’s climate is typically hot and dry, with occasional brief periods of precipitation. However, the combination of fog and high winds is uncommon and poses unique challenges for urban infrastructure and public safety. Historically, the region has issued yellow and orange alerts for moderate weather hazards, but red alerts have been rare, occurring only during significant storms or when visibility drops below safe thresholds.

Background and context

The Saudi Meteorological Authority has a long history of issuing weather warnings to protect the public. Its warning system follows a color‑coded scheme: green for normal, yellow for caution, orange for moderate risk, and red for high risk. The red level is triggered by conditions that could lead to accidents or significant disruption, such as low visibility, strong winds, or heavy rainfall. Previous red alerts in the region have been linked to severe sandstorms or heavy rain events that caused road closures.

Background and context

The current alert follows a pattern observed during the last winter season, when a low‑pressure system moved across the Gulf and produced similar fog and wind conditions. That event led to temporary road closures and a brief pause in the Hajj procession. The authorities used that experience to prepare emergency response plans and to coordinate with transportation agencies. Lessons learned from the previous incident informed the current approach to communication and resource allocation.

What to know next

Officials advise that the weather conditions are expected to improve after 5:00 p.m. local time, when the low‑pressure system is projected to move northward. Travelers should monitor official channels for any changes to the alert status. In case of sudden visibility loss, drivers should use fog lights and maintain a safe distance from other vehicles. Those on foot should wear bright clothing and carry a flashlight if walking during low visibility periods.

What to know next

The Ministry of Interior has set up a dedicated hotline for emergency reporting, and the Red Crescent Society is on standby to provide medical assistance if needed. Residents are encouraged to keep emergency supplies, such as water, non‑perishable food, and a battery‑powered radio, in case of extended disruptions. For those planning to attend religious services, organizers are offering alternate routes and times to accommodate the weather conditions.
