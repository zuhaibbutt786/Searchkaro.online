Shubman Gill named ICC Men's Player of the Month for September 2026

Shubman Gill has been honoured as the ICC Men's Player of the Month for September 2026. The award comes as India prepares for its upcoming series in New Zealand.

Latest developments

The International Cricket Council (ICC) announced on 30 September 2026 that Shubman Gill has been named the Men's Player of the Month. The decision follows Gill's consistent performances in the international calendar, where he contributed heavily with the bat in both bilateral series and the ICC Champions Trophy. In the month under review, Gill amassed over 400 runs across three ODIs and two Tests, maintaining an average above 70. His innings included a century against England and a crucial half‑century in the final of the Champions Trophy, helping India secure a narrow victory.

The ICC release highlighted Gill's ability to adapt to different conditions, noting his technique against both pace and spin. The award ceremony, held virtually, featured a short video of Gill's top moments, which were praised by former players for their composure under pressure. The accolade adds to a growing list of Indian cricketers recognized by the ICC in recent months, reflecting the team's strong form.

Ahead of the New Zealand tour, the Indian team management welcomed the news, describing Gill's recognition as a morale boost for the squad. The upcoming series, scheduled to begin in early November, will be the first overseas assignment for Gill since his Player of the Month award. Team captain Rohit Sharma, who recently became the first cricketer to achieve the THI (Total Hit Index) milestone, expressed confidence that Gill's form will translate into success on New Zealand pitches.

In related cricket news, Shafali Verma's September performance was highlighted by Open Magazine, where she scored 360 runs and claimed six wickets, moving her into the ICC's top four women’s players. While Verma's achievements are in the women's circuit, they underscore a broader trend of Indian talent gaining global recognition during the same period.

Why it matters

Gill's award arrives at a crucial juncture for Indian cricket. The team is set to face a competitive New Zealand side known for exploiting home conditions. Having a batsman in peak form provides strategic depth, especially as the series will feature both Test and limited‑overs matches. Fans in Pakistan, where cricket enjoys massive viewership, will be closely watching the outcomes, as regional rivalries often heighten interest.

The recognition also has commercial implications. Player of the Month awards typically lead to increased endorsement opportunities and higher viewership numbers for matches featuring the awardee. Broadcasters in the sub‑continent are likely to promote Gill's appearances, potentially boosting advertising revenue. Moreover, the accolade reinforces India's standing in the ICC rankings, influencing seedings for upcoming tournaments.

Finally, the award contributes to the narrative of a new generation of Indian cricketers stepping into leadership roles. With senior players like Rohit Sharma achieving historic milestones, younger stars such as Gill and Verma are establishing themselves as reliable performers on the world stage. This shift may affect team selection policies and long‑term planning for the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI).

Background

The ICC Player of the Month award was introduced to acknowledge outstanding individual performances across all formats within a calendar month. Since its inception, the award has been presented to players from a variety of cricketing nations, reflecting the global nature of the sport. In 2025, the award gained additional visibility through a partnership with major sports broadcasters, leading to wider public awareness.

Shubman Gill made his international debut in 2020 and quickly earned a reputation for technical proficiency and temperament. Over the past six years, he has accumulated more than 5,000 runs in ODIs and over 2,000 runs in Tests. His rise coincided with a period of transition for the Indian batting line‑up, as veterans retired and new talent emerged. Gill's recent form, highlighted by his September performances, marks a continuation of his upward trajectory.

The Indian captain's THI achievement mentioned in the India.com article refers to a statistical metric that combines strike rate, boundary frequency, and consistency. Rohit Sharma's record as the first cricketer to cross the THI threshold underscores the evolving emphasis on aggressive yet efficient batting in modern cricket. This context helps explain why Gill's steady accumulation of runs is valued alongside more explosive styles.

Shafali Verma, featured in Open Magazine, represents the rapid growth of women's cricket in India. Her September blitz of 360 runs and six wickets demonstrates the depth of talent emerging from the country, which complements the men's achievements and adds to the overall cricketing narrative.

What happens next

India's next major challenge will be the three‑match Test series against New Zealand, beginning in early November 2026. Team selectors are expected to retain Gill in the top order, giving him the opportunity to convert his recent form into match‑winning innings on foreign soil. The series will also serve as preparation for the 2027 ICC World Test Championship final, where India aims to secure a top‑two finish.

The ICC will likely feature Gill in its promotional material throughout the month, and the BCCI may schedule media appearances to capitalize on his heightened profile. Observers will watch how Gill handles the pressure of expectations, especially as the Indian captain continues to set new performance benchmarks.
