FTC Moves to Hold Martin Shkreli in Contempt Over Pharma Ban Violation

The Federal Trade Commission and multiple state attorneys general have filed a contempt complaint against former biotech executive Martin Shkreli. The filing alleges he violated a lifetime ban that bars him from any involvement in the pharm

Latest developments

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) on Thursday filed a civil complaint seeking a finding of contempt against Martin Shkreli, the former biotech executive known for his 2015 price‑hike of the drug Daraprim. The complaint alleges that Shkreli violated a lifetime ban imposed by the FTC and several state regulators that prohibits him from participating in the pharmaceutical industry.

According to the filing, Shkreli continued to engage in activities that the agency classifies as “pharmaceutical conduct,” including advising a start‑up that is developing a new antiviral medication. The FTC says the conduct occurred after the ban was formally entered into the public record in 2023.

State attorneys general in New York, California and Texas have joined the FTC in the contempt claim, arguing that the ban was intended to protect patients and investors from repeat misconduct. The joint complaint seeks monetary penalties and an order that would bar Shkreli from any future involvement in drug development or marketing.

If the court finds Shkreli in contempt, the penalties could include daily fines and the possibility of imprisonment for willful violations, although the FTC typically pursues civil sanctions. Shkreli’s legal team has not yet responded publicly to the filing.

Why it matters

The case highlights the FTC’s broader effort to enforce industry bans that were introduced after high‑profile scandals. By pursuing contempt, the agency signals that lifetime prohibitions are not merely symbolic and that violations will trigger enforceable consequences.

For patients, the enforcement action aims to reduce the risk that individuals with a history of price manipulation or other misconduct re‑enter the market. The pharmaceutical sector has faced criticism for pricing practices that can limit access to essential medicines, and the Shkreli case serves as a test of regulatory resolve.

Investors and startup founders also watch the outcome because the ruling could set a precedent for how quickly and severely bans are applied across state lines. A strong enforcement signal may encourage more diligent compliance among emerging biotech firms.

Background

Martin Shkreli rose to prominence in 2015 when his company, Turing Pharmaceuticals, raised the price of the antiparasitic drug Daraprim from $13.50 to $750 per tablet. The move sparked nationwide outrage and led to criminal investigations into his business practices.

In 2022, Shkreli was convicted on securities fraud charges unrelated to the Daraprim pricing, resulting in a seven‑year prison sentence. While serving his sentence, the FTC and several state regulators imposed a lifetime ban that bars him from any role that involves the development, marketing, or sale of pharmaceutical products.

The ban is part of a series of regulatory tools introduced after the 2010s, when several high‑profile cases exposed gaps in oversight of drug pricing and corporate governance. The FTC’s authority to issue such bans stems from its mandate to protect competition and prevent unfair business practices.

What happens next

The complaint will be heard in a federal court in Washington, D.C., where a judge will decide whether Shkreli’s alleged activities constitute contempt. A hearing is expected within the next six weeks, after which the court could impose fines or other sanctions.

Both the FTC and the participating state attorneys general have indicated they will monitor any further actions by Shkreli closely, and they may seek additional injunctions if the court’s ruling does not fully address the alleged violations.
