Saudi Daily Okaz Expands Digital Reach Amid Media Shifts

Saudi Arabia’s historic daily, Okaz, is accelerating its digital transformation to meet modern readership demands. The shift reflects broader changes in the kingdom’s media environment.

Latest developments

Okaz has rolled out a refreshed website and a dedicated mobile application designed to deliver news faster and with richer multimedia. The new platform supports video clips, interactive graphics and a personalized news feed that adapts to user preferences. In parallel, the newspaper announced a partnership with regional tech firms to improve content distribution across social media channels, aiming to capture younger audiences who increasingly rely on smartphones for information.

The editorial board has added several digital‑first journalists and appointed a chief digital officer to oversee the transition. These hires bring expertise in data‑driven reporting and audience analytics, allowing Okaz to experiment with longer investigative pieces and real‑time coverage of events. A modest English‑language section has also been introduced, targeting expatriates and international readers interested in Saudi affairs.

Print circulation remains steady, but advertising revenue has shifted toward digital formats. Okaz reports that banner ads and sponsored content on its online platform now generate a larger share of total income than traditional print ads. The newspaper continues to comply with Saudi media regulations, which have been updated in recent years to include guidelines for online content, ensuring that its digital expansion aligns with national policy.

Why it matters

For readers in Saudi Arabia, the digital upgrade means faster access to reliable news without the delay of print delivery. Mobile users can receive breaking stories, live updates and region‑specific analysis directly on their devices, which is especially valuable during fast‑moving events such as economic reforms or cultural festivals.

The move also signals a broader shift in the Saudi media market, where legacy outlets are adapting to compete with newer online platforms. By strengthening its digital presence, Okaz aims to retain its influence among older readers while attracting a new generation that prefers interactive and on‑demand content.

Background

Okaz (Arabic: صحيفة عكاظ) was founded in 1960 and quickly became one of the kingdom’s most respected Arabic‑language dailies. Owned by the Okaz Organization, the paper has traditionally been known for its coverage of politics, business and cultural affairs, often reflecting a moderate editorial stance that balances government perspectives with independent reporting.

Over the past decades, Okaz has weathered the rise of satellite television and the internet, maintaining a loyal readership base. Its archives contain extensive reporting on key moments in Saudi history, from the oil boom of the 1970s to recent Vision 2030 initiatives. The newspaper’s reputation for thorough journalism has earned it several national awards and a steady position among the top three circulations in the country.

What happens next

Okaz plans to introduce AI‑assisted tools to streamline fact‑checking and content personalization over the next twelve months. The newspaper also intends to expand its English‑language offerings, potentially launching a dedicated bilingual website to reach a broader regional audience.

Summary
