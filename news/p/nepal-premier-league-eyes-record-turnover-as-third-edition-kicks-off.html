Nepal Premier League eyes record turnover as third edition kicks off

The third edition of the Nepal Premier League T20 is set to generate record revenues and attract a growing cross‑border audience. Officials and the prime minister highlight its significance for regional sport.

Latest developments

The Nepal Premier League (NPL) T20 entered its third season this year, with organizers announcing a financial target of roughly Nepalese rupees 1.5 billion in turnover. The Cricket Association of Nepal (CAN) disclosed the figure as part of its annual report, noting that the league’s commercial deals, sponsorships and broadcast rights are expected to drive the growth. At the same time, the league’s popularity is expanding beyond Nepal's borders. A Yahoo Sports article detailed how fans in India can watch the matches via live streaming platforms and specific television channels, reflecting a rising demand for the tournament in neighboring markets. In Pakistan, interest has surged, with online searches indicating a spike in curiosity about how to follow the NPL. Prime Minister Pushpa Kamal Dahal, commonly known as Prachanda, praised the league as a celebration for Nepali sports, emphasizing its role in promoting cricket and national pride. The combination of financial ambition, cross‑border viewership, and political endorsement positions the NPL as a focal point of South Asian cricket this season.

Why it matters

For Pakistani cricket fans, the NPL offers an additional source of high‑quality T20 action, complementing domestic and international fixtures. The ability to stream the league from India also creates a shared viewing experience across the subcontinent, potentially strengthening commercial ties between broadcasters in Pakistan, India and Nepal. The projected 1.5 billion rupee turnover signals that the league is becoming a significant economic engine, attracting sponsors who see value in reaching a regional audience. Moreover, the prime minister's public endorsement underscores the government's willingness to support sports initiatives that can boost tourism, create jobs and foster a sense of community. As the tournament progresses, local businesses in Kathmandu and other host cities anticipate increased footfall, while young Nepali cricketers gain a platform to showcase talent on a larger stage.

Background

The Nepal Premier League was launched in 2022 as a franchise‑based T20 competition aimed at professionalising cricket in the country. Initially, the league featured four teams and modest prize money, but it quickly garnered attention for its competitive matches and enthusiastic fan base. The Cricket Association of Nepal, the sport's governing body, has overseen the league's expansion, securing broadcast agreements with regional networks and digital platforms. Over the past two editions, the NPL has attracted overseas players, raising the standard of play and drawing interest from cricket‑savvy audiences in South Asia. The recent financial target reflects both inflationary pressures and the ambition to position the NPL alongside other emerging T20 leagues in the region.

What happens next

The third edition will run for eight weeks, with matches scheduled across multiple venues in Nepal. Broadcast partners in India have confirmed live streaming slots, while Pakistani fans are expected to follow the action through online platforms and satellite channels that carry the feed. CAN has pledged to release detailed financial statements after the season, addressing questions raised by stakeholders about revenue distribution and transparency. Observers will watch whether the league meets its turnover goal and how the financial performance influences future sponsorship deals.
