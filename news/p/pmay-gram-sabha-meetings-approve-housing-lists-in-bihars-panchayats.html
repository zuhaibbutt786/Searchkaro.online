PMAY Gram Sabha meetings approve housing lists in Bihar's panchayats

Gram Sabha gatherings in three Bihar districts have cleared waiting lists for the Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana. Eligible families are set to receive permanent houses under the national rural housing programme.

Latest developments

On Monday, the Sanjhauli block in Rohtas district convened a Gram Sabha that released the final list of beneficiaries under the Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) rural housing scheme. The meeting, attended by local officials and community representatives, confirmed that the listed families will receive pucca (permanent) houses as part of the government's pledge to provide shelter to all rural households.

In a separate Gram Sabha held in Ramnagar, officials from the district administration presented and approved a waiting list for the same scheme. The list includes households that met the eligibility criteria but were not part of the earlier rounds. The approval marks a critical step toward expanding the reach of the programme in the district's panchayats.

Further north, the Khalwa panchayat in Siwan district conducted its own Gram Sabha where the names of 743 beneficiaries were formally adopted. The meeting recorded the names, family details and the allocation of plots for construction, aligning with the central government's target of delivering houses to 1.2 crore families by 2025.

Across all three locations, the Gram Sabha process involved verification of documents, cross‑checking of socio‑economic data and public disclosure of the final lists. The meetings were observed by district magistrates and local elected representatives, ensuring transparency and community participation in the allocation of housing resources.

Why it matters

The approval of beneficiary lists directly impacts thousands of rural households that have been living in inadequate conditions. Access to a permanent house improves health outcomes, educational attainment for children and overall economic stability. In Bihar, where a significant portion of the population resides in temporary structures, the PMAY scheme is a key instrument for reducing rural poverty.

For the state government, the successful conduct of Gram Sabha meetings demonstrates compliance with central directives and showcases progress in meeting national housing targets. It also strengthens the trust between local administrations and the communities they serve, as residents see tangible benefits from policy implementation.

Background

The Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, launched in 2015, aims to provide affordable housing for all urban and rural citizens by 2022, a deadline later extended to 2025. Under the rural component, known as PMAY‑GR, each eligible family is promised a pucca house with basic amenities. The scheme is funded jointly by the central and state governments, with a focus on the economically weaker sections.

Gram Sabha meetings are a statutory requirement for the finalisation of beneficiary lists. They serve as a platform for community members to review and challenge the proposed allocations, ensuring that the process is transparent and that no eligible family is omitted. In Bihar, the state has been actively organising such meetings across its districts to accelerate the rollout of the scheme.

What happens next

Following the approval of the lists, the next phase involves the release of funds to local contractors and the commencement of construction. Monitoring committees, comprising district officials and community members, will oversee the quality and timeliness of the work. The first houses are expected to be completed within the next six to eight months, subject to weather conditions and supply chain factors.

The state government has indicated that additional Gram Sabha meetings will be scheduled in the coming weeks to address any pending appeals and to update waiting lists in other districts.

Summary

- Gram Sabha meetings in Sanjhauli, Ramnagar and Siwan have approved PMAY beneficiary lists, covering thousands of families. - Approved lists trigger the release of construction funds and the start of house building, aiming for completion within eight months. - The process enhances rural living standards and aligns Bihar with the national goal of universal rural housing by 2025.
