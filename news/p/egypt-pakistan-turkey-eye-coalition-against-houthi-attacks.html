Egypt, Pakistan, Turkey eye coalition against Houthi attacks

Egypt announced readiness to support Saudi Arabia against Iran‑aligned Houthi strikes. Pakistan, Turkey and an African force are assessing joint defence measures.

Latest developments

Egyptian officials announced on Tuesday that they are prepared to provide military support to Saudi Arabia if the kingdom faces a direct confrontation with the Iran‑aligned Houthi rebels in Yemen. The statement, delivered by the foreign ministry, said Cairo would supply the necessary equipment and logistical assistance to help Saudi forces repel any Houthi aggression. The declaration follows a series of Houthi missile and drone strikes that have targeted Saudi oil facilities and civilian infrastructure over the past month. Saudi Arabia has repeatedly blamed Tehran for arming and directing the rebel group, a claim that Iran denies. In response, Riyadh has called on regional partners to strengthen its defence posture. In a parallel development, a senior military commander from an unnamed African nation said his forces are evaluating the possibility of joining a coalition that includes Pakistan and Turkey to protect Saudi interests against Houthi attacks. The commander highlighted the shared strategic concerns of the three countries and suggested that a coordinated response could deter further escalation. Washington has been monitoring the situation closely, with US officials warning that any expansion of the conflict could threaten international shipping lanes in the Red Sea. While the United States has not formally joined the proposed coalition, it has increased its naval presence in the region and continues to provide intelligence to Saudi and allied forces. The emerging alignment reflects a broader pattern of Middle Eastern and African states seeking to counter what they perceive as Iranian influence through proxy groups. Analysts note that Egypt’s willingness to act, combined with potential African involvement, could shift the balance of power in the Gulf, especially if Pakistan and Turkey formalise their support.

Why it matters

For Pakistan, the prospect of joining a Saudi‑Egypt‑Turkey coalition offers an opportunity to deepen defence ties with Gulf states and to showcase its role as a regional security partner. Pakistani media have highlighted the potential economic benefits of stronger Saudi‑Pakistani cooperation, especially in energy and trade. In Pakistan, the Red Sea route is a vital artery for imports and exports. Any disruption caused by Houthi attacks could raise shipping costs and affect the price of oil, which directly influences the Pakistani economy. A coordinated defence effort aims to keep these sea lanes open, safeguarding trade and energy supplies.

Background

The Houthi movement, officially known as Ansar Allah, seized control of Yemen’s capital Sanaa in 2014 and has since been engaged in a civil war that pits it against a Saudi‑backed government. Tehran has been accused of supplying the rebels with weapons, a claim it partially acknowledges but frames as political support. Egypt and Saudi Arabia have a long‑standing security partnership dating back to the 1970s, often coordinating on counter‑terrorism and regional stability. Recent years have seen Egypt adopt a more assertive stance against Iranian influence, aligning with Gulf states on several diplomatic fronts.

What happens next

The coalition’s next step is expected to be a series of high‑level meetings in Riyadh to define the scope of military assistance and rules of engagement. Observers say the outcome will depend on how quickly the parties can agree on logistics and command structures.

Summary

• Egypt has pledged to supply military aid to Saudi Arabia against Houthi aggression. • Pakistan and Turkey are evaluating participation in a joint defence effort. • An African military is also considering joining the coalition. • The United States is increasing naval presence but has not formally joined. • The alliance aims to protect Red Sea shipping lanes and curb Iranian proxy influence.
