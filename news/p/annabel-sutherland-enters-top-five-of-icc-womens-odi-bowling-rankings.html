Annabel Sutherland enters top five of ICC Women’s ODI bowling rankings

Australia's all‑rounder Annabel Sutherland has broken into the top five of the ICC women's ODI bowling rankings. The move reflects a rapid rise for the 23‑year‑old and adds fresh intrigue for fans in Pakistan.

Latest developments

Annabel Sutherland, the 23‑year‑old Australian pace bowler, has risen to fourth place in the ICC women's One Day International (ODI) bowling rankings, according to the latest release from Cricinfo. The ascent follows a series of strong performances in recent bilateral series, where she consistently delivered economical spells and key wickets. Her new position places her just behind England's Nat Sciver‑Brunt and New Zealand's Amelia Kerr, while teammate Megan Schutt holds the fifth spot.

The updated rankings also show New Zealand's Amelia Litchfield edging closer to the top three, while Sri Lanka's Anushka Sanjeewani (referred to as Mostary in the ICC release) achieved a career‑best climb. These movements indicate a tightening competition among the leading bowlers ahead of the upcoming ICC Women's World Cup qualifiers. The ICC's official statement highlighted the dynamic nature of the women's game, noting that several emerging talents are reshaping the hierarchy.

In parallel, the Daily Sun reported that Indian bowlers Sobhna and Joty have also gained ground in the rankings before the final Australia‑India ODI. Their progress underscores a broader trend of increased depth across the women's bowling pool, with multiple nations improving their standing simultaneously. The report emphasized that these gains could influence team selections for the forthcoming series.

Cricket analysts in Pakistan have taken note of Sutherland's rise, noting that her ability to swing the ball both ways and extract bounce on sub‑continental pitches could pose a significant challenge for Pakistani batters in future encounters. Social media buzz in Pakistan reflects heightened interest, with fans discussing how Sutherland's form might affect upcoming bilateral series and the ICC Women's Championship schedule.

For cricket followers in Pakistan, Sutherland's entry into the top five signals a shift in the competitive balance of women's cricket. The increased attention may also translate into higher viewership for women's matches, benefitting broadcasters and sponsors alike.

The ICC women's ODI bowling rankings are calculated based on a bowler's performance over the past 12 months, taking into account wickets taken, economy rate, and the strength of opposition.
