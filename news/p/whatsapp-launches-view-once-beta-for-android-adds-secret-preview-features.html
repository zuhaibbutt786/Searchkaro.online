WhatsApp Launches ‘View Once’ Beta for Android, Adds Secret Preview Features

WhatsApp has introduced a beta feature that allows Android users to view text messages only once. The update also offers a way to preview chats without sending read receipts.

Latest developments

WhatsApp’s latest beta release for Android users includes a ‘View Once’ text message feature that lets a message be seen only a single time before it disappears. The feature is currently available to a limited group of beta testers and is expected to roll out more broadly in the coming weeks. In addition to the one‑time view, the update introduces a preview mode that lets users glance at the content of a conversation without opening it, thereby preventing the automatic blue tick that normally indicates a message has been read. This preview functionality is designed to give users more control over when they acknowledge a message, a change that could appeal to those concerned about privacy.

The beta rollout follows a period of internal testing and feedback collection from early adopters. WhatsApp has not yet released official documentation on how the preview mode will affect message delivery or whether it will be available for all types of messages, including media and group chats. However, early reports suggest that the preview will work similarly to the existing notification preview, displaying a snippet of the message without revealing the full text until the user taps the conversation.

The company’s decision to add these features comes amid growing scrutiny over messaging apps’ handling of user data and the desire for more granular privacy controls. By allowing messages to be viewed only once and providing a non‑intrusive preview option, WhatsApp is attempting to give users a clearer sense of when their messages are truly read.

WhatsApp’s beta testing phase will likely include a mix of feedback mechanisms, such as in‑app surveys and usage analytics, to gauge how users interact with the new features. The company has indicated that it will use this data to refine the experience before a full release, ensuring that the final product aligns with user expectations and regulatory requirements.

Why it matters

For users in Pakistan, where WhatsApp is the dominant messaging platform, the new features could significantly alter daily communication habits. The ability to view a message only once may reduce the pressure to respond immediately, allowing users to manage conversations at their own pace.

The preview mode, which does not trigger a blue tick, addresses concerns about being constantly monitored by friends and family. In a society where social dynamics often hinge on prompt responses, this feature offers a subtle way to maintain privacy without overtly hiding messages.

Moreover, the beta release may influence how businesses and customer support teams in Pakistan interact with clients. Companies that rely on WhatsApp for service requests may need to adjust their response protocols to accommodate the new read‑receipt mechanics.

Background

WhatsApp, owned by Meta Platforms, has historically relied on end‑to‑end encryption to secure user data. Over the years, the app has introduced various privacy enhancements, such as disappearing messages and read receipts toggles.

The ‘View Once’ concept first appeared on the iOS version of the app in 2020, allowing users to send photos and videos that vanish after a single view. The Android implementation of this feature has been delayed, with beta testers now receiving the functionality.

The preview mode is reminiscent of notification previews found on many smartphones, but WhatsApp’s approach is unique in that it does not mark the message as read, thereby preserving the sender’s ability to gauge whether the message has been seen.

What happens next

WhatsApp is expected to monitor beta user engagement closely, collecting data on how often the preview mode is used and whether the one‑time view feature leads to changes in response times. The company will likely release a public update with detailed instructions and a timeline for a wider rollout.

Regulators in Pakistan may also scrutinize the new privacy controls, particularly in light of recent discussions about digital communication standards and data protection laws. If the features prove popular, WhatsApp could expand them to other regions, potentially setting a new benchmark for privacy in instant messaging.
