Sudanese Forces Strike RSF Command Centers in Nyala Amid Civilian Casualties

Latest developments

The Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) have announced the execution of retaliatory strikes against Rapid Support Force (RSF) command centers in Nyala, the capital of South Darfur state. According to reports from Al Jazeera, the military operations were designed to dismantle key leadership nodes of the paramilitary group, marking a significant escalation in the ground war within the region. These strikes represent a shift in tactical focus, aiming to degrade the organizational capacity of the RSF in one of the conflict's most volatile strongholds.

In a separate but related incident, Al-Arabiya reported that a drone attack in South Darfur resulted in the death and injury of more than 70 civilians. The high casualty count highlights the increasing danger posed by aerial warfare in densely populated areas. Local sources indicate that the attack targeted a crowded location, leading to widespread destruction and a severe strain on local medical facilities, which are already operating under extreme pressure.

The Democratic Unionist Party (DUP), a political faction aligned with the military, accused General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan of ordering strikes on civilians in Nyala. In statements to Erem News, the DUP alleged that the military's actions have disregarded international humanitarian law, further complicating the political narrative surrounding the conflict. These accusations add a layer of internal political tension to the ongoing military confrontation.

The simultaneous occurrence of military strikes and civilian casualties has drawn international attention. Observers note that the intensity of the fighting in Darfur has reached new heights, with both parties utilizing heavy weaponry in urban environments. The lack of clear demarcation between military targets and civilian infrastructure continues to be a primary driver of the humanitarian crisis.

Local residents in Nyala and surrounding areas have reported widespread power outages and communication disruptions following the strikes. The situation on the ground remains fluid, with sporadic clashes reported in various districts. Aid organizations are struggling to access affected areas due to active hostilities, leaving thousands without essential medical care and food supplies.

Why it matters

For readers in South Africa, the escalation in Sudan carries significant regional implications, particularly regarding migration and security. South Africa has a substantial community of Sudanese refugees and economic ties with the Horn of Africa. The intensification of the conflict in Darfur is likely to trigger new waves of displacement, potentially increasing pressure on neighboring countries and affecting regional stability.

The humanitarian crisis in South Darfur serves as a critical indicator of the broader conflict's trajectory. As civilian casualties mount, the global community faces increasing pressure to intervene. South African policymakers and civil society groups have historically been active in advocating for peace in the region, and the recent events may prompt renewed diplomatic engagement or humanitarian aid initiatives.

Furthermore, the use of drones and heavy artillery in urban centers underscores the evolving nature of modern warfare in the region. This has implications for regional security architectures and the potential for the conflict to spill over into neighboring states. Monitoring the situation is essential for understanding the broader geopolitical shifts in East Africa.

Background

The conflict in Sudan began in April 2023 when fighting erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces, led by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, and the Rapid Support Force, commanded by General Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as Hemedti. The two generals had previously been allies in the 2021 coup that ousted the civilian government, but their relationship deteriorated over disputes regarding the integration of the RSF into the regular army.

Darfur, a region in western Sudan, has been a focal point of the conflict since its inception. The RSF has maintained a strong presence in the area, while the SAF has sought to retake key cities. The conflict has resulted in a severe humanitarian crisis, with millions displaced and widespread reports of atrocities, including sexual violence and ethnic targeting.

International mediation efforts, led by the United States and Saudi Arabia, have so far failed to achieve a lasting ceasefire. The conflict has drawn in various regional actors, complicating diplomatic solutions. The recent escalation in Nyala and the high civilian casualty figures in South Darfur highlight the continued failure of these efforts to protect civilians and de-escalate tensions.

What happens next

In the immediate term, both parties are expected to continue their military operations in Darfur. The SAF may attempt to consolidate its gains in Nyala, while the RSF is likely to launch counter-offensives to reclaim lost territory. The humanitarian situation is expected to deteriorate further unless a ceasefire is implemented, with aid agencies warning of famine conditions in some areas.

Diplomatically, the international community may increase pressure on the warring parties to allow humanitarian access and protect civilians. There is a possibility of renewed mediation efforts, although the likelihood of success remains uncertain. The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the conflict expands further or if a fragile pause in hostilities can be achieved.
