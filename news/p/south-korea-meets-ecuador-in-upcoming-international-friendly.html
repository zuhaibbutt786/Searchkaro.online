South Korea meets Ecuador in upcoming international friendly

South Korea and Ecuador will face each other in a scheduled friendly match in June 2024. Fans in Pakistan are looking for line‑ups, broadcast details and how to watch the game live.

What is happening

The national teams of South Korea and Ecuador are set to play a friendly match on 15 June 2024 in Seoul, South Korea. The fixture is part of the teams' preparation schedules ahead of upcoming continental tournaments and World Cup qualifiers. Both federations have confirmed the date and venue, and the match will be played at the Seoul World Cup Stadium, a venue that hosted the 2002 FIFA World Cup final.

South Korea will enter the game with Son Heung‑min wearing the captain's armband, a decision confirmed by the Korean Football Association. Ecuador will be led by their regular captain, but the coaching staff has also announced that midfielder Roberto Moreno will make his senior debut in this encounter. The line‑ups are expected to feature a mix of established internationals and younger players seeking to impress ahead of competitive fixtures later in the year.

The match will be broadcast live on national television in both countries and streamed online through official federation channels. In South Korea, the game will be shown on KBS2, while Ecuadorian viewers can watch via TVC Sports. International fans can follow the live stream on the FIFA website and on platforms such as YouTube and Twitch that have secured streaming rights. Kick‑off is scheduled for 20:00 local time (13:00 GMT), a time that accommodates audiences across Asia, Europe and the Americas.

Background and context

South Korea and Ecuador have met only a handful of times in official competition. Their most recent encounter was a friendly in 2018, which ended in a 2‑2 draw. Historically, the two sides have not faced each other in World Cup qualifiers or major tournaments, making each meeting an opportunity to gauge each other's style. Both nations have been active in rebuilding their squads after recent qualification cycles, and the friendly provides a low‑stakes environment for tactical experimentation.

In the months leading up to the June match, South Korea has been preparing for the AFC Asian Cup qualifiers, while Ecuador has been focusing on the CONMEBOL World Cup qualifying campaign. South Korea’s recent performances have shown a solid defensive structure and an emphasis on quick transitions, whereas Ecuador has emphasized a more possession‑based approach under their current coach. The friendly will allow both coaches to test new formations and assess player fitness ahead of the competitive fixtures that follow.
