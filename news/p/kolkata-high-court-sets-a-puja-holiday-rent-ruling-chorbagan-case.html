Kolkata High Court sets a Puja holiday, rent ruling, Chorbagan case

The Calcutta High Court announced a revised Puja holiday schedule, a landmark rent‑allowance ruling for separated spouses, and new details in the Chorbagan stampede investigation.

Latest developments

The Calcutta High Court, commonly referred to as the Kolkata High Court, issued an order that will shift the start of the annual Puja holiday to the day after Mahalaya beginning in 2027. Mahalaya marks the beginning of the Durga Puja festivities in West Bengal, and the court's decision aligns the judicial calendar with cultural observances, allowing judges and court staff to observe the holiday without disrupting case management. The order was delivered in a written judgment released in early June and will be incorporated into the court's official holiday list for the next decade.

In a separate judgment, a bench of the High Court ruled that a husband or wife who lives separately from their partner is entitled to claim the full amount of house‑rent allowance under the state service rules. The decision clarified that the allowance, traditionally calculated on a prorated basis when spouses share a residence, can be granted in full when the marital home is not shared due to separation or other legitimate reasons. The bench cited recent service‑rule amendments and emphasized the principle of equal financial support for government employees regardless of personal circumstances.

The court also addressed developments in the long‑running Chorbagan stampede case. During a hearing, the wife of announcer Babu Majumdar testified that her husband had been instructed not to surrender to authorities before October 27, a detail that could affect the timeline of the investigation. The case stems from a tragic stampede that occurred at a public gathering in the Chorbagan area of Kolkata, resulting in multiple casualties and a series of criminal inquiries. The bench noted the new testimony and ordered the investigating agency to submit a revised report within four weeks.

Legal analysts noted that the three rulings, while unrelated in subject matter, collectively illustrate the High Court's active role in shaping administrative policy, social welfare, and criminal procedure. Bar associations in Kolkata welcomed the rent‑allowance decision as a progressive step for employee rights, while cultural groups praised the alignment of the Puja holiday with traditional observances. The Chorbagan case update, however, has reignited public debate over police handling of large gatherings and the transparency of investigative processes.

Why it matters

The adjustment of the Puja holiday schedule has practical implications for thousands of court employees, lawyers, and litigants who rely on predictable court calendars. By synchronising the holiday with the cultural calendar, the court reduces the likelihood of last‑minute case adjournments and improves access to justice during one of the busiest periods of the year. For the broader public, the change signals respect for regional traditions within the judiciary.

The rent‑allowance ruling directly affects government servants who are separated but continue to meet their financial obligations. Full reimbursement of house rent can ease the economic burden on families, especially women who often shoulder housing costs after a separation. This decision may set a precedent for other state tribunals to review their own allowance policies, potentially leading to wider reforms across India.

Finally, the Chorbagan stampede update touches on public safety and accountability. The revelation that a key witness was advised to delay surrender raises questions about investigative transparency and possible procedural interference. The outcome of the case could influence how authorities manage crowd control at large events and how evidence is gathered in high‑profile criminal investigations.

Background

The Kolkata High Court, established in 1862, is the highest judicial authority in the state of West Bengal. It oversees civil, criminal, and constitutional matters and frequently issues directives that affect state administration. The court's calendar traditionally includes a set of holidays that align with national and regional festivals, but occasional revisions are made to accommodate changing societal needs.

Durga Puja, the most celebrated festival in West Bengal, begins with Mahalaya, a day that signals the start of the festivities. Historically, the court observed a holiday period that sometimes began before Mahalaya, leading to scheduling conflicts for lawyers and litigants. The 2027 amendment seeks to standardise the holiday period, ensuring that the court resumes work after the first day of Puja.

House‑rent allowance for government employees has been a contentious issue for years. The allowance is intended to offset the cost of accommodation for staff posted away from their permanent residence. Prior to the recent ruling, the allowance was often reduced when spouses lived apart, a practice that many argued discriminated against separated families. The High Court's decision reflects a shift towards a more equitable interpretation of service rules.

The Chorbagan stampede occurred during a public event in October 2023, resulting in dozens of injuries and several fatalities. The incident prompted multiple inquiries, with the state police filing charges against organizers and officials. Over the past year, the case has seen several procedural delays, and the recent testimony about a delayed surrender adds another layer of complexity to the investigation.

What happens next

The new Puja holiday schedule will be formally incorporated into the court's annual calendar after the Ministry of Law and Justice issues a notification, expected within the next two months. Law firms and litigants are advised to adjust filing deadlines accordingly.

The rent‑allowance judgment will be circulated to all state government departments, and the finance ministry is expected to issue detailed guidelines on implementation. Affected employees can file for the full allowance starting from the date of the judgment, subject to standard verification procedures.

In the Chorbagan case, the investigating agency must submit its revised report by early December. The High Court will review the report and may schedule a further hearing to determine whether any procedural irregularities occurred. Both the prosecution and defence have indicated they may file appeals if the final order does not align with their expectations.
