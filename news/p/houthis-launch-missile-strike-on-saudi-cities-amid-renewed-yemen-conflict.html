Houthis launch missile strike on Saudi cities amid renewed Yemen conflict

Houthis launched missile strikes on Saudi cities, sparking fresh tensions in the Gulf. The move highlights Iran's alleged backing of the rebels and raises concerns over regional security.

Latest developments

On 18 October 2026, the Houthi movement in Yemen launched a series of missile strikes targeting several Saudi Arabian cities, including Riyadh and Dammam. The attack was reported by Al Jazeera and confirmed by Saudi air defence units, which intercepted most of the projectiles before they reached populated areas. The missiles were launched from positions along Yemen's northern front, close to the border with Saudi territory.

Saudi Arabia responded by deploying additional air defence batteries and calling for emergency meetings of the National Security Council. The government issued a statement warning that the country would take all necessary measures to protect its citizens and infrastructure. The incident has prompted the Saudi military to increase patrols along the border and to review its early warning systems.

The Yemeni foreign minister, Abdulmalik Al-Maqtari, stated that Iran is a party to the conflict and that those who supply arms to the Houthis cannot act as mediators. His remarks were published by Arab News and reflect growing concerns that external support is fueling the violence. Al-Maqtari also urged the international community to hold Iran accountable for its role in arming the rebels.

In a separate statement, Yemen rejected a mediation bid from Iran, even as Tehran continues to provide weapons to the Houthi fighters. The Asharq Al‑Awsat report highlighted that the Yemeni government views Iran's involvement as a direct threat to regional stability and has called for a comprehensive ceasefire that excludes external actors.

Why it matters

The missile strikes have immediate implications for Saudi Arabia's economic stability, as the country hosts some of the world's largest oil and petrochemical facilities. Disruption of supply chains or damage to critical infrastructure could affect global markets. The attacks also raise concerns among Saudi residents about personal safety and the effectiveness of the country's defence systems.

For the broader Gulf region, the incident signals a potential widening of the Yemen conflict. Neighboring states such as the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain are watching closely, as any escalation could spill over into maritime routes that are vital for international trade. The attacks also reinforce fears that the conflict could draw in more external actors, complicating diplomatic efforts.

Background

The Houthi movement, officially known as Ansar Allah, emerged in the 1990s as a Zaidi Shia group in northern Yemen. Over the past decade, it has expanded its influence, culminating in the takeover of the capital Sana'a in 2014. The group has been in conflict with the internationally recognised Yemeni government since 2015, with Saudi Arabia leading a coalition that supports the government.

Iran has been accused of providing financial, logistical and military support to the Houthis, a claim that the Iranian government denies. The relationship has been a point of contention in the broader Saudi–Iran rivalry, with both countries vying for influence in the Arabian Peninsula. The recent missile attacks have intensified scrutiny of Iran's role in the conflict.

The Yemen war has caused a humanitarian crisis, with millions displaced and widespread shortages of food and medicine. International organisations have called for an immediate ceasefire and a political solution that addresses the root causes of the conflict. The current escalation threatens to undermine these efforts.

What happens next

In the coming days, Saudi Arabia is expected to convene a joint security meeting with its Gulf allies to assess the threat level and coordinate a response. Diplomatic channels may be reopened to negotiate a ceasefire, though the involvement of external actors such as Iran could complicate negotiations.

The United Nations Security Council may also consider a resolution urging all parties to halt hostilities and to facilitate humanitarian access. Meanwhile, the international community will monitor the situation closely, as any further escalation could impact global oil prices and regional stability.
