Pakistani Court Bars Arrest of Former AG as US Actress Detained on Flight

Latest developments

The Peshawar High Court issued a directive on Monday ordering law‑enforcement agencies to refrain from arresting or harassing the former Attorney General of Pakistan. The court’s order came after a petition argued that the former AG was being targeted without clear evidence, and it emphasized the need for due process under the constitution.

In a separate incident that captured international headlines, actress Selma Blair was taken into custody after a confrontation with flight crew on a transatlantic flight. According to reports, Blair struck a crew member during a heated exchange, prompting the captain to request assistance from authorities upon landing. Police in the destination country detained her and she was later charged with assault.

Both events unfolded within a short span, highlighting how arrest procedures are scrutinised in different jurisdictions. While the Pakistani court’s order seeks to protect a high‑profile legal figure from perceived misuse of power, the airline incident underscores the challenges of maintaining order on commercial flights and the legal consequences that can follow.

Why it matters

The court's directive has immediate implications for the rule of law in Pakistan. By explicitly telling law‑enforcement agencies not to arrest the former AG, the judiciary is reinforcing the principle that arrests must be based on solid evidence and not on political pressure. Observers say the ruling could set a precedent for how former officials are treated in future investigations, potentially curbing arbitrary detentions.

For the aviation sector, Selma Blair's arrest raises questions about passenger conduct and crew safety. Airlines have strict protocols for handling disruptive behaviour, and the incident may prompt a review of training and response measures. The case also illustrates how public figures can influence public perception of airline security policies, especially when media coverage is extensive.

Together, these stories reflect broader concerns about accountability, the balance between authority and individual rights, and the ways in which high‑profile arrests shape public discourse in Pakistan and beyond.

Background

The former Attorney General, who served under the previous government, has been under investigation for alleged misuse of office. Critics argued that the investigations were politically motivated, while supporters insisted that accountability was necessary. Prior to the court's order, the former AG faced multiple summonses and brief detentions, which he described as harassment. The High Court’s intervention is rooted in constitutional safeguards that protect citizens from unlawful arrest, a principle that has been invoked in several high‑profile cases in recent years.

Selma Blair, known for her acting career and public advocacy, has previously spoken about health challenges and personal resilience. The flight incident occurred on a long‑haul route from New York to London, where she reportedly became upset after a seating dispute. Crew members reported that Blair struck a flight attendant, leading to a loss of composure that threatened the safety of other passengers. After the aircraft landed, local police detained her, and she later issued a statement saying she "did not intend to harm anyone" and apologized for her actions. The incident has been covered by major outlets, including the BBC and TMZ, each providing differing levels of detail.

What happens next

In Pakistan, the former Attorney General may seek to have any pending cases dismissed or reviewed in light of the High Court's order. Legal analysts expect the prosecution to file an appeal, which could lead to a higher court clarifying the standards for arresting former officials.

Selma Blair is expected to appear before a court in the jurisdiction where she was arrested. Her legal team has indicated they will argue that the incident was a momentary lapse and that she should face a reduced charge. Airline regulators may also issue new guidelines for handling disruptive passengers, especially high‑profile individuals, to prevent similar occurrences.
