Military developments in Sudan and Lebanon draw global attention

Sudan has begun a large‑scale ground operation in Kordofan while an international summit in Paris seeks to reinforce Lebanon's armed forces, prompting worldwide interest.

What is happening

On 17 September 2026, the Sudanese army announced the launch of a large‑scale ground operation across the central region of Kordofan. The operation, described by military officials as a coordinated effort to neutralize armed groups that have been active in the area for several months, involves infantry units, armored vehicles and artillery support. Commanders said the offensive targets several strongholds that are believed to be supplying weapons to rebel factions. The move follows a series of smaller engagements that have escalated tensions between the government and local militias, and it marks the most extensive deployment of troops in the province since the conflict began in 2023.

In parallel, a high‑level conference convened in Paris on 18 September to discuss ways to strengthen the Lebanese armed forces. Delegates from France, the United States, the United Nations and several Arab states gathered to review security assistance packages, training programs and equipment upgrades. The summit produced a joint statement emphasizing the need for a sustainable security framework that respects Lebanon's sovereignty while addressing cross‑border threats and internal instability.

The two events are linked by a broader pattern of regional actors seeking to assert influence through military means or support. France, which hosts the Paris meeting, has recently increased its advisory presence in the Middle East, while the United States continues to monitor Sudanese movements for potential spill‑over effects. Meanwhile, diplomatic channels in the African Union are attempting to mediate between Sudan's central government and opposition groups, highlighting the complex interplay of local and international security concerns.

Media outlets across the Middle East and Africa have reported on both developments, noting that the Sudanese operation could affect humanitarian corridors and that the Paris summit may reshape the balance of power within Lebanon's fragile political system. Analysts caution that while each initiative is presented as a step toward stability, the outcomes will depend on implementation, local acceptance and the broader geopolitical climate.

International journalists, policy makers and diaspora communities are looking for details on the commitments made, the scope of assistance, and how these measures might influence Lebanon's internal politics and its relations with neighboring countries.

Background and context

Sudan has been embroiled in intermittent conflict since the overthrow of President Omar al‑Bashir in 2019. The power vacuum allowed multiple armed groups to gain footholds, particularly in the resource‑rich Kordofan region. Previous ceasefire attempts have repeatedly broken down, and the government has struggled to maintain control over remote areas, leading to periodic escalations such as the current operation.

Lebanon's military, known locally as the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF), has faced chronic underfunding, political interference and the lingering presence of non‑state actors. The 2020 Beirut port explosion and subsequent economic collapse weakened state institutions, prompting calls for external support. The Paris summit builds on earlier agreements signed in 2021 and 2024, aiming to provide a more coordinated and long‑term security strategy.

What to know next

Observers will watch for official statements from Sudan's Ministry of Defense confirming the progress of the Kordofan operation and any reported civilian impact. Humanitarian agencies are likely to issue alerts if displacement increases, and neighboring countries may adjust border security measures accordingly.

In Lebanon, the next steps involve the deployment of training teams, delivery of equipment and the establishment of joint monitoring mechanisms as outlined in the Paris agreement. The Lebanese government is expected to publish a detailed implementation plan within the coming weeks, and international donors will assess funding allocations based on measurable benchmarks.
