Pakistan Launches New Cybercrime Agency to Tackle Online Fraud

Pakistan has created a new agency to tackle cybercrime, focusing on financial fraud and online harassment. The move follows a government directive to strengthen digital law enforcement.

Latest developments

In early September, the Pakistani government announced the creation of the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) to centralise efforts against online fraud. The agency will operate under the Ministry of Interior and will coordinate with federal and provincial law‑enforcement bodies. It follows a directive from Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi to intensify crackdowns on cybercrime. The NCCIA is expected to investigate a range of offenses, from phishing scams to online harassment. It will also focus on financial fraud cases that have seen a sharp rise during the pandemic. The agency will be equipped with advanced forensic tools and a dedicated cyber‑crime unit. Officials say the new body will help streamline investigations and improve inter‑agency communication. The NCCIA will also work with international partners to track cross‑border cyber‑criminals. The establishment comes as Pakistan seeks to align its cyber‑law framework with global best practices. The announcement was welcomed by civil society groups who have long called for stronger cyber‑crime laws. They noted that a single agency could reduce delays and bring more cases to court. The government has also pledged to provide training for law‑enforcement officers on cyber‑crime detection.

Why it matters

For ordinary citizens, the NCCIA could mean quicker resolution of online scams that have cost millions in lost savings. The agency’s focus on financial fraud aims to protect bank customers and small businesses that rely on digital transactions. It also signals a commitment to curb the rise in cyber‑harassment that has affected women and activists across the country. Businesses will benefit from clearer guidelines and faster enforcement of cyber‑crime laws. The new agency is expected to reduce the backlog of cyber‑crime cases that have piled up in provincial courts. By improving digital security, Pakistan hopes to attract more foreign investment in its growing tech sector.

Background

Pakistan’s cyber‑crime landscape has grown rapidly, with a spike in phishing, ransomware, and online fraud during the COVID‑19 lockdowns. Existing agencies such as the Federal Investigation Agency (FIA) and provincial police have struggled to keep pace with the volume and technical complexity of cases. In 2022, the government introduced the Cyber Crimes (Prevention and Suppression) Act, which expanded penalties for online offences. However, critics argued that implementation was uneven and that coordination between agencies remained fragmented. The NCCIA is intended to address these gaps.

What happens next

The agency will begin operations next month, with an initial team of cyber‑forensic specialists and investigators. The government plans to launch a public awareness campaign to educate citizens on how to report cyber‑crime. The NCCIA will also draft guidelines for digital evidence handling. Over the next year, the agency will assess its impact through metrics such as case clearance rates and prosecution success. It will also seek to collaborate with international cyber‑crime units to share intelligence on transnational threats.

Summary

- Pakistan has set up the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) to centralise cyber‑crime enforcement. - The agency will target online financial fraud, phishing, ransomware and harassment. - It operates under the Interior Ministry and will coordinate with federal and provincial bodies. - The move follows a government directive to strengthen digital law enforcement and improve inter‑agency communication. - Expected outcomes include faster case resolution, better evidence handling and a boost to Pakistan’s digital security posture.
