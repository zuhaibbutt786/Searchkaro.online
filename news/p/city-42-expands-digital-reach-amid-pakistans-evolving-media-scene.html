City 42 expands digital reach amid Pakistan’s evolving media scene

City 42 has launched a new mobile app and opened additional regional bureaus to serve a growing online audience. The moves reflect a broader shift toward digital news in Pakistan.

Latest developments

In the past six months City 42 has rolled out a dedicated mobile application for Android and iOS devices, allowing viewers to stream live broadcasts and access archived reports. The app, which recorded over 500,000 downloads within its first month, features push notifications for breaking news in Lahore and surrounding districts. Alongside the digital push, the channel has inaugurated two new regional bureaus in Multan and Faisalabad, staffed with local journalists to provide hyper‑local coverage of politics, traffic and community events. City 42 also expanded its presence on YouTube, where its subscriber count crossed the one‑million mark, and it now offers short video briefs tailored for social‑media platforms. The channel’s parent company, City Media Group, announced a partnership with a major Pakistani telecom operator to bundle the channel’s live stream with data packages, further increasing accessibility for users in rural areas.

Why it matters

The expansion is significant for Pakistani audiences because it offers more immediate access to regional news that was previously limited to national broadcasters. By strengthening its digital infrastructure, City 42 can compete with emerging online news portals and retain viewership among younger demographics who prefer mobile consumption. The new bureaus also mean that stories from smaller cities receive dedicated coverage, which can improve public awareness of local governance issues and civic initiatives. For advertisers, the broader reach across mobile and social platforms opens new opportunities to target specific geographic segments, potentially reshaping advertising spend within the country's media market.

Background

City 42 launched in 2012 as part of the City Media Group, a privately owned network headquartered in Lahore. Initially focused on television broadcasting in Urdu, the channel quickly became known for its round‑the‑clock coverage of Lahore’s traffic, weather updates and city council proceedings. Over the years it added investigative segments, talk shows and live election coverage, building a reputation for timely local reporting. The rise of internet penetration in Pakistan, especially through smartphones, prompted the channel to explore digital avenues, leading to the creation of a website and social‑media accounts that now attract millions of visitors each month. Its growth mirrors a broader trend in Pakistani media where regional outlets are leveraging technology to reach audiences beyond traditional broadcast limits.

What happens next

Industry analysts expect City 42 to continue investing in interactive features such as live polling during news segments and personalized news feeds based on user preferences. The channel is also reportedly testing an over‑the‑top (OTT) subscription service that would bundle premium documentaries and exclusive interviews with its standard news feed. Further expansion of regional bureaus into the northern Punjab region is planned for early next year, aiming to cover a wider range of local stories.
