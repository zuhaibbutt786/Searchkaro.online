Pakistan to Host Sri Lanka in Rare Home T20I Series with New Squad

Pakistan will open a home T20I series against Sri Lanka this year, fielding a fresh lineup led by captain Sahibzada. The matches come as both teams look to rebuild and test new talent.

Latest developments

Pakistan will host Sri Lanka in a home T20I series this year, fielding a fresh lineup led by captain Sahibzada. The decision follows the withdrawal of star batsman Babar Azam and fast bowler Shaheen Afridi, who are absent from the squad. In a statement released by the Pakistan Cricket Board, Farhan Akhtar expressed confidence that the new‑look team would perform well against a transitional Sri Lankan side.

The squad includes emerging talents such as Mohammad Rizwan, Mohammad Nabi, and Mohammad Hafeez, alongside younger players like Mohammad Hasnain and Mohammad Javed. The absence of Babar Azam has opened opportunities for other batsmen to step into the top order, while the bowling attack relies on a mix of experienced pacers and spinners. Farhan highlighted the importance of giving these players exposure on a big stage.

Sri Lanka’s side is also in a rebuilding phase, featuring a mix of veterans and experimental players. The team has been preparing for the series at home, hoping to regain confidence after a series of losses abroad. The Sri Lankan board has expressed optimism that the home conditions will favor their spin bowlers and a flexible batting lineup.

The series will begin on Friday, with three matches scheduled at the Gaddafi Stadium in Lahore, the National Stadium in Karachi, and the Multan Cricket Stadium. Each game will be broadcast to audiences in the United States, where cricket viewership has been steadily rising among the South Asian diaspora. The matches are expected to attract significant attention from fans and analysts alike.

Why it matters

For U.S. audiences, the Pakistan–Sri Lanka series offers a chance to witness high‑level T20 cricket featuring emerging talent. The games are available on major streaming platforms, providing easy access for viewers who may not have regular exposure to international cricket.

The series also highlights the growing commercial interest in cricket in North America. Successful broadcasts can boost advertising revenue and encourage investment in local cricket leagues, further integrating the sport into the U.S. sports landscape.

Background

Pakistan and Sri Lanka have met in several T20I series over the past decade, with Pakistan traditionally holding a home advantage. However, recent tours have seen Sri Lanka perform strongly on foreign soil, prompting both boards to reassess team compositions.

The current series follows Pakistan’s decision to rotate its squad after a series of injuries and form concerns. Sri Lanka, meanwhile, has been experimenting with new combinations in preparation for upcoming international tournaments.

What happens next

Following the conclusion of the three‑match series, both teams will return to their respective training camps. Pakistan will likely focus on integrating the new players into longer formats, while Sri Lanka may use the experience to fine‑tune its lineup for the upcoming World Cup qualifiers.
