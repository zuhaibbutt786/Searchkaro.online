Dakota and Elle Fanning Team Up for WWII Drama 'The Nightingale'

Dakota and Elle Fanning appear together for the first time as sisters in the film adaptation of Kristin Hannah's bestseller. The project has released its first images and a cover through Reese’s Book Club.

Latest developments

The first visual material from the upcoming film "The Nightingale" has been released, showing Dakota and Elle Fanning sharing the screen as sisters during World War II. The images, unveiled by Vanity Fair, highlight the siblings' close bond and hint at the emotional weight of the story. A trailer accompanying the release confirms that the sisters will navigate the war‑torn landscape of occupied France, mirroring the narrative of Kristin Hannah's best‑selling novel. In a separate announcement, Reese’s Book Club disclosed the official movie cover, featuring the two actresses against a muted, wartime backdrop. The cover, reported by Woman’s World, reinforces the period setting and the central sibling relationship.

Why it matters

The casting of the Fanning sisters marks a notable moment for American cinema, as it is the first time the siblings have acted together in a feature film. Their combined fan bases are expected to draw significant attention to a story that explores the hidden contributions of women during the war. For U.S. audiences, the film offers a fresh perspective on a historical period often dominated by male narratives, potentially expanding public interest in women's wartime experiences. Additionally, the involvement of Reese’s Book Club, a prominent literary community, signals a cross‑media collaboration that could boost both book sales and box‑office performance.

The project also arrives at a time when period dramas are seeing renewed interest on streaming platforms, suggesting that "The Nightingale" could benefit from broader distribution strategies beyond traditional theatrical releases.

Background

Kristin Hannah's novel "The Nightingale" was published in 2015 and quickly became a bestseller, praised for its dual‑protagonist structure and its focus on the French Resistance. The story follows two sisters, Vianne and Isabelle, who each find their own ways to survive and resist Nazi occupation. Film rights were acquired shortly after the book's success, and the adaptation has been in development for several years. The project is directed by a yet‑to‑be‑named filmmaker, with production companies aiming to retain the novel's emotional core while translating its expansive narrative into a two‑hour cinematic format.

Previous attempts to adapt the novel faced challenges due to its length and complex character arcs. The involvement of the Fanning sisters, both experienced actors with strong dramatic credentials, is seen as a strategic move to anchor the film's emotional journey.

What happens next

The film is slated for a summer 2025 release, with a limited theatrical run planned before a wider rollout on major streaming services. Marketing efforts will likely focus on the sisterly dynamic, the wartime setting, and the book's existing fan base. Industry analysts anticipate that the trailer and first‑look images will be used to generate buzz through social media campaigns and partnerships with literary influencers, especially those linked to Reese’s Book Club.
