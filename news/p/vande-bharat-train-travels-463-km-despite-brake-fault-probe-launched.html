Vande Bharat Train Travels 463 km Despite Brake Fault, Probe Launched

The Vande Bharat Express completed a 463‑kilometre run despite a brake malfunction, leading to an official probe. A separate stone‑throwing incident also damaged a coach, heightening safety worries.

Latest developments

On November 14, 2026, a Vande Bharat Express operating between Surat and Navsari travelled more than 463 kilometres even though its braking system was reported to be faulty. Railway officials discovered the anomaly after the train completed the journey without any emergency stop, prompting an immediate order for a detailed technical investigation. The probe, mandated by senior officials of Indian Railways, will examine the brake assembly, sensor data and maintenance records to determine why the safety system failed to trigger a halt.

The investigation has been assigned to the Central Railway's safety cell, which will coordinate with the rolling stock manufacturer and independent experts. Preliminary statements from the railway authority indicated that the train's onboard diagnostics had logged irregular brake pressure readings, but the crew continued the run after receiving clearance from the control centre. The authority has assured the public that no injuries were reported, and that the train's speed was reduced as a precautionary measure during the affected segment.

In a separate but related incident on the same route, a group of unidentified individuals threw stones at the Vande Bharat train, shattering the glass of the C‑4 coach. The attack occurred near a small village between Surat and Navsari, causing minor injuries to a few passengers who were seated near the impacted window. Local police have opened a case, treating the act as a possible act of sabotage, and have detained several suspects for questioning. Security forces have increased patrols along the corridor and are reviewing surveillance footage to identify the perpetrators.

Passengers on the affected service reported a delayed arrival of approximately 45 minutes, citing both the brake issue and the stone‑throwing episode as contributing factors. The railway issued an apology and announced that the train would undergo a comprehensive safety check before resuming regular operations. Several commuters expressed concern over the reliability of the flagship high‑speed service, which has been marketed as a symbol of modern Indian rail travel.

Why it matters

The Vande Bharat Express is a flagship project of the Indian government's push to modernise the rail network, offering faster travel times and advanced passenger amenities. A brake failure on such a high‑speed train raises serious questions about the robustness of safety protocols, especially as the network expands to include more premium services. Public confidence in the rail system could be eroded if technical glitches are perceived as recurring or inadequately addressed.

The stone‑throwing incident adds a layer of security concern, suggesting that the train could be a target for vandalism or extremist activity. Both issues intersect with broader debates on infrastructure investment, maintenance standards, and the need for heightened security measures on critical transport corridors. For daily commuters and long‑distance travelers alike, the reliability of the Vande Bharat service directly impacts their trust in the promises of faster, safer travel across the country.

Background

The Vande Bharat Express, also known as Train 18, was introduced in 2019 as India's first indigenously designed semi‑high‑speed train. Equipped with modern features such as automatic doors, onboard Wi‑Fi and a top speed of 160 km/h, it has been deployed on several key routes to showcase the country's engineering capabilities. Since its launch, the train has enjoyed a reputation for punctuality and comfort, though isolated technical glitches have been reported in the past, typically resolved through routine maintenance.

Brake systems on high‑speed trains are complex, involving pneumatic, hydraulic and electronic components that must function in harmony. Any failure can compromise the train's ability to decelerate safely, especially on gradients or in densely populated areas. While Indian Railways has a long history of operating conventional diesel and electric locomotives, the transition to newer technology like Vande Bharat demands rigorous testing and continuous monitoring to prevent incidents like the one currently under investigation.

What happens next

The safety cell's investigation is expected to submit a report within two weeks, outlining the root cause of the brake malfunction and recommending corrective actions. Depending on the findings, the railway may temporarily suspend the affected rake, mandate retrofits, or issue new maintenance guidelines for all Vande Bharat units. Simultaneously, police investigations into the stone‑throwing episode will aim to identify and prosecute those responsible, with increased security deployments planned for the Surat‑Navsari corridor.
