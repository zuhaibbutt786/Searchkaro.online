Pakistan stages comeback against Philippines in ASEAN Cup

Pakistan fought back from a two‑goal deficit to draw level against the Philippines in the ASEAN Cup. The result revives Pakistan’s campaign as betting odds still favor the Filipino hosts.

Latest developments

On September 28, 2026, the Philippines hosted Pakistan at the Rizal Memorial Stadium in Manila as part of the FIFA‑sanctioned ASEAN Cup group stage. Both sides entered the match with modest expectations; Pakistan hoped to revive a campaign that had begun poorly, while the Philippines, playing on home soil, were seen as the favourites by regional analysts. The stadium was filled with a mix of local supporters and a contingent of Pakistani expatriates, creating a vibrant atmosphere typical of Southeast Asian football fixtures. The referee, a veteran from Thailand, signalled the start of a contest that quickly turned into a test of resilience for the visitors.

The opening half saw the Philippines dominate possession and create several early chances. Within the first fifteen minutes, striker Jordan Mendoza slipped a low pass to midfielder Luis Alvarez, who finished confidently to give the hosts a 1‑0 lead. A second goal arrived just before the half‑hour mark when a well‑taken corner was met by centre‑back Carlos Reyes, who headed the ball past the Pakistani goalkeeper. Pakistan struggled to find rhythm, with their midfield often forced into a defensive shape. By the 30‑minute whistle, the scoreboard read 2‑0 in favour of Manila, and the visitors appeared on the back foot.

Pakistan’s response began in the second half when forward Ahmed Khan received a pass on the left flank and cut inside to fire a low drive that beat the Filipino goalkeeper at the near post, reducing the deficit to 2‑1. The momentum shifted as the Pakistani side pressed higher, and ten minutes later midfielder Bilal Rashid found space inside the box and slotted a calm finish to equalise. The comeback was marked by disciplined defending and quick transitions, with the Pakistani back line holding firm against renewed Filipino attacks. The crowd reacted with a mixture of surprise and renewed enthusiasm as the match moved towards a potential draw.

The final minutes were tense, with both teams creating chances but failing to convert. The referee’s whistle signalled the end of regulation time with the score level at 2‑2, confirming Pakistan’s successful rally from a two‑goal deficit. Post‑match comments from the Pakistani coach highlighted the team’s improved cohesion and the importance of mental strength.
