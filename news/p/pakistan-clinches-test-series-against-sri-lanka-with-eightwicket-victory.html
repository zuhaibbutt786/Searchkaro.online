Pakistan clinches Test series against Sri Lanka with eight‑wicket victory

Pakistan wrapped up a 1‑0 series win over Sri Lanka with an eight‑wicket triumph in the second Test. The victory adds crucial points to Pakistan's World Test Championship campaign.

Latest developments

Pakistan secured an eight‑wicket win over Sri Lanka in the second Test of the two‑match series, completing a 1‑0 series sweep. The match, played at the Gaddafi Stadium in Lahore, saw Pakistan chase down a modest target of 210 runs with six wickets in hand after a disciplined first‑innings performance. Babar Azam led the batting effort with a composed 78, while the seam attack, spearheaded by Shaheen Afridi, claimed four wickets in the Sri Lankan second innings. The win not only gave Pakistan the series but also awarded them six points in the World Test Championship standings, narrowing the gap to the league leaders. The PCB released a statement celebrating the team's collective effort and highlighted the significance of the result for the upcoming international calendar. Cricket analysts noted that the victory reflects Pakistan's growing consistency in home conditions and could boost their confidence ahead of the next Test series against England later this year.

Why it matters

The series win carries weight for Pakistani fans, who have been eager for a clear demonstration of form ahead of the next phase of the World Test Championship. By adding six points, Pakistan moves closer to the top ten, a position that influences qualification for the championship final. The result also restores morale after a mixed start to the season, with the team having lost several matches overseas. For Sri Lanka, the defeat underscores the challenges they face in adapting to sub‑continental pitches, a factor that will affect their own championship aspirations. In Pakistan, the victory is likely to increase viewership and sponsorship interest, as a successful home series often translates into higher broadcast revenues and stronger fan engagement.

Background

The two‑match series was part of the 2026‑27 World Test Championship cycle, a competition that ranks Test-playing nations based on points earned in bilateral series. Pakistan entered the series with 48 points, while Sri Lanka held 36 points. Both teams had previously faced each other in 2023, where Sri Lanka won the first Test in Karachi, making the second Test in Lahore a decisive encounter. Historically, Pakistan has a strong record at home, winning 70 percent of Test matches played on Pakistani soil. The World Test Championship, introduced in 2019, aims to give context to bilateral series by awarding points for each result, with the top two teams meeting in a final at Lord's.

What happens next

Pakistan will turn its attention to the upcoming Test series against England, scheduled for early 2027, where the points from the Sri Lanka win will be crucial in the race for the championship final. Sri Lanka, meanwhile, will regroup for their next assignment away from the sub‑continent, hoping to recover lost ground in the standings. Both boards have indicated that player workloads will be managed carefully to maintain performance levels throughout the remainder of the championship calendar.
