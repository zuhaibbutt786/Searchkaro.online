Pakistan dominate early in second Test against Sri Lanka

Pakistan seized control of the second Test in Rawalpindi, with fast bowlers and Ghori leading the charge. Sri Lanka’s Kumara fought back with a five‑for but could not prevent a large deficit.

Latest developments

On the opening day of the second Test between Pakistan and Sri Lanka in Rawalpindi, the home side established a clear advantage. Pakistan's pace attack, led by the likes of Mohammad Abbas and Naseem Shah, consistently troubled the Sri Lankan top order, extracting movement off the seam-friendly pitch. All-rounder Shadab Khan contributed with a vital breakthrough, while spinner Saifullah Khan added pressure in the middle overs. The Sri Lankan bowler Pramod Kumara responded with a five-wicket haul, showcasing skill and resilience, but his effort was insufficient to reverse the tide. Batsmen such as Imam-ul-Haq and Azhar Ali compiled steady runs, pushing Pakistan into a commanding first‑innings total. By stumps, the hosts held a substantial lead, setting the tone for the remainder of the match.

Why it matters

The early dominance in the Rawalpindi Test carries significant weight for Pakistani cricket fans, who have been eager for a strong home performance after a mixed start to the series. A solid lead not only improves Pakistan's chances of securing a series win but also boosts the team's confidence ahead of upcoming international fixtures. For Sri Lanka, the five-wicket spell by Kumara highlights the potential of their bowling unit, offering a glimmer of hope that they can still challenge the hosts in the later stages. The result also influences ICC Test rankings, where a series victory could see Pakistan climb higher, while a loss would dent Sri Lanka's standing.

Background

The two‑nation series began with a closely contested first Test in Karachi, where Sri Lanka managed to force a draw after a resilient batting display. Both sides entered the Rawalpindi encounter with a clear understanding of each other's strengths: Pakistan's fast bowlers excel on home pitches, while Sri Lanka relies on spin and disciplined seam bowling. Historically, Pakistan has held a favorable record against Sri Lanka in Test cricket, especially on home soil, where the conditions often favor their pace attack. The current tour also serves as preparation for both teams ahead of the upcoming ICC World Test Championship cycle, adding extra importance to each match outcome.

What happens next

The second day will see Sri Lanka's batsmen attempting to rebuild the innings under pressure, while Pakistan will look to extend their lead with additional wickets and strategic field placements. If Pakistan can enforce a substantial first‑innings lead, they may declare early to give themselves enough time to bowl out Sri Lanka twice. Conversely, a resilient Sri Lankan response could narrow the gap and set up a more competitive finish. The final result will be decided by the performances in the remaining three days, with both teams aware that momentum can shift quickly in Test cricket.
