Urban Meyer urges Ryan Day to tackle Ohio State’s woes after USC loss

Latest developments

After Ohio State's 31-24 loss to USC in the season opener, athletic director Urban Meyer publicly called on head coach Ryan Day to address the team's performance. Meyer emphasized that the defeat was "embarrassing" and that the issues extend beyond any single position, specifically stating that the situation "has nothing to do with the quarterback situation." He urged Day to conduct a thorough review of all facets of the program, from schematics to player development, in order to restore the Buckeyes' competitive edge.

In a press conference held at the university's athletic complex, Meyer reiterated his confidence in Day's leadership while pressing for immediate corrective measures. He highlighted concerns about the team's defensive lapses, special‑teams miscues, and a lack of discipline on third downs. Meyer also referenced the need for better preparation against high‑powered offenses like USC's, which exploited Ohio State's secondary vulnerabilities throughout the game.

Ryan Day responded later in the day, acknowledging the criticism and pledging to "do a better job around" senior wide receiver Emeka Fahey, whose drops and route miscommunications were cited as costly in the loss. Day said the coaching staff would increase film study, adjust practice routines, and hold players accountable for execution errors. He also noted that the quarterback play, while not the primary focus of Meyer’s comments, would still be evaluated as part of a broader performance audit.

The comments came as Ohio State prepares for its upcoming conference matchups, where the Buckeyes are expected to contend for a Big Ten title and a potential College Football Playoff berth. Analysts from Yahoo Sports and 247Sports have pointed out that the early-season stumble could jeopardize the team's ranking, especially if the issues identified by Meyer are not swiftly remedied. The athletic department has signaled that it will monitor progress closely and expects measurable improvement by the next home game.

Why it matters

Ohio State is a perennial powerhouse with a massive national fan base; a poor start can affect recruiting, television revenue, and the university’s brand. Urban Meyer’s intervention signals that the administration is unwilling to tolerate a decline in performance, which may influence how current and prospective players view the program’s stability.

The scrutiny also resonates beyond Columbus, as the Big Ten conference relies on Ohio State’s success to maintain its national relevance. A weakened Buckeyes team could shift the balance of power toward rivals such as Michigan and Penn State, altering the conference’s championship landscape and its representation in the College Football Playoff.

Moreover, the public nature of Meyer’s comments adds pressure on Ryan Day, whose job security has been solid but not invulnerable. The coaching staff’s response will be watched by media outlets and alumni, potentially affecting contract negotiations and future staffing decisions.

Background

Urban Meyer served as Ohio State’s head coach from 2012 to 2018, leading the Buckeyes to a national championship in the 2014 season. After stepping down, he returned to the university in 2023 as athletic director, overseeing all major sports programs. His tenure as AD has been marked by a hands‑on approach, often commenting on football strategy and personnel.

Ryan Day took over as head coach in 2019, inheriting a roster built by Meyer and guiding the team to multiple Big Ten titles and playoff appearances. However, the 2026 season began with heightened expectations after a strong recruiting class and a schedule featuring non‑conference opponents like USC, a program revitalized under Lincoln Riley.

The Buckeyes entered the USC game with a 1‑0 record but faced a defensive scheme that emphasized speed and press coverage, exposing gaps in Ohio State’s secondary. The loss marked the first time since 2019 that the team fell to a non‑conference opponent in the opening week, prompting immediate calls for accountability.

What happens next

In the coming weeks, Day is expected to implement adjustments highlighted by Meyer, including tighter defensive assignments and enhanced communication with receivers like Fahey. The coaching staff will likely hold a focused practice session before the next game against Indiana, using the loss as a teaching moment.

The athletic department will release a mid‑season assessment, and if progress is not evident, further actions—ranging from staff reshuffling to public statements—could follow. Fans and analysts will gauge success by the Buckeyes’ performance in the upcoming Big Ten clash and their ability to climb back into the top‑10 rankings.
