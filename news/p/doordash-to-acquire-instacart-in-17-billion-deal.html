DoorDash to Acquire Instacart in $17 Billion Deal

DoorDash has agreed to buy Instacart for $17 billion, creating a combined platform for restaurant and grocery deliveries. The deal is expected to close after regulatory review.

Latest developments

DoorDash disclosed on Monday that it will purchase Instacart for approximately $17 billion in cash, creating the largest U.S. on‑demand delivery operation to date. The agreement, announced in a joint press release, states that the transaction will be financed through DoorDash's existing cash reserves and a new credit facility. Both companies said the merger will be completed by the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary antitrust clearance and shareholder approval.

Regulators in the United States and Europe have been notified, and the Federal Trade Commission is expected to conduct a detailed review of the competitive impact. Industry analysts note that the combined entity could face scrutiny for potentially reducing competition in both restaurant and grocery delivery markets.

Investors reacted positively, with DoorDash shares rising 4.2 percent after the announcement, while Instacart, a privately held firm, saw its valuation climb back above the $10 billion mark, restoring founder Apoorva Mehta to billionaire status. The deal also sparked a modest uptick in the share price of rival delivery firms, reflecting market expectations of a reshaped competitive landscape.

Company executives emphasized that the merger will not result in immediate layoffs, but will focus on integrating technology platforms and expanding service coverage. The statement highlighted plans to combine DoorDash's logistics network with Instacart's grocery expertise, aiming to offer a single app for both meals and groceries across the United States.

Why it matters

The consolidation brings together the two strongest players in separate but overlapping segments of the on‑demand economy. Consumers could benefit from a unified ordering experience, potentially lower delivery fees, and broader geographic coverage as the merged platform leverages DoorDash's extensive restaurant network and Instacart's grocery partnerships.

For the gig workforce, the deal raises questions about employment terms, scheduling, and earnings. While the companies have pledged to maintain current driver contracts, the integration of two large labor pools may lead to new policies on incentives and performance metrics.

From a competitive standpoint, the acquisition narrows the field of major rivals, putting pressure on smaller regional services and prompting larger tech firms to reassess their own delivery strategies. The move may also influence pricing dynamics, as the combined entity could negotiate more favorable rates with restaurant and retailer partners.

Background

DoorDash, founded in 2013, grew rapidly by focusing on restaurant deliveries and expanding into convenience items, eventually becoming the market leader in the United States with a market share above 50 percent. Its public listing in 2020 provided capital for aggressive expansion into new verticals, including grocery and pharmacy deliveries.

Instacart launched in 2012 as a grocery‑delivery platform, partnering with major supermarket chains to offer same‑day fulfillment. Over the past decade, it built a robust network of personal shoppers and secured a valuation that peaked at $39 billion before a recent slowdown in funding. The company survived a challenging 2023 market correction by streamlining operations and focusing on profitability.

Prior to the acquisition, DoorDash and Instacart had occasionally collaborated on joint promotions, but each maintained distinct brand identities and technology stacks. Their separate growth trajectories made the merger a logical step for consolidating market share and reducing duplicate infrastructure costs.

What happens next

The companies will now enter a detailed integration phase, beginning with the alignment of payment systems, data analytics, and customer support platforms. A joint task force is expected to develop a roadmap for merging the two mobile applications into a single interface, a process projected to take 12 to 18 months.

Regulatory approvals remain the final hurdle. If the FTC approves the deal without major conditions, the transaction could close by early 2027, after which the combined firm will announce its first unified pricing and service strategy.
