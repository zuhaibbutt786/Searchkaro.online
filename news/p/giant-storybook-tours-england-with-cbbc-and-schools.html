Giant Storybook Tours England with CBBC and Schools

A massive travelling storybook is set to visit schools across England this autumn, with CBBC helping to turn it into a moving platform for children’s creativity. The broadcaster also rolls out a new Bitesize Daily programme aimed at the 5‑7

Latest developments

The BBC’s children’s channel, CBBC, has announced a partnership with a new giant storybook that measures 1.5 metres in length and will travel across England during the autumn months. The project, described by the network as a "Ministry of Stories", will make stops in community centres, libraries and schools, allowing children to read, listen and contribute their own short stories to the physical pages. The first leg of the tour begins in London, with a scheduled visit to Hackney where pupils from local primary schools are already preparing submissions. Organisers say the book will be updated in real time as new entries are added, creating a living archive of contemporary childhood imagination.

Hackney pupils have taken an active role, adding their own tales to the travelling volume as part of a school‑led initiative. Teachers reported that the activity sparked enthusiasm for writing, with many children eager to see their words printed alongside those of peers from other towns. The school collaboration was highlighted in a recent article by the Hackney Citizen, which noted that the 1.5‑metre book will continue to grow as it moves from one location to the next, turning each stop into a mini‑exhibition of local creativity.

In parallel with the travelling book, CBBC has confirmed the launch of a new Bitesize Daily slot aimed at children aged five to seven. The programme, which will be broadcast in high definition, offers short, curriculum‑aligned segments that blend storytelling, basic numeracy and simple science concepts. TVGuide.co.uk listed the exact broadcast times, confirming that the series will air each weekday morning, providing a reliable educational resource for parents and teachers alike.

The combined effort reflects a broader push by the BBC to integrate traditional literacy tools with modern broadcasting. By linking a physical, mobile book with a digital television schedule, the network hopes to reach children both in the classroom and at home. The initiative also aligns with the BBC’s public service remit to promote reading and creative expression among young audiences across the United Kingdom.

Why it matters

The travelling storybook offers a tangible way for children to see the impact of their writing, reinforcing the value of reading and composition at a formative age. For many schools, especially those in under‑resourced areas, the opportunity to contribute to a national project can boost confidence and encourage continued participation in literacy activities. The involvement of CBBC adds a familiar media brand that many families already trust, potentially increasing engagement with the project beyond the physical visits.

The Bitesize Daily slot addresses a gap in age‑appropriate educational content on television. By delivering short, focused episodes in HD, the programme caters to the visual learning preferences of young children while supporting the curriculum. Parents looking for safe, educational screen time options can rely on the schedule, which may also ease the pressure on teachers to find supplementary resources. Together, the two initiatives create a multi‑platform approach that could improve reading rates and overall academic outcomes for children across England.

Background

The concept of a travelling storybook is not entirely new, but this iteration is notable for its scale and partnership with a national broadcaster. Similar mobile libraries have operated in various regions, aiming to bring books to communities without permanent library facilities. The BBC’s involvement marks a shift toward integrating broadcast media with community‑based literacy projects, reflecting a strategic emphasis on cross‑media engagement.

CBBC, the children’s arm of the BBC, has a long history of producing educational content, from classic adaptations to interactive digital platforms. In recent years, the channel has sought to expand its reach beyond the screen, experimenting with live events, school workshops and online resources. The Bitesize Daily series continues this trend, offering concise lessons that align with the national curriculum for early years education.

Hackney, a borough in East London, has been active in promoting creative writing in schools, often partnering with local arts organisations. The recent involvement of Hackney pupils in the travelling storybook project builds on these existing efforts, showcasing how local initiatives can feed into larger national programmes.

What happens next

The storybook is scheduled to visit at least twenty locations across England before the end of the year, with each stop lasting several days to allow ample time for children to read and contribute. Organisers will collect the added pages and publish a digital version of the completed volume online, making the collective work accessible to a wider audience.

CBBC’s Bitesize Daily will begin airing next week, with a full weekly schedule released on the channel’s website. Viewers are encouraged to provide feedback through an online portal, which may influence future episode topics and formats.
