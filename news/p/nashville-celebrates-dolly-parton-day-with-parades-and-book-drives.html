Nashville Celebrates Dolly Parton Day with Parades and Book Drives

Nashville is celebrating Dolly Parton Day with parades, pink lights, and community book drives. The event also supports the Imagination Library, a program that distributes free books to children.

What is happening

On September 25, the city of Nashville officially declared Dolly Parton Day, a celebration that has become a yearly tradition. The day is marked by a series of parades that wind through downtown streets, featuring floats, marching bands, and local performers. In addition, the city lights up in pink, a color closely associated with Parton's iconic style, and displays banners that highlight her music and philanthropic efforts.

Community book drives are a central feature of the celebration. Volunteers gather donated books from residents and businesses, which are then distributed to the Imagination Library program. The library, founded by Parton in 2000, provides free books to children in Nashville and surrounding areas. On Dolly Parton Day, the library hosts special events where families can drop off books and learn about the program’s impact.

Local businesses and residents are encouraged to participate in the festivities. Many shops display Parton-themed décor, and several restaurants offer menu items inspired by her music. The city also partners with the Wonder Whip company, which released a limited edition product on September 25 to benefit the Imagination Library. Participants can purchase the item and receive a portion of the proceeds to support the book drive.

Background and context

Dolly Parton, born in 1946 in Tennessee, rose to fame as a country music singer, songwriter, and actress. Over her career, she has sold more than 100 million records worldwide and earned numerous awards, including multiple Grammys. Beyond her musical achievements, Parton is known for her generosity and commitment to community service.

In 2000, she founded the Imagination Library, a nonprofit that distributes free books to children before they start school. The program has expanded to dozens of cities across the United States and several countries, providing thousands of books each month. Parton’s vision is that early access to books fosters a love of reading and improves academic outcomes.

Nashville, often called the Music City, has long been a hub for country music. The city’s decision to honor Parton with an official day underscores her influence on the local culture and economy. Dolly Parton Day has become a platform for celebrating her artistic legacy while promoting literacy and community engagement.

The partnership with Wonder Whip, a local business that sells music-inspired merchandise, illustrates the collaboration between artists, businesses, and nonprofits. By releasing a limited edition item on Dolly Parton Day, Wonder Whip provides fans with a tangible way to contribute to the library’s mission.

What to know next

The official Dolly Parton Day schedule is available on the Nashville city website. The morning parade begins at 10:00 a.m., followed by a book drive drop‑off at the Imagination Library’s headquarters in downtown. The event concludes with a live concert featuring local musicians and a closing ceremony that honors Parton’s contributions.

Families who wish to donate books can bring them to the library’s designated collection points located throughout the city. Volunteers will sort and distribute the books to the library’s distribution centers. Those who cannot attend in person can still support the cause by purchasing the Wonder Whip special item online, with proceeds going directly to the Imagination Library.

For more information, visitors can check the city’s official page or contact the Imagination Library’s customer service line. The library also offers a newsletter that provides updates on upcoming book drives and volunteer opportunities.
