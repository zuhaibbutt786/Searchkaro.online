Two Sisters of Former PM Imran Khan Freed After Detention Orders Dropped

Uzma and Noreen Khan, the elder sisters of former prime minister Imran Khan, have been freed after a court lifted their extended detention orders. The decision has drawn reactions from political leaders and human‑rights groups.

Latest developments

On Saturday, a Lahore court ordered the release of Uzma and Noreen Khan, the elder sisters of former prime minister Imran Khan, after the extended detention orders against them were withdrawn, according to their lawyer.

The sisters had been held since early 2024 on suspicion of involvement in a case related to the former prime minister's political activities. Their lawyer said the court found insufficient evidence and lifted the orders, leading to their immediate release.

The release was confirmed by local media outlets including Dawn and Reuters, which reported that the sisters were taken into custody earlier this year and that their detention had drawn criticism from human‑rights groups who said the cases were politically motivated.

The move comes amid a broader crackdown on members of Imran Khan's family and close associates, many of whom face charges ranging from corruption to incitement. The government has not commented publicly on the decision to free the sisters.

Opposition leader Shehbaz Sharif welcomed the court's decision, calling it a vindication of the rule of law, while the ruling party's spokesperson dismissed it as a temporary setback for the government's anti‑corruption drive.

Why it matters

The release signals a potential shift in how the judiciary is handling cases linked to the former prime minister, offering a respite to his supporters who have long claimed the legal actions are selective.

For ordinary Pakistanis, the case highlights concerns about the use of detention as a political tool, and it may influence public opinion ahead of upcoming elections, where the former prime minister's party is expected to contest vigorously.

International observers, including the European Union's delegation in Islamabad, noted the release as a positive sign that Pakistan's judiciary can act independently, a factor that could affect foreign aid discussions.

Background

Imran Khan was ousted in a vote of no confidence in April 2022 and has since faced a series of legal challenges. His family members, including his sisters, have been targeted in investigations that critics describe as retaliation for his criticism of the military establishment.

The sisters, Uzma and Noreen, were first arrested in early 2024 on allegations of facilitating communications between the former prime minister and alleged conspirators. Their detention was extended multiple times, prompting protests from civil‑society groups who argued the extensions violated due process.

Earlier this year, Imran Khan's son and his close aide were also placed under house arrest, and several of the former prime minister's allies were sentenced in high‑profile corruption cases, reinforcing the perception of a systematic legal pressure campaign.

What happens next

Legal analysts expect the court to review other pending cases involving Khan's relatives, and the government may decide whether to pursue new charges or drop existing ones. The outcome could affect the political climate as the country approaches its 2027 general election.

Human‑rights NGOs have pledged to monitor any further arrests, and they plan to file a petition with the Supreme Court challenging the legality of extended detention orders under Pakistan's Constitution.
