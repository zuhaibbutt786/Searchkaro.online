Birla Harb’s ‘Wings of the Nation’ Outfit Sparks Controversy at Miss Universe 2026

The Saudi contestant Birla Harb caused a stir at the Miss Universe 2026 pageant with her “Wings of the Nation” ensemble. Social media users and regional media are debating the cultural implications of the outfit.

Latest developments

On the opening night of Miss Universe 2026, Saudi Arabian representative Birla Harb stepped onto the stage wearing a dramatic costume titled “Wings of the Nation.” The ensemble featured a flowing cape of embroidered fabric that resembled stylised wings, incorporating traditional Saudi motifs and the national flag’s colors. The design was intended to showcase the country’s cultural heritage while projecting a modern, confident image of Saudi women on a global platform. Within minutes of the live broadcast, the clip went viral on regional platforms, prompting a wave of commentary that ranged from praise for artistic ambition to criticism that the attire crossed accepted modesty norms. Viewers in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Egypt posted thousands of reactions on Twitter, Instagram, and TikTok, many using the hashtag #BirlaWings. Some praised the outfit as a bold statement of national pride, while others argued that the revealing cut of the cape conflicted with conservative dress expectations. The pageant’s official account posted a brief statement acknowledging the diverse reactions but did not issue a formal apology or defense. Meanwhile, other contestants, such as Egypt’s Sonia Jerjes, attracted attention for their own culturally inspired looks, and Jordan announced its return to the competition after a 66‑year hiatus with contestant Destiny Anbar, adding further layers to the night’s cultural narrative. Regional news outlets, including Al Arabiya and BBC Arabic, produced short video analyses that highlighted the tension between modern representation and traditional values, positioning Birla Harb’s appearance as a focal point of the broader debate on women’s visibility in Arab media.

Why it matters

The controversy surrounding Birla Harb’s outfit resonates strongly in Saudi Arabia, where public discussions about women’s dress and participation in international events remain sensitive. The country has been gradually easing restrictions on women’s public roles, yet the reaction to a high‑profile fashion statement underscores the ongoing negotiation between progressive aspirations and entrenched cultural expectations. For Saudi audiences, the episode offers a tangible example of how global platforms can amplify domestic debates about identity, modesty, and national branding. Additionally, the episode may influence how Saudi brands and designers approach future collaborations with international pageants, potentially prompting more cautious design choices or, conversely, encouraging bolder expressions of heritage. The broader Arab viewership is also watching, as the Miss Universe stage becomes a mirror reflecting shifting attitudes toward gender representation across the region.

Background

Miss Universe, founded in 1952, has long served as a showcase for national costumes that blend tradition with contemporary fashion. Arab participation has historically been limited, with occasional entries from Egypt, Lebanon, and more recently, Saudi Arabia and Jordan. In 2023, Egypt’s Sonia Jerjes generated headlines for a costume that highlighted ancient Egyptian symbols, while Jordan’s return in 2026 marks the first appearance since 1960, represented by Destiny Anbar. Past controversies in the pageant have included debates over swimsuit segments and the portrayal of cultural symbols, often sparking media scrutiny in conservative societies. The “Wings of the Nation” concept draws on Saudi iconography, echoing recent government initiatives that promote cultural heritage through modern artistic mediums.

What happens next

Pageant organizers have indicated they will review feedback from the Saudi delegation and regional audiences before finalising costume guidelines for the upcoming finals. No official change has been announced, but the heightened scrutiny suggests future contestants may face stricter oversight regarding modesty standards. In Saudi Arabia, the national pageant committee is expected to hold a press briefing to address public concerns and outline its vision for representing Saudi women on the world stage, potentially influencing the selection process for the next Miss Universe representative.
