Rohit Sharma becomes fastest opener to 10,000 ODI runs

Rohit Sharma broke the record for the quickest 10,000 runs in One Day Internationals, doing so as an opening batsman. The achievement places him in an exclusive group that includes only two other Indian greats.

Latest developments

In the third One Day International of the India‑West Indies series, Rohit Sharma compiled a steady innings that took his career tally to 10,000 runs. The milestone was reached in fewer matches than any other opener in the format, confirming his status as the fastest batter to achieve the figure from the top of the order. The run came as part of a 250‑run partnership that helped India post a competitive total and secure a series‑level win.

The International Cricket Council (ICC) issued a brief statement acknowledging Sharma's entry into an elite ODI run‑club. The governing body highlighted that the achievement places him among a short list of players who have crossed the 10,000‑run barrier, a benchmark traditionally associated with the longest careers in limited‑overs cricket. The ICC note also mentioned that the record will be recorded in its official statistics.

Indian cricket analysts noted that Sharma is only the third Indian to reach the landmark, joining the legendary Sachin Tendulkar and the contemporary star Virat Kohli. Both Tendulkar and Kohli achieved the milestone after longer careers, making Sharma's pace particularly noteworthy. The comparison underscores the rarity of the feat within the country's rich cricketing history.

Reactions across the cricketing community were swift. Former players, commentators and fans praised the consistency and longevity required to sustain such a run‑scoring rate. Social media platforms saw a surge in tributes, with many highlighting Sharma's adaptability as an opener and his ability to anchor innings while maintaining a high strike rate. The record is being described as a benchmark for future generations of Indian batsmen.

Why it matters

For cricket‑loving audiences in Pakistan, Sharma's record adds a new chapter to the long‑standing rivalry between the two nations. The achievement showcases the evolving standards of batting in the sub‑continent and provides a reference point for Pakistani players aiming to match or surpass Indian milestones. It also fuels discussions about the quality of domestic pathways that produce world‑class openers.

Beyond the sporting narrative, the record has commercial implications. Sponsors, broadcasters and merchandise partners monitor such milestones closely, as they often translate into higher viewership and increased market activity. The heightened interest can benefit regional broadcasters who hold rights to India‑Pakistan fixtures, potentially driving advertising revenue and fan engagement across South Asia.

Background

Rohit Sharma began his international career as a middle‑order batsman before being promoted to open the innings in limited‑overs cricket. Over the past decade, he has transformed his game, developing a reputation for powerful lofted shots and an ability to build large innings. Prior to this record, the fastest opener to 10,000 runs was England's Jonny Bairstow, who achieved the mark in 274 innings. Sharma reached the milestone in 251 innings, shaving off 23 matches from the previous benchmark.

His career includes multiple double centuries in ODIs, a record number of centuries as an opener, and leadership roles, including captaining the Indian side in limited‑overs formats. The 10,000‑run landmark adds to a portfolio that already features three World Cup titles and several series victories. The achievement also reflects his fitness and consistency, attributes that have allowed him to stay at the top of the game for more than a decade.

What happens next

India's schedule now moves towards a bilateral series against Australia, followed by the upcoming ICC Champions Trophy. Sharma will have the opportunity to extend his run tally further and possibly challenge the overall fastest 10,000‑run record, which remains held by a non‑opener. Observers will watch how he balances his batting responsibilities with captaincy duties in the remaining fixtures of the season.
