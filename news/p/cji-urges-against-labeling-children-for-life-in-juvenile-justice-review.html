The court's attention is focused on ensuring that the legal system balances accountability with the rehabilitative needs of young offenders.

This distinction is crucial for preventing the long-term social stigma that often follows young individuals after their legal cases are resolved. Background

The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015, was enacted to provide a framework for the care and protection of children who are in conflict with the law or in need of care and protection. It distinguishes between children below 18 and those who are older, with specific provisions for serious offenses. However, the implementation of these laws has often been inconsistent, leading to calls for greater clarity and uniformity in judicial decisions.

Recent incidents involving minors have sparked nationwide debates about the age of criminal responsibility and the effectiveness of rehabilitative measures. These discussions have led to increased scrutiny of the juvenile justice system, with various stakeholders, including legal experts, activists, and the public, urging for reforms that better protect the rights of children while maintaining public safety.
