Saudi Arabia Expands Vehicle Inspection Network to 116 Centers

Saudi authorities are accelerating the expansion of periodic vehicle inspection stations across the Kingdom. New facilities in southern regions are designed to handle hundreds of vehicles daily.

Latest developments

The Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO) has announced plans to increase the number of vehicle inspection centers to 116 across the Kingdom. This strategic move aims to enhance road safety standards and ensure that all vehicles on the road meet strict technical requirements. The expansion reflects a broader national effort to modernize infrastructure and improve public safety metrics.

In Jazan, a new periodic inspection station in Samta has recently opened its doors. The facility features four dedicated service lanes, allowing it to serve the southern part of the Jazan region efficiently. With a daily capacity of 400 vehicles, the station is expected to reduce wait times for drivers in the area. This addition is part of a coordinated rollout of new inspection hubs in underserved regions.

Simultaneously, SASO has approved the operation of a new technical inspection site in Dhahrat Al-Janoub in the Asir region. This development addresses the growing need for accessible inspection services in the southern highlands. The approval marks a significant step in decentralizing vehicle compliance checks, moving them closer to where residents live and work.

These expansions are not isolated incidents but part of a systematic approach to infrastructure development. By increasing the number of operational centers, the authorities aim to distribute the load more evenly across the country. This helps prevent overcrowding at existing stations and ensures a smoother experience for vehicle owners.

Why it matters

For residents in Saudi Arabia, the expansion of inspection centers directly impacts daily convenience and compliance. Periodic vehicle inspections, known locally as Al-Fahs Al-Dawri, are mandatory for maintaining roadworthiness. Having more centers nearby means shorter travel times for drivers who need to renew their inspection certificates.

Improved access to inspection services also contributes to overall road safety. Regular technical checks help identify mechanical issues before they lead to accidents. By ensuring a higher percentage of vehicles are inspected and maintained, the Kingdom aims to reduce traffic incidents and fatalities. This aligns with the goals of the Saudi Vision 2030, which prioritizes quality of life and safety.

Additionally, the expansion supports local economies by creating jobs in technical maintenance and administrative roles at these new facilities. It also encourages vehicle owners to stay compliant with regulations, fostering a culture of responsibility among drivers. The increased capacity helps the regulatory body monitor vehicle standards more effectively nationwide.

Background

Vehicle inspection in Saudi Arabia is a regulated process overseen by SASO. The system requires vehicles to undergo periodic technical checks to ensure they meet safety and environmental standards. These inspections cover critical components such as brakes, tires, lights, and emissions. Compliance is mandatory for vehicle registration renewal.

The Kingdom has been gradually modernizing its inspection infrastructure over the past few years. Previous initiatives focused on digitizing processes and improving the speed of service. The current push to reach 116 centers represents a significant scale-up from previous numbers. This growth is driven by the increasing number of vehicles on the road and the need for stricter enforcement of safety protocols.

The focus on southern regions like Jazan and Asir is notable, as these areas have seen rapid population growth and urbanization. Providing adequate inspection facilities in these regions ensures that regulatory standards are applied uniformly across the country, regardless of location.

What happens next

In the coming months, SASO is expected to continue opening additional inspection centers to meet the target of 116 sites. The organization will likely announce specific locations and opening dates for the remaining facilities. Public awareness campaigns may also be launched to inform drivers about the new locations and the importance of regular inspections.

As the new stations become fully operational, traffic flow at existing centers is expected to improve. The authorities will monitor performance metrics, such as processing times and customer satisfaction, to ensure the new facilities meet their intended goals. Further updates on the expansion plan may be released as each new center opens.
