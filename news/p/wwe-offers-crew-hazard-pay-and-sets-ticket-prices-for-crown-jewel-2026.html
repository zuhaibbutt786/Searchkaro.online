WWE offers crew hazard pay and sets ticket prices for Crown Jewel 2026

WWE disclosed a $3,000 hazard payment for staff heading to Saudi Arabia for Crown Jewel 2026 and published ticket details. The company also responded to the non‑appearance of CM Punk and Kevin Owens at the event.

Latest developments

WWE confirmed that all crew members assigned to the Crown Jewel 2026 show in Riyadh will receive a $3,000 hazard payment. The allowance is intended to cover additional costs and risks associated with travel to Saudi Arabia, where the event is scheduled for late 2026. The promotion also released the official ticketing schedule on its website, listing three price tiers ranging from PKR 8,000 for general admission to PKR 45,000 for premium ringside seats. Sales are expected to open next week through the WWE online portal and authorized local partners.

In a separate statement, WWE addressed the ongoing speculation surrounding the absence of former champion CM Punk and former NXT star Kevin Owens. Both wrestlers were originally rumored to appear at Crown Jewel, but WWE clarified that contractual negotiations and travel restrictions prevented their participation. The company emphasized that the event will still feature a full card of established talent, including local Saudi wrestlers and international stars.

Fans in Pakistan have shown heightened interest, with ticket search queries spiking after the announcement. Local travel agencies are already fielding inquiries about package deals that combine event tickets with accommodation in Riyadh. WWE’s regional office in Dubai is coordinating with Pakistani promoters to ensure smooth entry procedures for ticket holders.

The hazard pay announcement follows similar measures taken by other major sports organisations hosting events in the Kingdom, reflecting a broader industry trend of providing additional compensation for staff working in high‑risk environments. WWE’s decision is expected to set a benchmark for future overseas productions.

Overall, the combined rollout of crew compensation, ticket pricing, and clarification on talent line‑up marks a significant operational update for WWE’s most lucrative Middle Eastern venture to date.

Why it matters

For Pakistani wrestling fans, the Crown Jewel event represents one of the few opportunities to see WWE talent live without traveling to the United States or Europe. The release of ticket prices in local currency makes it easier for fans to assess affordability and plan travel. Moreover, the hazard pay policy signals WWE’s commitment to staff welfare, which may influence future employment decisions for Pakistani crew members seeking overseas assignments.

The absence of high‑profile names such as CM Punk and Kevin Owens could affect viewership expectations, but WWE’s reassurance that the card remains strong may mitigate disappointment. Local promoters see the event as a catalyst for growing the wrestling market in Pakistan, potentially leading to more regional shows and talent development programs.

Economically, the influx of Pakistani fans traveling to Saudi Arabia could boost tourism revenue for both countries. Ticket sales, hotel bookings, and related spending are likely to generate measurable financial activity, especially if the event draws large crowds from South Asia.

Background

Crown Jewel is part of WWE’s long‑term partnership with the Saudi General Sports Authority, launched in 2018. The series of events has been used to showcase WWE’s global brand while supporting Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 agenda to diversify its entertainment offerings. Previous editions have featured headline matches with world champions and have attracted significant international media attention.

WWE’s operational model for overseas shows typically involves transporting a sizable contingent of production crew, wrestlers, and support staff. The Kingdom’s climate, security protocols, and cultural considerations have historically required additional logistical planning, prompting the recent hazard pay decision.

CM Punk, a former WWE champion who returned to the ring in 2021, and Kevin Owens, a former NXT and main‑roster star, have both been linked to the 2026 Crown Jewel card through fan speculation and leaked contract talks. Their eventual non‑participation aligns with a pattern of last‑minute roster changes that WWE often cites as “business decisions” or “travel constraints.”

What happens next

Ticket sales for Crown Jewel 2026 will open globally within the next seven days, with a phased release for different price categories. WWE will continue to update fans on the final match card through official channels, and local Pakistani media are expected to provide coverage of travel arrangements and promotional events.

The company will also monitor crew feedback on the hazard pay scheme as the event approaches, potentially adjusting the policy for future overseas productions based on employee response and operational outcomes.
