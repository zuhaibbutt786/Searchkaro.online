Crypto hacks and network pauses dominate Pakistan's 2026 tech headlines

A major hack at Bitget and a sudden ThorChain network pause have put Pakistan's crypto community on alert. A Dawn cartoon captures the public's frustration.

Latest developments

The cryptocurrency exchange Bitget announced on September 25 that it would temporarily suspend all withdrawals after a sophisticated cyberattack resulted in the loss of approximately $350 million in user funds. The breach, described by the company as one of the largest in its history, prompted immediate action to protect remaining assets while investigators work with law enforcement. In a separate incident, the decentralized finance protocol ThorChain halted its network operations on the same day, citing an emergency security measure to prevent further exploitation. ThorChain officials clarified that the pause was not a targeted freeze of any specific user accounts but a precautionary step to safeguard the protocol's integrity. The dual events coincided with a political cartoon published by Dawn on September 28, which satirically depicted a frantic trader juggling burning wallets, reflecting widespread anxiety among Pakistani investors about the safety of digital assets. The cartoon quickly went viral, underscoring the public's growing concern over the volatility and security of crypto markets in the country.

Why it matters

For Pakistan's burgeoning crypto community, the Bitget hack represents a significant financial shock. Many local traders had turned to platforms like Bitget to access global markets, and the sudden loss of funds could erode confidence in both centralized exchanges and decentralized protocols. The ThorChain pause adds another layer of uncertainty, as it highlights vulnerabilities inherent in automated, code-driven financial systems. Together, these incidents have prompted calls from consumer groups and financial experts for clearer regulatory guidelines. The State Bank of Pakistan, which has previously warned against unregulated crypto activities, may feel increased pressure to intervene, potentially shaping the future landscape for digital asset trading in the country. Moreover, the viral Dawn cartoon illustrates how public sentiment can quickly coalesce around perceived threats, influencing policymakers' urgency to act.

Background

Cryptocurrency usage in Pakistan has surged over the past few years, driven by a young, tech-savvy population seeking alternative investment avenues amid economic challenges. While the State Bank has issued warnings about the risks of unlicensed exchanges, many users continue to operate on international platforms that fall outside domestic oversight. Previous incidents, such as the 2023 Binance phishing scams, have already exposed gaps in user education and security practices. ThorChain, launched in 2021, offers cross-chain liquidity without custodial intermediaries, attracting users looking for decentralized solutions. However, its reliance on smart contracts makes it susceptible to exploits, as demonstrated by the recent network pause. The Bitget breach, involving a coordinated attack on the exchange's cold storage and internal controls, underscores the ongoing battle between cybercriminals and crypto service providers worldwide.

What happens next

Regulators in Pakistan are expected to convene a special session to discuss immediate protective measures for crypto investors, possibly including mandatory licensing for exchanges operating with Pakistani residents. Bitget has pledged to cooperate with international law enforcement agencies to trace the stolen assets and has hinted at a compensation plan for affected users, though details remain sparse. ThorChain's development team is working on a post-mortem analysis to identify the vulnerability that triggered the network pause, with plans to roll out a security patch within weeks. Market analysts anticipate a short-term dip in crypto trading volumes in Pakistan as confidence rebuilds, but long-term adoption may continue if robust safeguards are implemented.

Summary
