Six Killed in Rahman Baba Express Crash as Investigation Reveals Safety Gaps

Six people died when the Rahman Baba Express derailed near Multan. An investigative report has uncovered key safety failures that authorities must address.

Latest developments

On Saturday morning, the Rahman Baba Express, a passenger train operating between Lahore and Multan, derailed near the town of Multan, resulting in the deaths of six passengers and injuries to dozens more, according to local news agencies. Emergency services arrived within minutes, and the wreckage was cleared by late afternoon. The incident has prompted an immediate response from Pakistan Railways, which announced a full investigation and pledged to cooperate with independent safety experts.

The investigative report released by a joint committee of railway officials and external auditors highlighted several critical failures. Among the findings were outdated track maintenance records, insufficient braking system checks, and a lack of real‑time monitoring equipment on the route. The report also noted that the train was operating at full capacity, which may have contributed to the severity of the crash.

Government officials have faced growing criticism for promises made during recent railway modernization campaigns. Critics argue that despite high‑profile announcements, concrete actions to upgrade infrastructure remain limited. The latest accident has intensified calls for accountability and accelerated implementation of safety upgrades.

In response, the Ministry of Railways issued a statement affirming that all pending projects will be reviewed and that additional funding will be allocated to address the identified deficiencies. The statement stopped short of naming specific timelines, but it emphasized a commitment to prevent similar tragedies in the future.

Why it matters

Rail travel remains a vital mode of transport for millions of Pakistanis, especially in the Punjab region where the Rahman Baba Express operates. A fatal accident of this magnitude not only causes immediate loss of life but also erodes public confidence in the national railway system. When passengers perceive the network as unsafe, they may shift to road or air travel, affecting the economic viability of rail services.

The incident also underscores a broader governance issue. Repeated promises to modernize the rail network have been a staple of political discourse, yet tangible progress has been slow. The gap between rhetoric and implementation can hinder foreign investment and limit the country's ability to meet its transportation needs. For ordinary commuters, the stakes are personal: safer trains mean fewer disruptions, lower travel costs, and reduced risk of injury.

Background

Pakistan Railways, established in the mid‑20th century, has struggled with aging infrastructure, limited funding, and operational inefficiencies. Over the past decade, the government launched several initiatives aimed at revamping tracks, upgrading signaling systems, and introducing newer rolling stock. However, many of these projects have faced delays due to budget constraints and bureaucratic hurdles.

The Rahman Baba Express, named after the revered Sufi poet Rahman Baba, is a popular intercity service that connects major urban centers. Historically, the route has experienced occasional delays and minor incidents, but the recent derailment marks one of the deadliest events on this line in recent memory. Previous accidents have prompted calls for systematic safety audits, but comprehensive reforms have been uneven.

What happens next

The investigative committee will submit its full findings to the Ministry of Railways within the next two weeks. Based on those recommendations, a task force is expected to draft a detailed action plan, which will include accelerated track replacement, enhanced braking system inspections, and the deployment of modern monitoring technology. Independent watchdog groups have pledged to monitor the implementation of these measures and to report any deviations.

In parallel, the government is likely to face parliamentary questions regarding the allocation of funds for railway upgrades. Public pressure may compel officials to prioritize safety projects over less urgent infrastructure works.
