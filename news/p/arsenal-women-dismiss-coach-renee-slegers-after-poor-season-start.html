Arsenal Women dismiss coach Renee Slegers after poor season start

Arsenal Women have announced the departure of head coach Renee Slegers following a weak start to the league campaign. The Dutch manager had guided the side to a historic Champions League triumph just 18 months earlier.

Latest developments

Arsenal Women announced on Tuesday that head coach Renee Slegers has left the club by mutual consent, ending a tenure that began in June 2022. Slegers guided the side to a historic UEFA Women's Champions League triumph in May 2023, the club's first European title. However, the team's performance in the 2024 Women's Super League has been below expectations, with only two wins from the opening six matches and a drop to fifth place in the table. The club released a statement saying the decision was taken after careful consideration of the current results and the need to reset the squad's direction. Slegers' departure comes just 18 months after the Champions League success and follows growing pressure from supporters and media. The announcement was made ahead of the club's next league match against Manchester United, and supporters gathered outside the Emirates Stadium to voice both disappointment and appreciation for Slegers' achievements.

Why it matters

The change in leadership has immediate implications for Arsenal's title challenge and its reputation in the Women's Super League. A poor start threatens the club's ambition to defend the domestic league crown and to qualify for next season's Champions League. Fans and sponsors closely monitor the stability of the coaching staff, as frequent turnover can affect player morale and commercial partnerships. Moreover, the decision highlights the increasing pressure on top clubs to deliver consistent results, even after historic achievements. Other WSL teams may view Arsenal's move as a signal that no success guarantees job security. The club also faces commercial considerations, as sponsors expect consistent performance to justify their investment, and a coaching change may affect upcoming marketing campaigns tied to the team's brand.

Background

Renee Slegers, a Dutch former midfielder, took over Arsenal Women after a successful spell at Everton, where she led the team to a top‑four finish and a strong cup run. Her appointment was seen as a progressive step, bringing a modern tactical approach to the Gunners. In her first season, Slegers steered Arsenal to the UEFA Women's Champions League final, defeating Lyon in the semi‑final and overcoming Barcelona in the final to secure the club's inaugural European trophy. The victory was celebrated as a milestone for English women's football. Prior to joining Arsenal, Slegers had also served as an assistant coach for the Netherlands women's national team, adding international experience to her résumé. During her playing days, Slegers featured for Dutch sides Heerenveen and FC Utrecht, earning caps for the Netherlands senior team before turning to coaching.

What happens next

Arsenal has appointed assistant coach Jonas Eidevall as interim manager while a comprehensive search for a permanent head coach begins. The club expects to name a new permanent coach before the next international break in October. Meanwhile, the squad will focus on upcoming league fixtures against Manchester United and Chelsea, matches that could determine whether Arsenal can climb back into the top three. The board has indicated that any new appointment will align with the club's long‑term vision of competing for domestic and European honours.
