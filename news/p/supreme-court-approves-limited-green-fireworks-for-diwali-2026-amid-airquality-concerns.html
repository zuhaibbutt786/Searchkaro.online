Supreme Court Approves Limited Green Fireworks for Diwali 2026 Amid Air‑Quality Concerns

India’s Supreme Court has set new rules for Diwali fireworks, permitting only green firecrackers during two time slots to reduce pollution. The decision follows rising concerns about air quality and safety near hospitals.

Latest developments

The Supreme Court of India has issued a landmark ruling that permits the use of green firecrackers during two specific time periods on Diwali 2026, while banning the bursting of fireworks near hospitals and other sensitive areas. The court’s decision aims to balance the cultural significance of the festival with growing concerns about air pollution and public health.

Green firecrackers, which are designed to produce lower levels of particulate matter and toxic gases, are expected to reduce the daily air‑quality index in major cities. In Delhi, where the AQI reached 312 earlier this year, the court’s guidance allows only environmentally friendly displays during the 9:00‑10:30 pm and 11:30‑12:30 am windows.

Despite the restrictions, the market has seen a surge in the sale of banned firecrackers. Retailers have been observed displaying fake green labels on products that do not meet the required standards, a practice highlighted by The Hindu. The influx of counterfeit items has prompted authorities to intensify inspections and impose penalties on violators.

Police and fire safety officials have announced a coordinated enforcement plan that includes checkpoints at major markets, public awareness campaigns, and the deployment of drones to monitor fireworks activity. Residents in densely populated neighborhoods have expressed mixed feelings, with some welcoming the reduced pollution while others worry about the enforcement of the new rules.

Why it matters

For the Indian diaspora in the United States, Diwali remains a key cultural event that involves large gatherings and the use of fireworks. The Supreme Court’s decision may influence how communities abroad plan celebrations, particularly in cities with significant Indian populations such as New York, San Francisco, and Chicago.

The ruling also has economic implications. Importers of fireworks and related goods may face new compliance requirements, potentially affecting trade flows between India and the United States. Companies that manufacture green firecrackers could see increased demand, while those producing traditional fireworks may need to adapt their product lines.

From a policy perspective, the decision offers a case study for U.S. regulators grappling with the environmental impact of fireworks. The balance between cultural tradition and public health achieved in India could inform local ordinances and environmental standards in American cities that host large fireworks displays.

Background

Diwali, known as the Festival of Lights, marks the victory of light over darkness and good over evil. Celebrated by millions of Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists worldwide, the festival typically spans five days and includes the lighting of lamps, feasting, and the bursting of fireworks.

India has a long history of regulating firecrackers due to their contribution to air and noise pollution. In recent years, several states have imposed bans or restrictions, citing health risks and environmental damage. The Supreme Court’s latest ruling builds on earlier directives that aimed to reduce particulate matter and protect vulnerable populations.

Health studies have linked fireworks to respiratory problems, eye irritation and cardiovascular stress. The high levels of particulate matter released during large celebrations have prompted public health agencies to issue advisories and, in some cases, temporary bans on fireworks in heavily polluted cities.

What happens next

The enforcement of the new rules will begin on the first day of Diwali, with authorities monitoring compliance through a network of checkpoints and surveillance equipment. The government has pledged to provide clear guidelines to manufacturers and retailers regarding the certification of green firecrackers.

If the initiative proves effective in reducing pollution and preventing accidents, it could serve as a model for future environmental regulations. Conversely, widespread non‑compliance or public backlash may lead to revisions of the time windows or stricter penalties.
