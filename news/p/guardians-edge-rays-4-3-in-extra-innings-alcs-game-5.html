Guardians Edge Rays 4-3 in Extra-Innings ALCS Game 5

The Guardians edged the Rays 4‑3 in a dramatic extra‑innings victory in Game 5 of the ALCS. Rookie pitcher Parker Messick earned his first postseason start, helping Cleveland take a 3‑2 series lead.

Latest developments

The American League Championship Series reached its fifth game on Sunday night, with the Cleveland Guardians hosting the Tampa Bay Rays at Progressive Field. After splitting the first four contests, the series is tied 2-2, making Game 5 a decisive encounter. Both clubs entered the matchup with strong pitching rotations, but Cleveland opted to start rookie right‑hander Parker Messick, marking his first appearance in a postseason setting.

Messick, a 2024 first‑round draft pick, earned his spot by posting a 3.45 ERA over 18 starts during the regular season. In his debut, he faced a Rays lineup that had averaged 4.8 runs per game in the series. Early innings saw Messick trade strikeouts for walks, but he settled after the third inning, limiting the Rays to one run and keeping the game within reach for the Guardians.

The Rays countered with veteran left‑hander Zach Eflin, who delivered a solid performance, allowing two runs over six innings. Tampa Bay’s offense, led by outfielder Randy Arozarena, generated several scoring opportunities, but defensive miscues in the eighth inning prevented a larger lead. The game remained close through the ninth, with the score tied at 3‑3 heading into extra innings.

In the top of the 10th, the Guardians broke the deadlock when shortstop Isiah Kiner‑Falefa drove in a go‑ahead run with a single to left field. Cleveland’s bullpen, anchored by closer Emmanuel Clase, shut down the Rays in the bottom half, preserving a 4‑3 victory. The win gave Cleveland a 3‑2 series lead and moved them one step closer to the World Series.

Background

The American League Championship Series determines the league’s representative in the World Series. This year’s matchup pits the Cleveland Guardians, who secured the AL Central with a 92‑70 record, against the Tampa Bay Rays, winners of the AL Wild Card and subsequent series against the Toronto Blue Jays. Both teams have relied heavily on strong pitching and disciplined defense throughout the season.

Cleveland’s path to the ALCS featured a sweep of the Minnesota Twins in the Division Series, while the Rays advanced by defeating the Blue Jays in a five‑game series. The two clubs have met three times in the regular season, with each side winning one game and one contest ending in a rain‑shortened no‑decision. The series has been marked by low‑scoring games and tight bullpen usage, setting the stage for a closely contested Game 5.
