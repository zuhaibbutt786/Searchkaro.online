Background

Autumn in the United States is typically characterized by a gradual cooling of temperatures and an increase in atmospheric instability. This period often sees the arrival of frontal systems that bring changes in wind direction and speed. The western states, in particular, experience significant wind events during this time due to their geographic features, such as mountain ranges and open plains.

The recent warm and sunny conditions were likely the result of a high-pressure system dominating the region. High pressure typically brings clear skies and calm winds, creating stable weather conditions. However, as this system weakens or moves, lower pressure systems can move in, bringing with them increased wind activity and potential precipitation.
