Ant & Dec criticised after awkward moment with Mariella Frostrup

Fans of the reality show raised concerns after an uncomfortable exchange between the hosts and guest Mariella Frostrup. The incident has ignited a wider discussion about presenter etiquette on live TV.

Latest developments

During the twelfth episode of ITV's reality series "I'm a Celebrity... Get Me Out Of Here!", hosts Ant McPartlin and Dec Donnelly were filmed alongside journalist Mariella Frostrup. Viewers noted that the pair seemed to speak over Frostrup, cutting her short as she attempted to contribute to the discussion. The clip quickly circulated on social media platforms, prompting thousands of comments that described the moment as "awkward" and "disrespectful".

The reaction was swift. Within hours, a petition on a popular change.org page called for the hosts to apologise, gathering over 5,000 signatures. Prominent TV critics on Twitter and Instagram echoed the sentiment, arguing that the hosts' behaviour set a poor example for live broadcasting standards. ITV issued a brief statement acknowledging the feedback but stopped short of confirming any formal apology.

Media outlets across the United Kingdom reported on the incident. Wales Online highlighted the "backlash" as a growing concern among viewers, while Metro.co.uk described the scene as "squirm-inducing" for fans who felt the hosts had ignored a respected guest. The ITV press release for episode twelve, posted on its official site, made no mention of the controversy, focusing instead on upcoming challenges for the celebrity contestants.

The episode's ratings remained strong, with overnight figures showing a 12% increase over the previous week. Nevertheless, the controversy has prompted a broader conversation about how live television handles guest contributions, especially when high‑profile personalities are involved. Industry analysts suggest that the incident may influence future production decisions regarding host‑guest dynamics.

Why it matters

The incident touches on public expectations of fairness and respect in televised interviews. Viewers expect hosts to facilitate conversation rather than dominate it, particularly when a guest brings professional expertise. When that balance appears to shift, audiences feel their trust in the programme is compromised.

For the UK television market, the episode serves as a reminder that social media can amplify viewer sentiment in real time. Broadcasters now monitor online reactions closely, as negative feedback can quickly affect brand perception. ITV, a major commercial network, may need to reassess its on‑air protocols to avoid similar incidents that could erode viewer loyalty.

Ant & Dec have been the face of "I'm a Celebrity... Get Me Out Of Here!" since its launch in 2002, building a reputation for light‑hearted banter and chemistry that appeals to a wide audience. Their partnership is one of the most enduring in British television, and they have previously been praised for handling sensitive topics with care.

Her appearance on the show was intended to provide a nuanced perspective on the celebrity challenges, adding depth to the entertainment format. Historically, guest contributions on reality programmes have been welcomed, with hosts typically allowing space for the guest to speak.
