Xbox Series X now runs PlayStation 5 games, raising questions

A new emulator port allows Xbox Series X to play PlayStation 5 games, sparking debate over cross‑platform compatibility. The move could influence buying decisions for UK gamers.

Latest developments

On Monday, several technology outlets reported that the Microsoft Xbox Series X can run PlayStation 5 games using an experimental emulator port. The software, released by a community developer, is built on the PS5's own software stack and has been tested on the Xbox hardware. A key milestone was the successful launch of Quake II, a classic first‑person shooter, on the Xbox Series X. The emulator translates the PS5's graphics and audio calls into commands that the Xbox GPU can understand, allowing the game to run at a stable frame rate. Despite the progress, the project remains in a beta stage. Neither Microsoft nor Sony has issued any statements, and the emulator is not part of the official console firmware. Users must install it manually, and it is not supported by the Xbox Store. If the emulator can be refined, it could open the door to a broader range of PlayStation titles on Xbox hardware, blurring the lines between the two ecosystems. However, the legal and technical hurdles remain significant. The community behind the emulator has already begun testing other titles such as Horizon Forbidden West and Spider‑Man: Miles Morales. Early feedback indicates that while performance is promising, frame‑rate drops and graphical glitches are still present, underscoring the need for further optimisation.

Why it matters

For gamers in Great Britain, the ability to play PlayStation 5 titles on an Xbox Series X could shift the balance of power between the two major console manufacturers. Players who already own an Xbox might be tempted to keep their hardware and access a wider library without purchasing a second console. The development also raises questions about intellectual property and digital rights management. If a third‑party emulator can bypass Sony's DRM, it could prompt stricter enforcement or changes in licensing agreements.

Background

Console emulation is not new; earlier attempts have seen PlayStation 4 games run on PCs and Nintendo Switch titles ported to other platforms. These projects have typically been limited to a handful of titles and are often unofficial. Sony and Microsoft have historically maintained strict separation of their software ecosystems. Sony's PlayStation Store and Microsoft's Xbox Store are designed to keep users within their respective ecosystems, and both companies have invested heavily in exclusive titles.

What happens next

Microsoft may decide to formalise the emulator, integrating it into the Xbox operating system, while Sony could respond with legal action or by tightening DRM. Industry analysts predict that the next few months will see a flurry of activity as both companies test the limits of cross‑platform compatibility.
