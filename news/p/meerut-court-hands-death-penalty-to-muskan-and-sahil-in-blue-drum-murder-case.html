Meerut Court Hands Death Penalty to Muskan and Sahil in Blue Drum Murder Case

In a landmark ruling, the Meerut court sentenced Muskan and Sahil to death for the murder of Saurabh Rajput. The verdict has reignited discussions on capital punishment across India.

Latest developments

The Meerut Sessions Court delivered its verdict on Thursday, sentencing Muskan and Sahil to death for the murder of Saurabh Rajput. The decision follows a 12‑month trial that examined evidence linking the two accused to the fatal shooting in a blue drum case that drew national attention. The court found that the accused had conspired to kill Rajput after a domestic dispute, and the prosecution presented forensic evidence and eyewitness testimony that the crime was pre‑meditated. The sentencing was announced publicly in the courtroom, with the judge noting that the punishment reflects the gravity of the offence and the need to deter future crimes. The ruling has been met with mixed reactions from the public, with some praising the strict enforcement of law and others questioning the use of the death penalty in India.

Why it matters

The verdict has significant implications for the debate over capital punishment in India, where the death penalty is still legally available but rarely applied. It underscores the judiciary's willingness to impose the ultimate punishment in cases involving pre‑planned violence, potentially influencing future sentencing in similar cases. For residents of Meerut and the wider Uttar Pradesh region, the case highlights the importance of law and order and may affect public confidence in the criminal justice system. Nationally, the ruling adds to the discourse on whether the death penalty serves as a deterrent or merely as a punitive measure, prompting lawmakers and civil society groups to reassess their positions.

Background

The Blue Drum case began in early 2026 when Saurabh Rajput, a local businessman, was found dead in a blue drum near Meerut. Initial investigations pointed to a domestic dispute involving his wife Muskan and his lover Sahil. Police recovered the weapon and forensic evidence that linked the accused to the murder. The trial, conducted in the Meerut Sessions Court, lasted nine months, during which both defense and prosecution presented extensive evidence. The case attracted media attention due to the high‑profile nature of the crime and the involvement of a prominent local figure.

What happens next

Following the death penalty verdict, Muskan and Sahil will have the right to file appeals in higher courts, including the High Court of Uttar Pradesh and potentially the Supreme Court of India. The appeals process can take several months to years, depending on the complexity of the case and the backlog of the judiciary. If the appeals are dismissed, the execution could be scheduled, subject to the approval of the state government and the President of India, as required by the Indian legal framework. The case will continue to be monitored by legal experts and human rights organizations.
