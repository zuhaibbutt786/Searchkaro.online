PNB Employees Accused of Misappropriating Crores from Fixed Deposits in Greater Noida

Employees at a Punjab National Bank branch in Greater Noida are under scrutiny after reports of crores missing from fixed deposits. The bank has suspended several staff members while investigations continue.

Latest developments

On 28 October 2026, reports surfaced that a branch of Punjab National Bank (PNB), a major public sector bank in India, had a large sum of money missing from fixed deposit accounts. The missing amount, estimated in the crores, was traced to the bank’s own records, raising immediate concerns among depositors. Investigations by the bank’s internal audit team and the local police found irregularities in the handling of fixed deposits. Employees at the branch were accused of manipulating account records to divert funds. Several staff members were suspended pending further inquiry. The incident has affected a wide range of customers, including farmers and small business owners who had placed their savings in fixed deposits. Many of them have reported that the money they expected to receive upon maturity was not available. The bank has issued a statement saying it is cooperating fully with authorities and will take disciplinary action against those found guilty. It also promised to refund affected depositors once the investigation is complete.

Why it matters

Fixed deposits are a common savings vehicle in India, especially among rural and semi-urban populations. When a public sector bank is implicated in a fraud, it erodes public trust and can lead to a loss of confidence in the banking system. For farmers and small traders in Greater Noida, the loss of crores can mean the difference between a successful harvest cycle and financial distress. The incident also highlights the vulnerability of depositors to internal malpractices.

Background

Punjab National Bank, founded in 1894, is one of India’s largest public sector banks. It operates a network of branches across the country and offers a range of banking services, including fixed deposits, loans, and retail banking. In recent years, PNB has faced scrutiny over a number of financial irregularities, most notably the 2018 fraud involving the transfer of ₹2.5 lakh crore to a shell company. The current case adds to a growing list of incidents that have raised questions about the bank’s internal controls.

What happens next

The Greater Noida police have opened a formal investigation into the alleged fraud. They are examining transaction records, interviewing staff, and collaborating with the Reserve Bank of India’s compliance unit. PNB has pledged to conduct a comprehensive audit of all its branches in the region and to strengthen its monitoring systems. Depositors are advised to keep records of their transactions and to report any discrepancies promptly.

Summary
