ICC announces 2027 World Cup schedule as South Africa co‑hosts qualifiers

The International Cricket Council has unveiled the complete schedule for the 2027 Men's Cricket World Cup, highlighting an India‑Australia clash on October 7. South Africa and Namibia will jointly stage the qualifying tournament later this

Latest developments

The International Cricket Council (ICC) confirmed on Monday the full fixture schedule for the 2027 Men’s Cricket World Cup. The calendar lists 48 matches spanning the group stage, quarter‑finals, semi‑finals and the final, with the tournament set to kick off in early October and conclude in late December. The opening group match will feature a high‑profile showdown between India and Australia on 7 October, a game that is expected to draw massive global viewership.

The released schedule details the venues across the three host nations – South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia – and allocates specific dates for each round. While the exact stadium for the India‑Australia clash has not been disclosed, the ICC has confirmed that the match will be played at a premier venue capable of accommodating large crowds and extensive broadcast requirements.

In parallel, the ICC announced that South Africa and Namibia will co‑host the World Cup Qualifiers from 26 February to 21 March 2024. Ten associate and lower‑ranked full‑member nations will compete for the remaining spots in the 2027 tournament, with the top‑two finishers earning direct entry and the third‑placed side entering a play‑off.

The qualifying series will be staged at multiple grounds in both countries, offering local fans the chance to see emerging cricket talent. Broadcast partners have secured rights to air the qualifiers across Africa, Asia and Europe, ensuring that performances in the lead‑up to the World Cup receive worldwide exposure.

Why it matters

For South African cricket supporters, the dual role of hosting both World Cup matches and the qualifiers represents a significant boost to the sport’s domestic profile. The qualifiers will bring international teams to regional venues, generating tourism revenue and creating opportunities for local businesses during the February‑March window.

The tournament also provides a pathway for Namibia, a rising associate nation, to showcase its infrastructure and organisational capabilities on a global stage. Successful co‑hosting could strengthen Namibia’s bid for future ICC events and encourage further investment in cricket facilities throughout Southern Africa.

From a competitive perspective, the schedule gives the South African national team a clearer picture of its preparation timeline. Knowing the exact dates of group matches allows the team’s management to plan training camps, player rotations and injury management well in advance, potentially improving performance when the World Cup arrives.

Background

The ICC, the global governing body for cricket, organizes the World Cup every four years, rotating host nations to promote the sport worldwide. The 2027 edition will be the first to be jointly hosted by three countries, a model previously used for the 2019 Women’s World Cup in England and Wales. This collaborative approach aims to spread the economic and sporting benefits across a wider region.

Historically, the World Cup Qualifiers have served as the gateway for associate members to earn a place in the main event. The last qualifying tournament, held in 2023, saw several emerging teams secure historic World Cup berths, highlighting the growing competitiveness of cricket beyond the traditional powerhouses.

South Africa last hosted a World Cup in 2003, a tournament remembered for its high attendance and memorable matches. Namibia, meanwhile, made its World Cup debut in 2021 as a qualifier, marking a milestone for the nation’s cricket development program.

What happens next

The next steps involve finalising venue preparations and ticketing for the qualifiers, with the ICC expected to release detailed match‑day information in the coming weeks. Following the conclusion of the qualifiers in March, the successful teams will join the pre‑qualified nations as the World Cup field is confirmed, setting the stage for the full draw and promotional activities ahead of the October kickoff.
