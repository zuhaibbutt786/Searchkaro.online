James Ransone Omitted from Emmy 'In Memoriam' Tribute Sparks Outcry

James Ransone was left out of the Emmy Awards' 'In Memoriam' tribute, leading his widow to publicly criticize the Television Academy. The omission has sparked discussions about recognition for late actors.

What is happening

At the 75th Primetime Emmy Awards, the televised 'In Memoriam' segment omitted the name of actor James Ransone, who passed away in 2023. The segment, which honors performers who died in the previous year, typically lists dozens of names in a montage of still images and footage.

The omission was noticed by Ransone’s widow, who posted a statement on social media criticizing the Television Academy for the oversight. Her message called the Academy’s decision a disrespectful omission that failed to recognize Ransone’s contributions to television.

Ransone was best known for his roles in series such as 'The Wire', 'The Walking Dead', and 'The Equalizer'. He had a career spanning more than two decades and received praise for his character work across multiple genres.

The Academy’s 'In Memoriam' segment is produced by a committee that selects names based on eligibility rules, including the actor’s death date and prior Emmy nominations. Critics argue that the selection process can be opaque and that omissions may occur unintentionally.

The public reaction to the omission has amplified the conversation around how the industry commemorates deceased performers, especially those who have not received major awards themselves.

Background and context

James Ransone began his career in the late 1990s and quickly became a recognizable character actor. His performances in crime dramas and science fiction series earned him a dedicated fan base.

Ransone’s death in 2023 was reported by major entertainment outlets, noting his impact on the shows he appeared in. He had not previously received an Emmy nomination, a fact that may have influenced the committee’s decision process.

The 'In Memoriam' segment has faced criticism before, most notably in 2015 when several high‑profile names were omitted. The Television Academy has responded to such incidents by reviewing its criteria and occasionally adding names after the broadcast.

The Academy’s selection committee is composed of industry professionals who review eligibility lists, but the process is not publicly disclosed in detail. This lack of transparency can lead to public speculation and disappointment when names are left out.

What to know next

The Television Academy has not yet issued a formal response to the widow’s criticism. Industry observers anticipate a statement clarifying the selection criteria and the steps taken to prevent future omissions.

If the Academy chooses to add Ransone’s name in a post‑broadcast update, it would set a precedent for correcting omissions. However, the timing of such updates is limited by broadcast schedules and contractual obligations.

The incident may prompt the Academy to review its nomination process for the 'In Memoriam' segment, potentially leading to more inclusive criteria or a public review of past omissions.
