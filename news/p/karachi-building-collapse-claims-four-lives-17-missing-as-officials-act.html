Karachi Building Collapse Claims Four Lives, 17 Missing as Officials Act

A six‑storey residential building in Karachi’s Baloch Colony collapsed early Thursday, killing four and leaving 17 missing. Authorities have ordered the removal of the SBCA chief and suspended 16 officers.

Latest developments

The collapse occurred at the corner of Main Road and Baloch Colony Avenue in Karachi on Thursday morning, when a six‑storey residential building suddenly gave way. Witnesses reported a loud crack followed by a massive collapse that engulfed the lower floors.

Initial reports from local hospitals confirm that four residents were killed and 17 others are missing. Rescue teams have been working around the clock to clear rubble and locate survivors.

In response, Chief Minister Murad ordered the immediate removal of the SBCA chief and the suspension of 16 officers who were overseeing the building’s compliance. The move comes after allegations that lax enforcement contributed to the tragedy.

The Sindh Building Control Authority (SBCA) has announced that it will conduct a full audit of all high‑rise structures in the city. Rescue operations are being coordinated by the Karachi Fire Department, the Pakistan Army, and local NGOs.

While the authorities have yet to release a formal statement on the cause of the collapse, preliminary investigations point to possible structural deficiencies and unauthorized modifications to the building’s design.

Why it matters

The incident highlights the urgent need for stricter enforcement of building codes in Karachi, a city that has seen several high‑rise failures in recent years. Residents of Baloch Colony and surrounding areas are now demanding accountability and better safety measures.

For the families of the victims, the loss is devastating, but the event also raises concerns about the safety of other residential towers that may have similar vulnerabilities. The government’s swift action signals a shift towards greater transparency and responsibility.

Background

The Sindh Building Control Authority is responsible for ensuring that construction projects comply with national safety standards. Over the past decade, several incidents involving building collapses have prompted calls for reforms and tighter oversight.

Baloch Colony, a densely populated neighbourhood in Karachi, has a history of rapid, informal development. Many structures were erected without proper permits, leading to long‑term safety risks that have only recently begun to receive official attention.

What happens next

A judicial inquiry will be launched to investigate the collapse, with the aim of identifying any negligence or regulatory failures. The inquiry will also review the SBCA’s processes and recommend reforms to prevent future incidents.

Compensation claims for the families of the deceased and the missing will be processed through a dedicated fund set up by the provincial government. The inquiry’s findings are expected to be released within the next 90 days.
