Pakistani Netflix series starring Sanam Saeed faces Indian ban request

Sanam Saeed appears in Netflix's first Pakistani series, prompting a call from India's Bollywood union to block it. The dispute highlights rising tensions over cultural content across the border.

Latest developments

The Bollywood Cine Workers Union (AICWA) has formally asked Prime Minister Narendra Modi to prevent the streaming of the Pakistani Netflix series "Jo Bachay Hai Sang Samait Lo" in India. The request was reported by Reuters on 25 November 2026 and echoed in local coverage by Dawn. The union argues that the series, which marks Pakistan's debut on the global streaming platform, could affect the domestic film and television market. The series features well‑known Pakistani actors, including Sanam Saeed, whose participation has drawn additional attention. Fawad Khan, another lead, responded to the controversy by saying the show speaks a "global language" and should be judged on its artistic merit rather than nationality. The Indian government has not yet issued a formal decision, but the appeal has intensified public debate in both countries.

Why it matters

For viewers in Pakistan, the series represents a milestone: a home‑grown production reaching a worldwide audience through Netflix. Sanam Saeed, celebrated for her roles in acclaimed dramas such as "Zindagi Gulzar Hai," brings star power that could encourage further investment in Pakistani content. In India, the call for a ban reflects concerns among local industry groups about competition from foreign streaming services and the perceived cultural impact of cross‑border media. The dispute also touches on broader political sensitivities between the two nations, where cultural exchange often becomes a proxy for diplomatic relations. The outcome may influence how future co‑operations or independent productions are handled across the border.

Background

Netflix entered the Pakistani market in early 2026, seeking to expand its catalogue with original regional content. "Jo Bachay Hai Sang Samait Lo" is billed as the platform's first Pakistani series, aiming to showcase local storytelling to a global audience. Sanam Saeed, a veteran actress with a strong following in South Asia, was cast alongside Fawad Khan, another high‑profile Pakistani star. The series' title roughly translates to "We Will Gather the Children Together," hinting at family‑oriented themes. Historically, Indian film and television unions have raised objections to foreign content that they believe could undermine domestic production, a stance that resurfaced with this Netflix release.

What happens next

The Indian Ministry of Information and Broadcasting is expected to review the union's petition in the coming weeks. If a ban is imposed, the series would be inaccessible to Indian Netflix subscribers, potentially limiting its viewership and advertising revenue. Conversely, a decision to allow the series could set a precedent for more South Asian content on Indian platforms, encouraging further collaboration. In Pakistan, producers are monitoring the situation closely, as international reception will affect future funding and distribution deals for similar projects.
