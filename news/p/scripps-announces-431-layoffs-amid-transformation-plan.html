Scripps announces 431 layoffs amid transformation plan

Scripps Media has announced a wave of layoffs affecting more than 400 employees. The cuts are part of a broader transformation that includes new technology and 100 promised hires.

Latest developments

The E. W. Scripps Company disclosed on Thursday that it will eliminate 431 positions across its television and digital operations. The move is framed as part of an "enterprise-wide transformation" aimed at integrating artificial intelligence tools and streamlining production workflows. In a statement, the CEO said the restructuring will create 100 new jobs focused on technology and digital content, though the timeline for those hires was not detailed.

The cuts have already hit local stations. In Cincinnati, WCPO-TV announced deep job reductions, affecting newsroom staff and technical crews. The station, a longtime Scripps affiliate, will see a significant reduction in its reporting team, with several roles being consolidated. Similarly, in Milwaukee, more than a dozen employees at TMJ4 were let go as the station aligns with the company’s broader cost‑saving measures. Both stations are expected to continue delivering local news, but with smaller teams.

Scripps attributes the layoffs to the need for greater efficiency in a rapidly changing media landscape. The company cited the rising costs of traditional broadcasting and the competitive pressure from digital platforms that leverage AI for content creation and distribution. By reducing labor costs and investing in automation, Scripps hopes to maintain profitability while expanding its digital footprint.

Employee reactions have been mixed. While some staff members expressed concern about job security and the potential impact on news quality, others acknowledged the inevitability of technological change in the industry. No union strikes have been reported, and the company has indicated that it will offer severance packages and outplacement assistance to affected workers.

Why it matters

The layoffs underscore a broader shift in American broadcast media, where legacy television groups are confronting declining ad revenues and the rise of streaming services. Reducing newsroom staff can affect the depth and breadth of local coverage, potentially leaving communities with fewer resources for investigative reporting and emergency alerts.

For the U.S. job market, the cuts represent a notable loss of media‑related positions in regions like Ohio and Wisconsin, where the affected stations are major employers. The promised 100 new roles may offset some of the impact, but those positions are likely to require technical expertise, creating a skills gap for workers displaced by the restructuring.

Background

Scripps, founded in 1878 as a newspaper publisher, entered the television business in the 1990s and has grown through a series of acquisitions, including the purchase of several local stations from the former Journal Communications. Over the past decade, the company has expanded its digital platforms, launching streaming news services and investing in online advertising.

Industry analysts note that many broadcast groups have turned to automation to cut costs, a trend accelerated by the COVID‑19 pandemic and the subsequent shift of audiences to online content. Scripps' recent strategy mirrors moves by peers such as Sinclair and Nexstar, which have also announced workforce reductions tied to technology upgrades.

What happens next

Scripps plans to roll out its AI‑driven workflow tools over the next six months, beginning with pilot projects at the stations most affected by the layoffs. The company has set a target to fill the 100 new technology‑focused positions by the end of the fiscal year, though recruitment may be competitive given the specialized skill set required. Observers will watch whether the transformation improves profitability without compromising local news quality.
