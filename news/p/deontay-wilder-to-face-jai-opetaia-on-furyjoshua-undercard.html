Deontay Wilder to face Jai Opetaia on Fury‑Joshua undercard

Deontay Wilder has confirmed a bout with New Zealand's Jai Opetaia on the undercard of the highly anticipated Fury‑Joshua heavyweight clash. The fight adds a new chapter to Wilder's career as he seeks to regain momentum in the division.

Latest developments

On Tuesday, boxing news outlets reported that former world champion Deontay Wilder has agreed to face New Zealand prospect Jai Opetaia on the undercard of the upcoming heavyweight showdown between Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua. The agreement was confirmed by Wilder's camp and the promoters of the event, adding a high‑profile fight to the card scheduled for 26 November in London.

The bout will take place in the main arena of the Wembley Stadium, where the Fury‑Joshua fight is set to headline. It is expected to be a 10‑round contest, with Wilder looking to demonstrate his power and regain a place in the top tier of the division after a series of mixed results in recent years.

Wilder, who has held the WBC heavyweight title on two separate occasions, has faced a number of challenges since his loss to Tyson Fury in 2020. His recent bouts have seen him win by knockout but also suffer defeats, prompting speculation about his future in the sport.

The decision to fight Opetaia, a rising star with a strong knockout record, is seen as a strategic move for Wilder. A victory could position him for a potential rematch with Fury or a new title opportunity, while a loss would raise questions about his standing in the heavyweight hierarchy.

Why it matters

For British boxing fans, the inclusion of Wilder on the undercard brings international attention to the event and offers a chance to see a seasoned veteran compete in the UK for the first time since 2019. The fight is likely to attract viewers who follow heavyweight boxing closely, boosting ticket sales and broadcast ratings.

The outcome of Wilder's match could influence the narrative surrounding the heavyweight division in the UK. A decisive win would reinforce the appeal of heavyweight bouts, while a setback might prompt discussions about the future of the sport's leading figures.

Background

Deontay Wilder, born in 1985 in Detroit, rose to prominence with a record 42 knockouts in 48 professional fights. He captured the WBC title in 2015 and defended it multiple times before losing to Fury in a controversial split decision.

Jai Opetaia, a 27‑year‑old from New Zealand, has built a reputation for his aggressive style and powerful punches. He has fought several opponents in the heavyweight division, earning a reputation as a dangerous contender.

What happens next

The fight is scheduled for 26 November, with training camps already underway. Both fighters will undergo medical examinations and weigh‑ins in the weeks leading up to the event. The result will be announced during the broadcast, with potential implications for future match‑ups in the heavyweight scene.
