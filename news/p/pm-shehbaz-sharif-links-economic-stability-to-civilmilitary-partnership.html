PM Shehbaz Sharif links economic stability to civil‑military partnership

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif highlighted the role of coordinated political and military leadership in stabilising Pakistan's economy, and called for an export‑focused growth strategy.

Latest developments

At a Pakistan‑United States business forum in Islamabad, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said the country's recent macro‑economic resilience is the result of a "productive partnership" between the elected government and the armed forces. He pointed to steady foreign exchange reserves, a modest rebound in GDP growth and the successful completion of the latest tranche of the IMF programme as evidence of this cooperation. The prime minister emphasized that coordinated decision‑making helped avoid abrupt policy shifts that had plagued earlier administrations. He thanked the military leadership for its role in stabilising the energy sector and supporting fiscal discipline, describing the alliance as a cornerstone of Pakistan's current economic outlook.

Shehbaz Sharif also used the gathering to outline an export‑led growth agenda. He urged Pakistani manufacturers, especially in textiles and engineering, to diversify markets beyond traditional destinations and to adopt higher‑value products. The prime minister announced a series of incentives, including tax rebates for exporters, streamlined customs procedures and a dedicated fund to support small and medium‑sized enterprises seeking to enter new overseas markets. He called on foreign investors to partner with local firms, arguing that such collaboration would generate jobs and reduce the country's trade deficit.

Business leaders at the forum welcomed the prime minister's remarks. The head of the Pakistan Business Council said the clear signal of government‑military cooperation reduced uncertainty for investors and could improve credit ratings. Representatives from the United States Chamber of Commerce noted that the proposed export incentives aligned with their own trade promotion strategies and expressed interest in joint ventures. However, some analysts cautioned that policy implementation would need transparent monitoring to ensure that benefits reach the intended sectors.

Following the event, the cabinet is expected to finalize a set of regulatory reforms aimed at easing access to finance for exporters. Sources within the Ministry of Finance indicated that a draft amendment to the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act will be presented to parliament within weeks. The prime minister also scheduled a bilateral meeting with the U.S. Secretary of State later this month to discuss trade cooperation and potential assistance for Pakistan's renewable energy projects.

Why it matters

For ordinary Pakistanis, a stable macro‑economic environment can translate into lower inflation and steadier prices for essential goods. By keeping the rupee relatively strong and maintaining foreign reserves, the government can curb import‑price shocks that have previously eroded household purchasing power.

An export‑focused strategy promises to create new employment opportunities, particularly for youth in manufacturing hubs such as Faisalabad and Karachi. Increased export earnings would also help narrow the current account gap, reducing the need for external borrowing and easing the fiscal pressure on the state budget.

Background

Civil‑military relations have long shaped Pakistan's political landscape. Since the early 2000s, periods of tension between elected officials and the armed forces have often coincided with economic volatility, while phases of cooperation have generally supported steadier growth. The current administration inherited a fragile economy after three consecutive balance‑of‑payments crises and a series of short‑lived governments.

The International Monetary Fund's 2023 programme set strict fiscal targets and called for structural reforms. Success in meeting those conditions required coordination across ministries and the military, which controls significant logistical and energy assets. The recent partnership cited by the prime minister reflects an effort to align these institutions around a common economic roadmap.

What happens next

In the coming weeks, parliament will debate the proposed financial reforms while the prime minister's office prepares detailed guidelines for the export incentive scheme. International donors, including the IMF and the World Bank, are expected to review Pakistan's progress, and the outcome of the upcoming U.S. meeting could shape future trade and investment flows.

Summary

- The prime minister credits civil‑military cooperation for recent economic stability. - He announced export‑oriented incentives to boost growth and jobs. - Legislative and diplomatic steps are planned to implement reforms and attract foreign investment.
