Powerus Secures Initial Drone Order from Pakistan Army

US drone manufacturer Powerus has secured an initial order from the Pakistan Army following a memorandum of understanding. The deal marks a step forward in defence collaboration between the two nations.

What is happening

In a recent development, American drone manufacturer Powerus announced that it has signed a memorandum of understanding with the Pakistan Army. The agreement formalises a partnership that will see the company supply unmanned aerial vehicles to the Pakistani military. The initial order, which was confirmed during a joint press briefing, represents the first commercial transaction between the two parties.

Powerus, known for its small‑to‑medium sized drones used for surveillance and target acquisition, will deliver a fleet of aircraft that can operate at various altitudes and provide real‑time data to ground commanders. The package includes training for Pakistani personnel and maintenance support, ensuring that the equipment can be integrated into existing operational frameworks.

The move follows a broader trend of increased defence cooperation between the United States and Pakistan. Over the past year, both governments have discussed joint exercises, technology sharing and capacity building. The MOU is seen as a practical extension of those discussions, allowing Pakistan to modernise its aerial reconnaissance capabilities.

According to the announcement, the delivery of the first batch of drones is expected within the next six months. Powerus will work with Pakistani logistics units to set up installation sites and conduct field testing. The company also plans to establish a regional support centre to handle ongoing servicing and upgrades.

Background and context

Powerus was founded in 2014 and has grown to become a prominent player in the global drone market. Its products are used by various air forces and security agencies worldwide, offering modular design and adaptable payload options. The company has previously supplied equipment to NATO allies and has been involved in joint research projects with U.S. defense contractors.

Pakistan’s military has historically relied on a mix of domestic production and foreign imports. In recent years, the army has increased its focus on unmanned platforms to enhance situational awareness and reduce risk to personnel. The partnership with Powerus follows earlier agreements with other drone makers, such as the purchase of Israeli UAVs and U.S. commercial platforms.

What to know next

The next phase will involve rigorous testing of the drones in Pakistani training facilities. Pilots and technicians will evaluate performance under various environmental conditions. Successful trials will pave the way for full deployment across multiple army units.

Stakeholders will also monitor the impact of the deal on regional defence dynamics. Analysts expect that the enhanced surveillance capability may influence strategic calculations in the broader South Asian region. The outcome of this cooperation could set a precedent for future technology transfers between the United States and Pakistan.
