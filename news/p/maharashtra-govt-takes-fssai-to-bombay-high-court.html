Maharashtra Govt Takes FSSAI to Bombay High Court

Latest developments

On October 26, 2026, the Maharashtra government approached the Bombay High Court with a petition that challenges the authority of the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI). The petition claims that the centre's food safety regulations encroach on powers that should belong to the state, citing constitutional provisions that grant states autonomy over certain public health matters.

The filing arrives at a time of heightened tension between the state administration, led by Chief Minister Eknath Shinde, and the central government headed by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Senior BJP leader Devendra Fadnavis, a former chief minister and current deputy chief minister, has publicly emphasized the need for greater state input in policy decisions that affect local industries and consumers.

Congress party representatives have quickly responded, suggesting that Fadnavis may be sending a subtle message to the Delhi establishment. Party spokespersons have questioned whether the legal challenge is intended to pressure the central government ahead of upcoming state and national elections, framing the move as a strategic political maneuver.

Legal analysts expect the High Court to hear arguments in the coming weeks. They note that the case could set an important precedent regarding the extent of the centre's regulatory reach over states, especially in sectors like food safety where uniform standards have traditionally been enforced at the national level.

Why it matters

Food safety standards influence the daily lives of millions of Indians, affecting everything from street food vendors to large-scale manufacturers. A ruling that limits the FSSAI's authority could lead to a fragmented regulatory environment, where each state adopts its own standards, potentially complicating trade and compliance for businesses operating across state borders.

Politically, the case highlights growing friction between the BJP‑led centre and state governments that are either allied or in opposition. The outcome may shape future negotiations on federal matters, especially as the next general election approaches and parties seek to demonstrate their commitment to federalism and local governance.

For the opposition, the lawsuit offers a platform to criticize what they describe as the central government's overreach. It also allows regional leaders like Fadnavis to position themselves as defenders of state rights, a narrative that could resonate with voters who feel that decisions affecting their daily lives are being made without adequate local input.

Background

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India was established in 2006 to consolidate multiple food safety laws under a single national framework. Its responsibilities include setting safety standards, conducting inspections, and enforcing compliance across the country. Over the years, the FSSAI has introduced several regulations aimed at improving food quality, labeling, and hygiene.

Maharashtra has long been a stronghold for the Bharatiya Janata Party, but internal dynamics shifted after the 2022 state elections. Devendra Fadnavis, who served as chief minister from 2014 to 2019, remains an influential figure in the state's politics and currently serves as deputy chief minister. His political stature gives weight to the state's legal challenge and amplifies its visibility at the national level.

Relations between Maharashtra and the centre have been strained on multiple fronts, including agricultural pricing, water sharing, and now regulatory authority. The current petition follows earlier disputes where the state sought greater control over the implementation of central schemes, arguing that a one‑size‑fits‑all approach does not account for regional variations in infrastructure and market conditions.

What happens next

The Bombay High Court will review the petition and may issue an interim order or schedule a full hearing to examine the constitutional arguments presented by both sides. Both the state and the centre are expected to submit detailed legal briefs outlining their interpretations of the division of powers under the Indian Constitution.

Depending on the verdict, the case could be escalated to the Supreme Court for a final determination. In parallel, the Maharashtra government may continue to lobby for legislative amendments at the national level to clarify the scope of state versus central authority in food safety matters.
