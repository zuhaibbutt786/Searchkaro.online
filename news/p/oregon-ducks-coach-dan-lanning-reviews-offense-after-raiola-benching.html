Oregon Ducks Coach Dan Lanning Reviews Offense After Raiola Benching

Oregon Ducks head coach Dan Lanning announced a full offensive review following the benching of quarterback Dylan Raiola. The move signals possible staff adjustments as the team seeks consistency on the field.

Latest developments

Oregon Ducks head coach Dan Lanning confirmed on Monday that the team will conduct a comprehensive review of its offensive unit after benching sophomore quarterback Dylan Raiola for the upcoming game against Washington State. Lanning told reporters that the decision was made after a series of inconsistent performances and that the coaching staff will evaluate every aspect of the offense, from play-calling to personnel assignments. The benching marks the first time Raiola has been sidelined this season, and it underscores the pressure on the Ducks to improve after a rocky start to the 2026 campaign.

In a separate interview with OregonLive.com, Lanning hinted that additional staff changes are forthcoming, though he declined to specify which positions will be affected. He described the upcoming adjustments as part of a broader effort to align the coaching philosophy with the talent on the roster. While the details remain confidential, sources close to the program suggest that the offensive coordinator could be reassigned, and that the team may bring in new position coaches to address gaps identified during the review.

The move has drawn attention from national analysts. Tom Fornelli of CBS Sports noted that Oregon's recent power‑rating fluctuations present a conundrum for the Ducks, who have struggled to translate recruiting success into on‑field results. Fornelli pointed out that the team's defensive metrics remain solid, but the offense has failed to sustain drives, leading to a lower overall rating despite a strong schedule. Lanning's decision to overhaul the offense is seen as an attempt to close that gap and restore balance.

Fans reacted on social media with a mix of optimism and concern. Some praised Lanning for taking decisive action, while others questioned whether benching a highly touted quarterback could disrupt team chemistry. The coaching staff is expected to hold a team meeting later this week to outline the new offensive approach and to address any lingering doubts among players.

Overall, the Ducks appear poised for a period of introspection and adjustment as they aim to finish the season with a winning record and a bowl invitation.

Why it matters

The Ducks' offensive struggles have direct implications for the Pac‑12 standings, where every conference win can determine bowl eligibility and revenue distribution. A more effective offense could lift Oregon back into contention for the conference title, which in turn would boost the program's national profile and recruiting appeal.

Beyond the immediate season, Lanning's willingness to make bold personnel decisions signals a shift in the culture of the program. By publicly acknowledging shortcomings and committing to change, the coach sets a precedent for accountability that could influence other teams in the league. This approach may also affect how fans and alumni view the administration's support for the football program, potentially impacting future funding and facility upgrades.

Finally, the situation offers a case study for other college football programs facing similar dilemmas. The balance between protecting a high‑profile quarterback and pursuing overall team improvement is a recurring theme across the sport, and Oregon's handling of the issue will be closely watched by peers and analysts alike.

Background

Dan Lanning took over as Oregon's head coach in December 2025 after a successful stint as a defensive coordinator at a major Power Five school. Known for his aggressive defensive schemes, Lanning was hired to revitalize a program that had experienced several coaching changes in the past decade. His first recruiting class was highly rated, bringing in several five‑star prospects, including quarterback Dylan Raiola, who was projected as a future star.

Raiola entered the 2026 season as the starting quarterback but faced criticism after a series of turnovers and missed reads in the first four games. The Ducks' offense, traditionally known for its speed and creativity, has struggled to find rhythm under the current playbook, leading to low scoring outputs and early deficits.

The Ducks' defensive unit, meanwhile, has performed relatively well, ranking among the top ten in the conference for points allowed. This disparity between a stout defense and a faltering offense has created a strategic imbalance that the coaching staff is now forced to address.

Historically, Oregon has been a trendsetter in college football, often adopting innovative offensive concepts. The current situation marks a departure from that tradition, prompting a reassessment of the team's identity and long‑term goals.

What happens next

Lanning and his staff will convene a series of film sessions and practice adjustments over the next two weeks to implement the findings of the offensive review. The first test of the new approach will come in the upcoming matchup against Washington State, where the Ducks will likely field a revised lineup.

If the changes produce measurable improvement, Oregon could see a resurgence in its power‑rating and a stronger push for a bowl game. Conversely, continued struggles may lead to further staff turnover and could jeopardize Lanning's tenure as head coach.
