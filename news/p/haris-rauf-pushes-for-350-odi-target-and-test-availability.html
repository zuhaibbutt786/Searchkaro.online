Haris Rauf pushes for 350+ ODI target and Test availability

Haris Rauf has asked Pakistan to set a 350‑run target in ODIs and highlighted the need for middle‑over wickets. He also confirmed his willingness to play Test cricket alongside Shaheen Afridi and Naseem Shah.

Latest developments

In a series of comments made after Pakistan's recent One Day International (ODI) fixtures, fast bowler Haris Rauf emphasized the importance of breaking through the middle overs as scores continue to rise. Rauf noted that opposition teams are posting totals that frequently exceed 300 runs, and he believes Pakistan must respond with a more aggressive batting approach, targeting 350 runs or more in future matches. He also reiterated his readiness to contribute to the Test side, stating that he remains fully available for the longer format despite his primary role in limited‑overs cricket. Rauf explained his bowling strategy alongside fellow pacemen Shaheen Afridi and Naseem Shah, describing a coordinated plan that blends his pace with Shaheen's swing and Naseem's reverse swing to create pressure throughout an innings. The bowler highlighted that each of them brings a distinct skill set, allowing Pakistan to attack at different stages of a game. His remarks come as the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) prepares for an upcoming home series against a strong opponent, where both batting depth and bowling variety will be under scrutiny.

Why it matters

Rauf's call for a 350‑plus target directly addresses a growing concern among fans and analysts that Pakistan's batting line‑up has struggled to post competitive totals against high‑scoring sides. By setting a higher benchmark, the team can aim to dominate the powerplay and maintain momentum into the middle overs, reducing the pressure on the lower order. Additionally, his willingness to play Test cricket adds depth to Pakistan's fast‑bowling resources, a factor that could influence selection decisions ahead of the next Test series. The coordinated bowling approach he described with Shaheen Afridi and Naseem Shah offers a template for the PCB to rotate its pacers efficiently, preserving fitness while maintaining pressure on opposition batsmen. If the team adopts Rauf's suggestions, it could improve Pakistan's standing in the ICC rankings and restore confidence among supporters across the country.

Background

Haris Rauf emerged on the international scene during the 2020 T20 World Cup, quickly gaining a reputation for his raw pace and ability to generate bounce on sub‑continental pitches. Over the past six years, he has become a regular fixture in Pakistan's limited‑overs squads, delivering key spells in both ODIs and T20s. His partnership with Shaheen Afridi began in early 2022, when the duo combined speed and swing to dismantle several top‑order line‑ups. Naseem Shah, another young fast bowler, joined the trio later, adding reverse swing to the mix. While Rauf has primarily focused on limited‑overs cricket, he has occasionally been named in Test squads, though injuries have limited his appearances. The recent statements reflect a shift toward a more inclusive role, signalling his desire to contribute across formats.

What happens next

The PCB is expected to review Rauf's suggestions during its upcoming selection meetings, with a particular focus on the batting order for the next ODI series against a high‑ranking opponent. Training camps will likely incorporate specific drills aimed at improving middle‑over wicket‑taking, as outlined by Rauf. Meanwhile, the fast‑bowling department will continue to experiment with rotation policies that keep Shaheen, Naseem and Rauf fresh for both home and away fixtures, ensuring a balanced attack in the Test arena as well.
