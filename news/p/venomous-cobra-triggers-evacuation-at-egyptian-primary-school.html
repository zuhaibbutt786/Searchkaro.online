Venomous cobra triggers evacuation at Egyptian primary school

A large cobra caused panic at a primary school in Fayoum, Egypt, leading to an immediate evacuation of students. Authorities acted quickly to secure the animal and reassure parents.

Latest developments

Early on Monday morning, teachers at an elementary school in the Fayoum governorate discovered a large, venomous cobra slithering across the school courtyard. The sighting set off an alarm among staff and pupils, and the school was promptly evacuated while local police and animal control units were summoned.

According to reports from Al Bawabh News, the snake was described as unusually large for the area, prompting concerns about the safety of the children and the possibility of further wildlife intrusions in the school grounds.

Within an hour, a team of wildlife experts arrived, using specialized equipment to capture the reptile without harming it. The cobra was safely relocated to a nearby wildlife sanctuary, and the school resumed normal activities after a thorough inspection of the premises.

The Ministry of Education in Fayoum later released a statement clarifying that the incident was isolated, that no students were injured, and that security measures would be reviewed to prevent similar occurrences.

Why it matters

The incident highlights the broader issue of wildlife encroachment on public spaces in rural Egypt, where agricultural expansion and habitat loss can drive snakes and other animals into populated areas. For parents in Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries, the story underscores the importance of school safety protocols when dealing with unexpected natural hazards.

Moreover, the rapid response by local authorities demonstrates the capacity of regional emergency services to handle potentially dangerous wildlife encounters. This reassures families that schools are prepared to protect children, even in rare and alarming situations.

Background

Snakes, known in Arabic as ثعبان (pronounced "tha'ban"), are common in many parts of Egypt, especially in agricultural zones where they hunt rodents. Cobras, a subset of venomous snakes, are particularly feared due to their potent neurotoxic venom.

In recent months, several schools across the country have reported sightings of snakes on their grounds, prompting a series of precautionary measures such as regular perimeter checks and the installation of simple barriers. The Egyptian Ministry of Education has previously issued guidelines for staff to follow when a snake is spotted, emphasizing evacuation, notification of authorities, and avoidance of direct contact.

The Fayoum governorate, known for its fertile lands along the Nile, has seen increased human‑wildlife interactions as farming activities expand into previously undisturbed habitats.

What happens next

Education officials in Fayoum have announced a review of security protocols at all primary schools in the region, including the addition of snake‑proof fencing where feasible. Animal control teams will also increase routine patrols around school perimeters during the breeding season, when snakes are most active.

Parents are being advised to stay informed about safety procedures and to report any wildlife sightings to school administrators immediately.
