Islamabad High Court Orders Reopening of Motorways After Legal Challenge

A legal challenge has forced the Islamabad High Court to order the reopening of the Islamabad‑Peshawar Motorway, which had been closed amid security concerns. The decision follows reports of stranded motorists and police blockades on the GT

Latest developments

The Islamabad High Court (IHC) has issued an order to reopen the Islamabad‑Peshawar Motorway after a lawyer filed a petition challenging the closure imposed by authorities. The closure had been in place since late last month, citing security concerns following a series of incidents on the route. The court's decision is expected to restore traffic flow for commuters and freight operators.

The closure had left thousands of motorists stranded, with many forced to detour through congested city roads. Reports from local media and social media highlighted the inconvenience, as well as the economic impact on businesses that rely on the motorway for transport. The IHC order was issued on Thursday, with the government expected to comply by the following day.

In parallel, authorities announced that all closed motorways would reopen on Sunday morning, a move that aligns with the court's directive. The announcement came after the government received pressure from transport associations and the public. The reopening is scheduled to take place at 6 a.m., with security forces on standby to manage traffic.

Meanwhile, police blockades on the GT Road ahead of a PTI march in Peshawar have compounded travel disruptions. The blockades were set up to control the flow of vehicles during the protest, but they also contributed to congestion on alternate routes. The IHC decision does not directly address the GT Road blockades, but it may reduce pressure on alternative roads.

Why it matters

For residents of the capital and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, the motorway is a vital artery that connects major cities. Its closure had disrupted daily commutes, delayed deliveries, and increased fuel costs. The court's ruling is likely to ease these burdens and restore confidence in the transport infrastructure.

The decision also signals the judiciary's willingness to intervene in matters that affect public welfare. By ordering the reopening, the IHC has demonstrated that legal recourse can be an effective tool for addressing government actions that have unintended consequences. This may encourage other citizens to seek judicial review in similar situations.

In the broader political context, the reopening comes amid heightened tensions between the ruling party and opposition groups. The PTI march and the subsequent blockades on the GT Road illustrate the volatile environment. The IHC order may help to mitigate some of the friction by reducing logistical bottlenecks.

Background

The Islamabad‑Peshawar Motorway, part of the larger GT Road network, has historically been a key link between the capital and the northern provinces. It was built in the 1990s and has undergone several upgrades to accommodate increasing traffic volumes. The motorway is also a strategic route for national security and economic development.

In recent months, security concerns have prompted authorities to close sections of the motorway. The closures were justified by the government as a precautionary measure to prevent potential attacks. However, critics argue that the closures have caused significant economic disruption and have not been accompanied by adequate alternative routes.

The legal challenge was filed by a lawyer representing a group of affected commuters and businesses. The petition argued that the closure violated the right to freedom of movement and imposed undue hardship. The IHC's decision reflects a balance between security considerations and civil liberties.

What happens next

The government is expected to implement the IHC order by Monday, with the motorway reopening at the scheduled time. Authorities will likely coordinate with police and traffic management agencies to ensure a smooth transition. The reopening is also expected to reduce congestion on alternate routes, including the GT Road.

In the coming weeks, the judiciary may review further petitions related to security measures on major roadways. The IHC may also issue guidelines on how to balance security and mobility. Meanwhile, opposition parties will monitor the situation closely to assess the impact on public sentiment.
