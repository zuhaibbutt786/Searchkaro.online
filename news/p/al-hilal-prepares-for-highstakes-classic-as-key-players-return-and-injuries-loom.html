Al Hilal prepares for high‑stakes classic as key players return and injuries loom

Al Hilal is set to face its arch‑rival in a decisive classic match after a series of roster changes. The team’s preparation is marked by the return of veteran Roshan, the addition of Mukhtar, and a looming surgery for forward Kadash.

Latest developments

Al Hilal confirmed on Monday that the upcoming classic match will be played on Saturday at King Fahd Stadium. The club’s medical department announced that forward Kadash will travel to Finland for a surgical procedure scheduled for Friday, a move aimed at addressing a lingering knee problem that has limited his playing time this season. The decision follows a consultation with specialists who recommended an arthroscopic operation to ensure a full recovery before the season’s second half.

In parallel, the club disclosed that veteran midfielder Roshan has completed his rehabilitation and will be available for selection. Roshan, who missed the last two fixtures due to a hamstring strain, is expected to start the match, bringing experience and leadership to a midfield that has struggled with consistency.

Adding to the reshuffle, Al Hilal announced that young winger Mukhtar has been integrated into the first‑team training camp ahead of the classic. The 22‑year‑old has impressed the coaching staff during recent practice sessions and will provide additional depth on the flanks. Coach Abdullah Al‑Fahad emphasized that Mukhtar’s pace and work rate could be decisive against a well‑organized opponent.

The club’s technical director also confirmed that the squad will travel together for a final tactical briefing on Thursday, focusing on set‑piece routines and defensive organization. The coaching staff has highlighted the importance of maintaining composure in the high‑pressure environment that classic matches typically generate.

Overall, the combination of a returning veteran, a new attacking option, and the temporary loss of a key forward creates a complex puzzle for Al Hilal’s management as they aim to secure a vital win.

Why it matters

The classic match is more than a single fixture; it is a cultural event that draws millions of viewers across Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf region. A victory would not only boost Al Hilal’s position in the league table but also reinforce the club’s brand value, attracting sponsorships and increasing merchandise sales. Conversely, a loss could widen the gap between Al Hilal and its closest rivals, potentially reshaping the title race.

Fans in the Kingdom have expressed heightened anticipation on social media, with hashtags related to the match trending daily. The presence of high‑profile players such as Roshan adds a narrative of redemption, while Mukhtar’s inclusion signals a shift toward integrating younger talent, a strategy that could influence future transfer policies.

Economically, the match is expected to generate significant revenue for local businesses, from stadium concessions to broadcast rights. The influx of supporters traveling to Riyadh for the game supports hospitality sectors, reinforcing the broader impact of football on the Saudi economy.

Background

Al Hilal, founded in 1957, is the most decorated club in Saudi football history, boasting multiple league titles and continental trophies. The term "classic" in Saudi football typically refers to the rivalry between Al Hilal and Al Nassr, two clubs that dominate the capital’s football scene. Their encounters are often decisive for championship aspirations and are marked by intense fan engagement.

Historically, the classic has produced memorable moments, including dramatic last‑minute goals and record‑breaking attendances. In the past five seasons, Al Hilal has won three of the six classic fixtures, while Al Nassr has claimed the remaining three, underscoring the competitive balance.

The current season has seen Al Hilal contend with injury setbacks and squad rotation, prompting the coaching staff to rely on a blend of seasoned professionals and emerging players. The return of Roshan, a former national team captain, is reminiscent of past comebacks that have shifted momentum in crucial matches.

What happens next

After the classic, Al Hilal will face a mid‑week cup tie against a lower‑division side, providing an opportunity to rotate the squad and give playing time to fringe players. The outcome of the classic will also dictate the club’s tactical approach in the remaining league fixtures, with a win likely prompting a more aggressive style, while a loss may lead to a defensive recalibration.

Kadash’s recovery timeline will be closely monitored during his post‑surgery rehabilitation in Finland. The club expects an update on his fitness within two weeks, which will influence squad selection for the upcoming Saudi Pro League matches.
