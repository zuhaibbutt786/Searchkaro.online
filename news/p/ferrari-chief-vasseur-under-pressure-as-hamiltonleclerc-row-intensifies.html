Ferrari chief Vasseur under pressure as Hamilton‑Leclerc row intensifies

Ferrari's new boss Fred Vasseur is navigating a growing feud between Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc. The team is set to hold a high‑stakes meeting before the Brazil race.

Latest developments

Lewis Hamilton has publicly refused to back down after criticism from his Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc, according to a report in The Guardian. The dispute, which centres on on‑track tactics and perceived team orders, has spilled over into the media, with Hamilton insisting his performance remains unaffected. Autosport notes that Ferrari is preparing for a "crunch meeting" as the tension reaches a peak ahead of the Brazilian Grand Prix. Sources close to the team say Fred Vasseur, who took charge of Ferrari earlier this season, will lead the discussion. Sky Sports adds that Leclerc claims he pushes Hamilton harder than other rivals, a statement that has further inflamed the situation. Vasseur is expected to balance the competing interests of his drivers while preserving the team's championship ambitions.

Why it matters

The rivalry between Hamilton and Leclerc could have a direct impact on Ferrari's points haul in the remaining races of the season. A split within the driver line‑up may lead to strategic errors, especially in qualifying and pit‑stop decisions, which could benefit rival teams such as Red Bull and Mercedes. For British fans, the involvement of a seven‑time world champion adds a layer of national interest, while the broader F1 audience watches how a high‑profile team manages internal discord. Moreover, the outcome of Vasseur's handling of the row may set a precedent for how future team principals address driver conflicts, influencing the sport's governance and team culture.

Background

Fred Vasseur, a French engineer and former boss of Alfa Romeo, was appointed Ferrari's team principal in early 2024 with a mandate to restore the Scuderia's competitive edge. His arrival coincided with the signing of Lewis Hamilton, who left Mercedes after a decade, and the promotion of Charles Leclerc as the team's lead driver. Historically, Ferrari has struggled with driver harmony, most notably during the early 2000s when internal rivalries affected performance. The current season has seen both drivers delivering strong qualifying times, but differing approaches to racecraft have created friction. The media narrative has amplified the tension, with each driver using interviews to subtly critique the other's style.

What happens next

Vasseur is expected to convene a closed‑door meeting with Hamilton, Leclerc and senior engineers before the Brazilian Grand Prix. The agenda will likely focus on clarifying team orders, establishing clear communication protocols, and outlining a shared strategy for the race. Observers anticipate that any resolution will be kept private to avoid further media speculation. Should the meeting fail to reconcile the drivers, Ferrari may consider adjusting race strategies to minimise direct competition between the two, potentially assigning one driver a supportive role in specific scenarios.
