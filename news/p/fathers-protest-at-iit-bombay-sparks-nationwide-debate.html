Father's protest at IIT Bombay sparks nationwide debate

A father in Maharashtra has begun a hunger strike demanding action against two IIT Bombay professors who were arrested, while broader concerns about student mental health and academic governance are gaining attention across India. The episo

What is happening

In early September, the father of a second‑year engineering student at a Maharashtra college announced a hunger strike to protest the arrest of two senior faculty members at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay. He claims the professors were detained on allegations of using a caste‑related slur against his son, a claim that has ignited heated discussion on campus and beyond.

The two professors were taken into custody by local police after a formal complaint was filed by the student’s family. Authorities say the complaint alleges that the professors made a derogatory remark that could be interpreted as a caste identifier, a serious offense under Indian law. The professors have denied the accusation and have been placed on administrative leave pending further inquiry.

IIT Bombay’s administration released a statement confirming the suspension of the faculty members and assuring that an internal committee will review the case. The institute also expressed concern for the well‑being of all parties involved and pledged to cooperate fully with law enforcement.

The father’s hunger strike, which began at the campus gate, has drawn media attention from regional newspapers and national news channels. Social media platforms are abuzz with commentary, ranging from support for the father’s demand for justice to calls for a fair investigation into the allegations against the professors.

Background and context

Parental involvement in education has long been a hallmark of Indian society, where fathers often play a decisive role in academic and career decisions. In recent years, this involvement has extended to public advocacy when students encounter perceived injustice, especially in higher‑education settings.

India’s top technical institutions have faced criticism over handling of harassment complaints and mental‑health support. Several student‑led movements have emerged, demanding transparent grievance mechanisms and stronger counseling services. The current episode adds to that narrative, illustrating how a single family’s grievance can quickly become a national conversation about institutional accountability.

What to know next

The internal review at IIT Bombay is expected to submit a preliminary report within the next two weeks. Depending on its findings, the institute may reinstate the professors, impose disciplinary action, or refer the matter to a higher authority for legal adjudication.

State education officials have indicated that they will monitor the situation closely and may issue guidelines to improve handling of caste‑related complaints across universities. Observers suggest that the outcome could influence policy discussions on campus safety and the role of parents in university governance.
