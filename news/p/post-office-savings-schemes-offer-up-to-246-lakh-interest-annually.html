Post Office Savings Schemes Offer Up to ₹2.46 Lakh Interest Annually

India’s postal savings schemes have seen a boost in interest payouts, with some plans offering up to ₹2.46 lakh per year. The move is drawing attention from Diwali bonus savers and those seeking secure investment options.

Latest developments

The Indian postal service has announced that certain savings schemes can now yield up to ₹2.46 lakh in interest each year. The figure is derived from the latest interest rates set by the Ministry of Finance and applies to the Super Savings Scheme (SCS) and other long‑term postal plans. The announcement follows a review of the postal savings framework that aimed to make the schemes more attractive amid rising competition from banks and digital platforms. By raising the maximum return, the post office hopes to draw investors who prefer a low‑risk, tax‑free vehicle. Financial analysts note that the new rates do not alter the underlying risk profile of the schemes, which are backed by the government. The higher payout is expected to boost enrolment, especially during festive periods such as Diwali when many people look for safe places to park bonus money. The post office has also highlighted four specific schemes that can be used to earn income from Diwali bonuses. These include the Super Savings Scheme, the Post Office Monthly Income Scheme, the Post Office Fixed Deposit, and the Post Office Monthly Income Scheme with a fixed tenure. Investors can choose the option that best matches their liquidity needs.

Why it matters

For many households, the prospect of earning up to ₹2.46 lakh annually is a significant incentive. It offers a return that is competitive with traditional fixed deposits while providing the safety of a government‑backed instrument. The tax‑free nature of the interest also makes the schemes attractive for individuals in higher tax brackets. As the Indian government encourages savings through postal schemes, more people may shift funds from informal channels to formal accounts.

Background

Postal savings have been a staple of India’s financial ecosystem since the early 20th century. They were introduced to promote thrift among the rural and semi‑urban population and have evolved to include a range of products such as savings accounts, fixed deposits, and income schemes. Over the years, the post office has adjusted rates to keep pace with inflation and market conditions. The recent increase in interest rates is part of a broader strategy to maintain relevance in a digital age where online banking dominates.

What happens next

The Ministry of Finance is expected to review the rates again in the next fiscal cycle. Meanwhile, the post office will launch an information drive to educate potential investors about the new rates and the application process. Customers can apply online or at any post office branch.

Summary

- Interest up to ₹2.46 lakh per year on certain postal schemes. - Tax‑free returns attract high‑income savers. - New rates aim to boost enrolment during festive seasons. - Applications available online and at post office branches.
