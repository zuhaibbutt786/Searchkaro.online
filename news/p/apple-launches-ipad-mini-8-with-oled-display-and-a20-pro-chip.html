Apple launches iPad mini 8 with OLED display and A20 Pro chip

Apple has introduced the iPad mini 8, the most substantial refresh of the compact tablet line, adding an OLED display and a new processor. The launch is expected to influence students and professionals in Pakistan seeking a portable yet pow

Latest developments

Apple announced the new iPad mini 8 at its October event, describing it as the most significant upgrade since the product line began. The device features a 8.3‑inch OLED screen, a first for the mini series, delivering higher contrast and deeper blacks compared to the previous LCD panel. Apple also introduced the A20 Pro processor, a six‑core chip that promises faster performance and improved graphics.

In addition to the display and processor, the iPad mini 8 includes acoustic display speakers that channel sound through the screen itself, creating a more immersive audio experience without the need for external speakers. The tablet retains the familiar compact form factor, weighing just 293 grams, and runs iPadOS 18 with new multitasking features.

Early comparisons between the iPad mini 8 and its predecessor, the iPad mini 7, show a noticeable boost in battery life, up to 12 hours of web browsing versus 10 hours on the older model. Storage options start at 128 GB, and the device supports the second‑generation Apple Pencil, making it attractive for students and professionals who value portability.

Prices in Pakistan start at PKR 89,999 for the base Wi‑Fi model, with a cellular variant priced higher. Apple’s official Pakistani website lists the new mini alongside its larger iPad lineup, and several local retailers have already placed pre‑orders.

Why it matters

The iPad mini’s smaller size and lower price point compared to the standard iPad make it a viable option for Pakistani students who need a portable device for online classes and digital textbooks. The OLED screen improves readability in bright sunlight, a common condition in many parts of the country.

With the introduction of the A20 Pro chip, the tablet can handle more demanding apps such as graphic design tools and video editing software, expanding its utility beyond basic browsing. This could influence local retailers to stock more accessories, including keyboards and stylus pens, stimulating the tech accessories market.

Background

Apple launched the first iPad mini in 2012 as a compact alternative to the full‑size iPad. Over the years, the line received incremental upgrades, most recently the iPad mini 7 in 2024, which kept the LCD display and used the A15 Bionic chip.

The shift to OLED and a new processor marks the first major hardware overhaul for the mini series. Industry analysts have noted that Apple’s decision aligns with a broader trend of offering high‑end features in smaller devices, a segment that has grown in popularity among younger consumers worldwide.

What happens next

Apple plans to roll out iPadOS 18.1 in December, which will add support for new multitasking gestures optimized for the mini’s screen. The company also announced that the iPad mini 8 will be available in Pakistani stores starting 5 November, with additional colors expected later in the year.

Summary

- iPad mini 8 arrives with an 8.3‑inch OLED display and A20 Pro processor. - Acoustic speakers deliver sound through the screen for a richer audio experience. - Battery life, storage and Apple Pencil support are improved over the iPad mini 7. - Starting price of PKR 89,999 positions the tablet as an affordable, high‑performance option for students and professionals in Pakistan.
