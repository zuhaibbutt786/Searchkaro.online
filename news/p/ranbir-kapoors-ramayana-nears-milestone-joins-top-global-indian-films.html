Ranbir Kapoor's 'Ramayana' Nears Milestone, Joins Top Global Indian Films

Ranbir Kapoor's mythic saga 'Ramayana' is closing in on a record‑breaking earnings figure. The film now ranks among the highest‑grossing Indian releases worldwide.

Latest developments

The Indian epic "Ramayana" starring Ranbir Kapoor has posted a box‑office collection that places it just ₹11.06 crore short of overtaking the nine‑year reign of "Baahubali 2" in the global top‑three Indian films list, according to data released on IMDb. On day 23 of its theatrical run, the film's earnings were reported by Sacnilk, confirming a steady climb that keeps it within striking distance of the historic benchmark. Earlier, on day 21, NDTV highlighted that "Ramayana" had already become the fourth highest‑grossing Indian film of all time, surpassing the earnings of "Pushpa 2".

Industry analysts note that the film's performance is driven by a combination of star power, extensive marketing, and a storyline that adapts the ancient Indian epic for modern audiences. The movie, directed by a veteran filmmaker, blends large‑scale visual effects with a contemporary narrative, attracting both urban multiplex goers and audiences in smaller towns. Ticket sales have remained robust across major Indian metros, and the film continues to expand into secondary markets, including overseas territories with significant South Asian diaspora.

The financial data released by the box‑office tracking agencies shows that "Ramayana" has accumulated more than ₹1,200 crore worldwide, a figure that not only cements its place among the most successful Indian productions but also signals a resurgence of high‑budget mythological cinema in the region. The film's earnings have also contributed to a noticeable uptick in ancillary revenues, such as digital streaming rights and merchandising, further enhancing its profitability.

Critics have offered mixed reviews, praising the visual spectacle while questioning the depth of character development. Nevertheless, audience reception remains overwhelmingly positive, as reflected in the film's sustained occupancy rates and repeat viewership. The combination of strong word‑of‑mouth and strategic release windows has helped "Ramayana" maintain its momentum despite competition from other regional releases.

The latest box‑office figures have prompted trade analysts to predict that the film could soon breach the ₹1,500 crore mark, a threshold that would place it firmly within the elite circle of Indian films that have crossed that revenue milestone. If the trend continues, "Ramayana" could rewrite the record books and set a new benchmark for future productions.

Background

"Ramayana" adapts the ancient Indian epic traditionally attributed to the sage Valmiki, recounting the journey of Prince Rama, his wife Sita, and his loyal companion Hanuman. The title translates to "the journey of Rama" and has been retold in countless artistic forms over centuries. Ranbir Kapoor, a leading Bollywood star, was cast as Rama, bringing contemporary star appeal to the legendary role.

The film is part of a recent wave of mythological and historical projects that aim to blend traditional narratives with modern filmmaking technology. Earlier attempts, such as "Baahubali" and "RRR," demonstrated that Indian audiences are receptive to grand spectacles rooted in cultural lore. Production for "Ramayana" began in early 2024, with shooting locations spanning historic forts, coastal regions, and specially constructed sets designed to recreate ancient kingdoms.

The Indian film industry, often referred to as Bollywood, has seen a steady increase in global box‑office share over the past decade. This growth is attributed to improved distribution channels, the rise of streaming platforms, and a heightened interest in Indian content abroad. "Ramayana" benefits from this trend, securing theatrical releases in key markets such as the United Kingdom, the United States, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia.

What happens next

Industry watchers expect the film to continue its theatrical run for at least another week, with distributors planning to push it into additional overseas territories before the summer holiday window closes. A digital streaming release is slated for early 2027, which could add a substantial revenue stream and introduce the film to a broader audience.

Trade analysts also anticipate that the success of "Ramayana" will encourage studios to green‑light more high‑budget mythological projects, potentially leading to a new cycle of epic storytelling in Indian cinema.
