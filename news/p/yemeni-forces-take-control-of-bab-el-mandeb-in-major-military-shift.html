Yemeni Forces Take Control of Bab el-Mandeb in Major Military Shift

Yemeni government forces have established control over the strategic Bab el-Mandeb strait, supported by extensive air operations from the Saudi-led coalition. The move signals a major shift in the regional power balance and raises questions

Latest developments

Yemeni military forces have announced they have taken control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a critical maritime chokepoint connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. This operation was conducted with significant air support from the Saudi-led coalition, which has been active in the region for years. The seizure of this strategic location represents a pivotal moment in the ongoing conflict, as it places a vital trade route under the influence of the internationally recognized government.

According to reports from Al Arabiya, the coalition deployed approximately 100 fighter jets to participate in an operation designated as "Dawn of Yemen." This massive aerial bombardment and support campaign was designed to clear the area of Houthi militia presence and secure the strait for government forces. The scale of the air campaign highlights the intensity of the military effort required to dislodge entrenched fighters from such a geographically significant position.

The Eastern Middle East newspaper provided live coverage of the events, confirming that Yemeni forces moved into the area with direct air cover from coalition assets. The coordination between ground troops and aerial strikes appears to have been a key factor in the rapid establishment of control. Military analysts note that the use of such a large number of aircraft in a single operation underscores the high priority the coalition places on securing this specific geographic bottleneck.

The Houthi militia, which has controlled much of western Yemen including the port of Hodeidah, faces a significant strategic setback with the loss of Bab el-Mandeb. The strait is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world, and its control has profound implications for global energy supplies and commercial shipping. The successful operation suggests a coordinated military strategy aimed at restoring the authority of the Yemeni government over key infrastructure.

International observers are closely monitoring the situation to assess the durability of the new control. While the initial seizure has been confirmed, the stability of the area will depend on sustained military presence and the ability to prevent counter-attacks. The involvement of the coalition in such a direct capacity indicates a renewed commitment to supporting the Yemeni government's military objectives.

Why it matters

For readers in Saudi Arabia, the development in Yemen is of immediate strategic importance. The Bab el-Mandeb strait lies just off the coast of the Red Sea, directly impacting regional security and trade routes that pass through Saudi waters. Securing this area helps to stabilize the southern border region and reduces the threat of missile or drone attacks originating from Houthi-controlled territories.

The control of Bab el-Mandeb also has significant economic implications. The strait is a vital artery for global commerce, carrying a substantial portion of the world's oil and gas shipments. By ensuring the safety of this route, the coalition and Yemeni forces aim to protect the economic interests of the region, including Saudi Arabia's own energy exports. A stable Bab el-Mandeb contributes to lower insurance premiums for shipping companies and ensures a more reliable flow of goods and energy resources.

Furthermore, the success of the operation may influence diplomatic dynamics in the region. Analysts suggest that restoring the Yemeni government's control over key strategic points could strengthen its negotiating position in any future peace talks. This shift in military balance may encourage other regional actors to engage more constructively with the internationally recognized government, potentially leading to a more stable political environment in the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula.

Background

The conflict in Yemen has been ongoing since 2014, when the Houthi militia, supported by Iran, seized control of the capital Sanaa and much of the western part of the country. In response, a Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015 to restore the internationally recognized government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. The war has resulted in a complex humanitarian crisis and a fragmented political landscape.

The Bab el-Mandeb strait has been a focal point of the conflict due to its strategic importance. Houthi forces have periodically targeted commercial vessels in the area, disrupting global shipping and raising concerns about the safety of the Red Sea corridor. The coalition has conducted numerous airstrikes on Houthi positions in the port of Hodeidah and surrounding areas to deter such attacks and protect maritime traffic.

The term "Bab el-Mandeb" translates to "Gate of Tears" in Arabic, reflecting the historical difficulty of navigating the narrow and often turbulent waters. The strait connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and is one of the most important chokepoints in the world, comparable to the Strait of Hormuz and the Suez Canal. Control over this area has been a key objective for both the Yemeni government and the Houthi militia throughout the duration of the conflict.

What happens next

In the immediate aftermath of the operation, military forces are expected to consolidate their hold on the Bab el-Mandeb strait. This will likely involve the establishment of checkpoints, the deployment of naval assets to monitor shipping, and continued air patrols to deter any Houthi counter-offensives. The coalition may also focus on securing the surrounding highlands to prevent the re-establishment of Houthi firing positions.

Diplomatically, the development could prompt new rounds of negotiations or statements from international bodies. The United Nations and other international organizations may call for a ceasefire or increased humanitarian access, citing the changing military situation. The outcome of these diplomatic efforts will depend on the willingness of all parties to engage in a constructive dialogue, with the recent military success potentially providing a new leverage point for the Yemeni government.
