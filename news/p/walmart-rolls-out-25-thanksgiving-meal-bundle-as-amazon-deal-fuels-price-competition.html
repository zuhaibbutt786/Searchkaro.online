Walmart rolls out $25 Thanksgiving meal bundle as Amazon deal fuels price competition

Walmart announced a $25 Thanksgiving meal bundle for families of six, directly challenging Amazon's recently launched offer. The move intensifies price competition ahead of the holiday rush.

Latest developments

Walmart unveiled a new Thanksgiving meal bundle priced at $25, aimed at feeding six people. The package includes a whole turkey, two side dishes, a dessert and a set of basic condiments, and can be ordered online for curbside pickup or home delivery. The announcement comes just days after Amazon launched a similar $25 Thanksgiving dinner for six, promising same‑day delivery in select markets. Retail analysts note that Walmart's entry appears to be a strategic response to Amazon's aggressive pricing, which has already generated significant consumer interest across the United States. Both retailers are emphasizing convenience, with Walmart leveraging its extensive network of stores and distribution centers to fulfill orders quickly. Early reports indicate that the Walmart bundle is available in most major metropolitan areas and will be stocked in stores beginning the first week of November, giving shoppers a narrow window to secure the deal before supplies run out.

Why it matters

The introduction of a $25 Thanksgiving meal bundle by two of the nation’s largest retailers could reshape holiday grocery spending for millions of families. For price‑sensitive consumers, the ability to purchase a complete dinner for a fixed low price reduces the need to shop across multiple aisles or compare individual item costs. Moreover, the bundles simplify meal planning, a factor that can be especially valuable for busy households. The competition also puts pressure on smaller grocery chains and local markets, which may struggle to match the deep discounts offered by Walmart and Amazon without sacrificing margins. In addition, the emphasis on same‑day delivery and curbside pickup reflects a broader shift toward convenience that has accelerated since the pandemic, potentially setting new expectations for holiday shopping experiences.

Background

Thanksgiving meal bundles have been a seasonal staple for many retailers, but the pricing strategy has evolved dramatically in recent years. Historically, large supermarkets offered pre‑assembled turkey packages at a premium, often exceeding $40 for a family‑size meal. The rise of e‑commerce and the entry of tech‑driven players like Amazon into grocery delivery have forced traditional brick‑and‑mortar chains to rethink their pricing models. In 2024, Amazon introduced a $25 Thanksgiving dinner for six, which included a turkey, sides and a dessert, and promoted it heavily through its Prime platform. The deal generated a spike in search interest, as reflected in recent Google Trends data, and prompted other retailers to consider comparable offers. Walmart, long known for its low‑price positioning, has previously run holiday meal bundles, but the $25 price point marks a notable shift toward ultra‑budget pricing to retain market share.

What happens next

Industry watchers expect the price competition to intensify as both Walmart and Amazon expand the availability of their bundles to additional regions. Consumers are likely to flock to the first retailers that can guarantee stock, potentially leading to rapid sell‑throughs and short supply windows. Retail analysts also anticipate that other grocery chains may introduce their own low‑cost bundles or offer discounts on individual Thanksgiving items to stay competitive. Supply chain logistics will be closely monitored, as the demand for turkeys and side dishes peaks earlier than usual, testing the capacity of meat processors and packaging suppliers. The outcome of this price war could influence pricing strategies for other holiday meals, such as Christmas and New Year’s offerings.
