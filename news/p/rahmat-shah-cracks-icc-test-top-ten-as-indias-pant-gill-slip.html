Rahmat Shah cracks ICC Test top ten as India’s Pant, Gill slip

Afghanistan's Rahmat Shah has entered the ICC Test top ten for the first time, marking a milestone for the nation. Meanwhile, Indian openers Rishabh Pant and Shubman Gill have slipped to lower positions in the same list.

Latest developments

The International Cricket Council released its latest men’s Test player rankings on Wednesday, confirming a historic entry for Afghanistan as Rahmat Shah moved into the top ten for the first time. Shah, who has been a mainstay of Afghanistan’s batting line‑up, now sits at ninth place with a points total that reflects his consistent performances in recent series. The ICC highlighted the achievement as a landmark moment for the emerging cricket nation, noting that no Afghan batter had previously reached this echelon. In contrast, India’s Rishabh Pant and Shubman Gill experienced a slide in the rankings, dropping to eighth and tenth respectively after a period of mixed results in Test matches. Their new positions reflect a modest dip in form compared with peers, and the ICC’s update placed them behind several established players from England, Australia and South Africa. The changes have sparked discussion among analysts, with many pointing to the evolving competitive balance in the Test arena across the sub‑continent.

Why it matters

For cricket fans in Pakistan, the reshuffle underscores the growing depth of talent in neighboring countries and the intensifying rivalry for regional supremacy. Afghanistan’s breakthrough signals that its investment in youth development and exposure to higher‑quality opposition is bearing fruit, potentially raising the standard of competition in future bilateral series. The dip in rankings for Pant and Gill may influence Indian team selection ahead of upcoming tours, prompting selectors to reassess the balance between experience and form. Pakistani commentators see Shah’s ascent as both a challenge and an inspiration, highlighting that emerging players from associate nations can now compete with traditional powerhouses. The shift also adds a new narrative to the South Asian cricket calendar, where matches involving Afghanistan could attract greater viewership and commercial interest.

Background

The ICC’s player ranking system assigns points based on individual performances, match context and the strength of opposition, updating weekly after each Test. Historically, the top ten has been dominated by batsmen from England, Australia, South Africa and India, with occasional entries from New Zealand and the West Indies. Afghanistan gained Test status in 2017 and has gradually climbed the rankings, but until now no batsman had breached the top ten barrier. Rahmat Shah, a right‑handed opener, has accumulated runs against strong bowling attacks, contributing to his rise. Pant and Gill, both debutants in the early 2020s, quickly rose to high rankings after prolific series, but recent innings have been less impactful, leading to their current positions.

What happens next

The next ICC ranking update is scheduled for early next month, following the conclusion of the ongoing England‑Australia Test series, which could further alter the standings. Afghanistan is set to host a Test series against Bangladesh later this year, providing Shah an opportunity to consolidate his position. India will commence a home Test series against Sri Lanka, where Pant and Gill will aim to regain form and improve their rankings. Observers will watch these fixtures closely, as performances will directly influence the next set of rankings and potentially affect player selections for upcoming World Test Championship matches.
