ISRO Satellites Track Cyclone Akvan as Andhra Prepares for Third Storm

ISRO’s remote‑sensing satellites are delivering real‑time data on Cyclone Akvan, aiding authorities in Andhra Pradesh as they prepare for a third storm in three weeks. The energy sector remains on high alert amid the looming threat.

Latest developments

Cyclone Akvan, a low‑pressure system that has intensified over the Bay of Bengal, is now classified as a cyclonic storm by the India Meteorological Department. ISRO’s remote‑sensing satellites, including the RISAT‑2B and the Indian National Satellite System, are providing high‑resolution imagery that helps track the storm’s eye and wind patterns. The data is transmitted to the Meteorological Department in real time, allowing for more accurate forecasts. In response to the growing threat, the Energy Minister has directed all distribution companies (DISCOMs) to remain on high alert. The directive, issued ahead of Cyclone Akvan, emphasizes the need for rapid load‑shedding plans and backup power arrangements. The aim is to minimise power outages that could affect hospitals, water supply and communication networks during the cyclone’s passage. Andhra Pradesh, already bracing for a third cyclone in three weeks, has activated its emergency response framework. The state government has issued warnings to coastal districts, urging residents to secure properties and stock essential supplies. The cyclone’s projected path over the eastern coast raises concerns about storm surges and heavy rainfall. ISRO’s satellite data plays a pivotal role in these preparations. By delivering near‑real‑time observations of sea‑surface temperature, wind speed and atmospheric pressure, the agency enables meteorologists to refine intensity estimates and track potential landfall points. This information feeds into the National Disaster Response Plan, guiding evacuation orders and resource deployment. For communities along the coast, the combination of satellite intelligence and government directives offers a chance to mitigate damage. Early warnings allow fishermen to return to port, farmers to protect crops and households to secure homes. The coordination between ISRO, the Meteorological Department and state authorities illustrates how space technology can support disaster resilience.

Why it matters

Cyclone Akvan poses a significant threat to millions of people in Andhra Pradesh and neighbouring states. The storm’s high winds and heavy rainfall could damage infrastructure, disrupt power supply and threaten lives. Accurate forecasting is therefore essential to protect vulnerable populations. The energy sector, in particular, faces heightened risk. Power outages can halt essential services, including hospitals and water treatment plants. By keeping DISCOMs on high alert, the government aims to ensure that critical facilities remain operational during the cyclone’s impact. Beyond immediate safety, the cyclone could have longer‑term economic consequences. Damage to ports, roads and agricultural land can delay recovery and increase costs for the state’s budget. Timely data from ISRO helps planners assess damage early and allocate resources efficiently.

Background

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) was established in 1969 to develop space technology for national development. Over the decades, it has launched a fleet of satellites that serve a range of purposes, from telecommunications to Earth observation. One of ISRO’s key contributions to weather monitoring is the Indian National Satellite System (INSAT), which provides real‑time data on atmospheric conditions. The RISAT series, launched in 2012, offers radar imaging that can penetrate cloud cover, making it invaluable for tracking cyclones. Cyclone Akvan, named by the World Meteorological Organization, is the latest storm to threaten the Bay of Bengal region. Classified as a cyclonic storm, it has a central pressure of 985 hPa and sustained winds of 80 km/h, placing it in the lower range of the cyclone scale.

What happens next

Meteorologists will continue to monitor Akvan’s trajectory using ISRO’s satellite feeds, refining intensity forecasts as the storm approaches the coast. The government is expected to issue updated advisories and, if necessary, evacuation orders for the most vulnerable districts. After the cyclone passes, authorities will assess damage using satellite imagery and ground surveys. ISRO’s data will help map affected areas, guiding relief distribution and infrastructure repair efforts.
