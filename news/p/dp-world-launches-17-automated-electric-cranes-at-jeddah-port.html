DP World Launches 17 Automated Electric Cranes at Jeddah Port

The Port of Jeddah is set to become one of the most technologically advanced in the region after DP World installed 17 fully automated electric cranes. The move is part of a broader push to reduce emissions and increase throughput.

Latest developments

DP World announced the installation of 17 fully automated electric cranes at the Port of Jeddah, a major hub on the Red Sea. The project cost 206 million Saudi riyals, roughly 56 million US dollars. The cranes are the first of their kind in the region and are designed to operate without human intervention, using advanced sensors and software.

The automated cranes are expected to lift containers up to 40 tonnes and can work around the clock, improving the port's handling capacity. They are powered by electricity, reducing fuel consumption and lowering greenhouse‑gas emissions. The move aligns with Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030, which prioritises sustainability and digital transformation.

DP World said the new equipment will increase the port's throughput by up to 15 percent, allowing it to handle more cargo without expanding its footprint. The cranes will also reduce the risk of accidents, as they are programmed to maintain safe distances and respond to obstacles automatically.

The installation was carried out in partnership with local suppliers and engineers, who provided training for port staff on maintenance and operation. The project also included upgrades to the port's control systems to integrate the cranes into the existing traffic management network.

While the cranes are a significant investment, DP World highlighted that the long‑term savings from lower operating costs and higher efficiency will benefit the port's stakeholders. The company plans to monitor performance closely and share lessons learned with other ports in the Gulf.

Why it matters

The new cranes are a key step in modernising Jeddah's maritime infrastructure, which handles more than 30 million tonnes of cargo annually. By increasing efficiency, the port can accommodate larger vessels and reduce turnaround times, making Saudi Arabia a more attractive destination for global shipping lines.

From an environmental perspective, the shift to electric, fully automated equipment cuts emissions by an estimated 30 percent compared to diesel‑powered cranes. This reduction supports the country's commitments under the Paris Agreement and helps the kingdom meet its carbon‑neutral targets by 2060.

The project also creates opportunities for local businesses. Training programmes for technicians and engineers have already begun, and the port expects to hire additional staff to manage the new systems. The investment signals confidence in the region's labour market and technology sector.

Background

Jeddah port, located on the western coast of Saudi Arabia, has been a critical gateway for trade between the Middle East, Africa, and Asia since the 19th century. It serves as the main entry point for pilgrims heading to Mecca and for goods destined for the kingdom's growing industrial base.

DP World, a Dubai‑based port operator, has managed the port since 2019, following a joint venture with the Saudi government. The company has previously invested in infrastructure upgrades, including the expansion of container terminals and the installation of advanced cargo‑handling equipment.

The current project builds on earlier initiatives to digitise port operations. Saudi Vision 2030, launched in 2016, calls for a diversified economy and aims to transform the kingdom into a global logistics hub. Automation at Jeddah port is one of the flagship projects under this vision.

What happens next

DP World plans to roll out additional automation across other terminals in the Gulf over the next five years. The company will also explore the use of autonomous trucks and drones to further streamline cargo movements within the port.

The port authorities will conduct a series of performance reviews to assess the cranes' impact on throughput and safety. Based on the findings, they may adjust operating schedules and expand the fleet to meet rising demand.
