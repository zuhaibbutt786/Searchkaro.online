Vivo Y32 5G launches in Pakistan with 8,000mAh battery and Snapdragon 4 Gen 2

Vivo has introduced the Y32 5G in Pakistan, offering a massive 8,000 mAh battery and a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 processor. The model also carries an IP69 protection rating and is expected to reach India soon.

Latest developments

The Chinese smartphone maker Vivo confirmed the official specifications of its new Y32 5G model on Monday. The device is equipped with a large 8,000 mAh battery, a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 chipset, a 6.58‑inch display and a triple‑camera array that includes a 50 MP primary sensor. It also meets an IP69 rating for dust and water resistance, a feature rarely seen in this price segment. Pricing information released by local retailers places the phone at roughly PKR 45,000, positioning it between mid‑range and entry‑level 5G options. The launch comes after a brief leak of the device’s details on Chinese tech forums, which sparked speculation about its arrival in South Asian markets. Vivo’s local partner announced that the Y32 5G is now available through major e‑commerce platforms and selected brick‑and‑mortar stores across major cities such as Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad. Early sales data suggests a steady uptake, with inventory moving quickly in the first week of availability. The company also hinted that a version tailored for the Indian market will be released later this year, though no exact launch date has been confirmed.

Why it matters

For Pakistani consumers, the Y32 5G offers a combination of long battery life and 5G connectivity at a price that competes with other Chinese brands such as Xiaomi and Realme. The 8,000 mAh capacity addresses a common pain point for users who rely on their phones for extended periods without frequent charging, especially in areas where power supply can be intermittent. The inclusion of the Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 processor means the phone can handle everyday tasks, moderate gaming and video streaming with reasonable efficiency, which may encourage more users to adopt 5G services as the network expands nationwide. Additionally, the IP69 rating provides added durability for users in dusty or humid environments, potentially extending the device’s lifespan. The launch also signals Vivo's intent to strengthen its foothold in Pakistan, a market that has seen rapid growth in smartphone penetration over the past five years.

Background

Vivo entered the Pakistani market in 2018 and has since built a modest but growing presence, primarily through online sales and partnerships with local distributors. Its earlier models, such as the Y20s and Y31, focused on affordable specifications and solid camera performance, gaining a following among budget‑conscious buyers. The global rollout of 5G networks has prompted manufacturers to introduce lower‑cost 5G devices, and Vivo’s Y32 5G is part of that wave. The Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 chipset, launched by Qualcomm in early 2024, targets mid‑range smartphones, offering improved AI processing, better power efficiency and support for advanced connectivity features. The IP69 rating, equivalent to the highest level of protection against water ingress and dust, is typically reserved for rugged devices, making its inclusion in a mainstream handset noteworthy. Pakistan’s telecom regulators have been awarding 5G licenses to major operators, and the network coverage is expected to reach major urban centers by the end of 2025, creating a ready market for devices like the Y32 5G.

What happens next

Vivo is expected to roll out the Y32 5G in India within the next few months, likely mirroring the specifications offered in Pakistan but possibly adjusting pricing to suit local competition. The company may also introduce software updates to improve battery management and network performance as 5G infrastructure matures. Analysts predict that further price reductions could occur if demand slows, or if newer models are announced later in the year. Consumers interested in the device should monitor official Vivo channels for warranty information and after‑sales support options.

Summary
