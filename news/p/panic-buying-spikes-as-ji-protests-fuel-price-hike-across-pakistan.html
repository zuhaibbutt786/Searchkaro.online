Panic buying spikes as JI protests fuel price hike across Pakistan

Consumers across Pakistan are rushing to stores as protests over a new petroleum levy disrupt fuel supplies. The surge in demand is pushing up prices of staple items.

Latest developments

On Oct 24 2026, Jamaat-e-Islami (JI) announced nationwide protests against the recently announced petroleum levy, prompting concerns about fuel supply disruptions. The announcement was covered by The Nation, noting that the party called for immediate action to block the levy, which they say will burden ordinary citizens.

The Express Tribune reported that JI entered the third phase of its protest on Oct 26, expanding sit-ins to major highways and fuel depots. The escalation has led to intermittent closures of petrol stations in Punjab and Sindh, fueling uncertainty among commuters and traders.

Business Recorder highlighted that JI scheduled a further wave of sit-ins for Oct 23, targeting key distribution points for petroleum products. The coordinated actions have triggered a surge in consumer purchases of cooking gas, wheat flour, and bottled water, as households anticipate possible shortages.

Market analysts observed that supermarkets in Karachi and Lahore reported a 30‑40 percent rise in sales of staple goods over the past week. The panic buying has also pushed prices of some items, such as rice and cooking oil, up by 5‑7 percent, according to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.

In response to the surge, the Competition Commission of Pakistan has instructed major retailers to monitor price changes and report any gouging. Meanwhile, social media platforms are flooded with videos of long queues at grocery stores, reflecting the heightened anxiety among citizens.

Why it matters

The sudden spike in demand strains supply chains already stretched by rising transportation costs. Small retailers report difficulty restocking, and larger chains are rationing items to prevent stockouts, which could exacerbate food insecurity for low‑income families.

The protests also highlight broader public discontent with the government's fiscal policy. By targeting the petroleum levy, JI aims to pressure the cabinet to reconsider the tax, but the resulting consumer panic may undermine confidence in the stability of essential markets.

The panic buying episode also puts pressure on the Central Bank, which monitors inflation indicators closely. A sustained rise in food prices could force policymakers to adjust interest rates, affecting loans and mortgages for households.

Background

The petroleum levy was introduced in early October as part of a broader budget package intended to raise revenue for infrastructure projects. Critics argue that the levy adds to the cost of fuel, which already accounts for a significant portion of household expenses in Pakistan.

Jamaat-e-Islami, a long‑standing political party, has a history of mobilising mass protests against economic measures it deems unfair. Earlier this year, the party organized rallies against rising electricity tariffs, setting a precedent for its current campaign.
