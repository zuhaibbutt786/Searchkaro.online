Former Pakistan pacer Mohammad Amir eyes IPL return as UK citizen

Latest developments

Pakistan’s former fast bowler Mohammad Amir is reportedly preparing to register for the Indian Premier League (IPL) 2027 mini‑auction after obtaining British citizenship. News18 reported that the former international, who retired from Pakistan’s national side in 2022, is now eligible to play as a UK national, a status that could ease the franchise’s overseas player restrictions. Yahoo Sports confirmed that Amir’s registration is expected to be submitted in the coming weeks, positioning him among a small group of South Asian cricketers who have recently explored opportunities in the IPL despite past controversies.

The move comes at a time when the IPL is expanding its talent pool ahead of the 2027 season, with franchises keen to add experienced pace options. Amir’s record of 269 wickets in international cricket, including a reputation for delivering in high‑pressure moments, makes him an attractive prospect for teams seeking depth in their bowling attack. While no franchise has publicly announced a signing, analysts suggest that his experience could be valuable for a side looking to balance youth with seasoned skill.

In parallel, the Afghanistan Premier League (APL) has been bolstering its support staff, appointing former Pakistan and West Indies players such as Waqar Younis and Mark Boucher to coaching and advisory roles, according to ESPN. Although Amir is not directly linked to the APL, the broader trend of South Asian cricketing talent moving across leagues underscores a shifting landscape where former international players are finding new avenues to extend their careers.

Why it matters

Amir’s potential IPL comeback carries significant implications for cricket fans in Pakistan. The country’s cricket board has long grappled with the loss of fast‑bowling talent, and Amir’s return to a high‑profile tournament could revive interest in the sport domestically. Moreover, his status as a UK national raises questions about eligibility rules that could affect future Pakistani players who acquire dual citizenship.

The decision also highlights the economic dimension of franchise cricket. IPL contracts are among the most lucrative in the sport, and a successful stint could provide Amir with financial security while offering a platform to showcase his skills to a global audience. For Pakistani cricket, his visibility abroad may inspire younger bowlers and reinforce the notion that a professional career can continue beyond national team selection.

Background

Mohammad Amir burst onto the international scene as a teenager in 2009, quickly establishing himself as one of Pakistan’s leading fast bowlers. Over a decade, he amassed 269 Test and ODI wickets, playing a key role in Pakistan’s 2017 Champions Trophy triumph. However, his career was marred by a 2010 spot‑fixing scandal that led to a five‑year ban, after which he returned to the national side in 2016.

In 2022, Amir announced his retirement from international cricket, citing a desire to focus on domestic leagues and personal commitments. He later relocated to the United Kingdom, where he qualified for citizenship through residency. This new status permits him to be classified as a local player in leagues that restrict overseas participants, a factor that directly influences his eligibility for the IPL mini‑auction.

What happens next

The next step is Amir’s formal registration with the IPL governing body, expected to be completed before the auction deadline in early 2027. Once registered, franchises will evaluate his recent form in domestic leagues and decide whether to bid for his services. Observers will watch closely to see if his inclusion prompts any policy review by the Pakistan Cricket Board regarding players who represent foreign leagues while holding dual nationality.
