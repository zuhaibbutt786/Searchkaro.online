Cyclone Arnab Threatens Coastal India as Monsoon Intensifies

Cyclone Arnab is set to strike India's eastern coast, intensifying monsoon rains that have already caused extensive road damage. Residents and authorities brace for widespread flooding and landslides.

Latest developments

Cyclone Arnab has formed in the Bay of Bengal and is moving steadily toward the eastern coast of India, with meteorological agencies predicting landfall within the next 48 hours. The system has already begun to pick up strength, drawing moisture from the warm sea surface and feeding the heavy rainfall that has been a hallmark of the current monsoon season.

A new low‑pressure area has developed over the Bay, according to daily weather reports, which is expected to intensify the monsoon rains across the region. The pressure drop is causing wind speeds to climb and rainfall rates to increase, leading to a higher risk of flash flooding in low‑lying districts.

Roads in coastal Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu have suffered significant damage as the cyclone’s winds and heavy rains swept through the area. A BBC Tamil report highlighted a stretch of highway that collapsed under the force of the storm, cutting off travel for several hours and forcing emergency crews to deploy sandbags and temporary bridges.

Local authorities have issued warnings for residents in vulnerable coastal villages, advising them to stay indoors and to secure loose objects. The Indian Meteorological Department has issued a cyclone alert for the next 72 hours, and the National Disaster Response Force has been mobilised to assist with evacuation and relief operations. The combination of the cyclone and the intensified monsoon has raised concerns about the potential for widespread flooding and landslides, especially in districts with fragile riverbanks.

Why it matters

The convergence of Cyclone Arnab and a low‑pressure monsoon system poses a serious threat to millions living along India's eastern coastline. Heavy rainfall can overwhelm drainage systems, leading to urban flooding that disrupts transportation, commerce, and daily life. In rural areas, saturated soils increase the likelihood of landslides, which can isolate communities and damage infrastructure.

For residents, the immediate risk is to personal safety and property. The damage to roads hampers emergency response, while the potential for sudden flooding means that even well‑planned evacuation routes may become impassable. The economic impact could be significant, as the region relies heavily on agriculture and fishing, both of which are vulnerable to prolonged waterlogging and saltwater intrusion.

Background

India’s monsoon season typically brings heavy rainfall from June to September, but this year’s pattern has been unusually intense. The formation of low‑pressure areas over the Bay of Bengal is a common precursor to cyclonic activity, and when combined with high sea surface temperatures, it can lead to rapid intensification of storms.

Cyclone Arnab is one of several tropical depressions that have tracked along the coast in recent weeks. Historically, such systems have caused widespread damage, prompting the government to strengthen early warning systems and invest in coastal defence infrastructure. Despite these measures, the sheer volume of water and wind energy released by modern cyclones often exceeds the capacity of existing safeguards.

What happens next

Meteorologists predict that Cyclone Arnab will make landfall in the next 48 hours, with peak winds expected to reach 120 km/h. Authorities are preparing for a coordinated response that includes the deployment of additional emergency personnel, the opening of evacuation centres, and the reinforcement of critical infrastructure such as bridges and drainage channels.
