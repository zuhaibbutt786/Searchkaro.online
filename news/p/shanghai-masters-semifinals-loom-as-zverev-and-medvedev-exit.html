Shanghai Masters Semi‑Finals Loom as Zverev and Medvedev Exit

The Shanghai Masters has seen two of its top seeds fall, clearing the way for an all‑Spanish semi‑final. Fans in China and around the world are watching the tournament’s final stages unfold.

Latest developments

Alexander Zverev and Daniil Medvedev, the top two seeds in the Shanghai Masters, were widely regarded as the most likely contenders to reach the final. Both players entered the tournament with strong recent form, having won multiple ATP titles earlier in the season. Their early exits in the quarter‑finals were a surprise to many observers and have reshaped the draw for the remaining matches.

The removal of the two favourites has opened the door for an all‑Spanish semi‑final, a scenario that has not occurred in the history of the event. Spanish players such as Alejandro Davidovich Fokina and Carlos Alcaraz have advanced to the last eight, setting up a clash that could see Spain claim the title for the first time since the tournament’s inception.

The Shanghai Masters is one of the nine ATP Masters 1000 tournaments that make up the elite tier of the ATP Tour. It is played on outdoor hard courts at the Shanghai International Tennis Centre and attracts the world’s top players. The event has been held annually since 2009, and its prize money and ranking points make it a key target for players seeking to improve their year‑end rankings.

Background

The Shanghai Masters was inaugurated in 2009 as part of the ATP’s expansion into Asia. It replaced the former Shanghai Open and quickly became one of the most prestigious hard‑court events outside of the Grand Slams. The tournament has attracted a mix of established champions and emerging stars, with past winners including Roger Federer, Novak Djokovic and Rafael Nadal.

Over the years, the Shanghai Masters has seen a number of memorable matches, including a dramatic five‑set final in 2015 and a record‑setting 35‑game match in 2018. The venue’s high‑altitude conditions and fast surface have often produced exciting rallies, making it a favourite among players who thrive on speed and precision.

What happens next

The semi‑finals will take place on Sunday and Monday, with the winners advancing to the final scheduled for the following weekend. If the all‑Spanish showdown proceeds as expected, it would be the first time that Spain has reached the final of an ATP Masters 1000 event. Players and fans alike will be watching to see whether the tournament’s narrative shifts again as new champions emerge.
