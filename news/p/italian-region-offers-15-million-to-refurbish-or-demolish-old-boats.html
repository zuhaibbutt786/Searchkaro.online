Italian Region Offers €1.5 Million to Refurbish or Demolish Old Boats

The Friuli Venezia Giulia region has launched a €1.5 million programme to assist owners of ageing boats with refitting or demolition. The initiative also offers up to €15,000 in grants for specific works, aiming to modernise the local marit

Latest developments

The Friuli Venezia Giulia regional government announced on Thursday that it will allocate €1.5 million to support owners of old boats in the region. The fund covers either the refitting of vessels to meet modern safety standards or the demolition of those that are no longer seaworthy.

The initiative follows a broader European push to modernise maritime fleets and reduce environmental impact. By offering financial assistance, the region hopes to encourage owners to upgrade engines, install new navigation systems, or safely decommission obsolete hulls.

In addition to the main fund, the government will provide grants of up to €15,000 for specific works such as hull repairs, structural reinforcements, or the installation of eco‑friendly propulsion systems. These smaller grants are aimed at making the transition more affordable for small‑scale operators.

The programme is part of a larger strategy to support the local maritime industry, which employs thousands of people across shipyards, maintenance shops, and fishing fleets. By stimulating demand for refurbishment services, the region expects a boost to its economy.

Applications for the €1.5 million fund will open in early December, with a deadline set for the end of February. Applicants must submit detailed plans and cost estimates, and the regional council will assess projects based on technical feasibility and environmental benefits.

Why it matters

For boat owners in Pakistan, the news highlights a model of regional support that could inspire similar programmes back home. While the financial figures are specific to Friuli Venezia Giulia, the underlying principle of subsidising modernisation is relevant to any coastal community.

The initiative could reduce the number of derelict vessels that pose safety hazards and environmental risks. By encouraging owners to either upgrade or responsibly dispose of old boats, the programme aligns with global efforts to improve marine safety and reduce pollution.

Moreover, the economic stimulus from refurbishment projects could create jobs in related sectors such as marine engineering, welding, and logistics. In regions where maritime activity is a key part of the economy, such investment can have a multiplier effect.

Background

Friuli Venezia Giulia, located in northeastern Italy, has a long maritime tradition with a coastline along the Adriatic Sea. The region's economy relies heavily on shipping, fishing, and tourism, making the condition of its fleet a priority for local authorities.

Previous initiatives have focused on improving navigational safety and reducing emissions. The new fund builds on these efforts by directly addressing the ageing infrastructure of the region's vessels, many of which were built before the 1990s.

What happens next

The regional council will review applications in March, with a shortlist announced in April. Projects that receive funding will be monitored for compliance with safety and environmental standards. The council also plans to publish guidelines on best practices for boat refitting and demolition.
