Saudi Riyal shifts modestly against Egyptian pound in latest updates

Mid‑October 2026 saw the Saudi Riyal adjust slightly against the Egyptian pound and neighboring currencies. Major Saudi banks posted new rates following the Central Bank's latest guidance.

Latest developments

On Sunday, 7 May 1448 Hijri (corresponding to early October 2026), the Saudi Riyal (ريال سعودي) was reported at a marginally higher value against the Egyptian pound, according to Ajel.sa. The publication noted that the Riyal traded at 4.68 Egyptian pounds per unit, a small rise from the previous day's level. Ten days later, Youm7.com recorded the Riyal's rate on 17 October, citing updates from both the National Commercial Bank and the Saudi Central Bank. The rate listed was 4.66 Egyptian pounds, indicating a slight correction after the earlier increase. The following day, Al Masry Al Youm reported that the Riyal continued to fluctuate, with banks posting a rate of 4.65 Egyptian pounds on the morning of 18 October. These adjustments were reflected across other regional currencies, including the Jordanian dinar and the Kuwaiti dinar, which also saw minor shifts in line with the Riyal's movement. The Saudi Central Bank confirmed that the changes were market‑driven and did not signal a policy shift, emphasizing that the Riyal remains effectively pegged to the US dollar.

Why it matters

Even modest changes in the Riyal's value can affect Saudi businesses that import goods from Egypt, as price calculations are often done in local currency. A higher Riyal relative to the Egyptian pound reduces the cost of Egyptian imports, potentially easing inflationary pressure on consumer goods. For Saudi travelers and expatriates, the exchange rate influences the purchasing power of their money when visiting Egypt or conducting transactions in Egyptian pounds. Remittance flows, which constitute a significant portion of household income for many families, are also sensitive to these shifts; a stronger Riyal means recipients in Egypt receive slightly fewer pounds for the same amount sent. Moreover, regional investors monitor the Riyal's performance as an indicator of broader currency stability in the Gulf, influencing portfolio decisions across the Middle East.

Background

The Saudi Riyal has been officially pegged to the US dollar at a rate of 3.75 Riyals per dollar since the early 1980s. This fixed exchange regime has provided a stable monetary environment for Saudi Arabia, supporting its oil‑driven economy and attracting foreign investment. Historically, the Riyal's relationship with neighboring currencies, such as the Egyptian pound, has been more flexible, reflecting market supply and demand as well as bilateral trade volumes. In recent years, regional economic pressures, including fluctuating oil prices and divergent monetary policies, have introduced modest volatility in cross‑currency rates, even though the Riyal's peg to the dollar remains unchanged. The Central Bank of Saudi Arabia routinely publishes reference rates for major foreign currencies, which commercial banks then adopt for retail and corporate transactions.

What happens next

The Saudi Central Bank is expected to continue monitoring the Riyal's performance against the Egyptian pound and other Arab currencies throughout the fourth quarter of 2026. Analysts suggest that unless there is a significant shift in oil revenues or external economic shocks, the Riyal will likely remain within a narrow band around its current level. Banks are anticipated to update their posted rates on a daily basis, reflecting any minor market movements. Stakeholders are advised to watch official communications from the Central Bank for any guidance on potential adjustments to the reference rates.

Summary
