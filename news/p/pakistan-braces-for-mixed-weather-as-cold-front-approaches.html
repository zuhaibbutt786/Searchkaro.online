Pakistan braces for mixed weather as cold front approaches

Pakistan is experiencing a shift in weather as a cold front moves in, bringing cooler temperatures and intermittent showers. Residents in Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi are adjusting plans amid the changing conditions.

Latest developments

Meteorological officials in Islamabad reported that a cold front advancing from the north is expected to lower daytime temperatures by up to eight degrees Celsius across the Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provinces. The Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) issued alerts for scattered rain showers in Islamabad, Rawalpindi, and the surrounding hill stations, with the possibility of localized thunderstorms late in the afternoon. In Karachi, the western coastal city, the impact will be milder, with a slight dip in humidity and a brief spell of drizzle expected during the evening hours.

Why it matters

The temperature drop and rain have immediate implications for agriculture, especially for wheat and barley crops that are nearing the harvesting stage in the central plains. Cooler temperatures can slow the ripening process, while unexpected rain may increase the risk of grain spoilage if fields become waterlogged. Farmers are being urged to monitor field conditions closely and to use available drainage measures to protect yields.

Urban commuters are also feeling the effects. In Lahore, the sudden rain has caused traffic snarls on major arteries such as the Ring Road, prompting the Lahore Traffic Police to issue advisories for drivers to reduce speed and keep headlights on. In Islamabad, the rain has disrupted outdoor events, leading organizers to move several cultural programs indoors. The PMD's warnings have helped schools and businesses plan for possible closures or delayed start times, reducing the risk of accidents.

Energy demand is expected to rise as households switch on heating devices to counter the cooler weather. The national grid is preparing for a modest increase in load, particularly in the northern districts where electricity consumption typically spikes during cold spells. The government has reminded consumers to practice energy conservation to avoid strain on the system.

November in Pakistan traditionally marks the transition from the monsoon season to the winter period. The country experiences a wide range of climatic conditions, from the dry heat of the south to the cooler, more variable weather of the north. Historically, a cold front moving southward from the Himalayas can bring a noticeable temperature decline and occasional rain, especially in the northern and central regions.

The PMD expects the cold front to move further south over the next 48 hours, bringing cooler conditions to Karachi and the southern coastal belt by Thursday.
