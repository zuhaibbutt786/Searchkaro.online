Al Nassr Face Pakhtakor in Elite Stage Clash as Ronaldo Skips Match

Al Nassr will take on Pakhtakor in the Asian Champions League Elite stage, but the Saudi club will be without star striker Ronaldo. The match sees Bostikoglu stepping up after Cristiano's exclusion.

Latest developments

The Asian Champions League Elite stage match between Al Nassr (النصر) and Pakhtakor (باختاكور) kicked off on Saturday night at the Pakhtakor Stadium in Tashkent, Uzbekistan. The fixture is the first of the group phase, with both clubs eager to secure a top spot and a place in the knockout rounds.

Al Nassr’s leading forward Cristiano Ronaldo (رونالدو) was absent from the squad, a decision that has sparked speculation across social media and sports forums. The club cited a brief injury and a need for rest, while the player’s agent confirmed he will return for the next match.

With Ronaldo sidelined, the coaching staff turned to Bostikoglu (بوستيكوجلو) to fill the void. The midfielder, who has been a regular starter in recent league games, was named in the starting XI and is expected to take on a more attacking role, according to reports from Kooora.

Liveblog coverage from Al Jazeera highlighted several key moments: an early corner by Pakhtakor, a defensive error by Al Nassr, and a counter‑attack that saw Bostikoglu create a dangerous chance. The commentary noted that the match remains tight, with neither side able to break the deadlock.

At the time of writing, the score stands at 0-0, with both teams having a single possession advantage. The result will influence the group standings, as Al Nassr aims to offset the loss of their talismanic striker.

Why it matters

For Saudi football fans, the match is a showcase of the country’s growing presence in continental competitions. Al Nassr’s performance against a seasoned Uzbek side will be closely watched by supporters and analysts alike.

The absence of a high‑profile player like Ronaldo also raises questions about squad depth and injury management in the league. It underscores the importance of developing local talent to maintain competitiveness.

Background

Al Nassr, founded in 1955, has become one of Saudi Arabia’s most successful clubs, boasting multiple domestic titles and a growing fan base. Their recent signing of Ronaldo has drawn international attention to the Saudi Professional League.

Pakhtakor, established in 1946, is a dominant force in Uzbek football, having won numerous national championships. Their participation in the Asian Champions League Elite stage reflects the increasing competitiveness of Central Asian clubs.

What happens next

Al Nassr will next face Al Hilal in the second group match, while Pakhtakor will travel to play Al Ittihad. The outcome of the upcoming fixtures will determine which clubs advance to the knockout phase, making the next week a critical period for both sides.
