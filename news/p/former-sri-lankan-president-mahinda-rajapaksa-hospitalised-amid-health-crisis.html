Former Sri Lankan President Mahinda Rajapaksa Hospitalised Amid Health Crisis

Mahinda Rajapaksa, the former president of Sri Lanka, has been admitted to a hospital for urgent care. His wife and son are currently facing corruption charges, adding to the political tension in the country.

Latest developments

Mahinda Rajapaksa, the former president of Sri Lanka, was admitted to a private hospital in Colombo on Thursday for urgent medical care after a sudden health issue. The Hindu reported that the former leader was taken to the facility following a brief but serious episode that required immediate attention from specialists.

According to local media, Rajapaksa was moved to the intensive care unit after his condition was deemed critical. The hospital staff have not released a detailed diagnosis, but the admission has raised concerns among his supporters and critics alike.

At the same time, reports from Sri Lanka Guardian indicate that Rajapaksa’s wife, Shiranthi, and his son, Mahinda Jr., are facing separate corruption cases related to alleged misappropriation of public funds during his administration. The legal proceedings are ongoing, and the cases have intensified scrutiny of the Rajapaksa family's political influence.

In a related political development, Chamara Sampath, a senior member of the Sri Lanka Freedom Party, arrived at the Ceylon International Airport Authority Board of Control (CIABOC) to discuss strategic matters. His presence at the board suggests that the party is preparing for upcoming internal elections and may be seeking to consolidate support ahead of the next general election.

While Rajapaksa’s health status remains uncertain, the combination of his hospitalization and the legal challenges faced by his family has amplified political tensions across the island. No official statement has been released by the former president’s office, leaving the public to rely on media reports for updates.

Why it matters

The hospitalization of a former head of state at a time of political unrest has implications for Sri Lanka’s governance. With the country still recovering from a severe economic crisis that culminated in a debt restructuring deal in 2022, the public’s confidence in political leadership is fragile.

The simultaneous corruption investigations against Rajapaksa’s immediate family members could influence voter sentiment in the upcoming parliamentary elections scheduled for 2024. Opposition parties are already calling for a thorough audit of the Rajapaksa era’s financial dealings, while supporters argue that the investigations are politically motivated.

Background

Mahinda Rajapaksa served as Sri Lanka’s president from 2005 to 2015, a period marked by the end of the civil war and a series of controversial policies that many critics say exacerbated economic inequality. His administration was accused of nepotism, with several family members holding key government positions.

During his second term, the country faced a severe economic crisis, leading to shortages of fuel, medicine, and basic supplies. The crisis prompted widespread protests and ultimately forced Rajapaksa to resign in 2015, after which he returned to politics as a prominent opposition leader.

The Rajapaksa family remains a powerful force in Sri Lankan politics, with several members holding parliamentary seats and ministerial portfolios. Their influence has been both praised for stability and criticized for stifling democratic processes.

What happens next

Medical updates on Rajapaksa’s condition are expected in the coming days as hospital officials are likely to release statements once a stable prognosis is established. Meanwhile, legal teams representing his wife and son are preparing for court hearings that could take place as early as next month.

Political analysts anticipate that the Rajapaksa faction will seek to mitigate the fallout by rallying loyalists and framing the legal challenges as a political attack. The opposition, however, is expected to use the situation to push for reforms and a more transparent governance framework.
