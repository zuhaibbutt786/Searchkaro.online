Pakistan, Qatar, Turkey, Indonesia Sign 'Badr' Defence Cooperation Pact

The four countries have formalised a defence cooperation agreement that will facilitate joint training, technology exchange and strategic coordination. The pact is expected to strengthen regional security and enhance military readiness.

Latest developments

On 21 October 2026, leaders of Pakistan, Qatar, Turkey and Indonesia signed the 'Badr' defence cooperation pact in Ankara. The agreement was announced by Turkish Foreign Minister and Pakistani Defence Minister. It follows earlier meetings in Doha and Jakarta.

The pact includes provisions for joint military exercises, sharing of intelligence, and cooperation in procurement of defence equipment. It also establishes a joint committee to oversee implementation and to coordinate training programmes across the four armed forces.

According to the press releases, the countries will conduct a combined exercise in the near future, with participation from air, naval and land units. The agreement is seen as a step toward greater interoperability among the participating militaries.

The signing ceremony was attended by senior officials from each delegation, and the event was broadcast live on national television in all four countries. The pact is named after the historic Battle of Badr, symbolising collective defence.

Why it matters

For Pakistan, the pact offers access to advanced training facilities in Turkey and Indonesia, which can help modernise its armed forces. It also provides a platform for sharing best practices in counter‑insurgency and maritime security.

Qatar's involvement strengthens its strategic ties with regional partners and diversifies its defence partnerships beyond traditional allies. The agreement may also support Qatar's efforts to maintain stability in the Gulf region.

The collaboration could influence the balance of power in South Asia and the Middle East, as the four nations coordinate on intelligence sharing and joint procurement. It may also encourage other regional states to pursue similar arrangements.

Background

The 'Badr' pact is the latest in a series of defence agreements that Turkey has pursued with Muslim-majority countries in recent years. Earlier this year, Turkey signed a similar accord with Qatar and Indonesia, and Pakistan has been seeking closer military ties with its neighbours.

The name 'Badr' refers to the 624 CE battle where early Muslims secured a decisive victory. The choice of name reflects the intention to build a united front against common security challenges.

What happens next

The four ministries of defence are expected to draft a detailed implementation plan within the next month. The first joint exercise is likely to take place in the first half of 2027, with a focus on air and naval coordination.
