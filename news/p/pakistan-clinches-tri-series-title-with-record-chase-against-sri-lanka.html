Pakistan clinches tri-series title with record chase against Sri Lanka

Pakistan edged Sri Lanka in a thrilling T20 final, posting a record chase thanks to a century from Fakhar Zaman and a blitz from Arafat Minhas. The win revives hopes for a stronger Pakistani side ahead of upcoming international fixtures.

Latest developments

Karachi, 1 November 2026 – In front of a packed crowd at the National Stadium, Pakistan secured the tri-series title by defeating Sri Lanka in a high‑octane T20 final. After a rain‑affected start, the hosts set a target of 210 runs, a score that had seemed daunting in the closing stages of the match.

Opening the chase, Fakhar Zaman anchored the innings with a masterful 127 runs, the first T20 century for Pakistan in a final since the format’s inception. His partnership with the middle order steadied the run‑rate and kept the required total within reach.

Midway through the innings, Arafat Minhas unleashed a blistering counter‑attack, adding 61 runs off just 31 balls. His aggressive stroke‑play, featuring sixes over the mid‑wicket and long‑on boundaries, accelerated the scoring rate and shifted momentum decisively in Pakistan’s favour.

The partnership propelled Pakistan to a total of 210‑4, matching the joint‑highest chase ever recorded in a T20 final. The achievement, noted by cricket statisticians, places Pakistan alongside a select group of sides that have successfully chased such a target in a high‑pressure encounter. The victory was celebrated by fans and officials alike, marking a significant milestone for the national team.

Why it matters

The win arrives at a crucial juncture for Pakistani cricket, which has been seeking consistency after a mixed performance in recent bilateral series. By overcoming a competitive Sri Lankan side, Pakistan demonstrates depth in both batting and bowling, reinforcing confidence ahead of the upcoming ICC T20 World Cup qualifiers.

Fakhar Zaman’s century not only showcases his personal form but also signals a resurgence of top‑order stability that has been missing in several recent matches. Arafat Minhas’s rapid 61 highlights the emergence of young talent capable of delivering under pressure, a factor that selectors are likely to consider for future squad composition.

For Pakistani fans, the triumph revives national pride and stimulates renewed interest in the sport, potentially boosting viewership and sponsorship opportunities. The record chase also adds a statistical landmark to the team’s history, providing a narrative of resilience that can inspire the next generation of cricketers.

Background

The tri-series, featuring Pakistan, Sri Lanka and a third associate nation, was organized by the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) to provide competitive exposure ahead of major international tournaments. Pakistan entered the tournament with a mixed record, having lost a few warm‑up matches but showing flashes of brilliance. Sri Lanka, on the other hand, had built momentum with a series of convincing wins, positioning themselves as the favourites for the final.

Historically, Pakistan’s T20 performances have been marked by occasional brilliance but a lack of consistency, especially in chase scenarios. The joint‑highest chase record, previously set in a 2022 final, had remained unbroken until this encounter. The PCB’s strategic emphasis on nurturing young players like Arafat Minhas has been part of a broader plan to rejuvenate the squad after a period of under‑achievement.

What happens next

Following the victory, the PCB is expected to announce an expanded training camp focusing on finishing skills and pressure handling, with both Fakhar Zaman and Arafat Minhas likely to feature prominently. Pakistan’s next assignment is a bilateral T20 series against the West Indies in December, which will serve as a final rehearsal before the World Cup qualifiers. Sri Lanka will regroup and aim to fine‑tune their bowling attack, preparing for an upcoming tour of Australia.
