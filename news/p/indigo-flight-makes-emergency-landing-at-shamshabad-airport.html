Indigo Flight Makes Emergency Landing at Shamshabad Airport

An Indigo flight had to land abruptly at Shamshabad Airport, sparking questions about flight safety. New rules on power banks may affect future passengers.

Latest developments

On Thursday, an Indigo flight operating from Chennai to Hyderabad was forced to make an emergency landing at Shamshabad Airport, a major hub located just outside the city of Hyderabad. According to reports from local media, the aircraft experienced a sudden loss of engine power shortly after take‑off, prompting the pilots to declare an emergency and return to the nearest airport. The crew and all 140 passengers on board were evacuated safely, and no injuries were reported.

Airport officials confirmed that the aircraft landed on the main runway without incident. Passengers were transferred to the terminal and offered assistance while the aircraft was inspected by maintenance crews. The flight was originally scheduled to depart at 10:30 a.m., but the emergency landing took place at 11:15 a.m. The incident has prompted a review of the aircraft’s maintenance records by the airline’s safety team.

The emergency landing comes amid the enforcement of new DGCA rules that ban certain types of power banks on domestic flights. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation issued a directive earlier this month restricting the use of power banks that exceed 10,000 mAh or contain lithium‑ion cells. Some passengers had reported carrying such devices, raising questions about whether the power bank restrictions may have played a role in the incident.

Why it matters

For passengers, the incident highlights the importance of adhering to safety regulations, especially those concerning electronic devices on board. Airlines and regulators are expected to reinforce compliance with power bank rules to prevent future emergencies. The incident also underscores the need for clear communication between pilots and ground crews during in‑flight emergencies.

The event draws attention to the broader issue of aircraft safety in India, where rapid growth in domestic air travel has increased the number of flights and passengers. Ensuring that all safety protocols are strictly followed is essential to maintaining public confidence in the aviation sector.

Background

Shamshabad Airport, officially known as Rajiv Gandhi International Airport, serves as a key aviation hub for the Hyderabad region. The airport has handled over 20 million passengers annually and is equipped with advanced navigation systems. In recent years, the DGCA has tightened safety protocols, particularly regarding the use of electronic devices that may interfere with aircraft systems.

The airport’s proximity to the city makes it a busy gateway for both domestic and international flights. It has previously handled emergency landings, but this incident is notable for its connection to new power bank restrictions that were introduced earlier this month.

What happens next

The airline has launched a formal investigation into the cause of the engine failure, with findings expected in the coming weeks. The DGCA will review the incident in the context of its power bank restrictions and may issue further guidance to airlines and passengers. Passengers affected by the flight may be offered refunds or rebooking options.

Summary
