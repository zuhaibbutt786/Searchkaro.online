Raj Shamani's 'Figuring Out' Podcast Hits 22 Million YouTube Subscribers, Surpasses Global Leaders

The Indian podcast Figuring Out has reached 22 million subscribers on YouTube, making it the platform's most‑followed show. The milestone places host Raj Shamani ahead of global names such as Joe Rogan and Steven Bartlett.

Latest developments

Raj Shamani, an Indian entrepreneur‑turned‑podcaster, announced that his weekly series Figuring Out has crossed the 22 million subscriber mark on YouTube. The achievement was confirmed in a press release on Podnews, which described the show as the first podcast in the world to reach that level of audience on the video platform. The milestone was celebrated with a short video posted by Shamani, showing the subscriber count climbing in real time.

Industry observers noted that the figure puts Figuring Out ahead of long‑standing podcasts such as The Joe Rogan Experience and The Diary of a CEO, hosted by Steven Bartlett. The American Bazaar reported that Shamani's show now occupies the top position in the global ranking of YouTube podcasts, a list traditionally dominated by English‑language creators from the United States and United Kingdom.

The surge in subscribers has been attributed to a combination of factors, including Shamani's focus on personal‑development topics, his use of high‑production video content, and strategic collaborations with other Indian digital creators. The show regularly features interviews with business leaders, motivational speakers, and technology innovators, appealing to a young, aspirational audience across South Asia and the diaspora.

YouTube analytics released alongside the announcement indicated that the majority of new followers are located in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and the United Arab Emirates. Engagement metrics such as average watch time and comment activity have also risen, suggesting that the audience is not only growing in size but also in loyalty.

Why it matters

For viewers in Pakistan, the rise of Figuring Out offers a locally relevant alternative to Western‑origin podcasts that dominate the market. The show's emphasis on entrepreneurship, self‑improvement, and regional success stories resonates with a demographic that is increasingly turning to digital platforms for career guidance and inspiration.

The milestone also highlights the growing importance of YouTube as a distribution channel for audio‑centric content in South Asia. While traditional podcast apps remain popular, many users in Pakistan prefer video‑enabled platforms that allow simultaneous listening and visual engagement. Shamani's success may encourage other regional creators to invest in video production, potentially diversifying the media landscape and creating new revenue streams for independent producers.

From an advertising perspective, the 22 million subscriber base creates a sizable audience for brands seeking to reach young, affluent consumers in the sub‑continent. Marketers are likely to view the show as a premium inventory source, especially for products related to education, technology, and lifestyle.

Background

Raj Shamani began his career as a serial entrepreneur, founding several startups before moving into content creation in 2020. His first foray into podcasting was a modest audio series, but he quickly pivoted to a video format to capitalize on YouTube's algorithmic reach. Figuring Out launched in early 2021 and initially focused on interviews with Indian business figures.

The podcast industry has seen rapid growth worldwide, with platforms like Spotify and Apple Podcasts reporting millions of new shows each year. However, the convergence of podcasting and video has been slower, particularly in South Asia, where bandwidth constraints and content preferences have favored short‑form video. Shamani's decision to blend long‑form interview content with high‑quality video production set his show apart from many competitors.

Internationally, podcasts such as The Joe Rogan Experience have long held the top spots in subscriber counts on platforms like Spotify and YouTube. Rogan's show, which began in 2009, amassed over 11 million YouTube subscribers before being overtaken. Steven Bartlett's Diary of a CEO, launched in 2017, also built a substantial following. Shamani's overtaking of these figures marks a shift in the geographic distribution of digital influence.

What happens next

Industry analysts expect that the Figuring Out team will leverage the new subscriber base to expand into live events, merchandise, and premium membership tiers. The channel may also explore regional language subtitles to attract a broader Pakistani audience that prefers Urdu or Punjabi content.

YouTube is likely to promote the show further through its recommendation engine, potentially increasing visibility among users who have not yet subscribed. Competitors may respond by enhancing their own video‑podcast offerings, intensifying the race for audience share in the South Asian market.
