Houthis claim missile strike on Saudi capital as conflict escalates

Yemen's Houthi movement says it fired ballistic missiles that struck Riyadh, prompting renewed tension between the two neighbours. The incident has drawn global attention as the war in Yemen continues to affect regional stability.

What is happening

In the early hours of the latest reported incident, the Houthi forces in Yemen announced that they had launched a series of ballistic missiles aimed at the Saudi capital, Riyadh. The group said the missiles reached the vicinity of the city and caused visible flames and smoke near a major airport. Saudi authorities confirmed that a fire broke out at a fuel depot close to the airport, but they did not immediately confirm any damage to the capital itself.

Local media in Saudi Arabia reported thick smoke and a brief period of reduced visibility at the airport, prompting temporary flight disruptions. Emergency services responded quickly to contain the blaze at the fuel depot, and the area was declared secure after the fire was extinguished. No casualties were reported in the immediate aftermath, according to official statements.

The Houthi movement, which has been engaged in a protracted conflict with Saudi Arabia since 2015, claimed responsibility for the attack and described it as a response to Saudi military actions in Yemen. The group said the missiles were launched from territory it controls in northern Yemen and that the operation demonstrated its growing capability to strike deep inside Saudi territory.

Saudi Arabia's defense ministry issued a statement warning that any aggression against its sovereign territory would be met with a decisive response. The ministry also indicated that its air defense systems had intercepted some of the incoming projectiles, though details on the number of interceptions were not disclosed. The incident has heightened alert levels at key infrastructure sites across the kingdom.

Why people are searching this now

The timing of the reported missile strike coincides with a series of diplomatic and military moves in the region, including renewed Saudi air campaigns in Yemen and ongoing peace talks mediated by the United Nations. As the conflict shows no sign of abating, international observers and the public are looking for updates on the security of Saudi cities and the potential for escalation.

Online interest is also driven by concerns over the safety of global energy markets. Saudi Arabia is a leading oil exporter, and any disruption to its production or transport infrastructure can have ripple effects on fuel prices worldwide. The sight of smoke near a major airport and a fuel depot fire has amplified worries about the vulnerability of critical energy assets.

Background and context

The Houthi movement, officially known as Ansar Allah, emerged from the Zaidi Shia community in northern Yemen and has been fighting the Saudi-led coalition since the coalition intervened in 2015 to restore the internationally recognized Yemeni government. Over the years, the Houthis have increasingly used missile and drone technology to target Saudi installations, including oil facilities, airports, and urban centers.

Saudi Arabia views the Houthi attacks as a direct threat to its national security and has conducted extensive air operations in Yemen to degrade the rebel group's capabilities. The conflict has caused a humanitarian crisis in Yemen, with millions of people displaced and in need of aid. International efforts to negotiate a ceasefire have repeatedly stalled, and each new attack raises the stakes for both sides.

What to know next

Analysts expect Saudi Arabia to respond with a combination of diplomatic protests and potential military action aimed at Houthi positions in Yemen. The kingdom may also increase its air defense readiness around key urban and industrial sites to prevent further strikes.

The international community, including the United Nations and several regional powers, is likely to call for restraint and a return to negotiations. Monitoring agencies will keep an eye on flight schedules, oil market indicators, and any statements from the parties involved to gauge whether the incident will lead to a broader escalation or remain an isolated event.

• Global attention focuses on the potential impact on energy markets and the broader stability of the Middle East.
