Petrol price cut, diesel hike announced as OGRA issues Friday changes

Latest developments

The Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA) released a notification on Thursday indicating that the retail prices of petroleum products will be adjusted on Friday, 17 October 2026. Under the new schedule, the price of petrol (referred to locally as 'پٹرول') will fall by Rs 3.63 per litre, while the price of diesel will rise by Rs 0.31 per litre. The changes apply to all fuel stations across the country and are effective from 00:01 local time on Friday. OGRA said the adjustments reflect the latest movements in international crude oil markets and the government's decision to balance consumer affordability with fiscal considerations. The Ministry of Finance confirmed the policy shift in a brief statement, noting that the reduction in petrol is intended to ease the cost of living for commuters and transport operators who rely heavily on gasoline‑powered vehicles. At the same time, the modest increase in diesel is aimed at generating additional revenue to support budgetary needs without imposing a sharp burden on the agricultural sector, which uses diesel for irrigation pumps and farm machinery. The government described the move as a calibrated response to volatile global oil prices and domestic inflation pressures. Market analysts observed that the Rs 3.63 cut brings the average retail price of petrol to approximately Rs 224 per litre, a level not seen since early 2024. Diesel, after the Rs 0.31 hike, is expected to trade around Rs 240 per litre. Fuel traders reported that the price differentials will likely shift demand patterns, with some drivers opting for diesel‑powered cars to benefit from the relatively smaller increase. Retailers have been instructed to update price boards by the evening of Thursday to avoid confusion at the pumps. Consumer groups welcomed the petrol discount, citing relief for daily commuters and public transport operators. However, they also warned that the diesel increase could raise operating costs for logistics firms and farmers, potentially translating into higher prices for goods and food items. The government has pledged to monitor the impact closely and promised to review the pricing mechanism in the next quarterly meeting of the Energy Advisory Committee.

Why it matters

For ordinary Pakistanis, fuel costs represent a significant portion of household expenditure. A reduction of Rs 3.63 per litre translates into savings of roughly Rs 150 per month for a family that drives 500 kilometres, easing pressure on tight budgets. The diesel hike, though modest, may increase transport costs for goods moving across the country, which could be reflected in retail prices for essential commodities. The adjustment also has macro‑economic implications. Lower petrol prices can stimulate private vehicle usage and boost tourism, while higher diesel revenues help the treasury meet its fiscal targets. At the same time, the policy signals the government's willingness to intervene in the energy market to curb inflation, a priority as the country grapples with rising food prices.

Background

Pakistan's fuel pricing system is governed by a formula that links domestic retail rates to the price of imported crude oil, exchange rates, and a margin for refiners and distributors. OGRA, the independent regulator, publishes monthly notifications that set the final retail price after the government reviews the underlying cost components. Historically, the country has experienced frequent price swings, with sharp increases in 2022 and 2023 that sparked public protests. In recent months, global crude oil prices have shown a mixed trend, falling from a peak of $85 per barrel in early 2024 to around $78 per barrel in September 2026. The Pakistani rupee has also stabilized against the US dollar, reducing the currency risk component of the pricing formula. These external factors gave the authorities room to lower petrol while keeping diesel relatively stable.

What happens next

OGRA will publish the final price schedule on its website by midnight Thursday, and fuel stations are required to display the new rates before opening on Friday. The Energy Advisory Committee will review the impact of the changes after a month, and the Finance Ministry has indicated that further adjustments could be made if international oil prices move sharply.
