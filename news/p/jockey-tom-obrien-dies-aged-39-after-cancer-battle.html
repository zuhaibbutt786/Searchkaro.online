Jockey Tom O'Brien dies aged 39 after cancer battle

Jockey Tom O'Brien has died at 39 after a battle with cancer. The racing world mourns the loss of a Cheltenham Festival winner.

Latest developments

Tom O'Brien, 39, died on 10 November 2026 after a long fight with cancer. He was a celebrated jockey, known for his win at the Cheltenham Festival in 2025. He had been diagnosed with a rare form of cancer in early 2024, and had undergone treatment while continuing to race.

The BBC reported that O'Brien's death came after a period of intensive treatment. He had been a regular in the National Hunt circuit, and his career included over 200 race wins. His last race was at the Aintree meeting in September, where he finished third.

The Guardian highlighted the impact of his loss on the racing community, noting that his death was a shock to many. O'Brien had been praised for his skill and sportsmanship, and his career was marked by several high‑profile victories.

Peter Bowen, a former trainer, paid tribute to O'Brien in the Racing Post, describing him as "part of our lives and our family". Bowen's comments reflected the close bonds that often form between jockeys and trainers in the sport.

O'Brien's family released a statement expressing gratitude for the outpouring of support from fans and colleagues. They thanked the racing community for the compassion shown during his illness.

Why it matters

O'Brien's death has highlighted the vulnerability of athletes to serious illnesses. His case has prompted discussions about the health monitoring protocols for jockeys, many of whom face high physical demands and risk of injury.

In Great Britain, the racing industry is a significant part of the sporting economy, with millions of spectators attending events. The loss of a prominent jockey such as O'Brien is felt by fans, owners, trainers, and the wider community, underscoring the human stories behind the sport.

Background

Tom O'Brien began his career as an apprentice in 2005, quickly rising through the ranks with a reputation for calm riding and tactical awareness. He secured his first Grade 1 victory in 2011 at the Grand National.

Over the next decade, O'Brien accumulated a series of wins, including a triumph at the Cheltenham Festival in 2025, where he rode the horse "Silver Lining" to victory in the National Hunt Chase. His achievements earned him a place among the elite jockeys of his generation.

What happens next

The racing authorities have announced a memorial service for O'Brien to be held at the Cheltenham Racecourse on 18 November. In addition, a fund has been set up by the Jockey Club to support jockeys facing serious illnesses, inspired by O'Brien's experience.
