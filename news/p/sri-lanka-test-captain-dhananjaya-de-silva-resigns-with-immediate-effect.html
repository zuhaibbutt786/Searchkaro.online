Sri Lanka Test captain Dhananjaya de Silva resigns with immediate effect

Dhananjaya de Silva has resigned as Sri Lanka's Test captain with immediate effect. The board will now seek a new leader ahead of upcoming fixtures.

Latest developments

Dhananjaya de Silva announced his resignation as Sri Lanka's Test captain on Tuesday, with the decision taking effect immediately, according to the International Cricket Council (ICC) and reports from Cricbuzz and Dawn.

The move follows a period of mixed results for the Sri Lankan side in the longest format, where the team has struggled to secure victories in recent series. Sources within Sri Lanka Cricket (SLC) said the board and the player discussed the issue over several weeks.

SLC issued an official statement confirming that de Silva's resignation has been accepted and that a review will be conducted to appoint a new captain. The board emphasized continuity and the need to stabilise the leadership ahead of upcoming Test fixtures.

De Silva, who took over the Test captaincy earlier this year after the departure of previous leaders, becomes the latest in a line of short‑term captains for Sri Lanka. His departure adds to the ongoing reshuffle within the national side.

Why it matters

For cricket fans in Pakistan, the development is significant because Sri Lanka is a regular opponent in regional tournaments and bilateral series. Changes in leadership can affect team composition, strategy and the competitiveness of matches that draw large viewership across South Asia.

The resignation also highlights broader challenges faced by Sri Lankan cricket, including player turnover and administrative pressures. Pakistani commentators may view the situation as an opportunity for their own team to gauge Sri Lanka's stability before upcoming series, influencing selection and preparation decisions.

Background

Dhananjaya de Silva, a right‑hand batsman and occasional off‑spinner, made his international debut in 2016 and quickly established himself as a reliable middle‑order player. He earned the Test captaincy in early 2024 after a series of injuries and retirements left the side without a permanent leader.

Prior to de Silva, Sri Lanka experimented with several captains, including Dimuth Karunaratne and Kusal Perera, each serving brief tenures amid fluctuating form. The team's recent Test record includes a handful of draws and losses, prompting the board to seek a steady hand at the helm.

De Silva's tenure was marked by attempts to rebuild the batting order and integrate younger talent, but inconsistent performances and a lack of clear direction led to mounting pressure from media and supporters.

What happens next

The immediate next step is for SLC to appoint an interim captain, likely drawn from the senior pool of players such as Niroshan Dickwella or Kusal Mendis, while a formal selection process runs in parallel with the team's preparation for its next Test series.

Observers expect the board to use the transition period to evaluate leadership options and possibly restructure the coaching staff, aiming to restore confidence before the upcoming home series against Bangladesh later this year.
