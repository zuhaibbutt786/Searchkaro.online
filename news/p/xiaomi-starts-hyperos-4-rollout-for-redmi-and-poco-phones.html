Xiaomi Starts HyperOS 4 Rollout for Redmi and Poco Phones

Xiaomi has launched the first phase of its HyperOS 4 update for several Redmi and Poco smartphones in Pakistan. The rollout promises a refreshed UI, improved security and performance tweaks.

Latest developments

Xiaomi announced on Monday that the first phase of its HyperOS 4 rollout has begun in Pakistan. The company said the update, built on the upcoming Android 17 platform, will be delivered over‑the‑air to a curated list of Redmi and Poco smartphones. According to the schedule released on the official Xiaomi website, the initial batch includes the Redmi Note 12 Pro, Redmi Note 12 Pro 5G, Redmi Note 12 Turbo, Poco X5 Pro and Poco F5. Users of these models will receive a notification in the system settings prompting them to download the new firmware. The rollout is expected to span the next three weeks, with subsequent waves covering additional devices as testing confirms stability.

The HyperOS 4 upgrade brings a refreshed user interface, a redesigned quick settings panel, and a suite of performance optimisations aimed at reducing app launch times. Security has also been bolstered with a new privacy dashboard that lets users control sensor access and view permission histories. Xiaomi highlighted a native AI assistant that can suggest shortcuts based on usage patterns, as well as improved battery management that learns daily routines to allocate resources more efficiently. The update retains compatibility with existing MIUI themes while introducing a set of fresh visual assets that align with the HyperOS branding.

In Pakistan, the rollout aligns with the country’s growing demand for longer software support on mid‑range devices. Analysts note that extending updates to models such as the Redmi Note 12 series helps manufacturers differentiate their offerings in a crowded market. The OTA process will be managed through Xiaomi’s Mi Community app, which also provides a feedback channel for early adopters. Xiaomi has warned users to back up personal data before initiating the upgrade, as the transition involves a full system flash that replaces the previous MIUI 14 build.

The company has already opened a public beta for a small group of users to test stability on the Poco X5 Pro. Early reports indicate that the new OS runs smoother on the device’s Snapdragon 7+ Gen 2 chipset, though a few users have mentioned occasional UI glitches that are being addressed in a forthcoming patch. Xiaomi’s regional support team in Karachi has set up a dedicated hotline to assist customers encountering issues during the update. The firm expects the finalised version of HyperOS 4 to be fully rolled out to all eligible devices by the end of December.

Why it matters

For Pakistani consumers, the HyperOS 4 rollout represents a significant extension of software longevity for devices that were originally launched with only two years of official updates. By receiving the latest Android core and security patches, owners can protect their phones against emerging threats and benefit from new features without purchasing a new handset. The update also improves the resale value of the devices, as buyers often look for recent operating system versions when evaluating second‑hand phones.

The introduction of HyperOS 4 also intensifies competition among Android OEMs in the region. Samsung, Realme and Vivo have all pledged multi‑year support for their flagship and mid‑range lines, and Xiaomi’s move to shift from MIUI to a more Android‑centric experience may influence consumer preferences. Moreover, the enhanced battery and performance tweaks could reduce the need for frequent device replacements, aligning with broader sustainability goals promoted by local regulators.

Background

Xiaomi’s custom skin, MIUI, has powered its smartphones since the company entered the market in 2010. Over the years, MIUI evolved with its own app ecosystem, theme store and frequent feature updates. However, the growing complexity of maintaining a heavily customised layer on top of Android prompted the firm to develop HyperOS, a lighter framework that aims to integrate more closely with the native Android codebase.

HyperOS 4 is the first version to be built on Android 17, the upcoming release that introduces refined privacy controls, improved multitasking, and a more modular system architecture. Xiaomi has positioned HyperOS as a bridge between its legacy MIUI experience and the future direction of Android, promising faster OTA delivery and broader device compatibility. The shift mirrors a broader industry trend where manufacturers streamline their software stacks to reduce fragmentation and accelerate feature rollouts.

What happens next

The next phase of the HyperOS 4 rollout is slated to begin in early January, covering additional Redmi models such as the Note 13 series and the Poco M5 Pro. Xiaomi has indicated that each wave will be monitored for crash reports and user feedback before proceeding to the following batch. Industry observers expect the company to announce a full list of supported devices for the 2025 calendar year by March, potentially expanding the update to older flagship models that have not yet received Android 13.

Summary

- HyperOS 4 rollout started in Pakistan for select Redmi and Poco phones, with a three‑week initial phase. - The update, based on Android 17, adds a new UI, privacy dashboard, AI shortcuts and battery optimisations. - Xiaomi plans further waves through early 2025, extending support and reinforcing its position in the competitive mid‑range market.
