Tamil Nadu High Court Orders Probe into Temple Property Encroachment and Unauthorised Fee Collection

A Madurai high court panel has asked for a detailed report on illegal occupation of temple property. Authorities also face scrutiny over fee collection without receipts at festival shops.

Latest developments

The Madurai bench of the Tamil Nadu High Court has issued an order seeking a comprehensive report on the encroachment of temple lands in the state. The court's directive follows a series of complaints that local groups and devotees have raised about the illegal occupation of temple premises by private individuals and commercial entities. In parallel, law enforcement agencies have begun investigating allegations that temple executive officers (EOs) have been collecting fees from vendors operating in festival markets without issuing receipts, a practice that has drawn criticism for its lack of transparency.

According to reports from regional newspapers, several temple authorities have been accused of demanding payments from shopkeepers during major religious festivals, such as the annual கோயில (temple) fairs in Tamil Nadu. Vendors claim they were asked to pay a fee to continue operating, yet no official receipts were provided, raising concerns about possible financial misappropriation. The practice, described by some as "money hunting in the name of devotion," has prompted calls for stricter oversight of temple finances.

The high court's order also requests that the state government submit a status report on ongoing efforts to reclaim temple property that has been seized by private parties. Officials are expected to detail the extent of the encroachments, the legal steps taken to remove unauthorized occupants, and any compensation measures for affected religious institutions. The court has set a deadline of two weeks for the submission of this report.

In response, the Department of Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) has announced that it will cooperate fully with the court's inquiry. A senior HR&CE official stated that the department is reviewing all pending cases of property disputes and will provide the necessary documentation to the judiciary. The department also indicated that it would initiate disciplinary action against any temple officials found guilty of irregular fee collection.

Local activists have welcomed the court's intervention, viewing it as a necessary step to protect the sanctity of religious sites and ensure that public funds are not misused. They have urged the government to implement robust monitoring mechanisms to prevent future incidents of encroachment and financial malpractice.

Why it matters

Temples in Tamil Nadu are not only places of worship but also significant cultural and economic hubs. Illegal occupation of temple land can disrupt community activities, affect pilgrim access, and lead to loss of revenue that traditionally supports temple maintenance and charitable work. When unauthorized parties take control of temple property, they often repurpose the land for commercial gain, undermining the heritage value of these sites.

The alleged fee collection without receipts undermines public confidence in temple administration. Devotees who contribute financially expect transparency and accountability. Unrecorded transactions can lead to misallocation of funds, depriving temples of resources needed for renovations, festivals, and social services. Moreover, the practice can create an uneven playing field for vendors, some of whom may be forced to pay hidden charges while others are exempt, fostering resentment within the local business community.

For the broader public, the issue highlights the need for stronger governance of religious institutions. In a state where temples manage extensive assets, clear legal frameworks and vigilant oversight are essential to prevent exploitation. The high court's involvement signals a willingness of the judiciary to address systemic weaknesses and protect public interest.

Background

Tamil Nadu is home to thousands of temples, many of which are administered by the HR&CE department. Over the past decade, there have been periodic reports of encroachment on temple lands, often by local businesses seeking to expand their premises. These disputes frequently involve complex legal battles, as temples hold historical titles that are sometimes contested by private claimants.

The practice of collecting fees from vendors during festivals is not new, but it has traditionally been regulated through official receipts and clear guidelines. Recent media investigations have uncovered instances where temple officials demanded payments without providing documentation, leading to accusations of corruption. Such incidents have fueled public outcry and prompted calls for reforms in temple financial management.

The high court's current order builds on earlier judicial interventions that sought to clarify the legal status of temple properties and enforce stricter compliance with financial protocols. Previous rulings have mandated audits of temple accounts and the removal of illegal structures from sacred grounds.

What happens next

The HR&CE department will compile the requested report and submit it to the Madurai high court within the stipulated deadline. Simultaneously, the state police are expected to launch a focused investigation into the alleged unreceipted fee collection, potentially leading to disciplinary action against implicated officials. Civil society groups have pledged to monitor the implementation of any court-mandated measures, ensuring that encroachers are removed and financial practices are regularised.
