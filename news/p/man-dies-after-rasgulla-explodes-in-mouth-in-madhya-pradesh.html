Man dies after 'rasgulla' explodes in mouth in Madhya Pradesh

A youth in Madhya Pradesh lost his life after a sweet he believed to be a rasgulla exploded in his mouth. Police have arrested a neighbour and are probing the incident as a possible homemade bomb case.

Latest developments

Earlier this week, a 24‑year‑old resident of a village in Madhya Pradesh suffered fatal injuries after a sweet he was offered burst in his mouth. According to the BBC Hindi report, the victim was eating what he thought was a regular rasgulla – a popular syrup‑soaked dessert – when the confection detonated, causing severe trauma to his face and throat.

Local police investigations revealed that the sweet was a makeshift explosive device, locally referred to as a "suar bomb" or "pig bomb". The device was reportedly prepared using a small amount of high‑explosive material wrapped in edible sugar syrup to mimic the appearance of a rasgulla. Forensic experts who examined the remnants confirmed the presence of a low‑order explosive charge.

The suspect, identified as the victim's neighbour and close friend, was taken into custody shortly after the incident. Police statements, echoed by The Lallantop, indicate that the accused had handed the device to the victim under the pretense of sharing a treat. The motive behind the act remains unclear, and investigators are exploring whether the incident was a prank gone tragically wrong or part of a larger pattern of illicit explosive manufacturing.

Further examinations are being conducted at the district forensic laboratory to determine the exact composition of the explosive and to trace its source. Authorities have also collected statements from other villagers who reported hearing a loud pop followed by a scream, confirming the sudden nature of the blast.

Community leaders have expressed shock and grief, calling for stricter monitoring of homemade explosive materials. The incident has sparked a broader conversation about safety in rural areas where such devices can be fabricated with relative ease.

Why it matters

The death highlights a growing concern over the accessibility of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) in India's interior regions. While most public discourse focuses on larger terrorist attacks, this case shows how small‑scale, locally produced bombs can cause lethal outcomes in everyday settings, such as a family gathering or a casual visit between friends.

For residents of Madhya Pradesh and other states, the incident serves as a stark reminder that traditional sweets, a cultural staple, can be weaponised. It may prompt households to exercise greater caution when accepting food from acquaintances, especially in areas where law‑enforcement reports have noted a rise in the clandestine manufacture of low‑order explosives for personal vendettas or criminal intimidation.

The police response also underscores the importance of rapid forensic capability in rural districts. By quickly identifying the explosive nature of the device, investigators were able to arrest the suspect before further harm could occur, potentially preventing additional incidents.

Background

Rasgulla, a spongy cheese‑based dessert soaked in sugar syrup, is a beloved sweet across India and Bangladesh. Its soft texture and sweet taste make it a common offering during festivals, celebrations, and casual hospitality. However, the dessert's shape – a round, glossy ball – can be easily mimicked, which has occasionally been exploited by criminals to conceal harmful substances.

Improvised explosive devices have been documented in various Indian states, often linked to criminal gangs, insurgent groups, or personal disputes. The use of edible disguises for bombs is not new; similar cases have been reported where candies or chocolates were rigged with explosives, primarily to evade detection.

Madhya Pradesh, a central Indian state with a large rural population, has seen sporadic incidents involving homemade bombs, typically related to land disputes or local feuds. The state's police force has been urged in recent years to strengthen community policing and to conduct awareness campaigns about the dangers of unregulated explosive material.

What happens next

The arrested neighbour will face charges of homicide, possession of an explosive substance, and causing grievous bodily harm. The case is expected to proceed through the district court, where forensic testimony will play a central role. Meanwhile, the district administration has announced plans to launch a public awareness drive, urging citizens to report suspicious activities and to verify the safety of food items offered by acquaintances.

State police are also reviewing other recent complaints of unexplained injuries to determine if similar devices have been used elsewhere. Enhanced surveillance of chemical sales and tighter regulation of precursor materials are being discussed at the state level.
