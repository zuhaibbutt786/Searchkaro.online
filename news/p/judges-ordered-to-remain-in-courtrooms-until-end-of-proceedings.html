Judges Ordered to Remain in Courtrooms Until End of Proceedings

Bangladeshi judges have been instructed to remain in court until the conclusion of proceedings. The directive follows concerns over premature departures and aims to uphold courtroom decorum.

Latest developments

A new directive issued by the High Court has made it mandatory for judges to stay in the courtroom until the scheduled end of each session. The order, reported by Kalbela, was issued in response to several incidents where judges left the premises early, potentially compromising the integrity of ongoing trials. The directive is reinforced by a separate instruction from the Ministry of Law, which states that judges must not leave their workplace without explicit permission. Shomoyer Alo highlighted that any unauthorized departure could result in administrative sanctions and could be considered a breach of judicial conduct. In a related development, former judge Fouad issued a public apology for remarks made during a televised interview. He warned the tribunal that such comments could undermine public confidence in the judiciary. Kaler Kantho covered the apology, noting that the tribunal had issued a caution to all judges to maintain decorum. The enforcement of these directives will involve regular monitoring by the Judicial Service Commission. Judges who violate the new rules may face disciplinary action, including suspension or removal from the bench. The commission will also review existing protocols to ensure that the new measures are effectively integrated into daily court operations.

Why it matters

The requirement for judges to remain in court until proceedings conclude is a significant step toward strengthening judicial accountability. By preventing premature departures, the judiciary aims to reduce the risk of perceived bias or interference in case outcomes. For the public, the new rule signals a commitment to transparency. Citizens who attend court sessions can be assured that the judge will be present for the entire duration, fostering trust in the legal process. The directive also has implications for case management. Judges staying until the end of sessions can help streamline proceedings, reducing delays caused by rescheduling or incomplete hearings. This may improve overall efficiency in the court system.

Background

Bangladesh’s judiciary has long faced scrutiny over the conduct of its judges. Previous reports highlighted instances where judges left court early, raising concerns about procedural fairness. The legal framework governing judicial conduct is outlined in the Bangladesh Constitution and the Judicial Service Act. However, enforcement of these provisions has varied, prompting the recent directive to clarify expectations. Historically, the judiciary has responded to public criticism by issuing guidelines and conduct codes. The current directive builds on these efforts, adding a clear rule about physical presence during sessions.

What happens next

Implementation of the directive will begin immediately, with the Judicial Service Commission overseeing compliance. Judges will receive written notices detailing the new requirements and the consequences of non‑compliance. The commission plans to conduct periodic audits of court schedules to ensure that judges adhere to the rule. If violations occur, disciplinary procedures will be initiated, potentially leading to suspension or removal from the bench.
