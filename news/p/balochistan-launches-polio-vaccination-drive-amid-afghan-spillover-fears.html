Balochistan launches polio vaccination drive amid Afghan spillover fears

Balochistan has begun a large-scale polio vaccination campaign after concerns that the disease could spread from Afghanistan. Health officials say the drive will cover over a million children in the province.

Latest developments

Balochistan’s provincial government announced the start of a comprehensive polio vaccination campaign on 2 November, citing rising concerns that the virus could cross the border from Afghanistan. The initiative, launched by the provincial health department, aims to vaccinate 1.2 million children in districts that have historically reported low coverage. The move follows reports of a surge in polio cases in neighbouring Afghan provinces, prompting Pakistani authorities to act pre‑emptively.

In a separate development, the District Commissioner of Quetta ordered strict action against health workers and local officials who failed to meet vaccination targets. The Daily Independent reported that the DC will impose penalties on teams that do not achieve the set coverage levels, a step that underscores the urgency of the campaign. The order comes after a review of recent immunisation data revealed that several districts in Balochistan lagged behind national averages.

Balochistan Pulse highlighted that the drive will focus on children aged 0 to 5 years, with mobile teams set to visit remote villages and urban slums alike. The campaign will use the oral polio vaccine (OPV) and will be supported by international partners, including UNICEF and WHO, who have provided both funding and technical assistance. Health officials say that the campaign will run for six weeks, with a final assessment scheduled at the end of the period.

The provincial government has also pledged to strengthen surveillance systems to detect any new polio cases promptly. According to the Ministry of Health, a network of field workers will monitor vaccination coverage and report any adverse events. The campaign’s success will depend on community cooperation, especially in areas where mistrust of health workers remains high.

Why it matters

Polio remains a significant public health threat in Pakistan, a country that has struggled to eliminate the disease for decades. The new campaign is crucial for preventing a resurgence that could undermine years of progress. By targeting over a million children, the drive seeks to close gaps in coverage that have left pockets of the population vulnerable.

For families across Balochistan, the campaign offers a chance to protect their children from a debilitating disease that can cause paralysis or death. The initiative also signals the government’s commitment to meeting international polio eradication targets set by the Global Polio Eradication Initiative. A successful rollout could boost public confidence in the health system and encourage higher participation in future immunisation programmes.

Background

Polio has long been endemic in Pakistan, with the country accounting for a large proportion of global cases until recent years. The disease is transmitted through contaminated water and food, making rural and peri‑urban areas especially vulnerable. In 2018, Pakistan and Afghanistan jointly declared a polio-free status, but sporadic cases have since re‑emerged, largely due to vaccine hesitancy and logistical challenges.

The current situation is further complicated by the movement of people across the porous border. Afghan refugees and seasonal workers can carry the virus into Pakistani districts, creating a risk of cross‑border transmission. Health authorities have therefore intensified surveillance and vaccination efforts in border provinces like Balochistan.

Previous campaigns in the region have faced obstacles such as limited infrastructure, security concerns, and misinformation. These challenges have often resulted in uneven coverage, leaving some communities with insufficient protection against polio.

What happens next

The campaign will be monitored closely by provincial health officials and international partners. Data on vaccination rates will be published weekly to track progress and identify districts that require additional support. If coverage targets are met, the government plans to expand the initiative to other provinces that have reported low immunisation rates.

Health experts warn that sustaining high coverage will require continued community engagement and addressing vaccine hesitancy. The authorities have announced plans to conduct educational outreach, using local media and community leaders to spread accurate information about the safety and importance of polio vaccines.

Summary

• Balochistan launches a 6‑week polio vaccination drive targeting 1.2 million children. • The campaign is a response to fears of virus spread from Afghanistan and low coverage in the province. • Health officials will monitor progress and impose penalties on teams that fail to meet targets.
