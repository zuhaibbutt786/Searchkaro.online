Fatima Sana to captain Pakistan in West Indies series

Fatima Sana has been named captain for Pakistan's women's cricket tour of the West Indies. The squad also sees the recall of fast bowler Tuba Hassan for the ODI series.

Latest developments

The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) confirmed on Monday that all-rounder Fatima Sana will lead the national women's side on its forthcoming tour of the West Indies. The announcement came alongside the full squad list, which features a blend of experienced players and emerging talent. Among the notable inclusions is fast bowler Tuba Hassan, who has been recalled to the One Day International (ODI) squad after a brief hiatus from international duty. The tour is scheduled to begin in early December and will comprise three ODIs followed by a three‑match Twenty20 series. PCB officials highlighted the importance of the tour for building team cohesion ahead of the next ICC women's events. The board also noted that the selected squad reflects the depth of talent currently available in Pakistan's women's cricket pipeline, aiming to provide competitive exposure against a strong West Indian side.

Why it matters

The appointment of Fatima Sana as captain underscores the PCB's commitment to nurturing leadership within the women's game. Sana, who previously captained the side in limited-overs formats, brings a wealth of experience and a calm on‑field presence that many analysts believe will benefit a relatively young squad. The recall of Tuba Hassan adds pace to the bowling attack, addressing a key area identified by coaches after the last series. For Pakistani fans, the West Indies tour offers a chance to see the team compete in varied conditions, which is vital for performance improvement. Moreover, the series is expected to draw media attention and sponsor interest, potentially boosting funding and resources for women's cricket development programs across the country.

Background

Pakistan's women's cricket team has been on an upward trajectory since gaining full ICC member status in 1998. Over the past few years, the side has recorded historic wins against higher‑ranked opponents, signalling progress in skill and confidence. Fatima Sana emerged as a leading all‑rounder, contributing both with bat and ball, and earned her first captaincy stint during a home series earlier this year. Tuba Hassan, a right‑arm fast bowler, made her debut in 2022 and quickly impressed with her speed and accuracy, though she missed the last few matches due to injury. The West Indies, a traditional rival in women's cricket, have also been strengthening their squad, making the upcoming series a competitive test for both teams. Historically, tours between Pakistan and the West Indies have provided valuable experience for players adapting to different pitches and climates.

What happens next

The Pakistani team will depart for the Caribbean in late November, with a pre‑tour camp scheduled at the National Cricket Academy in Lahore. Training sessions will focus on adapting to the slower, spin‑friendly tracks typical of the West Indies. The first ODI is set for December 2, followed by two more matches on December 5 and 8. After the ODIs, the T20 series will commence on December 12. Performance in these games will influence selection decisions for the upcoming ICC Women's World Cup qualifiers, making each match crucial for players aiming to cement their places.
