ECINet Review Sparks Scrutiny Over Voter Deletion Requests in Maharashtra

A review of ECINet has uncovered over 25,000 voter deletion requests in Maharashtra, raising concerns about the integrity of the electoral roll. Activists demand greater scrutiny and a cap on Form‑7 applications to safeguard voter rights.

Latest developments

Recent scrutiny of ECINet, the electronic voter registration platform used across India, has revealed that 25,661 voters in Maharashtra have requested deletion from the electoral roll. The figure emerged after activists examined the database and highlighted the number of requests that have been logged but not yet processed. The Election Commission has yet to publish a definitive response, but the volume of requests has prompted calls for a transparent audit of the deletion process. The controversy follows earlier reports of irregularities in voter rolls and has reignited debate over the integrity of the electoral system.

In response to concerns about the accuracy of voter lists, the Election Commission has intensified home‑visit campaigns targeting flagged voters. The campaign, launched after the 2020 Supreme Court directive, aims to verify voter details and ensure that only eligible citizens remain on the roll. Recent reports indicate that the Commission has also introduced changes to the Standard Identification Record (SIR) protocol, tightening the criteria for accepting new entries. These measures are part of a broader effort to curb manipulation of the voter database, a problem that has been cited in several state‑level investigations.

The surge in voter deletion requests is mirrored by a parallel rise in Form‑7 applications, the official form used to request removal from the electoral roll. Telangana alone has submitted 5.43 lakh Form‑7s in the last fiscal year, according to a state‑level report. The high volume has strained the Commission’s processing capacity and raised questions about the criteria used to approve deletions. Analysts suggest that a cap on Form‑7 submissions could help streamline the process and reduce the likelihood of erroneous deletions.

Why it matters

For voters, the unchecked deletion process threatens to disenfranchise eligible citizens who may be mistakenly removed from the roll. In a country where voter turnout can determine the outcome of elections, even a small percentage of lost voters can influence results in marginal constituencies. The lack of a clear audit trail also erodes public confidence in the electoral system, potentially discouraging participation in future polls.

The controversy arrives just months before the next general election, when political parties are finalising candidate lists and campaigning strategies. If the Commission does not address the backlog, the risk of contested rolls could lead to legal challenges and delays in vote counting. Moreover, the perception of a flawed voter database may fuel political narratives that question the legitimacy of the electoral process.

Background

ECINet is an online portal that consolidates voter registration data from state electoral offices into a single database. The system was introduced to streamline the management of electoral rolls and to provide a uniform platform for submitting Form‑7 requests. While it has improved accessibility for citizens, critics argue that the centralised nature of the database makes it vulnerable to systemic errors and data manipulation.

Earlier this year, the Supreme Court directed the Election Commission to conduct a comprehensive audit of voter rolls, citing concerns over irregularities in the deletion process. The audit revealed that many deletion requests had been accepted without sufficient verification, prompting calls for stricter oversight. In response, the Commission introduced a cap on the number of Form‑7 applications that can be processed per month, a measure that has been met with mixed reactions from civil society groups.

What happens next

The Election Commission is expected to publish a detailed report on the status of deletion requests within the next fortnight. It may also revise the criteria for approving Form‑7 applications to include mandatory cross‑verification with local polling units. If the audit identifies systemic flaws, the Commission could recommend legislative amendments to strengthen the oversight of voter rolls.

Should the audit findings lead to a backlog of pending deletions, affected voters may seek judicial review, potentially delaying the finalisation of the electoral roll ahead of the general election. Political parties will likely monitor the Commission’s actions closely, as any perceived bias could influence voter sentiment in key battleground states.

Summary
