Sri Lanka clinches series win over Pakistan in U19 women's T20 showdown

Sri Lanka edged Pakistan by six wickets in the U19 women's T20 tri‑series, earning a spot in the final. The win adds momentum to Sri Lanka's campaign ahead of the showdown with Bangladesh.

Latest developments

The Under‑19 women's teams of Sri Lanka and Pakistan met in the final round of the three‑nation T20 tri‑series hosted in Colombo. Sri Lanka chased down the target set by Pakistan with six wickets in hand, sealing a 6‑wicket victory that confirmed their place in the tournament final. The match was highlighted by a composed innings from Sri Lanka's top order, with Sanjana Kumar anchoring the chase. She guided the side past the required total after Pakistan posted a competitive score, showcasing both skill and temperament under pressure. The win also meant that Sri Lanka would face Bangladesh in the final, a match that will determine the overall champion of the tri‑series. Pakistan, despite the loss, displayed promising talent, with several batters contributing valuable runs, but fell short as Sri Lanka's bowlers restricted the scoring in the middle overs. The series, which also featured Bangladesh, has been a platform for emerging female cricketers from the sub‑continent to gain international exposure.

Why it matters

For cricket fans in Pakistan, the result is a reminder of the growing gap in women's cricket development between the two nations. While Pakistan's senior women's side has made strides in recent years, the U19 performance underscores the need for deeper investment in grassroots programmes, coaching, and competitive opportunities. The victory also fuels Sri Lanka's confidence ahead of the final, positioning them as a serious contender for the title and potentially boosting interest in women's cricket back home. In Pakistan, the loss may prompt the Pakistan Cricket Board to reassess its talent pipeline, ensuring that promising players receive the support required to compete at the highest level. Moreover, the series has attracted considerable viewership across South Asia, highlighting the commercial potential of women's cricket and encouraging sponsors to consider greater involvement.

Background

Pakistan and Sri Lanka have a long‑standing rivalry in cricket, dating back to the early days of the sport in the region. In the senior men's format, both sides have exchanged series wins, with memorable contests in World Cups and bilateral tours. The women's game, however, is still in a developmental phase. Sri Lanka's women's team qualified for the ICC Women's T20 World Cup in 2023, while Pakistan's women have struggled to make a similar impact. The introduction of the U19 women's tri‑series in 2026 marks a concerted effort by the Asian Cricket Council to nurture young talent and provide a structured pathway to senior international cricket. Previous encounters at the junior level have been limited, making this series a landmark event for both boards.

What happens next

Sri Lanka will face Bangladesh in the final of the tri‑series later this week. A win would secure the tournament trophy and further cement Sri Lanka's rising status in women's cricket. Pakistan, meanwhile, will regroup and focus on upcoming regional qualifiers for the ICC U19 Women's World Cup, aiming to translate the experience gained into better performances. Both boards are expected to announce squad selections and training camps in the coming days, with an eye on strengthening their pipelines ahead of the senior international calendar.
