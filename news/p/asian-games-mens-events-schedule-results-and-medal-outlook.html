Asian Games Men’s Events: Schedule, Results and Medal Outlook

The 2026 Asian Games are underway with men's events drawing strong interest across South Asia. This explainer covers the current schedule, recent results and what to watch next.

What is happening

The 2026 Asian Games, hosted in Japan, have entered the second week of competition, and men’s events across a range of sports are in full swing. Cricket matches are being played according to a pre‑published schedule, with points tables updated after each round. In addition to cricket, athletics, swimming, and martial arts are delivering daily medal ceremonies that are closely followed by fans in Pakistan and beyond.

In cricket, the men’s tournament features eight national teams divided into two groups. Group A includes India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, while Group B comprises Japan, Malaysia, Hong Kong and the United Arab Emirates. The group stage matches have produced a mixture of high‑scoring games and tightly contested low‑run affairs, leading to a clear set of qualifiers for the knockout rounds.

Parallel to cricket, the men’s track and field events have already seen several Asian records challenged. The 100‑metre dash, long jump and discus throw have produced podium finishes that are likely to influence the overall medal tally for the participating nations. Swimming events have similarly generated excitement, with multiple finals scheduled each evening.

The medal table after the first ten days shows China maintaining a lead, while Japan and South Korea are close behind. Pakistan’s men’s cricket team currently sits in the top two of Group A, positioning them for a quarter‑final appearance. The ongoing results are being compiled into live dashboards that fans can access through official apps and partner websites.

Background and context

The Asian Games, organized by the Olympic Council of Asia, are held every four years and feature athletes from 45 member nations. The 2026 edition marks the first time Japan has hosted the Games since 1994, and the event includes a record number of sports, with cricket making its return after a long hiatus.

Men’s cricket was reintroduced following a successful pilot at the 2022 Asian Games in Hangzhou. The format follows the Twenty20 style, designed to fit within the multi‑sport schedule while delivering fast‑paced action. The tournament’s structure mirrors that of other international cricket events, with group stages leading to knockout rounds.

Beyond cricket, the men’s program encompasses traditional Asian sports such as kabaddi and sepaktakraw, alongside globally recognized disciplines like athletics and swimming. The Games serve as a qualifying pathway for several sports to the 2028 Olympic Games, adding an extra layer of importance for athletes and national federations.

What to know next

The next few days will feature the quarter‑finals for men’s cricket, followed by semi‑finals and the final match. Fans should watch the schedule for match times, as the knockout games are set to be broadcast live on regional sports networks. In athletics, the men’s 400‑metre hurdles and the 4 × 100 metre relay finals are slated for the weekend, offering additional medal opportunities.

Looking ahead to the closing ceremony, the overall medal standings will be finalized, and the performance of men’s teams will be a key narrative in post‑Games analysis. Observers will also monitor how the results impact qualification for upcoming world championships and the 2028 Olympics, especially in sports where Asian athletes have strong representation.
