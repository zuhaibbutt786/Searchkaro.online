Shadman Islam stretchered off with ankle injury in Bangladesh's Test

Bangladesh's opening batsman Shadman Islam was taken to the hospital after twisting his ankle during the second Test against West Indies. His injury could force a reshuffle in the team's top order.

Latest developments

Shadman Islam, Bangladesh's left‑handed opening batsman, was forced to leave the field in the second Test against West Indies after he twisted his ankle while attempting a quick single. The injury occurred midway through Bangladesh's first innings, prompting the on‑field umpires to call for medical assistance. He was helped onto a stretcher and escorted off the ground, with the match officials pausing play briefly while the medical team assessed the situation.

Medical staff examined Islam on the field and decided to transport him to a nearby hospital for further evaluation. Team doctors reported that the ankle appeared to be badly sprained, and they warned that he would be unlikely to bat in the remainder of the innings. Bangladesh's captain, after consulting with the coaching staff, announced that the team would continue with a revised batting order, promoting the next‑in‑line opener to face the West Indian bowlers.

Bangladesh's innings, already under pressure from a disciplined West Indian attack, lost momentum as the team adjusted to the sudden change. At the time of Islam's departure, Bangladesh had lost three wickets for 45 runs, and the loss of a key opener added to the uncertainty about reaching a competitive total. The West Indies bowlers, sensing an opportunity, intensified their line‑up, forcing Bangladesh to adopt a more defensive approach.

Team management issued a brief statement confirming that Islam had been taken to the hospital and would undergo scans to determine the extent of the injury. They emphasized that the player's health was the priority and that a decision on his participation in the remainder of the match would be made after medical clearance. The coaching staff also hinted at possible adjustments for the upcoming third Test, should Islam be sidelined for an extended period.
