India Rejects OIC Remarks on Jammu and Kashmir, Accuses Pakistan of Misuse

New Delhi dismissed a resolution from an Islamic organization on Jammu and Kashmir and warned that the OIC is being used by Pakistan for political purposes. The move highlights rising diplomatic tension between India and the Muslim bloc.

Latest developments

New Delhi’s Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) issued a formal response on Monday, rejecting a resolution presented by an Islamic group that called for international intervention in Jammu and Kashmir. The MEA described the document as "motivated propaganda" and said it ignored the constitutional status of the region after the abrogation of Article 370 in 2019. In the same statement, the ministry accused the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) of echoing "malicious" remarks that portray India in a negative light. The MEA warned that such statements could damage the credibility of the OIC and called for a factual, balanced approach to any discussion on the disputed territory.

The Indian government also highlighted that the OIC’s recent statements appear to be aligned with Pakistan’s diplomatic strategy. Officials in the MEA pointed to a pattern of coordinated messaging that seeks to internationalise the Kashmir issue, a narrative that India says is driven by Islamabad rather than genuine concern for the people of the region. The ministry noted that the OIC’s references to "anti‑India" sentiments were not based on any concrete evidence and were instead part of a broader campaign to undermine India’s sovereignty.

In response, the OIC has not issued an immediate rebuttal, but its spokesperson previously emphasized the organization’s commitment to supporting Muslim communities worldwide, including those in Jammu and Kashmir. The spokesperson’s earlier comments were interpreted by Indian officials as an attempt to politicise the issue and to pressure India through multilateral forums. The Indian side reiterated that any resolution or statement that questions the legal status of the region will be rejected as interference in internal affairs.

Analysts say the exchange reflects a deepening rift between New Delhi and the OIC, with Pakistan playing a pivotal role in shaping the narrative. The Pakistani government has repeatedly raised the Kashmir question at the United Nations and within the OIC, urging member states to take a stand against what it calls "human rights violations" in the region. India, for its part, maintains that the situation in Jammu and Kashmir is stable and that its development initiatives are improving the lives of residents.

Why it matters

The diplomatic clash has immediate implications for India’s foreign relations, especially with countries that are members of the OIC. A strained relationship could affect trade, security cooperation, and India's broader strategic outreach to the Muslim world. For Indian citizens, the government’s firm stance signals a commitment to protecting national sovereignty and countering narratives that it deems unfounded.

Domestically, the issue resonates with voters who view the Kashmir decision as a matter of national pride. The government's rejection of external criticism reinforces a narrative of self‑reliance and resilience, which may influence public opinion ahead of upcoming state elections in several northern states. Moreover, the controversy could shape media coverage, with Indian outlets likely to emphasize the alleged misuse of the OIC by Pakistan, while international media may focus on the human‑rights dimension of the Kashmir debate.

For Pakistan, the episode underscores its reliance on multilateral platforms to keep the Kashmir issue alive. Any perceived loss of credibility within the OIC could weaken Islamabad’s diplomatic leverage, prompting it to seek alternative avenues to raise the dispute, such as the United Nations or bilateral talks with other regional powers.

Background

Jammu and Kashmir, a mountainous region in the northern part of the Indian subcontinent, has been a flashpoint between India and Pakistan since both countries gained independence in 1947. In August 2019, the Indian government revoked the region's special autonomous status, integrating it fully into the Indian Union. This move was met with international attention and criticism from several Muslim-majority countries, many of which are members of the OIC.

The OIC, founded in 1969, is a collective of 57 member states that aims to safeguard the interests of the Muslim world. Over the years, the organization has issued statements on various conflicts involving Muslim populations, including the Kashmir dispute. Pakistan, a founding member, has consistently used the OIC platform to highlight alleged human‑rights concerns in Jammu and Kashmir, seeking broader diplomatic support.

The recent resolution from an Islamic group, which was not directly affiliated with the OIC but was presented within its forums, called for an international mechanism to monitor the situation in the region. India’s rejection of the resolution follows a pattern of dismissing external interventions that it perceives as infringing on its internal matters.

What happens next

The MEA is expected to engage with OIC member states individually to clarify India’s position and to counter what it describes as misinformation. Diplomatic channels may see a series of bilateral meetings aimed at preventing further escalation. Meanwhile, Pakistan is likely to continue raising the Kashmir issue in OIC assemblies and other international forums, seeking to sustain global attention.

Observers anticipate that the dispute will remain a recurring theme in Indo‑Pakistani relations, with periodic flare‑ups in diplomatic rhetoric. The outcome will depend on how both sides manage the narrative within multilateral bodies and whether any concrete steps are taken to address the underlying concerns of the Kashmiri population.
