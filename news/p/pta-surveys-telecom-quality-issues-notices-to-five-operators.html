PTA surveys telecom quality, issues notices to five operators

The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority has completed a city‑wide quality survey and identified gaps in service delivery. Five operators have now received formal notices to explain poor internet performance.

Latest developments

On 5 November 2026 the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) carried out a comprehensive Quality of Experience (QoE) survey across the twin cities of Islamabad and Rawalpindi. Field teams measured voice clarity, call drop rates, data speed, latency and overall user satisfaction for the major mobile network operators. The survey used a combination of drive tests, stationary probes and crowdsourced feedback from subscribers who volunteered to participate through a PTA‑hosted portal.

The results released on 6 November showed that Jazz topped the rankings for both voice and data performance. Jazz recorded the lowest call drop rate at 1.2 per 1,000 calls and delivered an average download speed of 28.5 megabits per second, outperforming its rivals by a noticeable margin. The second‑place operator posted a drop rate of 2.4 and an average speed of 22.1 megabits per second, while the remaining operators fell below the benchmark set by the regulator.

In a separate enforcement action, PTA issued show‑cause notices to five telecom operators on 7 November, citing persistent complaints about slow broadband, frequent outages and failure to meet the minimum service standards established in the 2023 telecom quality framework. The notice requires each operator to submit a detailed corrective plan within 15 days, outlining steps to improve network capacity, upgrade infrastructure and enhance customer support.

The affected operators have not publicly commented on the notices, but PTA officials indicated that the regulator will monitor compliance closely. Failure to provide an acceptable response or to achieve measurable improvements could trigger penalties ranging from fines to temporary suspension of licences, according to the authority’s enforcement guidelines.

Why it matters

Internet and mobile connectivity are essential for daily life, commerce and education in Pakistan. Persistent service problems increase costs for businesses that rely on reliable broadband, and they limit access to digital services for households in urban and peri‑urban areas. By publicly benchmarking network performance, PTA creates market pressure for operators to invest in upgrades and to address consumer grievances more swiftly.

The show‑cause notices signal a shift toward stricter regulatory oversight. Operators that ignore quality standards risk losing market share as consumers gravitate toward providers that can demonstrate better performance. For the average user, the enforcement actions could translate into faster download speeds, fewer dropped calls and more responsive customer service within the next few months.

Background

The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority is the statutory body responsible for licensing, regulating and monitoring the country's telecom sector. Established in 1996, PTA has periodically conducted QoE surveys to gauge network health and to inform policy decisions. The 2023 framework introduced stricter minimum thresholds for call reliability and broadband speed, reflecting the growing demand for data‑intensive applications.

Pakistan's telecom market is dominated by a handful of large operators, with Jazz, Telenor, Zong, Ufone and a newer entrant sharing the majority of subscribers. Competition has driven rapid mobile penetration, but infrastructure gaps remain, especially in high‑density urban zones where network congestion can degrade user experience. Previous PTA surveys have highlighted similar issues, prompting incremental improvements but also exposing the need for more decisive enforcement.

What happens next

Each of the five operators must file a corrective action plan with PTA by mid‑December 2026. The regulator will review the submissions and may order targeted upgrades, such as expanding fiber backhaul or deploying additional cell sites in congested districts. A follow‑up audit is scheduled for early 2027 to verify compliance. If operators fail to meet the stipulated improvements, PTA may impose financial penalties or, in extreme cases, suspend specific service licences.

Consumers are encouraged to report ongoing issues through PTA’s online grievance portal, which will feed into the next round of quality monitoring. The authority has pledged to publish a comparative performance report after the audit, providing transparent data for the public and for industry analysts.
