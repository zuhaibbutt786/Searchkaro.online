NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang Resigns Amid Second Affair Allegation

NVIDIA's founder and chief executive Jensen Huang has resigned following a second affair allegation. The company has announced a leadership transition that will see an interim CEO take charge while a search for a permanent replacement begin

Latest developments

On November 12, 2026, NVIDIA announced that its co‑founder and long‑time chief executive Jensen Huang would step down from the board. The announcement came after a second allegation of a personal relationship with an executive who reports to him, a claim that the company said had been investigated internally. Huang, who has led the firm since its inception in 1993, is widely credited with turning NVIDIA into a dominant player in graphics processing units and, more recently, artificial intelligence hardware. The company said that the decision was made after a thorough review of the allegations and that it was in the best interests of the organization and its shareholders. Huang will remain on the board in an advisory capacity until the end of the year, according to the statement. The board named former senior executive Lisa Su as the interim chief executive officer, a move that was approved by a majority of the board members. NVIDIA’s stock fell by 3.2 percent in early trading following the announcement, reflecting investor uncertainty about the company’s leadership continuity. Analysts noted that the company’s AI chip business, which has seen explosive growth, could face short‑term disruptions as the transition takes place. The company also stated that it would continue to pursue its strategy of expanding its data‑center and automotive divisions. The resignation follows a pattern of leadership changes in the tech industry, where personal conduct has increasingly become a factor in executive accountability. NVIDIA’s board emphasized that the company’s culture of transparency and accountability would remain unchanged. The company also announced that it would conduct a formal search for a permanent replacement, with a timeline expected to be completed within the next six months.

Why it matters

For Pakistan, NVIDIA’s AI chips are a critical component of the country’s growing technology ecosystem. The firm supplies processors that power cloud services, machine‑learning research and autonomous vehicle projects that many Pakistani startups and universities rely on. A leadership shift could influence the company’s supply chain decisions and the pricing of its products, which in turn may affect the affordability of AI tools for local developers. The stock market reaction to the resignation also has implications for investors in Pakistan who hold NVIDIA shares through international funds. A decline in the company’s valuation could ripple through technology indices that Pakistani investors track, potentially prompting a reassessment of exposure to high‑growth semiconductor firms. Moreover, NVIDIA’s influence on global AI research feeds into academic collaborations that Pakistani scholars participate in. Any change in the company’s strategic priorities could alter the availability of research grants or partnerships that support local innovation initiatives.

Background

Jensen Huang co‑founded NVIDIA in 1993 with the aim of delivering high‑performance graphics solutions for gaming and professional markets. Under his leadership, the company introduced the GeForce line of GPUs, which became the industry standard for real‑time rendering. Huang’s vision extended beyond gaming, positioning NVIDIA at the forefront of AI computing with the introduction of the CUDA platform and the DGX series of AI supercomputers. The company’s market capitalization surpassed $700 billion in 2024, making it one of the most valuable semiconductor firms globally. Huang’s reputation for innovation earned him recognition as a leading figure in the technology sector, and he has been a frequent speaker at industry conferences. His departure marks the end of an era for NVIDIA, as the company has built its brand around his entrepreneurial spirit. NVIDIA’s recent focus on data‑center and automotive applications has positioned it as a key player in the broader AI ecosystem. The firm’s chips are used in everything from cloud‑based language models to autonomous driving systems, underscoring the strategic importance of its leadership in shaping the direction of AI research and deployment.

What happens next

Lisa Su, who previously served as senior vice president of product development, will lead the company as interim CEO until a permanent replacement is found. The board has indicated that the search will involve a global shortlist of candidates with experience in scaling technology companies and a strong track record in AI. The company will maintain its current strategic initiatives while the transition takes place. Investors are watching closely for signals about the company’s future direction, particularly its plans to expand into new markets such as edge computing and quantum‑inspired processors. The board has assured stakeholders that NVIDIA’s commitment to innovation and customer support will remain unchanged during the interim period.
