Egypt Revokes Citizenship of Two Expatriate Brothers Amid Dual Nationality Controversy

Egypt has revoked the citizenship of brothers Tariq Ibrahim and Anas Ibrahim, citing dual nationality. The decision follows a broader crackdown on expatriates holding foreign passports.

Latest developments

On 12 November 2026, the Egyptian Ministry of Interior announced revocation of citizenship of brothers Tariq Ibrahim and Anas Ibrahim, residents of the Netherlands, citing dual nationality. The decision follows a policy that targets expatriates who hold foreign passports, a measure that has been applied to several activists in recent years. The brothers, who have been involved in political commentary from abroad, had previously been listed as Egyptian citizens with dual Dutch nationality. The Ministry stated that the revocation was carried out in accordance with Article 25 of the Egyptian Nationality Law, which allows removal of citizenship for dual nationals. The announcement was made public through a press release and covered by local media outlets, including Al Arabiya and Okaz, which reported on the legal basis and the brothers’ prior residence in the Netherlands.

Why it matters

For readers in Saudi Arabia, the revocation highlights the growing scrutiny of dual nationality among expatriates in the Middle East, raising concerns about legal protection and consular support. The move may affect other Egyptian expatriates who hold foreign passports, prompting them to reassess their legal status and consider the risks of political engagement abroad. Diplomatic relations between Egypt and the Netherlands could be strained, as the Dutch government may view the action as a violation of the rights of its residents, potentially leading to calls for diplomatic dialogue. The case also feeds into broader discussions about the rights of citizens living abroad and the extent to which home governments can enforce nationality laws on expatriates.

Background

Egypt’s Nationality Law, amended in 2014, allows for revocation of citizenship if an individual holds dual nationality, a provision that has been invoked against activists and dissidents. In recent years, the Egyptian government has revoked citizenship of several expatriates, including journalists and political commentators, citing dual nationality as the legal basis. The brothers, Tariq and Anas Ibrahim, have been active on social media and in diaspora networks, often criticizing government policies, which may have contributed to the decision. The revocation process typically involves a court order and a formal notification to the individual, though the brothers reportedly received no such notice before the public announcement. The case reflects a broader trend in the region where governments use nationality laws to control dissent among citizens living abroad.

What happens next

The brothers have not yet filed a legal challenge, but they may seek assistance from Dutch authorities or international human rights bodies to contest the revocation. Egyptian officials have stated that the decision is final, and no appeals will be entertained, according to the Ministry’s statement. The move could prompt diplomatic protests from the Netherlands, potentially leading to a formal complaint lodged with the Egyptian foreign ministry.
