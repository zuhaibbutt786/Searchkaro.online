Witcher 3 Remaster gets patch, free Switch copies as AMD updates drivers

CD Projekt Red released a patch that aligns console versions of The Witcher 3 Remaster with the PC build. A separate loophole lets two Switch players claim a free copy, while AMD rolls out a new driver to boost performance.

Latest developments

CD Projekt Red announced the first official patch for The Witcher 3 Remaster on consoles, aiming to bring the gameplay experience closer to the PC version. The update addresses texture scaling, lighting inconsistencies, and several stability issues that were reported after the November launch. According to iXBT.games, the patch also synchronises frame‑rate caps and adds support for higher resolution modes on PlayStation and Xbox platforms, narrowing the gap that many players noticed between the console and PC builds.

In a separate development, Polygon reported that two Nintendo Switch owners can obtain a free copy of The Witcher 3 Remaster by exploiting a promotional loophole. The method involves linking two separate Nintendo accounts, each of which receives a digital voucher that can be redeemed for the game at no cost. The article explains that the loophole stems from a misconfiguration in the regional pricing system, allowing the free distribution of the title while the publisher monitors the situation.

Meanwhile, AMD released Radeon Software version 26.9.2, which includes driver optimisations for the latest titles, including The Witcher 3 Remaster. Overclock3D noted that the new driver improves Vulkan and DirectX 12 performance on Radeon GPUs, offering smoother frame rates and reduced latency. The update is positioned as part of AMD's ongoing effort to ensure that PC gamers in Pakistan and elsewhere can enjoy the remastered title without compromising visual fidelity.

Why it matters

For gamers in Pakistan, the patch represents a tangible improvement in the value of recent console purchases. Many players reported that the original remaster suffered from lower texture quality and occasional crashes, which limited the appeal of the $39 price point. By aligning console performance with the PC version, CDPR helps protect consumer confidence and encourages further investment in the platform.

The free Switch copy loophole could have a notable impact on the local market, where the Nintendo Switch remains a popular but relatively expensive device. If the loophole spreads through online communities, it may increase the number of players experiencing the game, potentially boosting word‑of‑mouth promotion. At the same time, AMD's driver update is relevant for the growing PC gaming segment in Pakistan, where Radeon GPUs are widely used. Better optimisation means that players with mid‑range hardware can enjoy higher frame rates, making the remaster more accessible across a broader range of systems.

Background

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt originally launched in 2015 and quickly became a benchmark for open‑world design and narrative depth. CD Projekt Red announced a remastered edition in early 2024, promising enhanced graphics, faster load times, and support for newer console hardware. The remaster was released on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, and later on Nintendo Switch, with a PC version arriving via the Epic Games Store.

Despite the hype, early reviews highlighted disparities between the console and PC experiences. Players on older console generations reported lower texture resolutions and occasional frame‑rate drops, while PC users benefited from higher settings and more frequent updates. CDPR pledged to address these gaps, leading to the October patch that targets the most critical performance gaps on PlayStation and Xbox platforms.

What happens next

Industry observers expect CD Projekt Red to continue refining the console builds, with additional patches likely to focus on quality‑of‑life features such as UI scaling and optional performance modes. AMD has indicated that future driver releases will incorporate further tweaks for Vulkan‑based titles, suggesting that performance gains for The Witcher 3 Remaster may continue throughout the year. Meanwhile, Nintendo may close the Switch loophole once it is fully documented, but the temporary availability of free copies could drive a short‑term surge in player numbers.
