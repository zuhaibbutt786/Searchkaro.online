Police clash with Imran Khan supporters amid protests in Khyber

Police and supporters of former Prime Minister Imran Khan clashed in the Khyber region, sparking fears of wider unrest. Authorities say the situation is under control, but tensions remain high.

Latest developments

Police on high alert in the Khyber region after a visit by the Peshawar City Counter Terrorism Police Officer (CCPO) to Bara, a town that has seen growing unrest. The officer’s presence was intended to reassure residents and prevent the escalation of protests that have been linked to supporters of former Prime Minister Imran Khan. The visit coincided with a series of demonstrations that drew thousands of people to the streets, demanding political reforms and a review of the current government’s policies.

According to a Reuters report, police clashed with Imran Khan supporters in northwestern Pakistan on 13 October, deploying tear gas rounds to disperse crowds. The clash took place near the Bara bazaar, where demonstrators had gathered to protest against alleged corruption and to call for the resignation of the current administration. The police said they had used non-lethal measures to control the situation, but the demonstrators resisted, leading to a brief confrontation.

The Express Tribune reported that local police officials claimed the situation in Khyber was under control after the dispersal of the protest. They stated that no serious injuries were reported and that the crowd had been cleared from the main thoroughfares. However, the police acknowledged that tensions remained high and that they were monitoring the area closely to prevent any further flare‑ups.

National media coverage has highlighted the growing frustration among opposition supporters, who accuse the government of suppressing dissent. Opposition leaders have called for a nationwide dialogue to address the root causes of the unrest, while security officials have warned that any escalation could threaten the stability of the region. The incident has prompted calls for a review of policing tactics in protest situations.

Why it matters

The clashes in Khyber carry significant implications for the security situation in northwestern Pakistan. The region has historically been a flashpoint for political unrest, and any escalation could disrupt local trade routes and affect the livelihoods of residents who depend on cross‑border commerce. The police response, while aimed at maintaining order, also risks alienating a segment of the population that feels marginalized by the current administration.

Politically, the protests reflect a broader dissatisfaction with the ruling party’s handling of economic challenges and governance issues. The demonstrations may influence voter sentiment ahead of the upcoming provincial elections, as opposition parties seek to capitalize on the unrest to gain support. The government’s handling of the situation will be closely scrutinized by both domestic and international observers.

If the unrest spreads to other provinces, it could create a chain reaction that would strain Pakistan’s already stretched security apparatus. The government has indicated that it will deploy additional forces to key hotspots, but the effectiveness of such measures depends on the ability to address underlying grievances. A failure to manage the situation could lead to a broader national crisis.

Background

Khyber has long been a site of political tension, dating back to the early 2000s when the region saw frequent protests against central government policies. Over the years, the area has been a focal point for opposition movements, including those led by former Prime Minister Imran Khan’s Pakistan Tehreek‑e‑Insaf (PTI). The region’s strategic location near the Afghan border adds to its importance in national security considerations.

Imran Khan’s political party has consistently positioned itself as a critic of the current administration, accusing it of corruption and mismanagement. In recent months, the PTI has organized large‑scale rallies across the country, calling for a new political order and reforms in governance. The protests in Khyber are part of a broader campaign to mobilize public support and pressure the government into action.

The police force in Khyber operates under the provincial security framework, with a mandate to maintain public order and counter insurgency threats. In recent years, the force has faced criticism for its handling of civilian protests, with calls for reform and greater accountability. The current incident has reignited debates over the balance between security and civil liberties.

What happens next

In response to the unrest, authorities have increased patrols along key routes and are monitoring social media for any signs of further mobilization. The provincial government has announced that it will hold emergency meetings with local leaders to discuss measures to restore calm and prevent future clashes.

Political parties are expected to issue statements in the coming days, with the PTI likely to call for a national dialogue and the ruling party to emphasize the need for stability. The government may also consider policy adjustments to address some of the protesters’ concerns, though the extent of such changes remains uncertain.
