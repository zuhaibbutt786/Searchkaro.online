Egyptian Dollar Rate Rises Amid Hot Money Flows, Pound Shows Signs of Recovery

The Egyptian dollar has risen again even as large volumes of speculative capital pour into the market. Meanwhile, the local pound is holding steady after a recent recovery phase.

Latest developments

Egyptian foreign‑exchange markets have seen the US dollar climb again this week, a movement reported by Al Arabiya on Friday. The rise comes despite a noticeable influx of hot money – short‑term speculative capital that typically fuels volatility. Analysts note that the surge in hot‑money flows has not translated into a further depreciation of the Egyptian pound, suggesting that market participants are absorbing the pressure without triggering a sharp sell‑off.

On Thursday, Al Yawm confirmed that the dollar‑to‑pound rate remained stable across Egyptian banks. The central bank’s daily bulletin showed the official rate unchanged at 30.90 Egyptian pounds per US dollar, matching the previous day’s level. Traders described the stability as a temporary pause after a period of rapid fluctuations.

Investing.com added that the dollar has now lost more than three Egyptian pounds from its recent peak of around 34.50 EGP. The pound, after a steep decline earlier in the year, is continuing its modest recovery, gaining roughly 0.5 % against the greenback in the last trading session. The combined data points indicate a market that is reacting to both external capital movements and domestic policy adjustments, while avoiding a full‑scale devaluation.

The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) has not announced any immediate policy shift, but it continues to intervene in the foreign‑exchange market to smooth out extreme swings. The bank’s recent purchases of foreign currency and its willingness to provide liquidity to banks have helped keep the official rate within a narrow band.

Overall, the latest figures suggest a delicate balance: the dollar is rising, hot money is present, yet the pound’s recovery persists, and the official exchange rate stays steady for the day.

Why it matters

For readers in Saudi Arabia, the movement of the Egyptian dollar influences several economic channels. First, the cost of Egyptian imports – especially agricultural products and textiles – is tied to the dollar‑pound rate. A stronger dollar raises the local price of these goods, potentially affecting Saudi retailers who source from Egypt.

Second, many Saudi expatriates work in Egypt and send remittances back home. A weaker Egyptian pound means their earnings convert into fewer Saudi riyals, reducing household purchasing power. Conversely, a stabilising pound can protect the value of these transfers.

Third, the tourism sector feels the ripple effect. Saudi tourists travelling to Egypt will find hotel and service prices measured in pounds, which are indirectly linked to the dollar. A rising dollar can make a trip more expensive, influencing travel decisions and seasonal demand.

Finally, regional investors monitor Egypt’s currency dynamics as a barometer for broader Middle‑East financial stability. Persistent hot‑money inflows may signal speculative interest that could spill over into neighboring markets, including Saudi Arabia’s own bond and equity arenas.

Background

Egypt has been grappling with foreign‑exchange volatility since the devaluation of the pound in early 2022. The government responded with a series of reforms, including the removal of subsidies, the introduction of a flexible exchange‑rate regime, and the securing of IMF-backed financing.

Hot money – typically short‑term capital seeking quick returns – surged into Egypt in 2024 as investors chased higher yields in the country’s sovereign bonds and corporate debt. While such inflows can boost liquidity, they also raise the risk of rapid outflows that could destabilise the currency.

The Central Bank of Egypt has employed a mix of direct market interventions and policy tools to manage these pressures. By selling foreign reserves and providing forward contracts to banks, the CBE aims to dampen abrupt swings and maintain confidence in the pound.

Historically, the Egyptian pound has experienced periods of sharp depreciation followed by gradual recoveries. The most recent recovery began in mid‑2025, driven by improved export performance, tourism rebounds, and tighter monetary policy that curbed inflation.

What happens next

Analysts expect the CBE to continue its cautious stance, intervening as needed to prevent the dollar from breaching the 31‑EGP threshold that could reignite inflationary pressures. Market watchers will also monitor the flow of hot money; a sudden reversal could force the bank to tighten liquidity further.

In the short term, the dollar‑pound rate is likely to hover within a narrow band, with any significant moves tied to external shocks such as changes in US interest rates or regional geopolitical developments.
