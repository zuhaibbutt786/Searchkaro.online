Fancode streams Singapore Grand Prix as Indian fans tune in

Fancode has secured rights to stream the 2026 Singapore Grand Prix for viewers in India. The platform will provide live timing, race commentary and post‑race highlights as the event unfolds at Marina Bay.

Latest developments

Fancode announced on Monday that it will broadcast the 2026 Singapore Grand Prix live across its digital platforms, targeting the growing Indian fan base for Formula 1. The service will include a live video feed, real‑time timing data and a dedicated commentary team that will follow the race from the Marina Bay Circuit. The announcement coincided with the release of the official grid, which listed the starting positions for the 2026 event. Notable entries include the defending champion on pole, a rookie driver making his debut on the second row, and a veteran returning after a season‑long injury.

The official grid released by Formula 1 shows a mix of seasoned competitors and emerging talent. The pole sitter, a multiple‑time world champion, will line up alongside his main rival from the previous season. Behind them, a rising star from the Asian racing circuit has secured a front‑row start, highlighting the increasing diversity of the sport. The grid also reflects the strategic tyre allocations and qualifying performance that teams will need to manage during the race.

During the race, Fancode will mirror the live‑blog style used by Sky Sports, delivering minute‑by‑minute updates, lap‑by‑lap commentary and instant highlights. Viewers can expect push notifications for key moments such as overtakes, safety car deployments and pit‑stop strategies. The platform will also host a post‑race analysis segment, featuring expert insights on driver performance, team tactics and the impact of the Singapore street circuit’s unique layout. In addition, Fancode will provide a replay library, allowing fans to watch the race at their convenience.

The BBC’s weather forecast for the Singapore weekend predicts a high probability of intermittent rain and temperatures around 30°C, conditions that could influence tyre choices and race pace. Organisers have confirmed that the race will start at 20:00 local time, aligning with prime viewing hours in India. Fancode’s streaming schedule reflects this timing, offering a pre‑race show that will cover driver interviews, circuit history and a technical breakdown of the cars’ aerodynamic setups for the high‑humidity environment.

Why it matters

India’s interest in Formula 1 has surged in recent years, driven by the popularity of Indian drivers in junior categories and the sport’s expanding digital presence. By providing a dedicated streaming service, Fancode taps into a market that previously relied on international broadcasters or delayed highlights. The platform’s real‑time coverage gives Indian fans the same immediacy as viewers in traditional Formula 1 markets, potentially increasing engagement and subscription revenue.

The move also highlights the broader shift in sports consumption toward over‑the‑top (OTT) platforms. As more Indian households gain high‑speed internet access, services like Fancode are positioned to compete with legacy broadcasters. Offering exclusive Formula 1 content strengthens Fancode’s portfolio, which already includes cricket, football and mixed martial arts, and may encourage advertisers to target a younger, tech‑savvy audience during high‑profile events such as the Singapore Grand Prix.

Background

Fancode launched in 2019 as a sports‑focused streaming service, initially concentrating on cricket and domestic leagues. Over the past three years, the platform expanded its rights portfolio to include international football, tennis and combat sports, positioning itself as a one‑stop destination for Indian sports enthusiasts. In early 2025, Fancode signed a multi‑year agreement with Formula 1 to stream selected races, qualifying sessions and exclusive behind‑the‑scenes content, marking its first foray into motorsport.

The Singapore Grand Prix, held on the Marina Bay street circuit since 2008, is a marquee event in the Formula 1 calendar. Known for its night race format, the event combines high‑speed straights with tight corners, testing drivers’ skill and teams’ aerodynamic setups. The 2026 edition is the 17th race of the season and serves as a pivotal point for championship contenders, as points earned here can shape the title fight heading into the final rounds.

What happens next

Following the Singapore round, the Formula 1 calendar moves to the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka, where Fancode will continue its live coverage. The platform has hinted at upcoming interactive features, such as fan polls during pit‑stop windows and augmented‑reality replays, aimed at deepening viewer involvement. Subscribers can also look forward to a season‑long leaderboard that tracks engagement across all streamed races.
