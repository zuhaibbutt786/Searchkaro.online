Cyclone Akvan Threatens West Bengal with Heavy Rain, Forecasts Stormy Tomorrow

Cyclone Akvan is expected to hit West Bengal tomorrow, bringing heavy rain and strong winds. Residents in Kolkata and nearby districts are advised to stay indoors and monitor official updates.

Latest developments

Cyclone Akvan, a low‑pressure system that has already caused severe weather in parts of the Bay of Bengal, has altered its trajectory toward the coast of West Bengal. Meteorological reports indicate that the storm’s path now points toward the Puri region, with the potential to affect the entire southern belt of the state.

The Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a Category‑2 cyclone warning for the coastal districts of West Bengal. According to the latest radar sweeps, the system is expected to bring sustained winds of up to 90 km/h and gusts exceeding 110 km/h, accompanied by heavy rainfall.

Kolkata, the capital city, is poised to experience a wet, stormy day tomorrow. Forecast models predict rainfall totals ranging from 80 to 120 mm across the metropolitan area, with the possibility of localized flooding in low‑lying neighborhoods.

Local authorities have urged residents to secure loose outdoor items, close windows, and avoid unnecessary travel. The West Bengal state government has activated its emergency response plan and is coordinating with the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) to monitor the situation closely.

Why it matters

The impending weather conditions pose significant risks to daily life and infrastructure in West Bengal. Heavy rainfall can lead to flash floods, especially in the densely populated areas of Kolkata and the adjacent districts of Howrah and North 24 Parganas.

Strong winds threaten to damage buildings, power lines, and transportation routes. The risk of power outages could affect hospitals, schools, and businesses, potentially disrupting essential services.

For the agricultural sector, sudden rainfall can damage crops, particularly rice and maize, which are cultivated in the fertile plains of the state. Farmers are advised to secure their fields and livestock to minimize losses.

The public health sector must prepare for increased cases of waterborne diseases and ensure that emergency medical facilities are ready to handle any surge in patients due to weather‑related injuries.

Background

Cyclone Akvan entered the Bay of Bengal in early November, initially tracking toward the eastern coast of India. The system intensified as it moved over warm sea surface temperatures, drawing attention from meteorologists across the region.

Historically, cyclonic activity in the Bay of Bengal has caused significant damage in West Bengal, with the 1999 Odisha cyclone and the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami leaving lasting impacts. The current storm is being monitored closely because of its potential to cause both wind damage and heavy rainfall.

The Indian Meteorological Department has a long history of issuing timely warnings for cyclonic events. Their forecasting models incorporate satellite imagery, sea‑surface temperature data, and atmospheric pressure readings to predict storm paths and intensities.

Residents of West Bengal have experienced cyclonic weather in previous years, leading to the establishment of robust disaster management protocols at both state and local levels.

What happens next

As the storm approaches, the IMD will continue to update its forecasts every six hours, providing real‑time information on wind speeds, rainfall totals, and the cyclone’s projected path.

State authorities will activate emergency shelters in vulnerable districts. The National Disaster Response Force is on standby to deploy rapid response teams if flooding or structural damage occurs.

The next day, officials will assess the damage and begin recovery operations, focusing on restoring power, clearing debris, and providing medical assistance to affected communities.

Residents are encouraged to keep emergency kits ready, stay informed through local radio and television broadcasts, and follow any instructions issued by the authorities.
