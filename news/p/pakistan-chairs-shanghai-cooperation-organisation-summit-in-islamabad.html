Pakistan Chairs Shanghai Cooperation Organisation Summit in Islamabad

Pakistan will host SCO leaders from September 28 to 30 under its presidency, aiming to boost trade with Central Asian partners. The summit, highlighted by Dawn, marks a key step in Islamabad's diversification strategy.

Latest developments

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) is set to convene in Islamabad from September 28 to 30, with Pakistan serving as the chair nation for the summit. According to a report by Dawn, senior officials from the 14 member states will gather to discuss a range of issues, including security cooperation, economic integration, and cultural exchange. The agenda places particular emphasis on expanding trade links with Central Asian economies, a priority highlighted in recent statements from Pakistan's Ministry of Commerce.

During a pre‑summit briefing, Pakistani officials outlined a series of initiatives designed to lower trade barriers, streamline customs procedures, and promote joint investment projects in sectors such as energy, agriculture, and transport infrastructure. Delegates are expected to sign memoranda of understanding that will pave the way for increased market access and joint ventures.

The meetings will also address regional security challenges, with member states reviewing collaborative mechanisms to combat terrorism and illicit trafficking. The host country has pledged to provide logistical support and to facilitate high‑level dialogues that could lead to a more coordinated approach across the SCO's diverse membership.

In parallel, the Arab News outlet reported that Pakistan’s broader economic strategy includes diversifying its trade portfolio beyond traditional partners, seeking new opportunities in Central Asia as part of a long‑term diversification push. The SCO summit is therefore being framed as a diplomatic platform to accelerate these objectives.

Why it matters

For Pakistani businesses, the summit represents a rare opportunity to access markets that have historically been under‑explored due to logistical and regulatory hurdles. By leveraging the SCO framework, Pakistan hopes to attract investment that can boost its export capacity and reduce reliance on a narrow set of trading partners.

The focus on Central Asian trade is especially significant given the region’s abundant natural resources and growing demand for Pakistani goods such as textiles, rice, and pharmaceuticals. Strengthening these ties could also enhance Pakistan’s strategic depth, offering alternative routes for energy imports and transit corridors that bypass more volatile regions.

From a security perspective, deeper cooperation within the SCO could improve intelligence sharing and joint training exercises, contributing to stability along Pakistan’s north‑western borders. This is likely to reassure both domestic audiences and international investors about the country's commitment to a secure operating environment.

Background

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation was founded in 2001 as a political, economic, and security alliance comprising China, Russia, and several Central Asian states. Over the years, its membership has expanded to include India, Pakistan, and Iran, turning the bloc into a significant platform for Eurasian dialogue. Pakistan assumed the SCO chairmanship in 2026, marking its first turn at leading the organization.

Historically, Pakistan’s trade has been heavily oriented toward China, the United Arab Emirates, and the United States. Recent economic reports, however, have highlighted the need for diversification to mitigate external shocks and to tap into emerging markets. The Central Asian region, with its strategic location along the Belt and Road Initiative, offers a complementary set of opportunities that align with Pakistan’s economic goals.

The Dawn newspaper, a leading English‑language daily in Pakistan, has been covering the lead‑up to the summit closely, providing updates on diplomatic engagements, preparatory meetings, and policy statements from key ministries.

What happens next

Following the three‑day summit, participating countries are expected to finalize a set of joint statements and action plans that will be circulated to member governments for implementation. Pakistan will likely host follow‑up working groups to monitor progress on trade agreements and security cooperation. Observers anticipate that the outcomes could shape the region’s economic landscape for the next five years, especially if the agreed projects move swiftly from paper to practice.

Summary

- Pakistan chairs the SCO summit in Islamabad (Sept 28‑30). - Focus on expanding trade with Central Asian nations and improving security coordination. - Agreements on customs simplification, joint investments, and infrastructure projects are on the agenda. - The summit supports Pakistan’s broader diversification strategy, reducing dependence on traditional markets. - Post‑summit, working groups will track implementation of trade and security initiatives.
