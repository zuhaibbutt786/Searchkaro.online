Yastika Bhatia signs with Brisbane Heat as Jess Jonassen replacement

Indian cricketer Yastika Bhatia has been signed by Brisbane Heat to replace the injured Jess Jonassen for the ongoing WBBL season. The move highlights growing opportunities for Indian women players in overseas T20 leagues.

Latest developments

On 28 October 2026, Brisbane Heat announced that Indian batter Yastika Bhatia has been added to its squad as a direct replacement for Australian all‑rounder Jess Jonassen, who was ruled out after sustaining a shoulder injury during the league's opening matches. The club confirmed that Bhatia will be eligible to play for the remainder of the Women's Big Bash League (WBBL) season, which runs through December.

Bhatia, a right‑handed top‑order player known for her aggressive stroke play, joins the Heat at a crucial juncture. Her recent domestic performances for Mumbai in the Women's Senior One Day Trophy, where she averaged over 45, caught the attention of overseas scouts. The Heat management indicated that her inclusion is intended to bolster the middle order and provide additional firepower in the death overs.

The signing has generated considerable buzz on Indian social media platforms, with many fans expressing optimism that Bhatia's exposure to Australian conditions will accelerate her development. Cricket analysts note that she becomes one of only a handful of Indian women to feature in the WBBL, following earlier pioneers such as Harmanpreet Kaur and Smriti Mandhana.

Brisbane Heat's roster now lists Bhatia alongside established stars like Beth Mooney and Sophie Devine. The team, currently placed third on the points table, hopes that her fresh perspective and experience in high‑pressure T20 scenarios will help offset the loss of Jonassen's all‑round contributions.

Why it matters

The appointment of Yastika Bhatia underscores the expanding pathways for Indian women cricketers to compete in elite overseas leagues. Participation in the WBBL offers exposure to varied pitch conditions, advanced coaching methods, and a higher intensity of competition, all of which can translate into stronger performances for the Indian national side in upcoming ICC events.

For Brisbane Heat, the replacement addresses a tactical gap left by Jonassen's injury. As a premier bowler and dependable lower‑order batter, Jonassen's absence threatened the team's balance. Bhatia's arrival provides a specialist batting option, allowing the Heat to maintain depth in the lineup while seeking alternative bowling solutions.

Background

The Women's Big Bash League, launched in 2015, is Australia's premier domestic T20 competition for women and is widely regarded as one of the most competitive leagues globally. It attracts talent from Australia, New Zealand, England, and increasingly from the Indian subcontinent, offering players a platform to hone skills against top‑class opposition.

Jess Jonassen, a left‑arm orthodox spinner and a key figure for both Brisbane Heat and the Australian national team, has been a mainstay since the league's inception. Her injury, confirmed as a grade‑II rotator cuff strain, forced the Heat to seek an immediate replacement to preserve squad continuity. Yastika Bhatia, born in 2002, made her domestic debut at age 17 and quickly rose through the ranks, earning a reputation for power hitting and composure under pressure.

What happens next

Bhatia is scheduled to travel to Brisbane within the next 48 hours, where she will join the team's training camp and undergo a brief acclimatization period. The coaching staff plans to integrate her into the batting order for the upcoming match against the Sydney Sixers, with performance reviews after each game to determine her role for the remainder of the season.
