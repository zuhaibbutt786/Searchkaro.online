Marco Jansen battles as Australia seize control in SA second Test

South Africa’s lower order collapsed to 97-5 under the relentless spell of Australia’s pace attack. Marco Jansen entered the fray, hoping to turn the tide but the visitors kept the pressure on.

Latest developments

The second Test between South Africa and Australia on the Durban pitch turned into a showcase of Australian pace on Day Two. Scott Boland ripped through the South African batting line‑up, claiming four wickets and leaving the hosts precariously placed at 97 for 5. The BBC report highlighted Boland’s spell as the catalyst for the collapse, noting that the South African top order could not negotiate the short‑run, swinging deliveries.

Following Boland’s breakthrough, South Africa turned to their own fast bowler Marco Jansen to try and stem the flow of runs and create a breakthrough of his own. Jansen, who has been a key figure in the Proteas’ pace attack, was brought on after the fall of the fifth wicket. While the ball‑by‑ball commentary from Cricinfo shows that Jansen bowled a tight spell, he was unable to claim a wicket during this phase, and his economy was modest as the Australian batsmen continued to rotate the strike.

The hosts’ batting woes were further exposed when the Australian pacemen kept the pressure relentless. The Dawn article described the situation as South Africa being "close on 97‑5," emphasizing the severity of the collapse. The Proteas’ middle order failed to build partnerships, and the scoreboard reflected a growing deficit that left the team in a defensive posture.

By the end of the session, Australia had consolidated their advantage, with the scoreboard reading a substantial lead. The combination of Boland’s four‑wicket haul and the inability of South Africa’s bowlers, including Jansen, to make inroads into the Australian batting line‑up meant that the momentum was firmly with the visitors heading into the final day of the Test.

His role in the Proteas’ attack is to provide variety and to exploit conditions that favor seam movement.
