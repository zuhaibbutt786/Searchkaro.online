Korea dominates BMIC 2026 opening as Indian teams claim early wins

The BMIC 2026 tournament launched in Mumbai with strong Korean representation and early victories for Indian squads Outrage Esports and K9 Esports. DJ Alan Walker will also perform at the event, adding a musical highlight to the esports sho

Latest developments

The BMIC 2026 International Cup kicked off on Thursday at Mumbai's newly refurbished indoor arena, drawing five Korean teams that travelled to India for the first time this season. In the opening day of competition, Korea's squads displayed a high level of coordination, winning the majority of their matches against other international participants. Indian representatives Outrage Esports and K9 Esports each secured a victory in their respective fixtures, marking a promising start for the host nation. Organisers also announced that internationally renowned DJ Alan Walker will perform live on the main stage during the tournament's closing ceremony, a move intended to broaden the event's appeal beyond hardcore gamers. Ticket sales for the live audience have exceeded early expectations, with a mix of esports fans and music enthusiasts filling the venue.

Why it matters

The tournament highlights India's growing influence in the global esports ecosystem. By hosting a high‑profile event that features both top Korean teams and local Indian talent, the country showcases its capability to organise large‑scale competitive gatherings. Successful performances by Outrage Esports and K9 Esports may encourage further investment in Indian esports infrastructure, including training facilities and sponsorship deals. The inclusion of a mainstream artist such as Alan Walker signals a convergence of entertainment sectors, potentially attracting new audiences and advertisers. For local businesses, the influx of visitors and media coverage offers a boost to hospitality, transport, and related services in Mumbai.

Background

BMIC stands for BGMI International Cup, a tournament built around Battlegrounds Mobile India, the Indian version of the popular battle‑royale game PUBG Mobile. Since its launch in 2022, BMIC has become an annual fixture, rotating between major Indian cities. Earlier editions were largely domestic, but the 2026 edition marks a shift toward greater international participation, with five Korean teams confirmed as competitors. Korea has traditionally been a powerhouse in mobile esports, often leading global rankings. The partnership with music acts began in 2024, when a local DJ performed during the semifinals, setting a precedent for the 2026 crossover with Alan Walker.

What happens next

The tournament will continue over the next ten days, with group‑stage matches determining the quarter‑final line‑up. Indian teams are scheduled to face additional Korean squads later this week, while the organizers have released a detailed schedule for the remaining fixtures. Ticket holders can expect a series of side events, including meet‑and‑greets with players and a live streaming panel featuring industry experts. The final match and Alan Walker's performance are slated for the closing night, with a broadcast planned on major Indian sports channels and streaming platforms.
