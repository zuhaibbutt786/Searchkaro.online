SBI data breach claim: 375,000 customers' records possibly exposed

A hacker group claims to have accessed the personal and KYC details of roughly 375,000 State Bank of India customers. The bank has acknowledged the allegation and launched an internal probe.

Latest developments

On October 19, 2026, a self‑identified hacker group posted a claim on a public cyber‑crime forum that it had obtained sensitive information belonging to approximately 3.75 lakh (375,000) State Bank of India (SBI) customers. The post listed the types of data allegedly accessed, including KYC documents such as PAN cards, Aadhaar numbers, address proofs, as well as account numbers and limited transaction history. The group warned that the information could be used for fraudulent activities if not secured.

SBI responded later the same day, confirming that it had become aware of the allegation and was conducting a thorough internal investigation. The bank said it had engaged an external cybersecurity firm to verify the claim and assess any potential impact on its systems. SBI also informed the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and other relevant authorities, as required under Indian data‑protection guidelines.

The Economic Times reported that the alleged breach, if verified, would affect a significant portion of the bank's retail customer base, making it one of the largest reported data incidents in the Indian banking sector. The article noted that the hacker group had not released any raw data files, but had provided screenshots that appeared to show portions of KYC records. No official confirmation of data loss has been issued by SBI as of the time of writing.

Indian Television Dot Com echoed the same figures and added that the group claimed the data was being prepared for sale on underground markets. The report highlighted that the alleged exposure could increase the risk of identity theft, loan fraud, and unauthorized fund transfers. RBI officials, quoted anonymously, indicated that they would monitor the situation closely and could issue directives to strengthen security protocols across the banking industry.

Why it matters

The potential exposure of KYC data threatens the financial security of millions of Indians who rely on SBI for everyday banking. Personal identifiers such as PAN and Aadhaar numbers are central to many financial services, and their compromise can enable fraudsters to open new accounts, obtain credit, or impersonate victims in digital transactions. For customers, the breach could mean a need to monitor credit reports, change passwords, and possibly re‑verify their identity with the bank.

Beyond individual risk, the incident raises questions about the robustness of cybersecurity measures in India's largest public sector bank. SBI handles a substantial share of the country's deposits and credit flow; any erosion of trust could affect customer confidence and the broader perception of the banking system. Regulators may also reassess existing guidelines for data protection, potentially leading to stricter compliance requirements for all financial institutions.

Background

State Bank of India, founded in 1955, is the country's largest bank by assets and customer base. Over the past decade, Indian banks have faced a steady rise in cyber‑attack attempts, ranging from phishing scams to ransomware incidents. While SBI has previously reported isolated security alerts, a breach of this scale involving KYC documents would be unprecedented for the institution.

India's data‑protection framework, anchored by the Personal Data Protection Bill, mandates that banks report significant data breaches to the RBI within a prescribed timeframe. The bill also requires banks to implement technical safeguards, conduct regular security audits, and notify affected customers when personal data is compromised. The current allegation tests the practical enforcement of these provisions and highlights the challenges banks face in protecting vast amounts of sensitive information.

What happens next

SBI is expected to complete its forensic analysis within the next two weeks, after which it will publish a detailed report on the scope of the incident. The RBI may issue a formal directive requiring SBI and other banks to enhance encryption, multi‑factor authentication, and real‑time monitoring of data access.

Customers who receive a notification from SBI are advised to verify any communication through official channels, update passwords, and consider placing a fraud alert on their credit files. Law enforcement agencies are also likely to launch parallel investigations to trace the source of the alleged hacker group and prevent the sale of stolen data on dark‑web platforms.

Summary
