Visits to Ziaur Rahman Mausoleum Rise on Solidarity Day

Crowds gathered at the mausoleum of former president Ziaur Rahman on National Solidarity Day, paying floral tributes and attending a prayer ceremony. The event reflected the nation's reverence for the late leader.

Latest developments

A special prayer and Milad Mahfil were held at the mausoleum of former president Ziaur Rahman to celebrate National Revolution and Solidarity Day, drawing thousands of devotees who gathered to pay floral tributes. The ceremony, organised by local religious leaders, included recitations of the national pledge and a collective prayer for peace and prosperity. Deputy Speaker of the National Assembly attended the event, signalling official endorsement of the tribute.

In parallel, the School of the Holy Quran in Bogura marked the day with a school‑wide observance. Students and teachers participated in a march and a brief lecture on the significance of the day, which commemorates the historic revolution that shaped Bangladesh’s political landscape. The school’s event was covered by local media, highlighting the role of education in fostering national solidarity.

The mausoleum itself was a focal point for the day’s activities. Reuters Connect reported that visitors laid wreaths and offered flowers to the graves of both Ziaur Rahman and his wife, former Prime Minister Khaleda Zia. The floral tributes were arranged in a semicircle around the tombs, creating a solemn atmosphere that attracted both political supporters and neutral observers.

The combination of religious ceremony, political symbolism, and community participation underscored the enduring influence of Ziaur Rahman’s legacy. The day’s events were broadcast on national television, allowing viewers across the country to witness the collective homage and reinforcing the sense of shared history among Bangladeshis.

Why it matters

The gathering at Ziaur Rahman’s mausoleum serves as a reminder of the country’s turbulent past and the political divisions that still linger. By bringing together supporters of different parties, the event demonstrates a willingness to honour a controversial figure in a peaceful manner.

For many citizens, the day offers an opportunity to reflect on the promises of national unity that were made during the revolution. The presence of high‑profile officials, such as the Deputy Speaker, signals a tacit endorsement of the narrative that the nation’s political history can be celebrated without conflict.

The floral tributes also carry symbolic weight for the electorate. In a country where political allegiance often aligns with personal identity, the act of paying respects to a former president can influence public sentiment and potentially soften partisan tensions in the lead‑up to upcoming elections.

Background

Ziaur Rahman, who served as President of Bangladesh from 1977 until his assassination in 1981, is a polarising figure. His tenure was marked by significant economic reforms and a shift towards a more centralized governance structure. Despite controversy over his methods, many view him as a key architect of modern Bangladesh.

National Revolution and Solidarity Day, observed annually on 25th November, commemorates the 1971 Liberation War and the subsequent political upheavals that shaped the nation’s current form. The day is intended to foster a sense of collective identity and national pride.

The mausoleum of Ziaur Rahman, located in the capital Dhaka, has long been a pilgrimage site for his supporters. It houses not only his remains but also those of his wife, former Prime Minister Khaleda Zia, making it a central point for political remembrance and public mourning.

What happens next

In the coming weeks, the government is expected to release a statement outlining plans for further commemorations, including a national day of remembrance that may feature additional speeches by political leaders. The event could also set the stage for upcoming parliamentary debates on historical accountability.

Meanwhile, local NGOs and community groups are likely to organise smaller, grassroots gatherings to continue the spirit of solidarity. These activities will provide a platform for citizens to discuss the nation’s future and the role of historical figures in shaping policy.
