Jai Moondra's six-wicket haul levels Ireland-Namibia ODI series

Jai Moondra's six‑for secured a draw in the Ireland tour of Namibia, marking a historic performance for the Irish side. The result has drawn attention from cricket followers across Pakistan.

Latest developments

The fourth One Day International between Ireland and Namibia took place on 30 October 2026 at the Wanderers Cricket Ground in Windhoek. With the series level at one win each and one no‑result, Ireland needed a victory to avoid a series loss. In the decisive match, right‑arm fast bowler Jai Moondra produced a career‑best spell, taking six wickets for just 26 runs in 9.3 overs. His figures not only broke the previous record for an Irish bowler in ODIs but also proved pivotal in restricting Namibia to 215 runs. Ireland chased down the target with five wickets in hand, sealing a 2‑2 series tie. Moondra's performance was highlighted by the dismissal of Namibia's top order, including the crucial wickets of their opening batsmen within the first ten overs. The match concluded with Ireland's captain praising the team's resilience, while Namibia's side acknowledged the quality of the Irish bowling attack. The series now ends in parity, a rare outcome for a tour involving a full member and an associate nation.

Background

Ireland's tour of Namibia was arranged as part of the ICC's Future Tours Programme, aiming to give associate nations regular exposure to higher‑level competition. The tour comprised four ODIs, with the first three split evenly: Ireland won the opening match, Namibia claimed the second, and the third ended without a result due to rain. Jai Moondra, born to Indian parents and raised in Dublin, made his international debut in 2024 and quickly earned a reputation for swing and accuracy. Prior to the Windhoek game, Moondra had recorded modest figures, but his development was closely monitored by both Irish selectors and overseas scouts. Namibia, meanwhile, entered the series with a strong home record, having beaten other associate sides consistently in recent years. The series was also a preparatory platform for both teams ahead of the 2027 ICC Cricket World Cup qualifiers.

With the series ending in a tie, both Ireland and Namibia will turn their focus to upcoming qualification tournaments.
