Air France Boeing 777 returns to Paris after mid‑Atlantic U‑turn

Air France’s Boeing 777 was forced to return to Paris after a cockpit instrument fault caused a mid‑Atlantic U‑turn. The incident underscores the importance of rigorous maintenance for large aircraft.

Latest developments

Air France Flight AF123, a Boeing 777‑300ER, was scheduled to land at New York’s JFK Airport on 20 November 2026.

Early in the trans‑Atlantic leg, a fault in the cockpit’s primary flight display prompted the crew to perform an emergency U‑turn over the Atlantic, diverting the aircraft back to Paris Charles de Gaulle.

The diversion added several hours to the journey and caused inconvenience for more than 400 passengers, who were re‑accommodated on later flights.

The incident was reported by AirLive and confirmed by Air France’s operations team, who stated that the fault was isolated and did not compromise the overall safety of the aircraft.

An internal investigation is underway, with the French aviation authority expected to review the event within the next week.

The U‑turn was executed at a safe altitude, and the aircraft landed without incident.

Air France has not issued a formal safety notice, but the airline has pledged to conduct a thorough review of the affected flight data.
