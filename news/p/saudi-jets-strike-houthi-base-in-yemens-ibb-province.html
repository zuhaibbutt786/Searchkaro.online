Saudi jets strike Houthi base in Yemen's Ibb province

Saudi jets targeted a Houthi missile base in Yemen’s Ibb province, striking storage facilities. The attack comes amid rising tensions between Saudi Arabia and the Houthi movement.

Latest developments

On Monday, Saudi fighter jets conducted a strike on a Houthi missile base in Yemen’s Ibb province. The raid targeted missile storage facilities and a launch platform. The operation was reported by local media outlets. The Arabic word 'خبر' translates to 'news' in English. The attack also hit a Houthi camp in Yarem, a town in Ibb province. Sources say the strike destroyed several storage containers and damaged the base’s infrastructure. No casualties were reported in the brief statement. The Saudi-led coalition has repeatedly warned that it will respond to Houthi attacks on its aircraft and shipping lanes. The latest raid follows a series of incidents in which Houthi forces have fired missiles at Saudi aircraft and vessels. The coalition’s air campaign has intensified in recent weeks. The strike was confirmed by the Saudi Ministry of Defense, which said the operation was aimed at preventing the Houthi movement from developing or deploying missile systems. The ministry added that the attack was part of a broader effort to curb Houthi influence in Yemen.

Why it matters

For Saudi citizens, the raid signals a continued military response to Houthi threats. It shows the government’s commitment to protecting its airspace and maritime routes. The strike may reassure businesses reliant on Gulf shipping lanes. The operation also raises concerns about regional stability. If the Houthi movement responds with further attacks, it could lead to a broader conflict that would affect Saudi Arabia’s security and economic interests. The government has warned that it will act to defend its interests. The strike may influence public opinion about the war in Yemen. While some view it as a necessary defense, others worry about the humanitarian cost and the risk of escalation. The government has called for a diplomatic solution but remains prepared for military action.

Background

The Houthi movement, officially the Ansar Allah group, controls large parts of Yemen’s north and has been fighting the internationally recognized government since 2014. The Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015 to support the government against the Houthis. Ibb province, located in central Yemen, has been a strategic location for the Houthis. The province hosts several military installations and missile storage sites that the coalition has targeted repeatedly. The latest strike on a missile base in Ibb is part of a long‑term campaign to limit Houthi missile capabilities. The conflict has caused a humanitarian crisis, with thousands displaced and infrastructure destroyed. The coalition’s air campaign has been criticised for civilian casualties, though the ministry claims it targets only military sites. The war remains unresolved, with peace talks stalled.

What happens next

Saudi Arabia is likely to continue its air campaign against Houthi positions in Yemen. The ministry has warned that it will act against any further Houthi attacks on Saudi aircraft or shipping. The next steps may involve additional strikes on missile storage sites and launch platforms. The Houthi movement may respond by increasing missile launches or by targeting Saudi infrastructure. Diplomatic efforts by the UN and Gulf states will continue to seek a ceasefire, but a resolution remains uncertain.
