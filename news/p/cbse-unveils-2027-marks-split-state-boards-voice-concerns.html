CBSE Unveils 2027 Marks Split, State Boards Voice Concerns

The Central Board of Secondary Education has introduced a revised marks structure for its Class 10 and 12 examinations from 2027. Pakistani educators and parents are assessing how the shift could affect students in CBSE-affiliated schools.

Latest developments

The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) released an official notice outlining a new marks distribution for its Class 10 and Class 12 examinations beginning in 2027. Under the revised scheme, the total score will be divided among theory papers, practical components where applicable, and internal assessments such as projects and periodic tests. The Economic Times reported that the board intends to allocate specific percentages to each segment, aiming to provide a more balanced evaluation of student performance. The change replaces the earlier model where theory dominated the final score, and it aligns with a broader trend toward continuous assessment.

The board also confirmed that the schedule for the upcoming CBSE and Indian Certificate of Secondary Education (ICSE) examinations will remain on time, as reported by the Times of India. While the national timetable proceeds as planned, the announcement has sparked concern among students enrolled in state board systems, particularly in regions where CBSE schools operate alongside local curricula. The timing of the new assessment structure coincides with the current academic cycle, leaving schools a short window to adjust teaching plans and assessment calendars.

Educators in Pakistan, where a number of private institutions follow the CBSE syllabus, have expressed apprehension about the practical implications of the new marks split. They note that many Pakistani students rely on CBSE qualifications for admission to universities both domestically and abroad. A shift in assessment weightage could affect preparation strategies, especially for subjects that previously received minimal internal assessment credit. The uncertainty also extends to parents who choose CBSE schools for their perceived rigor and alignment with Indian higher‑education standards.

CBSE officials clarified that the revision is intended to reduce the pressure of a single high‑stakes exam and to encourage a more holistic learning process. By giving internal assessments a defined share of the final grade, the board hopes to reward consistent effort throughout the year rather than performance on a single day. The board has pledged to issue detailed guidelines and sample papers to help schools implement the new structure smoothly before the 2027 examination cycle.

Why it matters

For families in Pakistan that enrol their children in CBSE‑affiliated schools, the marks bifurcation could reshape study habits and resource allocation. A greater emphasis on internal assessments may require schools to invest in continuous testing infrastructure, teacher training, and record‑keeping, potentially increasing tuition fees. Moreover, university admission committees in Pakistan and abroad often compare CBSE scores with local board results; a shift in scoring could alter the perceived competitiveness of Pakistani CBSE graduates.

The change also has broader implications for the private education market. Schools that previously marketed themselves on the simplicity of a single exam may need to revise their curricula to meet the new assessment criteria. This could influence parental choice, driving some families toward state boards that retain the traditional exam format, while others may stay with CBSE for its updated, comprehensive evaluation model.

Background

The Central Board of Secondary Education is India's national board that conducts examinations for over 20 million students across South Asia and the Middle East. Historically, CBSE's grading system placed heavy weight on final theory papers, with limited credit for practical work or internal projects. Over the past decade, education policymakers worldwide have advocated for continuous assessment to better reflect student learning trajectories. CBSE's 2027 marks split is part of this global shift, aiming to balance theoretical knowledge with practical skills and regular classroom performance.

In Pakistan, CBSE schools have existed since the early 1990s, primarily serving expatriate communities and families seeking an internationally recognised qualification. These schools operate alongside the national Punjab and Federal Boards, which follow a different assessment model. The coexistence of multiple boards has created a competitive environment where parents evaluate the merits of each system based on university entry requirements and perceived academic rigor.

What happens next

CBSE has announced that detailed implementation manuals will be circulated to affiliated schools by early 2026, giving institutions a year to adapt curricula and assessment practices. Training workshops for teachers are planned in major cities across South Asia, including Karachi and Lahore, to ensure consistent application of the new marking scheme. In Pakistan, education authorities are expected to monitor the transition and may issue advisories to private schools to align their internal assessment policies with the board's requirements.
