Pakistan International Airlines Receives First Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner

On 30 September 2024, Pakistan International Airlines welcomed its first Boeing 787‑9 Dreamliner at Karachi’s Jinnah International Airport. The aircraft, registered as AP‑B787, was delivered as part of a wet‑lease arrangement with a European lessor, allowing PIA to operate the jet without immediate ownership transfer. Aviation officials said the Dreamliner will join the airline’s growing fleet of wide‑body aircraft, complementing the existing Airbus A330s and Boeing 777s. The arrival ceremony was attended by senior ministry representatives, who highlighted the aircraft’s fuel‑efficient design and passenger comfort features such as larger windows and improved cabin pressure.

The same aircraft left London Heathrow on 28 September, marking its maiden flight into Pakistan under the wet‑lease contract. The flight, operating as PIA‑787, carried a mixed load of expatriate workers, business travelers and tourists returning home for the festive season. Crew members were supplied by the leasing company, while PIA provided branding and ticketing. The successful completion of the London‑Karachi sector demonstrated the operational readiness of the Dreamliner for PIA’s long‑haul schedule and provided a test case for future wet‑lease or lease‑purchase agreements.

Following the Dreamliner’s entry into service, PIA announced an increase in its weekly flights to London, adding an extra Thursday departure from Karachi. Industry analysts note that the added capacity could enable the airline to restore its previously suspended Birmingham route, which was halted in 2022 due to low demand and fleet constraints. The airline’s commercial director indicated that the Dreamliner’s range and seat configuration make it suitable for both premium and economy cabins on the London‑Karachi corridor, potentially attracting a broader customer base and improving load factors.

Why it matters

The introduction of the 787‑9 expands PIA’s ability to offer more seats on one of its most profitable routes, London‑Karachi. For Pakistani diaspora in the United Kingdom, the additional flight provides greater flexibility and shorter waiting times, especially during peak travel periods such as Eid and the winter holidays. The Dreamliner’s lower fuel consumption also promises modest fare stability, a welcome development in a market where ticket prices have risen sharply over the past year.

From a broader economic perspective, the aircraft’s arrival signals renewed confidence in Pakistan’s aviation sector after a period of fleet reductions and operational challenges. Increased connectivity to Europe supports trade, tourism, and investment flows, and may encourage other carriers to consider expanding services to Pakistani cities. Moreover, the wet‑lease model reduces upfront capital outlay for PIA, allowing the airline to modernise its fleet while preserving cash for other strategic initiatives.

Background

The Boeing 787 Dreamliner family, introduced in 2009, is known for its composite airframe, advanced aerodynamics and efficient engines. The 787‑9 variant stretches the original design, offering up to 290 seats and a range of roughly 7,530 nautical miles, making it suitable for long‑haul routes such as Karachi‑London. Airlines worldwide have adopted the type for its ability to lower operating costs and improve passenger experience through higher cabin humidity and reduced noise levels.

PIA’s fleet modernization program began in 2020, aiming to replace aging aircraft with newer, more efficient models. Prior to the Dreamliner, the carrier relied heavily on Airbus A320 family for short‑haul and Airbus A330 or Boeing 777 for long‑haul services. Limited availability of wide‑body jets and financial constraints delayed the acquisition of additional 787s, prompting the airline to explore wet‑lease options as a short‑term solution to meet growing demand on Europe routes.

What happens next

PIA plans to operate the Dreamliner on a rotational schedule between Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, with primary focus on the London corridor. The airline expects to evaluate passenger response and operational performance over the next six months before deciding on a permanent lease‑purchase or outright purchase. If load factors remain strong, the carrier is likely to submit a formal request to the Civil Aviation Authority to reinstate the Birmingham service, targeting a launch in early 2025.
