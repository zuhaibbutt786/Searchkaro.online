Cyclone Murasu Forms Over Bay of Bengal, IMD Issues Alerts

A fresh cyclonic system, Murasu, is brewing over the Bay of Bengal after the recent cyclone Arnab. The Indian Meteorological Department has warned of possible heavy rainfall in Kerala and surrounding regions.

Latest developments

The Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) confirmed on Monday that a low‑pressure area over the Bay of Bengal is intensifying into a cyclonic storm. The system, designated as "Murasu," follows closely on the heels of Cyclone Arnab, which made landfall earlier this month. Satellite imagery shows the storm's cloud bands consolidating, and wind speeds are expected to reach cyclonic storm criteria within the next 24 hours.

IMD officials have issued a yellow alert for coastal districts of West Bengal, Odisha, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, advising fishermen to remain in safe harbors and residents to stay alert for possible heavy rain. The department also warned that the storm could bring gusts of up to 70 km/h as it moves westward toward the Indian mainland.

In Kerala, the state weather department has issued a separate advisory indicating a high probability of heavy rainfall starting later today. The forecast suggests that the rain could be accompanied by localized flooding in low‑lying areas, particularly in the districts of Alappuzha and Kottayam. Authorities have pre‑positioned relief teams and are monitoring river levels closely.

The IMD's warning comes amid a broader pattern of active monsoon conditions across the subcontinent. While the storm is still over water, its trajectory could influence weather patterns over the southern peninsula, potentially extending the monsoon's intensity in the coming days.

Why it matters

For residents of coastal states, the emergence of Cyclone Murasu raises immediate concerns about property damage, disruption to fishing activities, and the safety of vulnerable communities. The warning also signals a possible strain on disaster‑response resources that are already engaged with recovery efforts from Cyclone Arnab.

Kerala's agricultural sector could be affected by the anticipated heavy rain. While the monsoon is essential for crops such as rice and rubber, excessive rainfall can lead to water‑logging, delayed planting, and increased pest activity. Local authorities are urging farmers to secure their fields and to follow guidance from agricultural extension services.

On a broader scale, the development of another cyclonic storm highlights the challenges posed by climate variability in the Indian Ocean region. Repeated cyclones within a short period can exacerbate soil erosion, damage infrastructure, and affect tourism, which is a significant economic driver for many coastal communities.

Background

The Bay of Bengal is a known hotspot for cyclogenesis, especially during the pre‑monsoon and monsoon transition periods. Historically, the region has produced some of the most intense cyclones in the Northern Hemisphere. The recent Cyclone Arnab, which struck the eastern coast in early October, caused moderate damage but prompted a swift response from the IMD and state disaster agencies.

The term "ചുഴലിക്കാറ്റ്" (pronounced "chuzhalikkatt") is Malayalam for "cyclone" or "twister," reflecting the local language's description of such weather events. The naming of storms follows a list contributed by member countries of the World Meteorological Organization; "Murasu" is a Tamil word meaning "thunder" or "storm," underscoring the cultural diversity in naming conventions.

India's disaster management framework relies heavily on early warnings from the IMD, which uses satellite data, ocean buoys, and computer models to predict storm paths. The department's alert system categorises threats into green, yellow, orange, and red, each indicating a higher level of risk and prompting specific preparedness actions.

What happens next

Meteorologists expect Cyclone Murasu to maintain its intensity for the next 48 hours before beginning a gradual weakening phase as it approaches land. The IMD will likely upgrade the alert level if wind speeds increase or if the storm's path shifts toward densely populated coastal zones. In Kerala, local authorities will continue to monitor river gauges and may issue evacuation orders for flood‑prone villages if water levels rise sharply.

Stakeholders, including the fishing community, transport operators, and emergency services, are advised to stay tuned to official bulletins and to follow any directives issued by state governments.
