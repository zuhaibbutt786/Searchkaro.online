Jamie Chadwick completes first Formula 1 test with Williams

Jamie Chadwick has taken her first step into Formula 1, testing a Williams car at Portimão. The 22‑year‑old described the experience as the highlight of her career.

Latest developments

On 14 March, Jamie Chadwick completed a one‑hour test in a Williams F1 car at the Portimão circuit in Portugal. The session was part of the team’s pre‑season testing programme and saw the 22‑year‑old drive the car at speeds that match the demands of a Formula 1 weekend.

Chadwick described the experience as the highlight of her racing career, saying the feeling of pushing a car at 350 kilometres per hour was surreal. She also noted that the test gave her a clearer sense of how the Williams car handles compared to the machinery she has driven in the W Series.

The Williams team welcomed her participation, emphasising that the test would provide valuable data for the team’s development work. Chadwick’s performance was monitored closely by the engineering crew, who recorded telemetry and lap times to feed into the car’s setup.

This test marks a significant milestone for Chadwick, who has been a champion in the W Series and has expressed a long‑term ambition to race in Formula 1. Her involvement with Williams also highlights the growing partnership between the team and female drivers in the sport.

Why it matters

For British fans, Chadwick’s test is a source of national pride, showing that a UK‑based driver is gaining experience in the world’s premier racing series. It also demonstrates the increasing visibility of women in motorsport, encouraging young drivers to pursue careers at the highest level.

The test could influence Williams’ strategy for the upcoming season, as data from a seasoned driver like Chadwick may help refine the car’s aerodynamics and tyre performance. It also signals the team’s openness to diverse talent, which could attract further investment and support from sponsors.

Background

Jamie Chadwick rose to prominence after winning the W Series championship in 2019, 2020 and 2021. The all‑female racing series was created to provide a platform for women to showcase their talent and move into higher categories.

Williams, founded in 1977, has a long history in Formula 1 but has struggled to achieve consistent front‑row results. In recent years the team has focused on rebuilding its technical base and exploring partnerships with drivers who bring fresh perspectives.

What happens next

Williams will use the data collected from Chadwick’s test to fine‑tune the car for the 2024 season, with a focus on improving straight‑line speed and braking stability. Chadwick is expected to return to the track for further sessions as the team prepares for the full pre‑season programme.
