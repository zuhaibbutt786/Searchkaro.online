Pakistan to Chair World Bank and IMF Boards in 2027

Pakistan is set to take over the chairmanship of both the World Bank and IMF Boards in 2027, a position it has not held in more than 70 years. The appointment coincides with the Borrowers’ Platform launching its first Governing Council meet

Latest developments

Dawn reported that Pakistan will assume the chairmanship of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank Boards for the first time in more than seven decades, a milestone that will be formalised in 2027. Business Recorder echoed the announcement, confirming that the country will lead both governing bodies during the same year, a rare convergence of influence in the multilateral finance arena. The decision follows a series of diplomatic engagements in which Pakistan secured the support of major shareholder nations, positioning itself as a bridge between creditor and borrower economies. In a related development, the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) announced that the Borrowers’ Platform, a forum for developing countries that rely on multilateral financing, entered a new phase with its inaugural Governing Council meeting held in Bangkok. The meeting brought together finance ministers and senior officials from more than 30 borrower nations to discuss reforms aimed at improving access to affordable financing. Pakistani officials welcomed the platform’s progress, noting that the country’s upcoming chairmanship will enable it to shape the agenda of both the World Bank and IMF while also contributing to the Borrowers’ Platform’s objectives. The combined effect of these events is expected to raise Pakistan’s profile in global financial governance and provide it with a stronger voice in policy discussions that affect emerging economies.

Why it matters

For Pakistani citizens, the chairmanship offers a symbolic boost to national pride and may translate into greater leverage in negotiating development projects and loan terms. While the role does not automatically guarantee preferential treatment, it does give Pakistan a seat at the table where key decisions about funding priorities, climate finance, and debt sustainability are made. Economists suggest that the visibility could attract foreign investment by signalling political stability and a commitment to international cooperation. At the same time, the Borrowers’ Platform’s new Governing Council provides a collective voice for countries facing similar debt challenges, meaning that Pakistan can work with peers to push for reforms that ease financing conditions. The combined influence of chairing the two major institutions and participating actively in the Borrowers’ Platform could help Pakistan shape policies that align with its development goals, such as infrastructure upgrades, renewable energy projects, and social safety nets.

Background

Pakistan has been a member of the World Bank Group since its inception in 1944 and has engaged with the IMF for decades, often turning to the institutions for balance‑of‑payments support. Historically, the country has not held a board chairmanship, with the role typically rotating among larger economies. The last time a South Asian nation chaired both bodies was in the early 1990s, but Pakistan’s upcoming term marks the first time the country will lead both simultaneously. The Borrowers’ Platform was created by UNCTAD in 2020 to give low‑ and middle‑income countries a coordinated platform to discuss borrowing conditions and advocate for fairer loan terms. Its first Governing Council meeting in Bangkok represents a maturation of the forum, moving from concept to operational decision‑making. Pakistan’s involvement in the platform reflects its broader strategy to diversify financing sources and reduce reliance on bilateral aid.

What happens next

The formal handover of the chairmanship is scheduled for the World Bank and IMF spring meetings in early 2027, after which Pakistan will preside over board sessions, set agendas, and host key discussions. In parallel, the Borrowers’ Platform will convene its next council meeting later in 2027 to review progress on proposed reforms and to assess the impact of new financing mechanisms. Observers expect that Pakistan will use its dual role to promote policies that address debt vulnerability and encourage sustainable investment, while also navigating the expectations of creditor nations. The outcomes of these meetings will be closely watched by regional governments and development partners.
