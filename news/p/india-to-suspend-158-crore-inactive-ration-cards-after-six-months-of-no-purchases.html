India to suspend 1.58 crore inactive ration cards after six months of no purchases

The central government has announced that 1.58 crore ration cards will be deactivated after six months of inactivity. The move aims to tighten the public distribution system but has drawn criticism from migrant workers and rights groups.

Latest developments

The Union Ministry of Consumer Affairs announced on 1 October 2026 that about 1.58 crore ration cards that have shown no purchase of subsidised food grains for the past six months will be temporarily deactivated. The move is part of a broader effort to clean the Public Distribution System (PDS) database and curb leakages. Beneficiaries whose cards are deactivated will lose access to subsidised wheat, rice and kerosene until they re‑apply and prove eligibility. The decision was published in the official Gazette and has already triggered a wave of inquiries across several states. State governments, including Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, have begun notifying households that their cards may be suspended if they have not collected any ration since April. Local authorities are instructed to send SMS alerts and display notices at fair price shops. In many districts, officials are conducting field verification to confirm inactivity, and some have set up help desks to assist affected families in submitting the required documents. Critics argue that the six‑month inactivity rule could penalise migrant workers and seasonal labourers who move to other states for work and cannot regularly visit their home fair price shops. Consumer rights groups have filed petitions in several high courts, seeking a stay on the suspension until alternative verification mechanisms are introduced. The Ministry, however, maintains that the rule targets ghost beneficiaries and fraudulent entries that inflate the PDS rolls. The suspension will be effective from 1 October 2026, and the Ministry has promised a grace period of thirty days for card‑holders to reactivate their accounts by presenting a recent purchase receipt or a declaration of migration. Failure to comply within the window could lead to permanent cancellation, requiring a fresh application for a new ration card.

Why it matters

The PDS supplies essential food grains to roughly two‑thirds of India's population at heavily subsidised rates. Removing inactive cards could free up grains for genuine beneficiaries, potentially improving food security for millions. At the same time, any abrupt loss of access may push vulnerable households into nutritional risk, especially in rural areas where alternative sources of cheap staples are limited. The policy also has fiscal implications. Estimates suggest that inactive cards account for a loss of several hundred crore rupees each year due to the release of grains that never reach the intended families. By tightening the rolls, the government aims to reduce waste and improve the efficiency of subsidy spending, a priority in the current budget cycle.

Background

India's ration card system, introduced after independence, is the backbone of the Public Distribution System. Cards are issued at the household level and entitle families to a fixed quota of wheat, rice, sugar, and kerosene each month. Over the decades, the database has expanded to cover more than 800 million people, but it has also accumulated errors, duplicate entries and "ghost" beneficiaries who no longer reside at the registered address. Previous attempts to clean the database, such as the 2019 digitisation drive and the 2022 biometric linking of cards to Aadhaar, achieved mixed results. While those measures reduced some duplication, many inactive cards remained on the list, prompting the current decision to impose a six‑month inactivity clause.

What happens next

The Ministry will monitor the deactivation process through weekly reports from state PDS departments. If a significant number of legitimate beneficiaries report loss of access, the government may consider revising the inactivity period or introducing a more flexible verification system. Meanwhile, civil society groups are expected to continue legal challenges, and the final outcome will likely shape future reforms of India's food subsidy architecture.

Summary

- Approximately 1.58 crore inactive ration cards will be deactivated. - Suspension starts 1 October 2026 with a 30‑day reactivation window. - Aim is to curb fraud and improve efficiency of the PDS. - Migrant and seasonal workers risk losing access to subsidised grains. - Legal challenges are expected, and policy may be adjusted based on feedback.
