Sanju Samson Scores 82 in T20I but Faces Exclusion from India XI

Sanju Samson's explosive 82 against West Indies was a highlight of the series, yet his omission from the next T20I squad has raised questions about the selectors' criteria. Fans and analysts alike are debating whether his recent form warran

Latest developments

In the second T20I against West Indies, Sanju Samson struck 82 runs from 41 balls, including six fours and four sixes, at the M. Chinnaswamy Stadium. His innings was the highest individual score in the series and helped India post a competitive total of 179/7. The strike rate of 200.49 was the fastest by an Indian in a T20I against a full‑member side, drawing praise from commentators.

Despite the fireworks, the innings was marred by a single dismissal that left the wicketkeeper on the ropes. Social media erupted with mixed reactions; some applauded Samson's power, while others criticised the lack of wickets. The Telugu media, including Oneindia and myKhel, highlighted the paradox of a prolific score coupled with a perceived lack of control.

Following the match, the selectors announced a reshuffle of the T20I XI, with the inclusion of Vaibhav Sooryavanshi and the exclusion of Samson. The decision was met with surprise, as no official statement explained the rationale. Analysts speculated that the selectors were prioritising recent domestic form and consistency over a single high score.

The move has implications for India's batting balance. With Samson out, the team will rely on more experienced middle‑order batsmen, potentially reducing the power‑play options. The change also signals a shift in selection philosophy, emphasizing a broader evaluation of performance across formats.

Why it matters

For Indian fans, Samson's omission underscores the competitive nature of international selection. The decision fuels debate over whether a single explosive innings should outweigh a series of modest contributions. It also highlights the pressure on domestic players to maintain consistent form across multiple formats.

On a broader level, the move may influence the perception of the selectors' transparency. If players feel that selection is based on opaque criteria, it could affect morale within the domestic circuit. The incident also raises questions about the balance between experience and emerging talent in India's T20 strategy.

Background

Sanju Samson, born in 1994, has been a mainstay of the Karnataka state team and has represented India in all three formats. His career has been punctuated by flashes of brilliance, such as his 134 in the 2019 IPL, but has also seen periods of inconsistency that have led to intermittent exclusion from the national side.

India's T20I selection policy has historically favoured players who demonstrate both power and reliability. In recent years, the board has introduced a more data‑driven approach, incorporating metrics like strike rate, dismissal patterns, and fielding contributions. Samson's recent 82, while impressive, was not accompanied by wickets or a high strike‑rate in the final overs, which may have influenced the selectors.

What happens next

The selectors are expected to announce the final squad for the upcoming series against Bangladesh within the next week. Analysts predict that Samson may be recalled if he can replicate his domestic form and show improved wicket‑taking ability. Meanwhile, the team will likely experiment with new players to strengthen the middle order.
