Edinburgh weather today: rain, mild temps and sunshine ahead

Edinburgh is experiencing light rain and mild temperatures today, with breezy conditions. A clearer and warmer spell is expected to arrive later in the week.

What is happening

The Met Office has issued a short‑term outlook for Edinburgh that shows scattered showers throughout the day. Temperatures are expected to peak around 15 °C, which is modest for mid‑September but comfortable for most residents. Winds are forecast to reach speeds of up to 60 kph, primarily from the west, adding a noticeable chill factor despite the mild temperature.

Rainfall will be light rather than heavy, with most precipitation occurring in brief bursts during the late morning and early afternoon. The sky will remain partly cloudy, allowing occasional sunshine to break through between showers. This pattern is typical for the region during the transition from summer to autumn, when Atlantic fronts bring intermittent moisture.

In the evening, the rain is likely to taper off, leaving behind damp surfaces and a cooler night. Overnight lows are projected to fall to around 9 °C, which is slightly above the seasonal average for this time of year. The combination of lingering moisture and wind may make the streets feel cooler than the thermometer suggests.

Looking ahead to the weekend, forecasters anticipate a shift toward drier air. A high‑pressure ridge is expected to build over the British Isles, bringing clearer skies and a gradual rise in daytime temperatures. By early next week, temperatures could climb into the low 20s °C, and the wind will ease to more typical breezy levels.

Background and context

Scotland’s climate is heavily influenced by the North Atlantic, which brings moist air masses that frequently interact with the country's varied topography. Edinburgh, situated on the east coast, often experiences a blend of maritime and continental influences, resulting in changeable weather throughout the year. Historically, September marks the start of a cooling trend, but temperatures can still rise above 20 °C when high pressure dominates.

The Met Office uses a combination of satellite data, ground stations, and computer models to produce its forecasts. For short‑range predictions, the model resolution captures local effects such as the Firth of Forth’s impact on wind direction. The current forecast reflects a passing Atlantic low that is bringing the observed showers and gusty winds. As the low moves eastward, the influence will wane, allowing the upcoming high pressure to bring clearer skies.
