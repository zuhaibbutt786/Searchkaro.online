Hashim Thaci sentenced to 25 years for war crimes

Former Kosovo president Hashim Thaci has been handed a 25‑year prison term by the International Criminal Court for war crimes committed during the 1990s conflict. The verdict marks a significant moment for accountability in the Balkans and

What is happening

On 15 March 2024 the International Criminal Court (ICC) in The Hague delivered a verdict that former Kosovo president Hashim Thaci is guilty of war crimes and crimes against humanity. The court sentenced him to 25 years in prison for his alleged role in the forced displacement and murder of civilians during the 1998‑1999 Kosovo conflict. The judgment follows a lengthy trial that began in 2021 and concluded after the prosecution presented evidence linking Thaci to a joint criminal enterprise.

The ICC judges emphasized that the evidence demonstrated Thaci’s command responsibility for actions carried out by forces under his authority. Victims' representatives were allowed to speak at the sentencing hearing, describing the personal loss and trauma endured during the war. The court also ordered Thaci to pay reparations to identified victims, though the exact amount will be determined in a separate phase of the proceedings.

In Kosovo, the ruling has immediate political repercussions. Thaci, who has remained a prominent figure in the country’s ruling party despite stepping down from the presidency in 2020, was taken into custody by local authorities and transferred to The Hague to begin his sentence. His health, already a matter of public speculation, will now be monitored under the ICC’s custodial arrangements. The decision is expected to influence upcoming parliamentary elections and the broader discourse on reconciliation in the region.

Background and context

The Kosovo war erupted in 1998 as ethnic Albanian forces sought independence from Serbia, leading to a three‑year conflict marked by widespread violence and displacement. Hashim Thaci emerged as a leading figure in the Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA) and later served as the movement’s political spokesperson. After the war, he transitioned into formal politics, becoming prime minister in 2008 and president in 2016. Throughout his career, Thaci faced accusations of involvement in illicit arms trade and organized crime, but the war‑time allegations remained a point of contention.

The ICC opened an investigation into alleged war crimes in the former Yugoslavia in 2000, eventually issuing an arrest warrant for Thaci in 2020. The court’s jurisdiction covers crimes committed on the territory of Kosovo after the United Nations Interim Administration Mission took over in 1999. Prior to Thaci’s trial, the ICC had convicted other regional leaders, including former Bosnian and Rwandan officials, establishing a precedent for holding high‑ranking politicians accountable for atrocities committed during armed conflict.

What to know next

Thaci has the right to appeal the conviction and the length of the sentence. His legal team has indicated that they will file an appeal on procedural grounds, arguing that certain evidentiary standards were not met. The appeal process could extend for several months, during which time Thaci will remain in custody. Meanwhile, the ICC will begin the separate phase of determining reparations for victims, a step that could involve substantial financial compensation and community‑level projects.

The broader impact of the ruling may affect Kosovo’s diplomatic relations. European Union officials have welcomed the decision as a sign of progress in the region’s commitment to accountability, but they also caution that political stability must be maintained. Serbia’s government has condemned the verdict, describing it as politically motivated, which could strain already delicate negotiations on normalisation of relations. Observers will watch how both governments respond, as the outcome may influence future cooperation on security, trade, and EU integration.
