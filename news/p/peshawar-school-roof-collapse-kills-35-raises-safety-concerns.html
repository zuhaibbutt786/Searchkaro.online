Peshawar school roof collapse kills 35, raises safety concerns

A roof collapse at a school in Peshawar has left at least 35 dead and more than 50 injured. The tragedy has sparked nationwide debate over the condition of school facilities.

Latest developments

The roof of a public school in the Hayatabad area of Peshawar gave way early on Monday, trapping students, teachers and staff inside the collapsed structure. Rescue teams arrived within minutes and worked through the day to pull victims from the rubble. Official counts released by local authorities confirm at least 35 fatalities, including a teacher and more than 30 pupils, while medical services report over 50 injuries ranging from minor cuts to serious fractures. The provincial health department set up emergency wards in nearby hospitals to treat the injured, and the Pakistan Red Crescent Society dispatched volunteers with first‑aid kits. Community members gathered outside the site, chanting prayers and demanding answers from the education department about how such a disaster could occur in a government‑run institution.

Why it matters

The incident has struck a chord across Pakistan, where many parents already worry about the safety of school buildings that were constructed decades ago with limited oversight. Families of the victims are calling for immediate inspections of all school facilities, fearing that similar collapses could happen elsewhere. The tragedy also highlights broader challenges in the education sector, including insufficient funding for maintenance and a lack of clear standards for structural integrity. For students in the region, the loss of classmates and teachers is a profound emotional blow that may affect attendance and learning outcomes for months to come.

Background

Pakistan’s school infrastructure has long suffered from neglect, with many classrooms built from low‑cost materials and lacking regular safety audits. Reports from the Express Tribune have documented how overcrowded classrooms and deteriorating roofs are common in both urban and rural areas. Previous incidents, such as the 2014 collapse of a school in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa that killed several children, prompted limited reforms, but systematic implementation has remained uneven. Funding allocations for school construction often prioritize new buildings over the repair of existing structures, leaving older schools vulnerable to structural failures during heavy rains or seismic activity.

What happens next

The provincial government has announced a commission to investigate the causes of the Peshawar collapse and to recommend corrective measures. Officials say that a comprehensive audit of school buildings in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa will begin within the next two weeks, focusing on roof integrity and load‑bearing capacity. In addition, the education ministry is expected to propose new building codes and allocate emergency funds for the repair of damaged schools. Civil society groups have pledged to monitor the implementation of these measures and to advocate for transparent reporting to the public.
