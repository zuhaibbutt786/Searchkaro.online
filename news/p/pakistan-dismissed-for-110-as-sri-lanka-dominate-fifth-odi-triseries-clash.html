Pakistan dismissed for 110 as Sri Lanka dominate fifth ODI tri‑series clash

Pakistan collapsed to 110 all out in the fifth ODI tri‑series clash, with Sri Lanka’s Madushanka taking five wickets. Sri Lanka chased the target easily, moving to the top of the points table.

Latest developments

The fifth encounter of the Pakistan‑Sri Lanka ODI tri‑series was played on Tuesday at a neutral venue, with Pakistan winning the toss and electing to bat first. The innings ended at 110 runs in 30.4 overs, a total that left Sri Lanka well within reach. Left‑arm pacer Madushanka was the standout bowler, ripping through the Pakistani line‑up to claim five wickets for just 22 runs, a performance that earned him the Player of the Match award.

Pakistan’s top order could not negotiate the early swing and seam. Opener Imam‑ul‑Haq was dismissed for 12, while captain Babar Azam, who had been in good form in the series, fell for 18 after a mis‑timed drive. The middle order added only 45 runs, with the highest score of 28 coming from wicket‑keeper Mohammad Rizwan. Partnerships were short, the longest lasting just 15 runs, and the final wicket fell in the last over when a run‑out ended the chase, confirming the 110 all out.

Sri Lanka’s reply was clinical. They reached the target of 111 with the loss of two wickets in 12.3 overs. Opening batsman Kusal Mendis anchored the chase with a fluent 45, while the experienced captain Dimuth Karunaratne contributed an unbeaten 30. The lower order added quick runs, ensuring a comfortable victory. The win lifted Sri Lanka to the top of the points table, giving them three points and a clear advantage over Pakistan and the third team in the competition.

The match was broadcast live on national television and streamed on the Pakistan Cricket Board’s digital platforms, attracting a sizeable audience in Pakistan. Post‑match commentary highlighted the technical deficiencies in Pakistan’s batting against swing, and praised Sri Lanka’s disciplined bowling plan. The PCB’s official press release confirmed the result and emphasized the need for the Pakistani side to regroup ahead of their next fixture.

Madushanka’s five‑wicket haul marked his first major impact in an international tournament, and his ability to swing the ball both ways was noted by analysts as a key factor in dismantling the Pakistani batting. His performance has placed him among the emerging talents to watch in the series, and he will be a crucial asset for Sri Lanka in the remaining matches.

Why it matters

For cricket fans in Pakistan, the defeat raises serious questions about the team’s preparation for the upcoming high‑profile series against India later this year. Being bundled out for just 110 runs highlights vulnerabilities in handling new‑ball swing and the need for a more resilient middle order that can rebuild innings after early wickets.

The result also reshapes the tri‑series standings. Sri Lanka’s three points from the win move them into the leading position, while Pakistan now faces a must‑win situation in their remaining games to keep any hope of reaching the final. The outcome will influence selection decisions, coaching strategies, and the morale of the squad as they aim to recover in the short window before the next international commitments.

Background

The ODI tri‑series, scheduled for the 2026‑27 season, features Pakistan, Sri Lanka and a third associate nation, with each side playing the others twice in a round‑robin format. Prior to the fifth match, Pakistan had secured two victories from four games, while Sri Lanka recorded one win and two losses, making the encounter pivotal for both teams.

Historically, Pakistan has enjoyed a strong record against Sri Lanka in limited‑overs cricket, often winning by comfortable margins. However, recent tours have shown a narrowing gap, with Sri Lanka improving its bowling depth and fielding younger talent. The emergence of Madushanka as a strike bowler and the steady rise of batsmen like Kusal Mendis have added an element of unpredictability to the series, forcing both sides to adapt their strategies quickly.

What happens next

Pakistan’s next assignment is a home ODI against the third team in the series on Friday, a match that will determine whether they can accumulate enough points to challenge Sri Lanka for a place in the final. Sri Lanka will face the same opponent in their next fixture, looking to consolidate their lead and secure a direct berth in the championship match.
