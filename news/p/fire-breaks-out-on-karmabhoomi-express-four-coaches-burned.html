Fire Breaks Out on Karmabhoomi Express, Four Coaches Burned

Latest developments

On Friday evening, a fire erupted on the Karmabhoomi Express as it passed through Patiala district in Punjab. The blaze began in the fourth coach from the front and quickly spread to three additional coaches, leaving a trail of black smoke that was visible from miles away. Witnesses reported that the train was travelling at a reduced speed when the fire broke out. Passengers were forced to abandon their luggage and, in several cases, jump from the moving train to escape the heat. The incident caused widespread panic among the 300 or more passengers on board. Emergency services arrived within minutes, but the intensity of the fire made it difficult to control. Firefighters used high-pressure water hoses and portable extinguishers, but the flames persisted until the train was halted at the nearest station. The crew managed to bring the train to a stop before the fire reached the engine. The Punjab State Railway Authority has launched an investigation into the cause of the fire. Preliminary reports suggest a short circuit in the electrical wiring of the coaches may have triggered the incident, but officials have not yet confirmed the exact mechanism. No injuries have been reported, but several passengers suffered burns and respiratory irritation from the smoke. The train was taken to a maintenance yard for a thorough inspection, and the affected coaches were removed from service.

Why it matters

The incident underscores the vulnerability of India’s aging rail fleet. With more than 12,000 coaches in service, many of which are older than two decades, safety upgrades have become a pressing concern for the Ministry of Railways. Passengers rely on the Karmabhoomi Express for long-distance travel between Amritsar and Katihar. A disruption of this magnitude can affect thousands of commuters, freight operations, and regional economies that depend on rail connectivity. The fire also raises questions about emergency preparedness on Indian trains. While fire suppression systems exist, the speed at which the blaze spread suggests that additional safeguards may be needed, especially in older coaches.

Background

The Karmabhoomi Express, a daily service that links the northern city of Amritsar with the eastern town of Katihar, has been in operation for over 30 years. The name 'Karmabhoomi' translates to 'land of work' in Hindi, reflecting the train’s role in connecting workers across the country. In recent years, Indian Railways has introduced new safety protocols, including fire-resistant materials and automatic fire detection systems. However, many of the coaches that form the Karmabhoomi Express were built before these upgrades were mandated. The Punjab region has seen a rise in train incidents, with several reports of electrical faults leading to fires in the past decade. This pattern has prompted calls for a comprehensive audit of the rail network’s electrical infrastructure.

What happens next

The railway authorities have announced that the affected coaches will undergo a detailed inspection and will only return to service once they meet safety standards. Passengers who were on the train have been offered compensation and alternative travel arrangements. An independent inquiry is expected to be completed within the next 48 hours. The findings will likely influence future safety regulations and could accelerate the replacement of older coaches across the network.
