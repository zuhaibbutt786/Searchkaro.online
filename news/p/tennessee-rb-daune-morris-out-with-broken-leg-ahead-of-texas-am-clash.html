Tennessee RB Daune Morris Out with Broken Leg Ahead of Texas A&M Clash

Running back Daune Morris was hospitalized with a broken leg after a car crash. The injury forces him to miss the Volunteers' next matchup against Texas A&M.

Latest developments

Daune Morris, the University of Tennessee's starting running back, was involved in a single‑vehicle collision on a highway near Knoxville on Tuesday night. Emergency responders reported that Morris was conscious and alert when they arrived, but he sustained a compound fracture to his left tibia that required immediate orthopedic intervention. He was transported to a regional trauma center where surgeons stabilized the break and placed him under observation. The university confirmed that his condition is stable and that he will remain hospitalized for further evaluation and treatment. The accident occurred as Morris was traveling alone after a team meeting, and police are investigating the cause of the crash.

The injury comes at a critical juncture for the Volunteers, who are set to face Texas A&M in their next regular‑season contest. With Morris out, Tennessee's depth chart at running back shifts, promoting sophomore backup Jalen Carter to the primary role. The team also contends with injuries to wide receiver Jalin Hyatt and defensive end Jalin Hyatt. The coaching staff announced that they will rely more heavily on the passing game and adjust blocking schemes to accommodate the new backfield configuration.

Head coach Josh Heupel addressed the media on Wednesday, stating that the loss of Morris is a significant setback but emphasizing the squad's resilience. He noted that the medical team is providing the best care possible and that the players will rally around their teammate during his recovery. The staff also indicated that they will evaluate the health of other position groups before finalizing the game plan for the Texas A&M showdown.

The upcoming game against Texas A&M, scheduled for Saturday night, carries implications for the Volunteers' conference standing and bowl eligibility. Analysts had projected Tennessee to rely on Morris's dual‑threat ability to balance the offensive attack, and his absence forces a strategic shift. Fans and alumni expressed concern on social media, while the university's athletic department released a statement reaffirming its commitment to player safety and competitive performance.

Daune Morris entered the Tennessee program as a highly recruited four‑star running back from Nashville, quickly earning a starting role as a freshman. Over the past three seasons, he has amassed over 2,500 rushing yards and 25 touchdowns, establishing himself as one of the SEC's most dynamic playmakers. His blend of speed, vision, and pass‑catching ability has made him a focal point of the Volunteers' offensive scheme, often drawing defensive attention that opens opportunities for teammates.

The team will also monitor the health of other injured players during practice this week, making final roster decisions before kickoff.
