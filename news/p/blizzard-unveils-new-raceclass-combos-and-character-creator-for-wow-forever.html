Blizzard unveils new race‑class combos and character creator for WoW: Forever

Blizzard has opened early character creation for its upcoming WoW: Forever update and announced several new race‑class pairings. The changes aim to refresh the experience for long‑time players in the United Kingdom.

Latest developments

Blizzard Entertainment held a live interview this week to outline the scope of its forthcoming WoW: Forever project. The company said the update will revisit the classic look of Azeroth while integrating modern gameplay systems, aiming to satisfy both veteran players and newcomers. As part of the rollout, the studio has enabled players in the United Kingdom to begin creating their characters ahead of the official launch, allowing them to experiment with the new options and secure a place on the initial server list.

The early character creator includes a range of fresh race‑class combinations that were not available in previous expansions. Notably, undead paladins and dwarf shamans have been added, expanding the diversity of play styles. In addition, four further pairings have been confirmed, though Blizzard has not disclosed the exact races and classes involved. The new options are expected to be selectable at the moment of character creation, rather than being unlocked later through gameplay.

Technical details shared by the development team indicate that the underlying engine has been updated to support the expanded roster without compromising performance on typical UK broadband connections. Blizzard also confirmed that the update will retain the core subscription model, but will introduce a one‑time purchase option for players who prefer a non‑subscription route. The company has set a tentative launch window for early 2027, with a closed beta scheduled for the summer.

Industry observers note that the timing of the announcement coincides with a broader push by Blizzard to reinvigorate its flagship franchise after mixed reception to recent releases. By opening character creation early, the studio hopes to generate community buzz and gather feedback on balance issues before the full release. The move also aligns with a marketing campaign that highlights the nostalgic elements of Azeroth while promising fresh content.

Why it matters

For gamers in Great Britain, the new race‑class choices could reshape the social dynamics of guilds and raid groups. Undead paladins, for example, introduce a traditionally hostile race into a traditionally holy role, prompting players to rethink traditional composition strategies. This may lead to a surge in recruitment activity as guilds seek members who can fill these novel slots.

The early access to character creation also provides a tangible incentive for players who have been on the sidelines since the last expansion. By allowing them to secure a character before the launch, Blizzard reduces the barrier to re‑entry, which could boost subscription numbers and in‑game spending on cosmetics and services. The UK market, which has consistently ranked among the top regions for WoW revenue, stands to benefit from any uptick in player engagement.

Furthermore, the announcement has already impacted the secondary market for WoW-related merchandise. Retailers have reported increased demand for branded apparel and collector's items tied to the "Forever" branding, indicating that the update is resonating beyond the digital sphere.

Background

World of Warcraft debuted in 2004 and quickly became a cultural touchstone for online gaming. Over the years, Blizzard has released multiple expansions, each adding new continents, races, classes, and gameplay systems. The most recent major release, Shadowlands, concluded in 2022 and left a mixed legacy among the player base.

In response to community feedback, Blizzard announced a strategic shift toward preserving the core identity of Azeroth while integrating modern design principles. The "Forever" moniker signals an intention to create a lasting version of the game that can evolve without the need for frequent, large‑scale expansions. This approach mirrors trends in the broader industry, where developers aim to sustain long‑term player interest through incremental updates rather than massive overhauls.

The decision to introduce previously unavailable race‑class combinations reflects a longstanding request from the community. Historically, certain races have been limited to specific classes for lore or balance reasons. By loosening these restrictions, Blizzard acknowledges the desire for greater player agency and customization.

What happens next

Blizzard plans to open a closed beta in July, inviting a select group of UK players to test the new race‑class pairings and early character creation workflow. Feedback from this phase will inform final balance adjustments before the public launch. The studio has also indicated that a series of developer livestreams will continue throughout the autumn, offering deeper insight into story elements and gameplay mechanics.

If the beta proceeds without major issues, the full WoW: Forever launch is slated for early 2027. Players can expect a coordinated marketing push, including partnerships with UK gaming influencers and retail promotions.
