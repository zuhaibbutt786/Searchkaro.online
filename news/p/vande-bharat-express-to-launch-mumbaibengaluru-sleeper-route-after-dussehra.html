Vande Bharat Express to launch Mumbai‑Bengaluru sleeper route after Dussehra

The Vande Bharat Express is set to debut a new sleeper service between Mumbai and Bengaluru, covering 1153 km in just 16 hours. The train will offer sound‑proof coaches and hot‑water showers for the first time.

Latest developments

The Indian Railways has announced that the Vande Bharat Express will commence a new Mumbai‑Bengaluru sleeper service after the Dussehra festival. The 1153‑kilometre journey will be completed in about 16 hours, a significant reduction compared to conventional trains that take more than 20 hours for the same route. The train will feature 24 coaches, all sound‑proof to minimise noise from the high‑speed tracks. Each coach will be equipped with a hot‑water shower, a first for Indian passenger coaches, providing a comfortable experience for long‑distance travellers. In addition to the Mumbai‑Bengaluru link, the railways have approved a new Delhi‑Howrah route that will cut travel time by roughly three hours. The new route will serve key stations in Jharkhand, including Kodarma, Parsonath, and Dhanbad, thereby improving connectivity for the eastern states. The design of the 24‑coach train has been finalised by the Integral Coach Factory (ICF). The coaches will incorporate modern amenities such as Wi‑Fi, power outlets, and ergonomic seating, while maintaining the high‑speed capabilities that the Vande Bharat brand is known for. The train’s launch is expected to boost tourism and business travel between the western and southern parts of the country. Overall, the introduction of the sleeper variant of the Vande Bharat Express marks a shift in Indian Railways’ strategy, focusing on passenger comfort and time efficiency for long‑haul journeys. The new service will likely attract a wide range of travellers, from business commuters to leisure tourists, who value speed and convenience. The railways have also indicated that the train will operate with a 24‑hour schedule, allowing passengers to choose departure times that best fit their itineraries. This flexibility, combined with the enhanced amenities, positions the Vande Bharat Express as a competitive alternative to air travel on these routes.

Why it matters

For passengers, the new sleeper service means a drastic cut in travel time between Mumbai and Bengaluru, two of India’s largest economic hubs. A 16‑hour journey allows travellers to reach their destination with a single night’s sleep, reducing the need for overnight stays in cities. The inclusion of sound‑proof coaches and hot‑water showers addresses long‑standing concerns about comfort on Indian trains. These features are likely to increase the appeal of rail travel for business professionals who require a quiet environment to work or rest. The new Delhi‑Howrah route will also benefit commuters in the eastern states, providing a faster link to the capital. The time savings of three hours could translate into significant cost savings for daily commuters and reduce the overall travel burden on the rail network. By offering a high‑speed, comfortable alternative to air travel, the Vande Bharat Express could help reduce carbon emissions associated with short‑haul flights. This aligns with India’s broader sustainability goals and the railways’ commitment to greener transport solutions.

Background

The Vande Bharat Express, launched in 2019, was India's first semi‑automatic, high‑speed train. It was designed to provide faster and more comfortable travel across major cities. Since its inception, the train has expanded to several routes, including Delhi‑Mumbai and Delhi‑Bengaluru. The concept of a sleeper variant emerged from passenger feedback, which highlighted the need for overnight travel options that combine speed with comfort. The Indian Railways has been experimenting with sleeper coaches on other routes, but the Vande Bharat platform offers a unique blend of speed, safety, and modern amenities. The Integral Coach Factory in Chennai has been instrumental in designing the new 24‑coach configuration. The factory’s expertise in building high‑speed coaches has allowed for the integration of advanced features such as noise‑reduction panels and hot‑water systems. Historically, Indian trains have struggled with overcrowding and limited amenities. The introduction of the Vande Bharat sleeper is part of a broader initiative to modernise the rail network and improve passenger experience across the country.

What happens next

The railways will conduct trial runs of the new Mumbai‑Bengaluru route in the weeks following the Dussehra festival. Once the trials confirm safety and performance standards, the service will be fully operational. Ticketing will be integrated with the existing online reservation system, allowing passengers to book seats in advance. The success of the sleeper variant could pave the way for similar services on other long‑haul routes. The railways have indicated plans to explore additional routes, including Delhi‑Kolkata and Mumbai‑Chennai, where demand for fast, comfortable travel is high.
