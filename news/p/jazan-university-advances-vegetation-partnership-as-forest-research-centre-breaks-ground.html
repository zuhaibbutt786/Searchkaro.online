Jazan University advances vegetation partnership as forest research centre breaks ground

Latest developments

Jazan University has announced plans to activate a memorandum of understanding with the National Center for Vegetation Development, a Saudi agency tasked with restoring and expanding the country’s plant cover. The university’s senior officials met with centre representatives last week to outline joint research projects, field surveys and training programmes aimed at improving the health of native vegetation across the Jazan region. The cooperation is expected to channel funding and technical expertise into university laboratories and field stations, strengthening academic capacity in ecological science.

In a parallel ceremony, the Prince of Jazan laid the foundation stone for a new forest research centre that will serve as a hub for scientists studying the kingdom’s forest ecosystems. The building, slated to open in 2027, will house laboratories, a seed bank and a data centre for remote sensing of forest health. Officials said the centre will focus on reforestation, biodiversity monitoring and the development of climate‑resilient tree species, aligning with Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 environmental goals.

The region’s natural assets have also drawn attention, highlighted by a recent feature on Mount Sunadel, a peak in Jazan noted for its varied terrain and unique plant communities. Researchers from the university plan to use the mountain as a living laboratory for the new partnership, examining endemic species and the impact of altitude on vegetation patterns. The site’s biodiversity is expected to provide valuable data for the centre’s reforestation models and for broader conservation efforts in the southwestern part of the kingdom.

Why it matters

The collaboration between Jazan University and the National Center for Vegetation Development represents a concrete step toward Saudi Arabia’s broader environmental agenda, which seeks to increase green cover and mitigate desertification. By focusing on the Jazan region, which boasts a relatively high rainfall and diverse ecosystems compared to the rest of the country, the project can generate scalable solutions for arid and semi‑arid zones nationwide. Successful outcomes could influence policy, attract further investment, and demonstrate the viability of large‑scale ecological restoration.

For local communities, the initiatives promise economic and educational benefits. The forest research centre will create specialized jobs for botanists, agronomists and technicians, while also offering internship opportunities for university students. Enhanced research capacity may lead to the development of new agricultural practices and plant varieties suited to the region’s climate, supporting farmers and diversifying the local economy beyond traditional oil‑related activities.

Background

Jazan Province, located in the southwestern corner of Saudi Arabia, is characterised by a subtropical climate, mountainous terrain and a coastline along the Red Sea. The area receives more rainfall than most parts of the kingdom, supporting a richer array of flora, including several endemic species. Historically, the region’s natural resources have been under pressure from overgrazing, urban expansion and climate change, prompting calls for targeted conservation measures.

Established in 2006, Jazan University has grown into a major academic institution with faculties covering medicine, engineering, and environmental sciences. The university has previously engaged in collaborative projects with government agencies and international partners, focusing on water management, renewable energy and agricultural research. The current memorandum builds on this track record, aiming to integrate scientific research with practical vegetation management across the province.

What happens next

The memorandum is expected to be formally signed within the next two months, after which joint research teams will commence fieldwork on Mount Sunadel and surrounding areas. Construction of the forest research centre is already underway, with the first phase – including laboratory spaces and a seed bank – projected for completion by late 2025. A series of workshops and public outreach events are planned to involve local stakeholders and disseminate findings.

Looking ahead, the partnership may expand to include additional projects such as community‑led tree planting, development of eco‑tourism routes around Mount Sunadel, and the creation of a regional database on plant health. These steps aim to solidify Jazan’s role as a model for sustainable environmental management in Saudi Arabia.
