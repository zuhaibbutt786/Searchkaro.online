Sony pushes new PS5 firmware update as GTA VI launch looms

Sony has issued a fresh PS5 firmware patch aimed at blocking jailbreak tools, while reports indicate Rockstar's GTA VI will require the newer system software. The move fuels a growing firmware conflict ahead of the highly anticipated game.

Latest developments

Sony announced a new firmware update for the PlayStation 5 on Monday, version 23.04, aimed at closing security loopholes that have been exploited by jailbreak tools. The update was rolled out globally through the console’s system settings and includes patches that block known methods of gaining unauthorized access to the console’s operating system. Sony’s statement emphasized that the move is part of its ongoing effort to protect users and preserve the integrity of the PlayStation Network.

The timing of the update coincides with circulating reports that Rockstar Games has raised the minimum firmware requirement for its upcoming title Grand Theft Auto VI. According to sources cited by Wccftech, the game will only run on PS5 consoles that have firmware version 23.00 or later, a shift from earlier expectations that the title would support older system software. Industry analysts suggest the change may be intended to ensure the game can leverage the latest system features and security measures.

The new firmware also reignites a “firmware war” that has been unfolding between Sony and a community of modders who develop jailbreaks for the console. Push Square reported that Sony’s latest patch specifically targets the “PS5 Loader” and “OrbisOS” exploits that were previously used to run unsigned code. In response, some jailbreak developers have warned that they will release work‑arounds, but Sony has indicated that future updates will continue to prioritize security.

Consumer reaction in the United Kingdom has been mixed. While many players appreciate the added protection against malware and piracy, a segment of the user base expressed frustration that the update may limit the ability to run home‑brew applications or preserve older game saves that rely on modified system software. Retailers have noted a slight uptick in inquiries about the update’s impact on existing GTA VI pre‑order packages.

Why it matters

For UK gamers, the firmware requirement could affect the launch experience of one of the most anticipated games of the year. If a player’s console has not been updated, GTA VI may refuse to start, leading to potential delays in playing the game on release day. This could also influence resale values of older PS5 units that have not received the latest updates.

The crackdown on jailbreaks has broader implications for digital rights and the console ecosystem. By tightening security, Sony reduces the risk of malicious software, but it also curtails the ability of hobbyists to explore the hardware for educational or creative purposes. The balance between protection and openness is a recurring debate among tech communities in the UK.

Background

Sony has a history of releasing firmware updates that address both performance improvements and security vulnerabilities. Since the PS5’s launch in 2020, the company has issued dozens of patches, with notable ones in 2022 that blocked early home‑brew tools. The current update follows a series of rapid releases in 2026 aimed at countering a surge in jailbreak activity after a major exploit was disclosed in early October.

Rockstar Games announced Grand Theft Auto VI in early 2024 and has since provided limited technical specifications. Early developer briefings suggested the game would be compatible with the original PS5 firmware, but internal testing reportedly revealed incompatibilities that prompted the higher firmware threshold. The decision aligns with a trend among next‑generation titles to require the latest system software to support advanced graphics and streaming features.

What happens next

Sony is expected to monitor the effectiveness of the 23.04 patch and may issue additional updates if new vulnerabilities emerge. Meanwhile, Rockstar has not issued an official comment on the firmware requirement, but industry insiders anticipate that the company will work with Sony to ensure a smooth rollout of GTA VI on the release date scheduled for early 2027.
