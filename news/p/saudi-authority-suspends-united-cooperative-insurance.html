Saudi Authority Suspends United Cooperative Insurance

Saudi regulators have stopped United Cooperative Insurance from issuing or renewing any policies. The move impacts all of the company's insurance products and raises questions about future operations.

What is happening

The Saudi Insurance Authority announced on Monday that it is suspending United Cooperative Insurance Company (UCI) from issuing new insurance policies and from renewing existing contracts across all product categories. The decision was taken after a formal review of the insurer's compliance with regulatory requirements, and it takes effect immediately. The authority cited a series of documented deficiencies in the company's internal controls and risk management procedures, though it did not disclose the full details of the investigation.

Under the suspension, UCI must cease all underwriting activities, including the issuance of motor, health, property, and commercial insurance documents. Existing policies remain in force until their natural expiration dates, but the company cannot process any renewal applications or extend coverage beyond the current terms. The authority has instructed all distribution channels, including brokers and agents, to stop promoting or selling UCI products until further notice.

United Cooperative Insurance responded with a brief statement acknowledging the regulator's action and confirming that it is cooperating fully with the investigation. The company said it will address the identified gaps, implement corrective measures, and submit a remediation plan to the authority. While it did not comment on the likelihood of an appeal, the statement emphasized the insurer's commitment to protecting policyholders and restoring full operational status as quickly as possible.

For policyholders, the suspension creates uncertainty about the continuity of coverage, especially for those whose policies are due for renewal in the coming months. The authority advised customers to contact their agents or the insurer directly to verify the status of their contracts and to explore alternative providers if needed. Consumer protection groups have urged affected individuals to review their insurance needs and consider temporary coverage options while the regulatory matter is resolved.

Background and context

United Cooperative Insurance was founded in the early 2000s as a joint-stock company offering a range of personal and commercial insurance products. Over the years, it grew to become one of the larger insurers in the Kingdom, serving both individual and corporate clients through a network of branches and digital channels. The company has historically positioned itself as a provider of cooperative insurance solutions, emphasizing community-based risk sharing and customer-oriented service.

The Saudi Insurance Authority, established to supervise and develop the insurance sector, holds the power to issue licenses, set solvency standards, and enforce compliance with Shariah principles and local regulations. Its mandate includes conducting periodic audits of insurers, reviewing financial health, and ensuring that policyholders' rights are protected. When an insurer fails to meet the authority's standards, the regulator can impose sanctions ranging from fines to the suspension of business activities.

In recent years, the authority has taken a more proactive stance, issuing several warnings and corrective orders to firms that fall short of regulatory expectations. Prior to the UCI case, the regulator had issued notices to other insurers regarding underwriting practices, capital adequacy, and claims handling procedures. These actions reflect a broader governmental push to modernize the financial system, increase transparency, and align the insurance industry with international best practices.

What to know next

United Cooperative Insurance is expected to submit a detailed remediation plan to the authority within the next 30 days. The regulator will review the plan and may lift the suspension partially or fully if it is satisfied that the company has addressed the identified deficiencies. An appeal process is also available, allowing the insurer to contest the decision before an administrative board, though such proceedings can extend the timeline for reinstatement.

Policyholders should monitor communications from the insurer and the authority closely. Those with policies approaching renewal dates are advised to confirm whether their contracts will be honored under the current terms and to explore alternative coverage if renewal is not possible. Consumers can also contact the Saudi Consumer Protection Association for guidance on protecting their interests during the suspension period.
