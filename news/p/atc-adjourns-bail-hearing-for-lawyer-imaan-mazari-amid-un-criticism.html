ATC Adjourns Bail Hearing for Lawyer Imaan Mazari Amid UN Criticism

Prominent lawyer Imaan Mazari and her husband Hadi remain in custody after the anti‑terrorism court postponed their bail hearing. UN experts have demanded their release, citing concerns over Pakistan's broad counter‑terrorism statutes.

Latest developments

On Tuesday, the Anti‑Terrorism Court (ATC) in Islamabad postponed the bail hearing of human‑rights lawyer Imaan Mazari and her husband, Hadi, until 13 October. The delay followed the prosecution’s failure to produce the investigating officer, who was absent from the courtroom. The judge expressed frustration at the procedural lapse, noting that the case record had not been presented. Both defendants remain in custody pending further proceedings.

The couple was arrested last month on accusations of supporting extremist activities, charges that their legal representatives describe as politically motivated. Their detention has drawn criticism from domestic legal circles and international observers. The ATC’s decision to adjourn the hearing, rather than proceed, underscores the procedural challenges that have plagued the case since its inception.

UN human‑rights experts have publicly condemned Pakistan’s use of broad counter‑terrorism statutes to target lawyers and activists. In a statement released earlier this week, the experts called for the immediate release of Mazari and Hadi, arguing that the legislation lacks clear definitions and can be applied arbitrarily. The remarks echo similar concerns raised by the United Nations Special Rapporteur on the promotion and protection of human rights.

Meanwhile, the Ministry of Law and Justice has declined to comment on the specifics of the investigation, citing ongoing legal processes. Civil‑society groups have organized peaceful rallies in Lahore and Karachi, demanding transparency and adherence to due process. The case continues to attract media attention across Pakistan, with several outlets highlighting the potential implications for the rule of law.

Why it matters

The prolonged detention of a prominent lawyer raises questions about the balance between national security and civil liberties in Pakistan. If the anti‑terrorism framework is applied without clear evidentiary standards, it could deter lawyers from defending politically sensitive clients, weakening the country’s judicial independence.

Public confidence in the legal system may erode if high‑profile cases appear to be influenced by political considerations. Internationally, the episode could affect Pakistan’s human‑rights reputation, potentially influencing foreign aid and diplomatic relations, especially with nations that prioritize rule‑of‑law benchmarks.

Background

Imaan Mazari, a senior advocate at the Supreme Court, has built a reputation for representing victims of enforced disappearances and challenging state actions. Her husband, Hadi, is also a practicing lawyer who frequently collaborates on human‑rights cases. Their activism has placed them under scrutiny in a climate where the government has intensified its crackdown on dissent following a series of security incidents.

Pakistan’s anti‑terrorism laws, originally drafted in the early 2000s, have been amended several times to broaden the definition of terrorism. Critics argue that the language is vague, allowing authorities to label a wide range of activities as terrorist in nature. The UN experts’ recent criticism reflects a growing international concern that such statutes are being used to suppress lawful expression and advocacy.

What happens next

The next hearing on 13 October will determine whether Mazari and Hadi are granted bail or remain in custody. Legal analysts expect the prosecution to present the missing investigative officer’s report, while defence counsel is likely to intensify calls for the case’s dismissal on grounds of procedural irregularities and violation of fundamental rights.
