Shamyl Hussain shines as Pakistan A clinches narrow win over Nepal

Pakistan A edged past Nepal by 96 runs to stay alive in the Asia Cup Rising Stars. Young bowler Shamyl Hussain played a key role in the victory.

Latest developments

Pakistan A secured a 96-run victory over Nepal on Saturday, defending a modest total of 168 runs to stay in contention at the Asia Cup Rising Stars tournament. The match, played at a neutral venue, saw Nepal chase a steep target but fall short, ending their campaign with a heavy defeat. Pakistan A's bowlers applied pressure throughout the innings, limiting Nepal to 72 runs before the final overs. Among the bowlers, young pacer Shamyl Hussain delivered a disciplined spell, picking up crucial wickets and maintaining a tight line and length that frustrated the Nepalese batsmen. His performance contributed significantly to the team's ability to defend the low total.

The win marks Pakistan A's third victory in the group stage, keeping their hopes of progressing to the knockout rounds alive. The team's captain praised the collective effort, highlighting the resilience shown after early setbacks in the innings. While the scoreboard reflected a modest total, the disciplined fielding and strategic bowling changes turned the match in Pakistan's favour. Shamyl Hussain, who was included in the squad as a promising talent, demonstrated composure beyond his years, earning commendation from coaching staff.

The defeat sent Nepal packing from the tournament, as reported by The Kathmandu Post, which noted the 96-run margin as a decisive end to their campaign. ESPN and Cricinfo both covered the match, emphasizing Pakistan A's defensive tactics and the importance of the win in the context of the group standings. The match also provided a platform for emerging players like Hussain to showcase their skills on an international stage, potentially influencing future selections for the senior national side.

Held annually, the competition pits emerging talent against each other, offering exposure to different playing conditions and high-pressure situations. Pakistan A entered the 2026-27 edition with a blend of experienced youngsters and fresh faces, aiming to build a cohesive unit capable of challenging the region's best.
