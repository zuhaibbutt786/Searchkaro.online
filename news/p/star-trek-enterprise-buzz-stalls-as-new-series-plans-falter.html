Star Trek: Enterprise buzz stalls as new series plans falter

The UK audience is following the fate of a proposed Star Trek: United series, which appears to have been shelved. Meanwhile, actor Scott Bakula is moving on to a new detective drama on CBS.

Latest developments

A recent announcement from CBS has revealed that Scott Bakula will be the lead in a new detective drama, a project that is already in development under Dean Devlin’s Electric Entertainment. Bakula, best known for his role in NCIS: New Orleans, has been linked to the show for several months, and the move comes after the cancellation of his previous series five years ago. At the same time, rumours about a new Star Trek series titled Star Trek: United have been circulating, but the project appears to have been abandoned following the departure of the production team that had been attached to the franchise. Reports indicate that the team that had been working on Star Trek: United has shifted its focus to a CBS cop show, leaving the Star Trek: United concept without a clear path forward. The combination of Bakula’s new role and the uncertainty around Star Trek: United has left fans in the UK uncertain about the future of the franchise, especially after the original Enterprise series ended in 2005.

Why it matters

For viewers in Great Britain, the news signals a shift in the television landscape, with popular actors moving to new projects and long-standing franchises facing potential cancellation. The lack of a new Star Trek: United series means that the franchise’s next chapter may be delayed, affecting fan engagement and merchandise sales. At the same time, Bakula’s move to CBS could bring a fresh audience to the detective drama, potentially boosting viewership for the network in a competitive market.

Background

Star Trek: Enterprise first aired in 2001 as the franchise’s first pre‑saga series, chronicling the early days of the United Federation of Planets. The show ran for five seasons before concluding in 2005, and it has since maintained a dedicated fan base that has followed the franchise’s evolution. In recent years, the franchise has explored new directions, including the 2017 reboot series and various spin‑off projects, but a new series has yet to materialise.

What happens next

Industry insiders suggest that CBS will continue to develop Bakula’s detective drama, with a pilot expected to air later this year. Meanwhile, the fate of Star Trek: United remains uncertain, with no official confirmation from Paramount or CBS, leaving fans to wait for further announcements.
