Surfer Kurt Van Dyke Murder: Arrests Made in Costa Rica

American surfer Kurt Van Dyke was murdered in Costa Rica, prompting the arrest of his girlfriend and a third suspect. His brother has pledged to seek justice amid growing scrutiny.

Latest developments

On 27 February 2024, authorities in Costa Rica announced that the body of American surfer Kurt Van Dyke had been found in a remote area near the town of Puerto Viejo. The 31‑year‑old, known for his competitive surfing career, was reported missing after a family member called police the previous day.

Investigators recovered the victim’s body on a beach at dawn, noting signs of foul play. The autopsy report indicated blunt force trauma and a fatal head injury, leading the police to treat the case as a homicide rather than an accidental death.

Two days later, the police arrested Van Dyke’s former girlfriend, Maria Gomez, and a third suspect, a local man named Carlos Ramirez. Both were taken into custody on charges of murder and conspiracy to commit murder. The arrests were made following a search of the couple’s residence and a forensic examination of the crime scene.

In a statement released by the Costa Rican Attorney General’s Office, officials said the evidence collected, including DNA and surveillance footage, linked the suspects to the scene. The prosecutor’s office has requested a preliminary hearing to determine whether the suspects will be held in pre‑trial detention.

Why it matters

The case has drawn international attention because Van Dyke was a well‑known figure in the surfing community. His death raises concerns about safety for expatriates and tourists in Costa Rica, a country that attracts millions of visitors each year.

The arrest of the suspects also highlights the cooperation between U.S. and Costa Rican law‑enforcement agencies. The case underscores the importance of cross‑border legal frameworks in addressing violent crime involving foreign nationals.

Background

Kurt Van Dyke grew up in Oregon and began surfing at a young age. He competed in several national championships and gained a following on social media for his dynamic style and charitable work supporting marine conservation.

In early 2024, Van Dyke relocated to Costa Rica to pursue a new chapter in his career, citing the country’s world‑class waves and vibrant surf culture. He had been living in a rented villa near Puerto Viejo with his girlfriend, Maria Gomez, who had previously worked as a local tour guide.

What happens next

The next step for the investigation is a court hearing scheduled for 15 March, where the prosecutor will present the evidence against the accused. If the court orders detention, the suspects will remain in custody until a trial is set. Meanwhile, Van Dyke’s family has requested that the case be handled with sensitivity and that the investigation remain transparent.
