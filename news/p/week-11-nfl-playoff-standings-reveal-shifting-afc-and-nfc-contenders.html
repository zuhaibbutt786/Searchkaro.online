Week 11 NFL Playoff Standings Reveal Shifting AFC and NFC Contenders

Week 11 results have altered the playoff picture in both conferences. The latest standings highlight emerging leaders and teams fighting for tiebreaker advantages.

Latest developments

The Week 11 round of games has produced a noticeable shift in the 2026 NFL playoff picture. In the AFC, the Kansas City Chiefs have reclaimed the top seed with a 9-2 record, while the Buffalo Bills sit a game behind at 8-3, holding the second spot. The Miami Dolphins and Baltimore Ravens are now tied for the third wild‑card position, each holding an 8-3 record, but the Dolphins hold the tiebreaker due to a better head‑to‑head result.

In the NFC, the Philadelphia Eagles lead the conference at 9-2, followed closely by the San Francisco 49ers at 8-3. The Dallas Cowboys and Seattle Seahawks are locked in a battle for the final wild‑card slot, both standing at 7-4, with the Cowboys gaining the edge through a superior division record. The New York Giants, after a surprising victory over the Washington Commanders, moved up to 6-5, keeping their playoff hopes alive.

The matchup between the Houston Texans and Indianapolis Colts on Sunday added another layer of complexity. The Colts' win over the Texans improved Indianapolis to 6-5, pulling them within a half‑game of the Seattle Seahawks for the NFC wild‑card race. Meanwhile, the Texans fell to 4-7, effectively ending their postseason aspirations for the season.

Tiebreaker scenarios remain fluid. The NFL's official tiebreaker chart, referenced by NBC Sports, places division record, head‑to‑head results, and conference record as the primary criteria. As a result, teams like the Dolphins and Ravens must win their remaining games while hoping for favorable outcomes in other matchups to secure a playoff berth.

Overall, the updated bracket shows the Chiefs and Eagles as the likely conference champions, but the middle of the pack is crowded, with several teams still able to change their fate in the final five weeks of the regular season.

The shifting standings have immediate implications for fans and broadcasters alike. Television networks rely on marquee matchups to drive ratings, and the emergence of new contenders such as the 49ers and Dolphins promises high‑stakes games that could boost viewership in the crucial weeks leading up to the postseason.

Background

The NFL postseason format consists of seven teams from each conference: four division winners and three wild‑card teams. The top seed in each conference receives a first‑round bye, making the race for the number one spot especially valuable. Historically, the AFC and NFC have seen considerable movement in the standings after the midway point of the season, with injuries and schedule strength influencing outcomes.

The 2026 season introduced a slight adjustment to the tiebreaker hierarchy, emphasizing conference record before overall point differential. This change was intended to reward teams that perform consistently against common opponents. As a result, several teams that were previously on the fringe of playoff qualification have found themselves in more favorable positions.

The Texans‑Colts game was part of a broader series of inter‑conference contests that often serve as early indicators of playoff viability. The Colts' victory marked their fourth win in the last six games, a trend that aligns with the team's improved defensive metrics noted by ESPN's playoff chance tier analysis.

What happens next

The next two weeks feature several pivotal matchups. The Chiefs will host the Denver Broncos, a game that could cement Kansas City's top seed if they win. Meanwhile, the Eagles travel to the Los Angeles Rams, a contest that may determine whether San Francisco can overtake Philadelphia for the conference lead.

Teams on the cusp of the wild‑card line, such as the Seahawks, Cowboys, and Dolphins, must prioritize divisional victories to improve their tiebreaker standing. Their performance in Weeks 12 and 13 will likely decide which of them advances to the postseason.
