India raises windfall tax on diesel, cuts petrol export duty to zero

The government has lifted the export duty on petrol and raised the windfall tax on diesel and aviation fuel exports. The changes also see a ₹3 per litre increase in the diesel levy.

Latest developments

The Union Finance Ministry announced that the export duty on petrol will be set to zero, ending the small levy that had been in place for several years. This move is intended to boost domestic supply and lower retail prices for motorists. At the same time, the ministry increased the windfall tax on diesel and aviation turbine fuel (ATF) exports, adding an extra levy on each litre exported. Additionally, the diesel excise duty has been raised by ₹3 per litre, bringing the total tax burden on diesel to a higher level. The combined policy shift aims to balance fiscal needs with fuel price stability, according to the ministry's statement, and will take effect from the start of the next fiscal quarter.

Why it matters

For Indian consumers, the removal of the petrol export duty could translate into marginally lower pump prices, especially in regions where petrol demand is high. However, the ₹3 increase in the diesel levy may raise the cost of diesel‑powered transport, affecting logistics companies and public transport operators. Exporters of diesel and aviation fuel will face higher tax outlays, potentially reducing profit margins and making Indian fuel less competitive in overseas markets. The additional revenue is expected to support the government's fiscal deficit, which has widened due to pandemic‑related spending.

Background

Windfall taxes are levied on profits that exceed normal returns, often applied to sectors that benefit from sudden price spikes. In India, the government introduced a windfall tax on fuel exports in 2022 after global oil prices surged, aiming to capture excess earnings for public coffers. Petrol and diesel have long been subject to multiple layers of taxation, including excise, GST, and state levies. Export duties are used sparingly, primarily to protect domestic supply. The recent policy shift follows a pattern of adjusting fuel taxes in response to global price volatility and domestic inflation concerns.

What happens next

Industry groups have said they will monitor the impact of the higher windfall tax and may seek clarification on the calculation methodology. The finance ministry has indicated that it will review the tax rates after six months, allowing adjustments if export volumes or price dynamics change significantly.

Summary

- Export duty on petrol removed, aiming to keep domestic supply steady. - Windfall tax on diesel and aviation fuel exports raised, increasing government revenue. - Diesel excise duty lifted by ₹3 per litre, likely raising pump prices for diesel. - Consumers may see lower petrol prices but higher diesel costs. - Exporters face tighter margins; the government will review the measures in six months.
