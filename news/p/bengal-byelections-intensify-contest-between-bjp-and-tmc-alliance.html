Bengal by‑elections intensify contest between BJP and TMC alliance

Six seats across West Bengal are being contested in a high‑stakes by‑election. The results could reshape the Mamata‑Congress partnership and influence national power dynamics.

Latest developments

The Election Commission has scheduled by‑elections for six vacant seats in West Bengal, including the high‑profile constituencies of Nandigram and Rejigarh. Early returns show the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) holding a clear lead in Nandigram, a seat historically contested by the All India Trinamool Congress (TMC). In Nandigram, local party workers and opposition leaders have alleged irregularities in vote counting, prompting the Indian National Congress to call for a re‑poll. The demand for a fresh vote was amplified after a video surfaced showing ballot boxes being opened prematurely, a claim the Congress party described as "vote theft".

In Rejigarh, the contest is tighter. The BJP has positioned the seat as a strategic foothold to expand its influence in the southern part of the state, while the TMC‑Congress alliance is fighting to retain the constituency. Sources close to the alliance say that the outcome in Rejigarh, together with Nandigram, will determine whether the Mamata‑Congress partnership can survive the next electoral cycle. The BJP, meanwhile, has intensified its ground campaign, deploying senior leaders to rally support and highlighting development promises.

Beyond West Bengal, the by‑elections coincide with similar contests in Tamil Nadu, where the state chief minister secured two seats, and in Assam, where several constituencies are also up for voting. Analysts note that the simultaneous polls create a broader narrative about the BJP's national momentum and the opposition's ability to coordinate across state lines. However, observers caution that each state's local dynamics remain distinct, and the West Bengal results will be judged on their own merits.

Why it matters

West Bengal is India's most populous state, and its political climate often sets the tone for national discourse. A decisive win for the BJP in both Nandigram and Rejigarh would signal a weakening of the TMC's dominance and could embolden the party ahead of the 2029 general elections. Conversely, a strong showing by the TMC‑Congress alliance would reinforce Mamata Banerjee's claim that her regional platform remains resilient despite the BJP's aggressive expansion.

The by‑elections also have immediate implications for the Mamata‑Congress coalition, which has been strained by policy disagreements and seat‑sharing disputes. If the alliance loses the two pivotal seats, internal criticism may intensify, potentially leading to a realignment before the next state assembly election. For ordinary voters, the outcome could affect resource allocation, local development projects, and the implementation of welfare schemes that were promised during the 2021 state election.

Background

In the 2021 West Bengal assembly election, the TMC secured a commanding majority, winning 213 of 294 seats, while the BJP emerged as the principal opposition with 77 seats. Since then, the BJP has focused on building a robust party infrastructure, targeting districts where it performed well in 2021, such as North 24 Parganas and South 24 Parganas, which include Nandigram and Rejigarh. The by‑elections are the first major test of the BJP's post‑2021 strategy in the state.

Nandigram holds particular symbolic weight. The area was the flashpoint of a 2007 land acquisition movement that brought national attention to the region and helped launch Mamata Banerjee's political career. Over the past decade, the constituency has swung between TMC and BJP control, making it a bellwether for voter sentiment. Rejigarh, meanwhile, has been a contested seat since the 2016 assembly polls, with narrow margins that reflect a deeply divided electorate.

What happens next

Vote counting is scheduled to begin on October 12, with results expected by the evening. If the Congress's demand for a re‑poll in Nandigram is upheld, the Election Commission will announce a fresh voting date within two weeks, extending the uncertainty for all parties. Both the BJP and the TMC‑Congress alliance have signaled readiness to contest any legal challenges, and political analysts expect a flurry of post‑poll statements that will shape campaign narratives for the upcoming 2029 state elections.
