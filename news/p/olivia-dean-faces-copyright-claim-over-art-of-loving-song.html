Olivia Dean Faces Copyright Claim Over 'Art of Loving' Song

British singer Olivia Dean has been accused of copying Bill Withers' 1981 classic in her new single. The lawsuit has sparked debate over originality in the UK music scene.

Latest developments

In a filing submitted to the High Court in London, the estate of late soul singer Bill Withers has alleged that Olivia Dean’s track 'Art of Loving', released in March, reproduces the melodic hook and chord progression of Withers’ 1981 hit 'Just the Two of Us'. The complaint cites similarities in the song’s opening bars and the arrangement of the vocal harmonies. The lawsuit claims that the similarity is not coincidental and that Dean’s version was produced after Withers’ song had entered the public domain of influence. The claim lists specific timestamps where the two songs overlap, including the first 12 seconds and the bridge section that features a repeated vocal phrase. Industry observers have noted that the case follows a trend of high‑profile copyright disputes in the UK, as artists and record labels seek to protect intellectual property. Some commentators argue that the legal action could set a precedent for how contemporary pop songs are evaluated against older classics. As of now, Olivia Dean has not issued a public statement regarding the allegations. The court has scheduled a preliminary hearing for June, during which both parties will present evidence and expert testimony on musical structure.

Why it matters

For listeners in Great Britain, the case highlights the fine line between inspiration and infringement. The debate has prompted radio stations to review their playlists to ensure compliance with copyright law. The outcome could influence how emerging artists approach songwriting, potentially encouraging more careful research into existing melodies before recording new material. It also raises awareness among fans about the legal frameworks that govern the music industry.

Background

Bill Withers, best known for his smooth blend of soul and folk, released 'Just the Two of Us' in 1981 as a collaboration with jazz guitarist Grover Washington Jr. The track reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and remains a staple of classic‑rock and adult contemporary radio. Olivia Dean, a rising pop singer who gained prominence in 2021, has built a reputation for catchy hooks and heartfelt lyrics. Her 2023 single 'Art of Loving' was promoted as a fresh take on romantic balladry, drawing attention from both fans and critics.

What happens next

Legal experts predict that the case will proceed through discovery, with both sides gathering recordings, production notes and expert analyses. If the court finds that Dean’s work infringes on Withers’ composition, she could face financial penalties and a requirement to remove the track from streaming platforms.

Summary

Olivia Dean sued for copying Bill Withers’ 1981 hit 'Just the Two of Us' in her single 'Art of Loving'. The High Court in London will hold a preliminary hearing in June to examine the evidence. The case may set a precedent for future copyright disputes in the UK music industry.
