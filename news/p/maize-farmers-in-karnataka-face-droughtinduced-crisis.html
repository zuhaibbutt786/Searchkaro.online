Maize Farmers in Karnataka Face Drought‑Induced Crisis

Maize crops in Karnataka's Hassan district are wilting under severe drought, prompting farmers to seek relief. In neighboring Raichur, farmers are demanding a warabandi system to secure water for their fields.

Latest developments

The fields of maize in Hassan district have turned brown as the monsoon failed to deliver the expected rainfall, leaving farmers with little hope for a harvest. Local reports indicate that the crop is largely dry, with many farmers describing the situation as bleak.

The drought is not confined to Hassan alone. In Belagavi, grape growers are also experiencing severe water shortages, relying on tanker water as the only source of irrigation. This pattern points to a wider regional water crisis that is affecting multiple crops.

In Raichur, farmers have taken a different approach, demanding the implementation of a warabandi system – a rotational water distribution schedule that has been used in other parts of the state. They argue that such a system would allow them to receive water at regular intervals, improving the chances of crop survival.

So far, the state government has not announced a definitive plan to address the water shortage. Farmers are waiting for clear guidance on whether the warabandi system will be introduced or if alternative relief measures will be provided.

Why it matters

The failure of maize crops threatens the livelihoods of thousands of farmers in Karnataka, many of whom rely on this staple for both income and food security. A poor harvest could lead to increased prices and reduced availability of maize for local markets.

Beyond the immediate economic impact, the situation underscores the challenges of water management in a region that has historically depended on monsoon rains. The lack of reliable water sources forces farmers to seek alternative irrigation methods, which can be costly and unsustainable.

The crisis also has ripple effects on local economies. Reduced agricultural output can lead to job losses in processing and distribution sectors, and may strain municipal resources as communities cope with food shortages.

Background

Maize is a key crop in Karnataka, especially in districts like Hassan where it is grown for both domestic consumption and commercial sale. The crop typically requires a steady supply of water during the monsoon season.

Karnataka has experienced several droughts in recent years, prompting the government to experiment with various water conservation and distribution strategies. However, the effectiveness of these measures has varied across districts.

The warabandi system, which allocates water in a scheduled rotation among farmers, has been successfully implemented in some parts of the state. It aims to ensure that each farmer receives a fair share of water at set times, reducing competition and improving crop outcomes.

What happens next

The state government is expected to review the feasibility of implementing the warabandi system in Raichur and other affected districts. If approved, the system would require coordination between local water authorities and farmer groups.

In the meantime, farmers may look to alternative crops that require less water or adopt more efficient irrigation techniques. Government relief packages, if announced, could provide financial assistance or subsidies for water procurement.

Summary

• Maize fields in Hassan are drying up due to a severe drought. • Water shortages are affecting other crops, such as grapes in Belagavi. • Farmers in Raichur are demanding a warabandi water distribution system. • The state government has yet to announce a definitive response. • The crisis threatens food security, farmer incomes, and local economies.
