Quaid-e-Azam Trophy kicks off with 20 regional sides in November

Twenty regional sides will battle in the Gold and Silver divisions of the Quaid-e-Azam Trophy from 3 November. Babar Azam and Shaheen Afridi lead Karachi Whites and Lahore Blues respectively.

Latest developments

The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) released the official schedule for the 2026-27 Quaid-e-Azam Trophy on Monday, confirming that the tournament will start on 3 November. Twenty regional teams will compete across two divisions – Gold and Silver – with matches slated to run until late December. The board also published the complete list of squads, naming the players selected for each side. Among the announced line‑ups, Babar Azam will lead the Karachi Whites in the Gold division, while fast‑bowler Shaheen Afridi is set to captain the Lahore Blues in the Silver tier. Their teams are scheduled to meet in the opening round, a clash that has drawn considerable attention from fans and analysts alike. The PCB also confirmed that the Gold division will feature six matches per team, while the Silver division will have five, with points awarded on the traditional first‑innings lead system. Venues across the country, including the National Stadium in Karachi and Gaddafi Stadium in Lahore, will host the fixtures, ensuring a wide geographic spread for spectators. The schedule also outlines a mid‑tournament break in early December, allowing teams to regroup before the final round of matches. PCB officials indicated that the break will be used for player assessments and to accommodate any weather‑related disruptions that may arise. Television partner PTV Sports will broadcast all Gold division matches live, while the Silver games will be streamed on the PCB’s digital platform. Officials expect the coverage to attract a younger audience, especially as schools reopen after the summer break.

Why it matters

The Quaid-e-Azam Trophy remains Pakistan’s premier first‑class competition, serving as a key pathway for players aspiring to national selection. With Babar Azam and Shaheen Afridi leading two of the strongest sides, performances in the early rounds are likely to influence the composition of the upcoming international fixtures, including the home Test series against England. Domestic sponsors and broadcasters also view the tournament as a barometer for cricket’s commercial health in the country. Higher attendance and television ratings in the opening matches could boost revenue streams for the PCB, which has been seeking to stabilize its finances after recent budgetary pressures.

Background

The Quaid-e-Azam Trophy, named after the founder of Pakistan, Muhammad Ali Jinnah, has been contested since 1953. Over the decades it has produced several of the nation’s cricketing legends, and its format has evolved from a simple knockout to a multi‑tier league system. In recent years the PCB introduced a Gold and Silver division to increase competitiveness and provide more match practice for emerging talent. The two‑tier structure also allows weaker regional sides to develop without facing the full strength of the traditional powerhouses in every game.

What happens next

The opening round will be played on 3 November, with Karachi Whites taking on Lahore Blues at the National Stadium. The PCB has indicated that match results and individual performances will be closely monitored ahead of the selection meetings for the national Test squad later in the month.
