Kolkata High Court Issues 48‑Hour Ultimatum to Former BDO Prasanta Barman

The Kolkata High Court has issued a 48‑hour ultimatum to former BDO Prasanta Barman, threatening salary stoppage and property seizure if he fails to comply. The order follows allegations of misconduct and non‑compliance with court directive

Latest developments

On Thursday, the Kolkata High Court issued a formal notice to Prasanta Barman, the former Block Development Officer (BDO) of Rajganj, demanding that he surrender within 48 hours.

The notice warns that failure to comply will trigger the immediate stoppage of his salary and the seizure of all assets registered in his name.

The order follows a series of complaints lodged by local residents and officials alleging that Mr. Barman misappropriated development funds and ignored directives issued by the court and the state government. Earlier filings had highlighted irregularities in the allocation of municipal resources and a lack of transparency in his office.

In the notice, the court explicitly stated that any delay beyond the stipulated deadline would result in enforcement actions, including the freezing of bank accounts and the confiscation of property. The court also directed the state police to assist in the execution of the order if necessary.

Mr. Barman has not yet issued a public statement or responded to the court’s demand. His legal counsel is reportedly preparing a defense, but the court has made clear that the 48‑hour window is non‑negotiable. The case has attracted attention from media outlets across West Bengal, highlighting concerns about corruption in local governance.

The Kolkata High Court’s swift action is part of a broader effort to hold public officials accountable and restore confidence in the administrative machinery. Similar cases involving former BDOs have been brought to court in recent months, signaling a tougher stance on corruption.

Why it matters

For residents of Rajganj and the wider West Bengal region, the case underscores the importance of transparent governance and the rule of law. When officials are held accountable, it can deter future misconduct and encourage public participation in monitoring development projects.

The ruling also sends a clear message to other public servants that the judiciary will not tolerate breaches of duty. It may prompt a review of internal controls within district administrations and prompt reforms aimed at preventing misuse of public funds.

Background

Prasanta Barman served as the Block Development Officer in Rajganj, a subdivision of the North 24 Parganas district. The role of a BDO involves overseeing the implementation of development schemes, maintaining land records, and ensuring the proper use of government resources.

Earlier investigations revealed that Mr. Barman had failed to submit required reports and had allegedly diverted funds earmarked for infrastructure projects. These allegations led to a formal complaint filed by the district administration, which was subsequently forwarded to the High Court for adjudication.

What happens next

If Mr. Barman does not surrender within the 48‑hour period, the court will proceed with the enforcement measures outlined in the notice. This could involve the immediate suspension of his salary, the seizure of his bank accounts, and the confiscation of any property linked to the alleged misappropriation. Additionally, criminal charges may be filed under the Prevention of Corruption Act.
