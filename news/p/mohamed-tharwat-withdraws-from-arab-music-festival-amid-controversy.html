Mohamed Tharwat Withdraws from Arab Music Festival Amid Controversy

Egyptian artist Mohamed Tharwat has withdrawn from the Arab Music Festival following a disagreement over remarks he deemed insulting to Egyptian art. The decision has ignited criticism from prominent musicians and raised questions about art

Latest developments

On 1 October 2026, Egyptian singer Mohamed Tharwat announced he would no longer perform at the Arab Music Festival scheduled in Riyadh. The announcement came after a heated exchange with festival organizers over remarks he considered disrespectful to Egyptian culture.

Tharwat’s statement, posted on social media, cited the need to protect the dignity of Egyptian art. He said that the comments made by a senior festival official were offensive and could tarnish the reputation of the country’s artistic heritage.

The withdrawal was met with swift responses from Saudi musicians. Several prominent artists condemned the remarks and called for a broader dialogue on cultural respect. The incident has drawn attention to the growing tensions between artists and event organizers across the Gulf.

Festival officials confirmed that the remarks were made by a senior program director, who later issued a brief apology. However, they denied that the comments reflected the festival’s official stance.

Why it matters

Saudi Arabia’s music scene has been expanding rapidly, with festivals attracting international talent. The controversy threatens to stall this momentum, as artists fear their creative expression may be curtailed by political sensitivities.

For Saudi listeners, the incident highlights the delicate balance between preserving cultural values and embracing artistic diversity. It also raises concerns about censorship and the role of public figures in shaping cultural narratives.

The fallout could influence future collaborations between Gulf and Egyptian artists, potentially limiting cross‑regional performances and exchanges that have been pivotal for the region’s cultural economy.

If the dispute is not resolved, other artists may reconsider participating in Gulf‑hosted events, which could reduce the diversity of acts and limit opportunities for regional collaboration.

Background

Mohamed Tharwat, whose name translates to ‘wealth’ in Arabic, has been a prominent figure in Egyptian pop music for over a decade. His work has blended traditional melodies with contemporary pop, earning him a loyal fan base.

The Arab Music Festival, organized annually in Riyadh, aims to showcase leading Arab musicians and promote cultural unity. In recent years, it has become a platform for artists from Egypt, Lebanon, and the Gulf.

Previous disputes over artistic content have surfaced in the region, but this is the first high‑profile case involving a major artist withdrawing from a major festival over perceived insults.

Tharwat has previously faced criticism for his outspoken views on social media, but he has also been praised for championing traditional Egyptian music. His withdrawal marks a rare instance of an artist taking a stand against perceived cultural disrespect.

What happens next

Festival organizers have issued a statement promising to review the incident and ensure respectful dialogue with performers. They have also invited independent mediators to facilitate discussions.

Meanwhile, Tharwat has expressed willingness to return to the festival if conditions are adjusted. He has called for a transparent review of the remarks that triggered his withdrawal.

Both parties have agreed to keep the discussion confidential while a formal mediation process is set up. The outcome may set a precedent for how cultural disputes are handled in the region.

The festival’s organizers are also reviewing their internal policies to prevent similar incidents. They plan to introduce a code of conduct for all participants.
