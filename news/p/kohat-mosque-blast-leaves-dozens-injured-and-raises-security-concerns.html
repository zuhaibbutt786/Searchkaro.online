Kohat mosque blast leaves dozens injured and raises security concerns

A bomb detonated near the Police Lines mosque in Kohat, killing at least 15 and wounding more than 50. The attack has triggered national debate over security and counter‑terrorism policies.

What is happening

On a Saturday morning, an explosive device detonated close to the Police Lines mosque in the city of Kohat, located in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. The blast occurred as worshippers were gathering for prayers, resulting in a high number of casualties. Local authorities confirmed that at least fifteen people lost their lives and more than fifty others were treated for injuries ranging from shrapnel wounds to severe burns.

Emergency services arrived quickly, establishing a triage area and transporting the injured to nearby hospitals. The police cordoned off the surrounding streets, and bomb disposal units began a systematic search for additional devices. Initial statements from the provincial government described the incident as a "terrorist act" and promised a thorough investigation.

Witnesses reported hearing a loud bang followed by a cloud of smoke and dust. Many described panic as people tried to flee the scene, while others were unable to escape the confined space of the mosque courtyard. The explosion also caused damage to the mosque's structure, shattering windows and collapsing part of the roof.

The incident has been reported by several national news outlets, each providing slightly different casualty figures, but the consensus remains that the attack was one of the deadliest in the area in recent years. Security forces have detained several individuals for questioning, and a high‑level inquiry has been launched to determine the perpetrators and their motives.

Background and context

Kohat has historically been a flashpoint for militant activity due to its proximity to the tribal areas along the Afghan border. Over the past decade, the city has experienced intermittent attacks on security forces, schools, and public gatherings. The Police Lines area, in particular, houses a large contingent of law‑enforcement personnel, making it a symbolic target for groups seeking to undermine state authority.

The Pakistani government has launched multiple counter‑terrorism operations in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa region, aiming to dismantle insurgent networks. While these efforts have reduced large‑scale attacks in some districts, sporadic incidents continue to occur, often attributed to splinter factions. The recent mosque blast follows a series of attacks on religious venues, including a 2022 bombing in Peshawar that killed dozens.

Security experts point to a complex mix of ideological, political, and economic factors that fuel such violence. The ongoing conflict in neighboring Afghanistan also creates a porous environment where weapons and fighters can move across borders, complicating containment strategies.

What to know next

Authorities have pledged to increase security measures around places of worship, especially during peak prayer times. Additional checkpoints, metal detectors, and patrols are expected to be deployed in Kohat and other vulnerable cities. The investigation team is reviewing CCTV footage, forensic evidence, and intelligence reports to identify the bomb's origin and the group behind it.

Families of the victims are being offered counseling and financial assistance, while the government has announced a day of mourning. International aid agencies have expressed readiness to provide medical supplies and support to the affected communities. The situation remains fluid, and further updates are likely as the inquiry progresses.
