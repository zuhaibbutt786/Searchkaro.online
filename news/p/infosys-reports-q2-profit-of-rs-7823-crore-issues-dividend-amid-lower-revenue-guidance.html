Infosys reports Q2 profit of Rs 7,823 crore, issues dividend amid lower revenue guidance

Infosys announced a second‑quarter profit of Rs 7,823 crore and a Rs 26 interim dividend. The company also lowered its revenue outlook, leading brokers to adjust their ratings.

Latest developments

Infosys Ltd., one of India's largest IT services firms, reported a net profit of Rs 7,823 crore for the second quarter, a 6% increase year‑on‑year. The company declared an interim dividend of Rs 26 per share, which will be credited directly to shareholders' bank accounts. The profit rise was driven by higher margins in digital services and cost‑control measures, according to the earnings release.

Despite the profit growth, the firm trimmed its revenue guidance for the current fiscal year, citing a slower pace of new contract wins and heightened competition in the global outsourcing market. Analysts had expected a stronger top‑line performance, and the shortfall prompted several brokerage houses to revise their ratings on Infosys, with some moving the stock from "buy" to "hold".

The market reaction was mixed. Shares opened lower on the news but recovered partially during the trading session as investors weighed the attractive dividend against the softer revenue outlook. Institutional investors expressed caution, while retail investors appeared reassured by the cash return. The dividend payout, amounting to roughly 15% of the share price, is seen as a signal of confidence in the company's cash flow despite the guidance revision.

Why it matters

The dividend announcement provides immediate cash returns to shareholders, which is particularly relevant for income‑focused investors in a low‑interest‑rate environment. A Rs 26 per share payout translates to a sizable cash inflow for millions of Indian and global investors holding Infosys equity.

Infosys is a bellwether for the Indian IT sector, which contributes a significant share of the country's export earnings and employment. A reduction in revenue guidance may signal broader challenges for the industry, such as slower demand from North American and European clients. This could influence hiring plans, wage growth, and the sector's contribution to GDP, affecting a wide range of stakeholders from tech professionals to policy makers.

Background

Founded in 1981, Infosys has grown from a modest startup to a multinational corporation with operations in over 50 countries. The firm is known for its strong corporate governance, a diversified service portfolio, and a history of steady dividend payments.

In the previous quarter, Infosys posted a profit of Rs 7,300 crore and had projected a higher revenue growth rate for the year. The company traditionally sets ambitious targets, reflecting confidence in its order pipeline and the global demand for digital transformation services. However, recent macro‑economic headwinds, including currency fluctuations and geopolitical tensions, have prompted a more cautious outlook.

What happens next

Infosys is scheduled to hold its annual general meeting later this month, where shareholders will vote on the dividend and discuss the revised guidance. Analysts will closely monitor the company's next earnings release for signs of recovery in the order book. Potential actions such as share buybacks or strategic acquisitions could also be considered to support the share price and maintain investor confidence.
