Sri Lanka's Test squad for Pakistan series adds Nissanka, Bandara and Malinga

Latest developments

Sri Lanka Cricket (SLC) released its 15‑man squad for the three‑match Test series against Pakistan on Monday. The list features the much‑awaited return of opening batsman Pathum Nissanka after a six‑month injury lay‑off, the uncapped wicket‑keeper Anjala Bandara and fast‑bowler Eshan Malinga. Veteran middle‑order batsman Kusal Perera was not offered a place, and regular keeper Kusal Perera (Dickwella) was dropped in favour of the younger Bandara.

Nissanka, who scored a double‑century in the 2023 series against India, is expected to anchor the top order. His fitness was confirmed after a successful rehabilitation program, and the board highlighted his ability to play long innings on sub‑continental pitches. The decision to bring him back reflects SLC's desire for stability at the top of the batting line‑up.

Anjala Bandara has been a standout performer in domestic cricket this year, averaging 95 runs in the Premier League and taking 24 dismissals behind the stumps. His inclusion marks a rare opportunity for an uncapped keeper to debut in a high‑profile series. Bandara's recent form has convinced selectors that he can handle the pressure of Test cricket against a strong Pakistani side.

Eshan Malinga, a right‑arm pacer who impressed in the A‑team tour of Bangladesh, earned his first senior call‑up. His pace and ability to swing the ball with the new ball are seen as assets for Sri Lanka, which has struggled to find a consistent fast‑bowling attack in recent years. Malinga's addition gives the team a fresh option ahead of the first Test in Karachi.

Why it matters

Cricket remains one of the most followed sports in South Africa, with a large expatriate community and a strong domestic fan base. The Pakistan‑Sri Lanka series will be broadcast on major sports channels, offering South African viewers high‑quality Test cricket and the chance to see emerging talent like Bandara and Malinga.

The squad changes also have commercial implications. Sponsors and broadcasters monitor team selections closely, as star players can drive viewership and advertising revenue. Nissanka's return is likely to attract attention, potentially boosting ratings for the series in the South African market.

Background

Pakistan and Sri Lanka have a long history of competitive Test matches, with the last encounter in 2022 ending in a 2‑1 series win for Pakistan. Sri Lanka struggled with batting collapses and a thin pace attack, prompting a review of player performances and fitness.

Pathum Nissanka emerged as a key figure in Sri Lanka's 2023 tour of India, where he scored 215 in the second Test. His injury in early 2024 forced him out of the squad for several months, creating a gap that the team sought to fill with other players.

Anjala Bandara's domestic exploits have been highlighted in Wisden's recent feature, noting his impressive run tally and keeping statistics. His selection follows a trend of giving younger players exposure at the highest level to rebuild the team's core.

What happens next

The first Test is scheduled for early December in Karachi, followed by matches in Lahore and Rawalpindi. Both teams will announce their playing XIs a day before each match, allowing fans to see how the new selections are deployed. Sri Lanka will aim to field a balanced side that leverages Nissanka's experience and the fresh energy of Bandara and Malinga.

If the newcomers perform well, they could cement their places for future series, including the upcoming ICC World Test Championship cycle.
