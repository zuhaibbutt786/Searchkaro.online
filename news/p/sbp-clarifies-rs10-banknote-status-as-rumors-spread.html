SBP clarifies Rs10 banknote status as rumors spread

The State Bank of Pakistan denied reports that it plans to phase out the ten‑rupee note, calling the circulating graphic fabricated. The Rs10 banknote continues to be legal tender, and any official change will be communicated through the SB

What is happening

The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) issued a statement on the matter, denying reports that it has decided to phase out the ten‑rupee note. The bank said the circulating graphic suggesting a discontinuation is fabricated and that the Rs10 note continues to be legal tender. It emphasized that any change to currency denominations would be announced through official channels.

The denial came after several local media outlets, including Dawn and Business Recorder, published articles repeating the unverified claim. Both outlets quoted the SBP’s press release, which explicitly described the rumors as baseless. The agency also warned that misinformation could cause unnecessary panic among the public and businesses that handle cash transactions.

The rumor originated from a graphic shared on social media platforms that claimed the SBP had approved a plan to replace the Rs10 note with a higher‑value denomination. The image showed a mock‑up of a redesigned note and a timeline for its removal, but no authentic source was attached. Fact‑checking organisations have traced the graphic to an unverified user account, confirming that it does not reflect any official policy.

In its response, the SBP reiterated that the ten‑rupee note remains part of the current series of banknotes issued by the central bank. The statement also noted that the bank regularly reviews the design and security features of its currency, but any decision to withdraw a denomination would be communicated well in advance to banks, merchants, and the public.

Background and context

The ten‑rupee note has been part of Pakistan’s currency system since the early 2000s, undergoing several design updates to incorporate enhanced security features such as watermarks and holographic strips. It is widely used for everyday transactions, especially in rural markets and small retail settings where larger notes are less practical.

Historically, the SBP has occasionally withdrawn older series of banknotes to combat counterfeiting or to introduce new security measures. Such changes are typically announced months ahead, accompanied by public awareness campaigns and a transition period during which both old and new notes circulate simultaneously. No similar announcement regarding the Rs10 note has been made in the current fiscal year.

What to know next

For now, the ten‑rupee note will continue to be accepted for all payments and will be available at commercial banks and ATMs that dispense lower denominations. Consumers should rely on official SBP communications, which are posted on the central bank’s website and disseminated through reputable news agencies, for any future updates.

Observers suggest that the SBP may later issue a formal clarification on its currency strategy, especially if misinformation resurfaces. Stakeholders, including merchants and financial institutions, are advised to monitor the SBP’s press releases and to educate customers about the authenticity of circulating graphics.
