Egypt’s dollar rate climbs toward historic highs

The Egyptian pound is weakening against the US dollar, pushing official rates close to historic peaks. Consumers and businesses are watching the shift closely as it affects prices and financial planning.

What is happening

The official exchange rate published by the Central Bank of Egypt has risen steadily over the past weeks, now hovering around 51 Egyptian pounds per US dollar. This level is close to the highest recorded since the currency was floated in 2016, prompting headlines across regional media.

Major commercial banks, including the National Bank of Egypt and Banque Misr, have adjusted their retail rates in line with the central bank’s reference. Customers seeking to purchase foreign currency for travel, education fees, or remittance purposes are encountering higher costs than just a month ago.

Foreign exchange traders on the Cairo market report increased volatility, with bid‑ask spreads widening as supply tightens. The rise reflects a combination of external pressures, such as a stronger US dollar, and internal factors, including reduced foreign reserves and heightened demand for hard currency.

The broader economy feels the impact through higher import prices, which feed into inflationary pressures on food, fuel, and consumer goods. Households with fixed‑income budgets are especially vulnerable, and policymakers are monitoring the situation for signs of a wage‑price spiral.

Background and context

Egypt moved from a fixed peg to a managed float in November 2016 after a major devaluation that lifted the pound from roughly 8.8 to 13.5 per dollar. The policy was intended to attract foreign investment and stabilize the balance of payments.

Since then, the central bank has intervened intermittently to smooth extreme fluctuations, using foreign reserves and short‑term liquidity tools. Periodic adjustments have kept the official rate within a band that reflects market sentiment while avoiding abrupt shocks.

External factors, such as the US Federal Reserve’s interest‑rate hikes and geopolitical tensions affecting regional trade, have added pressure on emerging‑market currencies, including the Egyptian pound. Domestically, fiscal deficits and subsidy reforms have also contributed to the current environment.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has been engaged with Egypt on a multi‑year program that includes monitoring of exchange‑rate dynamics. Compliance with program benchmarks often influences the central bank’s willingness to intervene or to let the market set the rate.

What to know next

Analysts expect the central bank to continue using its foreign‑reserve buffer to temper excessive volatility, especially ahead of the upcoming budget cycle. Any sudden spikes could prompt temporary tightening of liquidity to protect the pound.

Long‑term outlook will depend on the pace of economic reforms, the success of export‑promotion strategies, and the trajectory of US monetary policy. If the dollar remains strong, the Egyptian pound may face further pressure, but gradual improvements in trade balances could provide a stabilising effect.
