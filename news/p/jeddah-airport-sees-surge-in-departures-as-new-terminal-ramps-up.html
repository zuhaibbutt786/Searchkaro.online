Jeddah Airport sees surge in departures as new terminal ramps up

The latest Flightradar24 data shows a notable increase in departures from Jeddah's King Abdulaziz International Airport. The growth follows the opening of a new terminal designed for pilgrimage traffic.

Latest developments

Data from flight‑tracking service Flightradar24 indicates that departures from King Abdulaziz International Airport (JED) have risen sharply over the past week. The platform recorded a higher number of outbound flights across domestic and international routes, with several carriers adding extra services to meet rising demand. The airport, which serves the Red Sea city of Jeddah, recently inaugurated a dedicated Hajj terminal that can handle up to 30,000 pilgrims per day. Since the terminal became operational, airlines have scheduled more flights to accommodate both religious travelers and regular passengers. In addition, the airport authority reported that runway utilization has improved, allowing for smoother scheduling and reduced turnaround times for aircraft.

Why it matters

The increase in departures directly affects travelers in Saudi Arabia, especially those planning to visit the holy cities of Mecca and Medina during the pilgrimage season. Faster processing and more flight options reduce travel bottlenecks and can lower ticket prices through competition. For businesses in Jeddah, the heightened activity supports the local economy by creating jobs in airport services, hospitality, and transport. Moreover, the expanded flight schedule enhances the city’s connectivity to key regional hubs such as Dubai, Doha, and Istanbul, facilitating trade and tourism. Residents in other parts of the Kingdom also benefit from improved access to international destinations without needing to transit through larger airports in Riyadh or Dammam.

Background

King Abdulaziz International Airport, named after the founder of modern Saudi Arabia, was originally opened in 1981 to serve the growing needs of the western region. Over the decades, the airport has undergone several expansions, including the addition of a dedicated terminal for Hajj pilgrims in 2019. The Hajj terminal was built to streamline the massive influx of worshippers each year, separating pilgrimage traffic from regular commercial flights. Historically, the airport has been a critical gateway for both domestic and international traffic, handling millions of passengers annually. Recent years have seen a strategic push by the Saudi aviation authority to increase capacity and improve service standards in line with Vision 2030 objectives.

What happens next

Airport officials expect the upward trend in departures to continue as more airlines adjust their schedules to the new terminal’s capacity. Seasonal adjustments for the Hajj period are likely to bring additional flights, while the summer travel season may see airlines introduce new routes to European and Asian destinations. The airport management has also announced plans to upgrade its air traffic control systems to further enhance efficiency and safety. Travelers are advised to monitor airline announcements for updated flight times and to take advantage of the expanded service offerings.
