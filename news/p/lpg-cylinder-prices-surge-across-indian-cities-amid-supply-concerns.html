LPG cylinder prices surge across Indian cities amid supply concerns

LPG cylinder rates have jumped in major Indian cities, prompting concerns over household fuel affordability. Consumers are scrambling for the lowest prices as discounts disappear.

Latest developments

The latest market data released on September 28 shows a noticeable increase in LPG cylinder prices across several Indian metros. In Kolkata, the price of a standard 12‑kilogram cylinder has risen to the highest level recorded this month, while Delhi and Bangalore report similar upward trends. News18 Bengali highlighted that shoppers in various cities are actively seeking the most affordable sources, comparing rates posted by local distributors. Aajkaal reported that the price hike is not confined to West Bengal; it extends to other urban centres, reflecting a broader pressure on the domestic LPG market. Meanwhile, The Dhaka Diary noted a rare discount of 2,000 rupees on a 12‑kilogram cylinder, a temporary relief that appears linked to a short‑term surplus rather than a sustained price correction. Analysts attribute the surge to rising crude oil costs, tighter supply chains, and increased demand for cooking fuel as winter approaches in northern regions. Distributors cite higher procurement expenses and transportation bottlenecks as primary drivers, prompting them to adjust retail rates to maintain margins.

Why it matters

For millions of Indian households, LPG is the primary cooking fuel, and any increase directly impacts household budgets. The price escalation coincides with a period of heightened inflation, stretching the financial capacity of low‑ and middle‑income families. In cities like Kolkata and Delhi, where a typical monthly LPG bill forms a significant portion of total energy expenditure, the rise translates into higher living costs and may force consumers to cut back on other essentials. Moreover, the reduction of promotional discounts, such as the 2,000‑rupee cut reported by The Dhaka Diary, removes a safety net that many families relied upon during previous price spikes. The broader economic implications include potential shifts in consumer behavior, with some households considering alternative fuels or bulk purchases to mitigate cost pressures. Policymakers are watching the trend closely, as sustained price growth could trigger public discontent and pressure on the government to intervene through subsidies or price caps.

Background

Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG), known locally as তরলীকৃত পেট্রোলিয়াম গ্যাস, has been a cornerstone of India's energy mix for cooking since the early 1990s. The government's Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana expanded access to LPG connections, dramatically increasing domestic consumption. Over the past decade, the market has been relatively stable, with periodic adjustments linked to global oil price fluctuations. However, recent geopolitical tensions in West Asia have disrupted crude oil supplies, indirectly affecting LPG pricing. Additionally, logistical challenges, including limited pipeline infrastructure and reliance on road transport, have amplified cost pressures. Seasonal demand spikes during winter months further strain the supply chain, often resulting in short‑term price hikes. The current scenario reflects a convergence of these factors, compounded by rising input costs for manufacturers and distributors.

What happens next

Industry observers expect that if crude oil prices remain elevated, LPG cylinder rates will continue to climb in the coming weeks. The government may consider targeted subsidies or temporary price controls to ease the burden on vulnerable consumers. Retailers are likely to intensify price comparison campaigns, urging shoppers to seek the lowest rates online or through local dealers. Monitoring of supply chain bottlenecks will be crucial to prevent further disruptions.

Summary

- LPG cylinder prices have risen sharply in Kolkata, Delhi, Bangalore and other major cities. - Temporary discounts, such as a 2,000‑rupee cut, are fading as market pressures mount. - Higher prices strain household budgets amid broader inflation concerns. - Supply chain issues and rising crude oil costs are the main drivers of the increase. - Government intervention or subsidies may be considered to stabilize consumer costs.
