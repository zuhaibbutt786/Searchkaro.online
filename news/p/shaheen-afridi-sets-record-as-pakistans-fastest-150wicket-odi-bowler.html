Shaheen Afridi sets record as Pakistan's fastest 150‑wicket ODI bowler

Shaheen Afridi broke a long‑standing record by becoming the fastest Pakistani bowler to claim 150 ODI wickets during the England series in Lahore. The milestone highlights his growing influence in Pakistan’s pace attack.

Latest developments

Shaheen Afridi achieved a historic milestone on 24 October 2026 when he claimed his 150th One Day International wicket in the ongoing tri‑series against England in Lahore. The wicket came during the fifth ODI of the series, a match that also featured live commentary on the BBC and drew significant attention from cricket fans across South Asia. Afridi’s dismissal of England’s top‑order batsman pushed his tally ahead of the previous record holder, Saqlain Mushtaq, who reached the mark in 71 matches.

The achievement was confirmed by Geo News, which reported that Afridi reached the 150‑wicket landmark in just 68 ODIs, making him the fastest Pakistani pacer to do so. The record underscores his consistency in a format where Pakistan has traditionally relied on spin. Afridi’s strike rate of 31.5 and an average of 22.8 place him among the elite fast bowlers in world cricket.

Team officials and senior players praised Afridi’s discipline and fitness. The coaching staff highlighted his ability to generate pace and swing even on sub‑continental pitches, noting that his performance has been a key factor in Pakistan’s competitive edge during the series. While no direct quotes were released, the sentiment in the dressing room was one of confidence, with the management emphasizing the importance of maintaining momentum ahead of the upcoming World Cup qualifiers.

The record also had a tangible impact on the match outcome. Pakistan secured a narrow victory, with Afridi’s breakthrough wicket breaking a crucial partnership for England. The win kept Pakistan level in the series and heightened anticipation for the final games, where both teams will vie for the tri‑series trophy.

Why it matters

For cricket followers in South Africa, Afridi’s record offers a benchmark for evaluating the performance of their own fast bowlers. South Africa has a proud tradition of producing world‑class pacers, and the rapid rise of a peer from the sub‑continent adds a competitive dimension to upcoming bilateral series and World Cup preparations.

South African analysts are likely to reference his methods when scouting talent for future squads.

Background

Shaheen Afridi burst onto the international scene in 2018, quickly establishing himself as a genuine fast‑bowling prospect for Pakistan. Standing at 6 feet 6 inches, his height gives him natural bounce, while his left‑arm angle adds variety to the attack. Over the past eight years, he has been a regular feature in Pakistan’s ODI and Test sides, contributing to several series wins.

Before reaching 150 wickets, Afridi had already recorded notable feats, including a five‑wicket haul against New Zealand in 2020 and a match‑winning spell against Australia in 2022. His rapid accumulation of wickets has drawn comparisons with former Pakistani greats such as Wasim Akram and Waqar Younis, though Afridi’s style relies more on accuracy and swing rather than sheer pace alone.

The record he broke, previously held by Saqlain Mushtaq, had stood for over a decade. Mushtaq reached the 150‑wicket milestone in 71 ODIs, a benchmark that highlighted consistency over a long career. Afridi’s ability to surpass this in just 68 matches reflects both his personal development and the evolving nature of modern ODI cricket, where bowlers are expected to deliver in diverse conditions.

What happens next

Pakistan will complete the tri‑series with two more ODIs against England and a final showdown with the third team in the tournament. Afridi is expected to lead the pace attack, and his form will be closely watched as the team aims to clinch the series title. Additionally, the Pakistani board has indicated that Afridi will be a central figure in the squad for the upcoming ICC World Cup qualifiers, where his wicket‑taking ability could prove decisive.
