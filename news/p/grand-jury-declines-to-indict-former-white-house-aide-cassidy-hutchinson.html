Grand Jury Declines to Indict Former White House Aide Cassidy Hutchinson

A federal grand jury announced it will not bring charges against former White House aide Cassidy Hutchinson. The decision ends weeks of speculation about possible criminal liability for her Jan. 6 testimony.

Latest developments

On Oct 27 2026 a federal grand jury in Washington D.C. announced it will not bring charges against Cassidy Hutchinson, the former White House aide who testified before the House Select Committee on the Jan. 6 attack. The decision was reported by The New York Times, The Washington Post and AP, confirming that the jury found insufficient evidence for an indictment. Hutchinson's testimony in November 2023 implicated senior officials and described internal White House discussions after the Capitol breach. Prosecutors had been reviewing whether her statements constituted false statements or obstruction, but the grand jury concluded the criminal standard was not met. The announcement came after weeks of speculation about a possible indictment, fueled by statements from some lawmakers and media commentary. The Justice Department did not release a detailed explanation, citing standard practice. Hutchinson, who served as deputy assistant to the former president, has continued to cooperate with investigators. Legal analysts noted that the decision does not preclude future investigations into other participants in the Jan. 6 events, but it closes the specific line of inquiry into Hutchinson's alleged wrongdoing. The outcome also reduces immediate political pressure on the former administration.

Background

Cassidy Hutchinson joined the White House in 2021 as a deputy assistant to the former president and became a central witness after the House Select Committee began its investigation into the Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol, where a mob attempted to overturn the election results. During her November 2023 testimony she described a meeting on Jan. 6 where senior staff discussed the possibility of invoking the 25th Amendment to remove the president and recounted conversations about the president's reaction to the breach, raising questions about potential obstruction of an official proceeding. Federal prosecutors opened a separate inquiry to determine whether Hutchinson had made false statements to Congress or obstructed justice. The grand jury, a group of citizens empowered to evaluate evidence, convened in early 2024 and heard testimony from multiple witnesses before reaching its decision in October 2026.
