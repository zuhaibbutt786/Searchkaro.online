Saudi King Awards Medal of Merit to 56 Expatriates for Blood Donations

King Salman has granted the third‑class Medal of Merit to 56 residents for donating blood ten times each. The award underscores Saudi Arabia's recognition of humanitarian acts by expatriates.

Latest developments

The Saudi Ministry of Interior announced on Monday that the خادم الحرمين الشريفين, King Salman, approved the conferral of the third‑class Medal of Merit to 56 expatriates residing in the kingdom. Each recipient has donated blood on ten separate occasions, a milestone that the authorities say reflects a sustained commitment to public health.

The award ceremony is scheduled to take place at the Ministry's headquarters in Riyadh later this month. Officials will present the medals in a formal gathering attended by senior government representatives, health ministry officials, and the honorees' families. The Ministry of Interior's press release highlighted the recipients' diverse nationalities, emphasizing the inclusive nature of Saudi society.

According to the Saudi Press Agency, the Medal of Merit (Mithaq al‑Istihqaq) is a state decoration awarded for meritorious service to the nation. The third‑class tier, while not the highest, still carries significant prestige and includes a medal, a citation, and a modest monetary stipend. Recipients will also be entered into a public registry of distinguished contributors.

The decision follows a broader campaign by Saudi health authorities encouraging regular blood donation, especially in light of ongoing medical needs across the kingdom. The Ministry of Health has reported a shortfall in blood supplies in several regions, prompting outreach to both citizens and expatriates to bolster the national blood bank.

Why it matters

Blood donation is a critical component of Saudi Arabia's healthcare infrastructure, and the recognition of expatriate donors sends a powerful message about the value placed on community service regardless of citizenship. By publicly honoring these individuals, the government aims to motivate others to participate in regular donation drives, potentially alleviating shortages that affect hospitals and emergency services.

The award also reflects Saudi Arabia's broader strategy of integrating expatriate communities into national narratives. Expatriates make up a substantial portion of the workforce and contribute to the economy; acknowledging their humanitarian contributions helps foster social cohesion and reinforces the kingdom's image as a welcoming environment for foreign residents.

Furthermore, the ceremony aligns with the Saudi Vision 2030 agenda, which emphasizes public health, social responsibility, and the empowerment of civil society. Recognizing blood donors supports the Vision's health objectives by encouraging preventive measures and community involvement.

Background

The Medal of Merit, established in 1975, is one of Saudi Arabia's most respected civilian honors. It is awarded in three classes, with the third class typically reserved for individuals who have demonstrated notable service in fields such as education, health, and public welfare. Historically, the award has been given to Saudi nationals, but recent years have seen a gradual inclusion of expatriates who have made significant contributions.

Blood donation campaigns in Saudi Arabia have intensified since the COVID-19 pandemic, which exposed vulnerabilities in the national blood supply. The Ministry of Health has launched multiple awareness initiatives, including mobile donation units and partnerships with private sector entities, to increase donor participation.

Expatriates in Saudi Arabia number over 10 million, representing a wide array of nationalities. While they are essential to the country's economic development, their integration into social and civic life has been an ongoing policy focus. Awards such as the Medal of Merit serve as tangible symbols of inclusion and appreciation.

What happens next

The Ministry of Interior will finalize the list of awardees and coordinate the logistics for the medal presentation ceremony. Media coverage is expected to highlight the personal stories of selected donors, showcasing their motivations and the impact of their contributions on patients across the kingdom. In parallel, the Ministry of Health plans to launch a new public awareness drive aimed at encouraging more frequent donations, leveraging the visibility of the awards to boost participation.

Stakeholders anticipate that the recognition will spur a measurable increase in blood donation rates over the coming months, as both expatriates and Saudi citizens respond to the government's call for civic engagement.
