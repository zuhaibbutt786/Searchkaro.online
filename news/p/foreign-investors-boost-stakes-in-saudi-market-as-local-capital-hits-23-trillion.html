Foreign Investors Boost Stakes in Saudi Market as Local Capital Hits $2.3 Trillion

Foreign investors expanded their presence in the Saudi stock market in October, adding to holdings in over a hundred companies. Meanwhile, Saudi investors’ market value topped $2.3 trillion, highlighting growing confidence in the kingdom’s

Latest developments

The Saudi exchange (Tadawul) reported that foreign investors raised their ownership in 113 listed companies during the second reporting session of October, according to data released by the Saudi Capital Market Authority. The increase was led by the investment firm Shaker, which emerged as the top buyer in this session. At the same time, foreign investors reduced their stakes in 160 other companies, indicating a selective rebalancing of portfolios. In parallel, the total net sales by foreign investors in Tadawul for October amounted to 2.07 billion Saudi riyals, a figure that reflects pressure from institutional investors seeking to adjust their exposure. The Saudi market’s overall capitalization for local investors reached $2.303 trillion by the end of October, underscoring the depth and resilience of domestic capital. These movements come as the kingdom continues to attract global capital following reforms that opened its equity market to a broader range of foreign participants.

Why it matters

The shift in foreign ownership patterns signals confidence in specific sectors of the Saudi economy while also revealing caution in others. Increased foreign stakes in 113 firms can boost liquidity, improve corporate governance, and potentially raise valuations, benefiting both local and international shareholders. Conversely, the reduction in holdings across 160 companies may signal concerns about earnings outlooks or sector-specific risks, prompting investors to concentrate on higher‑growth opportunities. The record $2.303 trillion market value held by Saudi investors highlights the growing depth of domestic savings and the effectiveness of recent reforms aimed at diversifying the economy away from oil. For Saudi citizens, a robust local investor base can translate into more stable market performance and greater access to capital for businesses.

Background

Saudi Arabia has pursued a series of financial market reforms since 2016, including the removal of quotas on foreign ownership and the introduction of a new legal framework to protect minority shareholders. These changes were designed to integrate the kingdom’s capital market into global investment flows and to support the Vision 2030 diversification agenda. Tadawul, the Saudi stock exchange, has seen a steady rise in foreign participation, with foreign holdings now accounting for a significant share of total market capitalization. The recent data reflects the ongoing impact of these reforms, as foreign investors adjust their positions in response to macro‑economic trends, oil price volatility, and regional geopolitical developments.

What happens next

Analysts expect foreign investors to continue monitoring sector performance closely, with particular attention to energy, petrochemicals, and emerging technology firms. The Saudi regulator may release further guidance on foreign ownership limits, which could influence future buying patterns. Domestic investors are likely to maintain their strong presence, especially as the kingdom rolls out new initiatives to encourage retail participation in the equity market.
