Merub Ali credits social media for fresh talent like Dananeer Mobeen

Veteran actor Merub Ali has highlighted the role of digital platforms in discovering new faces in Pakistan's drama sector. He specifically cited Dananeer Mobeen and Romaisa Khan as examples of success stories driven by online visibility.

Latest developments

Veteran Pakistani actor Merub Ali has recently spoken out in support of social media as a vital tool for the entertainment industry. In a recent interview, Ali expressed his view that digital platforms have become a crucial pathway for new talent to enter the mainstream. He argued that these platforms offer a level of exposure that traditional gatekeeping mechanisms often fail to provide, allowing raw talent to be discovered by a wider audience.

Ali specifically pointed to Dananeer Mobeen and Romaisa Khan as prime examples of this phenomenon. He noted that both performers gained significant traction and public recognition through their presence on social media before establishing themselves in formal television productions. According to Ali, this trajectory demonstrates that the industry is shifting towards a more inclusive model where online popularity can translate into professional opportunities. He emphasized that the authenticity often found in social media content resonates well with modern audiences, making it a fertile ground for discovering new stars.

The comments come at a time when the Pakistani drama industry is undergoing a significant transformation. For decades, entry into the industry was largely controlled by established casting directors and producers. However, the rise of platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube has decentralized this power. Ali’s remarks reflect a broader sentiment among senior industry figures who are beginning to acknowledge the value of digital-first careers. He suggested that ignoring this shift would be a mistake for any production house looking to stay relevant in the current market.

Ali’s praise for these specific individuals highlights a generational shift in how audiences consume and engage with content. Dananeer Mobeen and Romaisa Khan have built substantial followings by sharing their work and personal insights directly with fans. This direct connection creates a loyal fan base that supports their projects, providing a built-in marketing channel for producers. Ali believes that this model is not just a temporary trend but a sustainable change in how talent is identified and promoted within Pakistan.

Why it matters

For the Pakistani entertainment sector, this shift has profound implications for career accessibility. Historically, aspiring actors and artists faced significant barriers to entry, often requiring connections or extensive audition processes. Social media has lowered these barriers, allowing individuals from diverse backgrounds to showcase their skills without immediate reliance on traditional industry networks. This democratization of talent discovery is crucial for a country with a large and young population seeking creative outlets.

Furthermore, the economic impact of this trend is notable. Producers can now identify talent with proven audience engagement, reducing the financial risk associated with casting unknowns. When a performer already has a dedicated following, the likelihood of a show’s success increases, as the audience is pre-sold on the talent. This efficiency benefits the entire supply chain, from scriptwriters to post-production teams, by ensuring that resources are allocated to projects with a higher probability of commercial viability.

For viewers in Pakistan, this means a wider variety of faces and voices in their favorite dramas and films. The influx of new talent brings fresh perspectives and performance styles, preventing the industry from becoming stagnant. It also encourages existing actors to maintain their online presence, fostering a more interactive relationship between celebrities and the public. This dynamic keeps the industry vibrant and responsive to audience preferences.

Background

The Pakistani drama industry has long been a central pillar of the country's cultural identity. Shows broadcast on major channels like PTV, ARY, and HUM have shaped social norms and provided entertainment for decades. However, the industry has also been criticized for its repetitive casting patterns and limited opportunities for new entrants. The dominance of a few well-known actors often overshadowed emerging talents, making it difficult for newcomers to break through without significant backing.

The advent of high-speed internet and smartphones in Pakistan changed this dynamic in the last decade. Social media platforms became the primary space for young people to express themselves and share their creative work. This digital migration allowed artists to build personal brands independent of traditional media structures. Many successful careers in the region have now started with viral videos or consistent social media content, rather than with a debut in a major television drama.

Merub Ali, a respected figure in the industry, has observed these changes firsthand. His recent comments align with a growing consensus among industry leaders that social media is no longer a supplementary tool but a primary engine for talent discovery. By naming specific individuals like Dananeer Mobeen and Romaisa Khan, he provides concrete evidence of this shift, moving the conversation from abstract theory to observable reality.

What happens next

In the coming months, expect to see more producers actively scouting social media platforms for new faces. Casting calls may increasingly include requirements for a digital portfolio, reflecting the importance of online presence. This could lead to a surge in new dramas featuring actors who have transitioned from digital creators to television professionals.

Additionally, there may be a rise in collaborative projects between traditional media houses and digital influencers. These partnerships could create hybrid content formats that appeal to both traditional television audiences and younger, mobile-first viewers. The industry is likely to continue adapting to these changes, with more emphasis on digital marketing and audience engagement strategies.
