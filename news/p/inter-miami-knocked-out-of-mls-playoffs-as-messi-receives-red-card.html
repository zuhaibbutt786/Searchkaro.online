Inter Miami knocked out of MLS playoffs as Messi receives red card

Inter Miami were eliminated from the MLS playoffs after a defeat to Columbus Crew, with Lionel Messi receiving a red card that ignited debate. The outcome could affect the league's growing popularity in India.

Latest developments

Inter Miami's season came to an abrupt end on Tuesday when the club were eliminated from the Major League Soccer (MLS) playoffs by the Columbus Crew. The match, played at Columbus' stadium, ended with the Crew advancing to the next round while Miami's hopes of a deeper run vanished. The loss marked the first playoff defeat for the franchise since Lionel Messi joined the team last summer. The game turned contentious in the second half when Messi was shown a second yellow card for a foul on a Columbus midfielder, resulting in his dismissal. The decision sparked immediate debate among commentators and fans, with some arguing that the referee applied the rules correctly while others felt the punishment was excessive. The red‑card controversy dominated post‑match analysis and added to the disappointment surrounding Miami's early exit. While the spotlight remained on Messi, other headlines from the football world continued to draw attention. Cristiano Ronaldo, who is nearing the milestone of 1,000 career goals, recorded another goal that brought him within striking distance of the landmark. At the same time, Ronaldo set an unwanted record, as noted by ESPN India, though the specifics of the record were not detailed in the report. Inter Miami coach Gerardo Martino addressed the defeat in a brief press conference, acknowledging the team's disappointment but emphasizing the need to regroup ahead of the off‑season. He praised Messi's contributions throughout the campaign and said the club would review the disciplinary incident to determine if an appeal was appropriate. Fans across social media expressed a mix of frustration over the exit and admiration for the star's overall impact on the club's profile.

Why it matters

The elimination of Inter Miami has significant implications for football followers in India, where the arrival of Lionel Messi earlier this year generated a surge in MLS viewership. Indian fans, who have traditionally followed European leagues, turned to streaming platforms to watch Miami's matches, boosting the league's marketability and attracting new sponsors. The early exit dampens the momentum built around Messi's presence and may affect future broadcasting deals. Beyond viewership, the controversy surrounding Messi's red card raises questions about disciplinary standards in MLS, a league that is positioning itself as a competitive alternative to established competitions. Indian commentators and analysts are likely to discuss whether the refereeing decisions align with global norms, influencing public perception of the league's professionalism. The incident also highlights the growing influence of star players on league narratives, which can shape fan engagement across the subcontinent. The ripple effect extends to merchandise sales, with Messi-branded jerseys experiencing high demand in Indian e‑commerce sites. A sudden loss in the playoffs could temper that enthusiasm, prompting retailers to adjust inventory strategies. Moreover, the episode may influence young Indian players who view MLS as a viable pathway, underscoring the importance of consistent on‑field conduct by high‑profile athletes.

Background

Inter Miami CF was founded in 2018 as part of Major League Soccer's expansion strategy and began competing in the 2020 season. The club, co‑owned by former English footballer David Beckham, struggled in its early years but secured a place in the league's upper tier after a series of strategic signings. The arrival of Lionel Messi in July 2023 marked a turning point, attracting global attention and elevating the team's performance. Messi's debut season saw Inter Miami finish second in the Eastern Conference and qualify for the playoffs, a notable improvement over previous campaigns. His influence extended beyond the pitch, driving ticket sales, social media engagement, and commercial partnerships. The club's ownership leveraged Messi's brand to negotiate broadcasting agreements that expanded MLS's reach into Asian markets, including India. The 2025 season, however, presented challenges as injuries and squad rotation affected consistency. Despite these hurdles, Inter Miami secured a top‑four finish, ensuring a playoff berth. The team's tactical approach under coach Gerardo Martino emphasized possession football, with Messi often positioned as a false nine to create space for wingers.
