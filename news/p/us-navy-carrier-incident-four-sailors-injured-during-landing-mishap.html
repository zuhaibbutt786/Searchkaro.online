US Navy Carrier Incident: Four Sailors Injured During Landing Mishap

Four sailors were injured when a landing mishap occurred aboard the USS Dwight D. Eisenhower off Virginia. Two aviators ejected safely, highlighting ongoing risks in carrier operations.

Latest developments

On 28 September 2026, a landing mishap on USS Dwight D. Eisenhower off the coast of Virginia resulted in four sailors injured. Two aviators ejected from their aircraft and landed safely. The incident was reported by AP News and CNN. The aircraft carrier, a Nimitz-class vessel, was conducting routine training exercises when the mishap occurred. According to USNI News, the crew managed to recover the aircraft and the crew safely after the incident. The exact cause of the mishap remains under investigation. The Navy confirmed that all injured sailors received medical attention and were transported to a nearby naval hospital. No fatalities were reported. The carrier returned to port the following day to complete post-incident inspections. The event has prompted a review of carrier deck safety protocols. Officials have stated that the incident will be used to refine training procedures and equipment checks.

Why it matters

Incidents like this highlight the inherent dangers of operating large aircraft carriers. Even routine training can expose personnel to serious risks. The safety of sailors and aviators is a top priority for the U.S. Navy. The incident also raises questions about the adequacy of current safety measures during deck operations. The Navy has a long history of learning from accidents to improve procedures and reduce future risks. For families of sailors and the broader public, the event underscores the sacrifices made by naval personnel. It also reminds policymakers of the need for continued investment in training and equipment.

Background

The USS Dwight D. Eisenhower, commissioned in 1977, is one of the largest warships in the U.S. Navy. It serves as a mobile airbase for fighter jets, helicopters and support aircraft. Carrier operations involve complex coordination between flight deck crews, pilots and support staff. The risk of mishaps has led to the development of strict safety protocols over decades. Previous incidents on carriers have prompted reforms in training and equipment. The Navy regularly reviews incidents to refine its approach to safety.

What happens next

An investigation will be conducted by the Naval Safety Center to determine the root causes of the mishap. Findings will be shared with the fleet and used to update safety guidelines. The carrier will remain in port for additional inspections before returning to active duty. The Navy has indicated that it will continue to monitor deck operations closely.
