Ramayana joins Rs 1,700‑crore club as Indian cinema hits new heights

Ramayana has crossed the Rs 1,000‑crore mark in India and is on track to join the Rs 1,700‑crore global club. The film's performance revives memories of earlier hits such as Dangal, which reshaped Indian box‑office expectations.

Latest developments

The epic drama Ramayana, directed by Nitesh Tiwari and starring Ranbir Kapoor, has crossed the Rs 1,000‑crore threshold in the domestic market, according to data reported by The Hindu. The film's worldwide earnings are now estimated at over Rs 1,600 crore, putting it within striking distance of the Rs 1,700‑crore milestone highlighted by the Telegraph India. NDTV noted that on its 18th day, Ramayana earned more than the recent release Dhurandhar 2 in India, underscoring its strong hold on the box‑office. The film's success comes amid a crowded release calendar, yet it continues to draw audiences in both metropolitan and tier‑2 cities, driven by its mythological narrative and high production values. Industry analysts point to the film's robust marketing campaign and the star power of Kapoor as key factors behind the sustained revenue stream.

Why it matters

Ramayana's box‑office surge has significant implications for the Indian film industry, which has been seeking to replicate the overseas success of past blockbusters. The film's performance demonstrates that large‑scale mythological projects can still attract mass audiences, encouraging producers to invest in high‑budget ventures. For Indian viewers, the film offers a contemporary retelling of a classic epic, reinforcing cultural pride while delivering commercial entertainment. The success also benefits ancillary sectors such as merchandising, digital streaming rights, and regional distribution networks, creating jobs and revenue beyond the theatres. Moreover, the film's earnings echo the impact of earlier record‑breakers like Dangal, which in 2016 became one of the highest‑grossing Indian films worldwide, showing that Indian cinema can compete on a global scale when stories resonate across borders.

Background

Ramayana is based on the ancient Indian epic of the same name, which chronicles the journey of Prince Rama, his exile, and the battle to rescue his wife Sita. Nitesh Tiwari, known for his work on socially relevant films, approached the project with a blend of traditional storytelling and modern visual effects. Ranbir Kapoor, a leading Bollywood star, portrays Rama, bringing a fresh interpretation to the iconic character. The film's production involved extensive set construction, VFX work, and a large ensemble cast, reflecting a growing trend of big‑budget Indian films that aim for both domestic and international appeal. Dangal, released in 2016 and directed by Nitesh Tiwari's contemporary, Aamir Khan, set a precedent by earning over $300 million worldwide, proving that Indian narratives could achieve massive commercial success beyond the subcontinent.

What happens next

Industry watchers expect Ramayana to cross the Rs 1,700‑crore global mark within the next week, potentially joining an elite group of Indian films that have reached that level of earnings. The film is slated for release in additional overseas territories, including the Middle East and Europe, where diaspora audiences are showing strong interest. Distributors are also negotiating streaming deals with major platforms, which could further boost the film's revenue after its theatrical run. Meanwhile, producers are likely to green‑light similar mythological projects, betting on the proven appetite for culturally resonant, high‑production spectacles.
