Portugal backs Pakistan’s GSP+ bid, seeks stronger trade ties

The Portuguese foreign minister announced full backing for Pakistan's GSP+ status during talks in Islamabad and urged closer economic cooperation. He also praised Pakistan's contribution to worldwide peace efforts.

Latest developments

During a high‑level visit to Islamabad, Portugal's foreign minister said the European nation will give its full support to Pakistan's application for Generalised Scheme of Preferences plus (GSP+) status. The statement was made in a joint press conference with Pakistan's foreign minister, where both sides highlighted the mutual benefits of deeper economic engagement.

The Portuguese minister also outlined plans to expand trade and investment links between the two countries. He pointed to sectors such as textiles, agriculture, and renewable energy as areas where Portuguese firms could find new opportunities in Pakistan, while Pakistani exporters could benefit from reduced tariffs under the GSP+ framework.

In addition to economic matters, the minister praised Pakistan's role in promoting global peace, noting that Islamabad's diplomatic efforts in regional stability are valuable to the international community. He urged continued collaboration on security and multilateral initiatives.

Both governments agreed to set up a joint working group to monitor the progress of Pakistan's GSP+ application and to explore specific projects that could be launched under the enhanced partnership. The group will report back within the next three months.

The visit marks the latest in a series of diplomatic exchanges aimed at strengthening Pakistan‑Portugal relations, following earlier statements from Portuguese officials about seeking deeper trade and investment ties.

Why it matters

GSP+ status would allow Pakistani exporters to enjoy duty‑free access to the European Union market for over 66 product categories. This could significantly boost Pakistan's export earnings, especially in textiles and agricultural goods, which are key drivers of the country's economy.

For Pakistani businesses, the prospect of Portuguese investment opens new avenues for technology transfer and joint ventures, particularly in renewable energy and agro‑processing. Such collaboration could create jobs and enhance productivity.

The endorsement from a European foreign minister also signals broader international confidence in Pakistan's trade reforms and its commitment to meeting the EU's standards on labor rights, environmental protection, and good governance. This confidence may encourage other EU members to support Pakistan's GSP+ bid.

Beyond economics, the minister's remarks on Pakistan's peace‑building role reinforce the country's strategic importance in regional security dialogues, which can translate into greater diplomatic leverage for Pakistan on the world stage.

Background

The GSP+ scheme is a preferential trade arrangement that grants eligible developing countries reduced or zero tariffs on a wide range of goods exported to the EU. To qualify, a country must ratify 27 international conventions covering human rights, labor standards, environmental protection and good governance.

Pakistan has been pursuing GSP+ status for several years, arguing that it has made substantial progress in aligning its policies with the required conventions. However, the application has faced scrutiny from EU officials over concerns about implementation gaps.

Portugal, a member of the EU since 1986, has traditionally maintained friendly diplomatic and commercial ties with Pakistan. Trade between the two nations has grown steadily, with Portugal importing Pakistani textiles and exporting machinery and chemicals. Recent diplomatic outreach reflects a mutual desire to deepen this relationship.

The Portuguese foreign minister's visit follows a series of high‑profile meetings, including a recent trade forum in Lisbon where Pakistani business leaders met potential Portuguese partners. These engagements have set the stage for the current push to secure GSP+ support.

What happens next

The next step is for the European Commission to review Pakistan's GSP+ application in light of the Portuguese endorsement and the joint working group's findings. If the Commission recommends approval, the EU Council will need to adopt the decision, which could take several months.

Meanwhile, both governments plan to organize sector‑specific trade missions later this year to identify concrete projects and investment opportunities, keeping the momentum of the partnership alive.
