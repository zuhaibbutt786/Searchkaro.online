Abhishek Sharma shatters record with 30-ball century

Abhishek Sharma delivered a blistering 30‑ball century, smashing 11 sixes and 9 fours. The innings eclipsed Rohit Sharma’s previous fastest‑century record, igniting celebrations across India.

Latest developments

In a high‑profile domestic match held on Saturday, Indian batsman Abhishek Sharma produced a historic innings that has been described as "devastating" by local media. Facing just 30 deliveries, he reached the three‑figure mark while striking 11 sixes and 9 fours, a combination that pushed his personal strike rate to unprecedented levels. The rapid century not only secured a swift personal milestone but also erased the previous fastest‑century record held by senior teammate Rohit Sharma, who had set the benchmark at 31 balls earlier in the season. Sharma's explosive performance came at a crucial juncture of the game, stabilising his side's chase after early wickets fell and ultimately steering the team toward a comfortable finish. The match, part of the ongoing domestic league, concluded with Sharma's side posting a total that left the opposition with little chance of recovery, and the win was celebrated by fans both in the stadium and across social media platforms.

Why it matters

The innings carries significance beyond the immediate result. In a cricket‑loving nation like India, records of speed and power are closely watched, and breaking a milestone set by a player of Rohit Sharma's stature draws considerable attention. Young cricketers see such feats as proof that aggressive batting can thrive in modern formats, potentially influencing coaching strategies at the grassroots level. Moreover, the performance arrives at a time when the national selectors are reviewing talent for upcoming international fixtures, and a display of this magnitude could accelerate Sharma's inclusion in higher‑level squads. For the broader fan base, the spectacle reinforces the entertainment value of domestic competitions, encouraging higher attendance and viewership, which in turn supports the financial health of the sport's governing bodies.

Background

Abhishek Sharma, a right‑handed top‑order batsman, has been part of India's domestic circuit for several seasons, gradually building a reputation for his ability to accelerate the scoring rate in the middle overs. Prior to this innings, his highest score in the format was a brisk 85, achieved against a strong bowling attack. Rohit Sharma, who previously held the fastest‑century record at 31 balls, set that mark during a televised league match two years ago, a record that stood as a benchmark for power hitting in the country. Fast centuries are rare in cricket, with only a handful of players worldwide managing to cross the 30‑ball threshold, making Sharma's achievement noteworthy in the annals of the sport. The domestic league, known for nurturing future international stars, has seen several record‑breaking performances, but Sharma's 30‑ball century is the quickest in its history.

What happens next

Following the record‑breaking knock, Sharma's team will face a series of knockout matches that could determine their position in the tournament standings. Cricket analysts suggest that his form will be a key factor in the side's strategy, especially in the power‑play overs where quick runs are essential. Meanwhile, the national selection committee is expected to review the performance alongside other domestic statistics before finalising the squad for the upcoming international series against a visiting side. Fans and pundits alike will be watching closely to see whether Sharma translates his domestic dominance into success on the international stage.
