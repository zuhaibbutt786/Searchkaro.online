Paraguay Seizes Ueno Bank, Leaving 3 Million Customers in Limbo

The Paraguayan government has taken control of Ueno Bank, leaving the deposits of 3 million customers in uncertainty. The action has triggered a debate over the president’s role in the crisis.

Latest developments

On 12 March 2024 the Paraguayan authorities announced that they had taken control of Ueno Bank, a digital lender that had been linked to the president’s family. The move was described as a precautionary measure to protect the banking system after the bank’s rapid expansion and a series of regulatory breaches were uncovered.

The seizure covers the accounts of roughly 3 million customers, many of whom had deposited money in the bank’s online platform over the last two years. In addition, the government has seized $850 million in public funds that had been transferred to the bank for a state‑backed loan programme. The amount is being held in escrow while the authorities investigate the bank’s compliance record.

Lawmakers in the Paraguayan Congress have called for an investigation into the president’s involvement in the bank’s operations. Several opposition parties have demanded that the president be impeached over what they see as a conflict of interest and a breach of public trust. The president’s office has denied any wrongdoing and said the seizure was a routine regulatory action.

International observers have noted that the crisis could affect regional confidence in digital banking. The Central Bank of the Americas has urged that the situation be resolved quickly to avoid contagion. Meanwhile, the European Union has expressed concern over the protection of foreign investors who hold assets in the bank.

Why it matters

For residents of Great Britain, the crisis highlights the risks of investing in foreign digital banks that operate outside the UK’s regulatory framework. The incident serves as a reminder that even well‑capitalised institutions can face sudden regulatory intervention.

British investors who hold funds in overseas fintech firms should review the legal protections available to them and consider diversifying their portfolios to reduce exposure to jurisdictional risk.

Background

Ueno Bank was founded in 2019 and grew rapidly by offering instant loans and savings accounts through a mobile app. The bank’s business model relied heavily on short‑term borrowing from wholesale markets, a practice that attracted scrutiny from regulators in several Latin American countries.

The president of Paraguay has been a prominent supporter of fintech innovation, and the bank’s founder is a close associate of the president’s family. The relationship has raised questions about potential conflicts of interest and the extent of political influence over the banking sector.

What happens next

The government has set a 30‑day period for the bank’s management to submit a full audit of its operations. If the audit fails to satisfy the authorities, the bank may be liquidated and its assets distributed to creditors. The president’s office has pledged to cooperate fully with the investigation.
