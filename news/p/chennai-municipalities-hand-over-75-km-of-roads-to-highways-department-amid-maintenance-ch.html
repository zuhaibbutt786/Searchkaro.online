Chennai Municipalities Hand Over 75 km of Roads to Highways Department Amid Maintenance Challenges

Chennai’s municipal bodies have handed over 75 km of roads to the state highways department after struggling with maintenance. Meanwhile, Thanjavur is tackling pothole repairs across 51 wards.

Latest developments

In a move that has drawn attention across Tamil Nadu, the Chennai Corporation announced that it will hand over 75 kilometres of municipal roads to the state Highways Department. The decision follows a series of complaints from residents about deteriorating road surfaces and frequent potholes that have made commuting difficult.

The transfer was prompted by administrative challenges that the city council has faced in maintaining a sprawling network of roads. Officials cited limited manpower and funding constraints as key reasons for the decision, stating that the state agency is better equipped to manage large‑scale maintenance projects.

At the same time, the Thanjavur Municipal Council has begun a comprehensive pothole repair programme across 51 wards. The initiative, which began last month, aims to patch over 1,200 potholes and improve safety for pedestrians and vehicles alike.

Road maintenance is a critical issue for Chennai, a city that sees millions of commuters daily. Poor road conditions not only increase travel time but also raise vehicle operating costs and pose safety risks for motorists and cyclists.

Why it matters

For residents of Chennai, the handover means that daily traffic disruptions caused by road repairs may become less frequent. However, the transition period could see temporary confusion as responsibilities shift between municipal and state authorities.

In Thanjavur, the pothole repair drive is expected to reduce accidents and improve the quality of life for people who rely on local roads for work and education. The project also offers a chance for local contractors to secure contracts and boost the regional economy.

Both actions reflect a broader trend in India where local bodies are increasingly turning to state agencies for infrastructure support. The move is seen as a pragmatic solution to the resource gaps that many municipalities face.

Background

Road maintenance in Tamil Nadu has long been a point of contention between municipal corporations and the state government. While municipalities are responsible for local roads, they often lack the financial resources to carry out regular upkeep.

Previous attempts to centralise road maintenance have met with mixed results, with critics arguing that state agencies can be slow to respond to local needs. The recent handover in Chennai represents a new attempt to streamline responsibilities.

Thanjavur’s pothole repair programme follows a state‑wide push to improve rural and semi‑urban road conditions. The initiative is part of a larger effort to reduce travel times and enhance safety across the state.

What happens next

The Highways Department has outlined a schedule for the first phase of maintenance, which will focus on resurfacing the most heavily trafficked sections of the 75 km stretch. Work is expected to begin in early June and last for approximately three months.

Meanwhile, the Thanjavur council plans to complete the pothole repairs within the next 90 days, with a target of restoring all affected roads to a safe condition. The council will monitor progress through regular inspections and public feedback.

Summary

- 75 km of Chennai roads transferred to state Highways Department. - Thanjavur repairing potholes in 51 wards. - Shift aims to improve maintenance efficiency. - Expected to reduce traffic disruptions. - Follow‑up work scheduled for June and next 90 days.
