Veerappa Moily Says Congress Leaders Blocked Gujarat Riot Probe

Latest developments

On 18 November 2026, former Karnataka minister Veerappa Moily made a public statement that some senior members of the Indian National Congress intervened to stop an investigation into the Gujarat riots that took place in 2022. Moily, who is currently serving as a senior advisor to the UPA government, said that the obstruction was aimed at shielding Prime Minister Narendra Modi from scrutiny. The claim was reported by several regional news portals, including Manorama Online, Media One, and Reporter TV, each quoting Moily's allegation that the Congress leadership used its influence to halt the probe.

Moily explained that the alleged blockage occurred during the tenure of the UPA government, when Congress held significant sway over law‑enforcement agencies. According to his account, a group of senior Congress politicians approached senior officials and demanded that the investigation be shelved, arguing that it could destabilise the coalition. He added that the decision was taken without any formal judicial review, effectively preventing the Gujarat police and central agencies from pursuing leads that might implicate senior political figures.

The Congress party has not issued an official response to Moily's statements as of the time of writing. Party spokespersons declined to comment when approached by the press, and senior leaders have not been quoted in any of the reports. Political analysts note that the lack of a direct rebuttal may be a tactical choice, allowing the party to avoid escalating the controversy while internal discussions continue.

Legal experts and civil‑society groups have expressed concern over the alleged interference. They argue that any attempt to block a criminal investigation undermines the rule of law and could set a dangerous precedent for future cases. The Supreme Court of India has previously emphasised the independence of investigative agencies, and observers suggest that the allegations, if proven, could trigger judicial scrutiny or a parliamentary inquiry.

Why it matters

The accusation touches on the broader issue of political interference in law‑enforcement processes. If members of a ruling party are able to halt investigations into serious communal violence, public confidence in the justice system could erode, especially in a country as diverse as India where communal tensions remain a sensitive subject.

For voters in India, the claim raises questions about accountability and transparency. The Gujarat riots resulted in significant loss of life and property, and the demand for a thorough, impartial investigation has been a recurring theme in civil‑rights discourse. Any perception that political actors are shielding themselves from scrutiny may influence electoral behaviour, particularly in states where the Congress and the BJP compete closely for support.

Background

The Gujarat riots of 2022 were a series of violent clashes that erupted following a controversial political rally. The incidents led to dozens of deaths and widespread displacement. While initial investigations were launched by state authorities, critics argued that the probe lacked independence and that key witnesses were not protected.

Veerappa Moily, a former chief minister of Karnataka and a senior figure in the Congress party, has a long history of involvement in national politics. After serving as a Union Minister, he was appointed as an advisor to the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) government. His recent comments come at a time when the UPA is seeking to re‑establish its credibility ahead of upcoming state elections.

What happens next

Legal scholars anticipate that the Supreme Court may be petitioned to examine the allegations of obstruction. Meanwhile, opposition parties are likely to raise the issue in parliamentary debates, demanding a fresh, independent inquiry. The outcome will depend on whether the judiciary or the legislature decides to intervene.
