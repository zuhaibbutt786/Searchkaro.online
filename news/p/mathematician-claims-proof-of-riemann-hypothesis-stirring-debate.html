Background

The Riemann Hypothesis, proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, asserts that all non‑trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have a real part equal to one‑half. Its truth would explain the irregularities in the distribution of prime numbers and would provide a powerful tool for mathematicians working in analytic number theory. Over the past 160 years, many mathematicians have offered proofs that were later found to be incomplete or flawed. The problem’s difficulty has made it a benchmark for mathematical ingenuity, attracting both professional researchers and talented amateurs. The Clay Mathematics Institute’s Millennium Prize, announced in 2000, listed the hypothesis among seven problems that remain unsolved. The institute offers a $1 million reward for a correct proof, reflecting the problem’s significance in the mathematical community.
