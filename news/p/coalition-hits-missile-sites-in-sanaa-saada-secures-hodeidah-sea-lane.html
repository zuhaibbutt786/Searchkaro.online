Coalition hits missile sites in Sana’a, Saada, secures Hodeidah sea lane

The coalition announced a strike on missile facilities in Sana’a and Saada and a naval operation to protect shipping in Hodeidah. The moves aim to curb missile threats and keep sea lanes open.

Latest developments

The Saudi-led coalition said it launched a coordinated military operation in the Red Sea port of Hodeidah to protect commercial navigation, according to a BBC Arabic report. Naval assets were deployed to monitor and deter hostile actions, and the coalition emphasized that the operation was intended to ensure the safety of merchant vessels transiting the Gulf of Aden. The statement highlighted the strategic importance of Hodeidah as a gateway for humanitarian aid and trade. The coalition also reported that its forces successfully destroyed a missile launch platform in the capital, Sana’a, as detailed by the Saudi Press Agency. The target was identified as a site capable of firing ballistic missiles toward the coast, and the strike was described as part of ongoing efforts to neutralise threats to civilian infrastructure. In a separate action reported by Sabq, coalition aircraft hit a storage facility in the mountainous Saada province that housed around twenty ballistic missiles. The facility was located in a rugged area that had previously served as a logistics hub for the Houthi movement, also known as Ansar Allah. The destruction of the missile stockpile was presented as a preventive measure to stop future attacks on populated areas and critical infrastructure. Supporters of Ansar Allah responded by claiming they had tightened their grip on recent gains in the north, signalling a continued resolve despite the coalition’s setbacks. The coalition’s statements did not include casualty figures, focusing instead on the operational success and the broader objective of safeguarding maritime routes.

Why it matters

Securing the Hodeidah sea lane is vital for Yemen’s humanitarian supply chain, as the port handles a large share of food, medicine and fuel imports. Any disruption could exacerbate the already severe shortages affecting millions of civilians. By targeting missile sites in Sana’a and Saada, the coalition aims to reduce the frequency of rocket attacks that have threatened coastal cities and shipping vessels, thereby lowering insurance costs and encouraging commercial traffic to resume normal schedules. The operation also sends a signal to regional actors that the coalition remains capable of projecting force deep into Houthi‑controlled territory, which could influence diplomatic negotiations and the balance of power in the protracted civil war.

Background

Yemen’s conflict began in 2014 when the Houthi movement, formally called Ansar Allah, seized the capital Sana’a and forced the internationally recognised government into exile. In 2015, a coalition of Gulf states led by Saudi Arabia intervened to restore the government’s authority, launching air campaigns against Houthi positions. Since then, the war has caused widespread displacement, a humanitarian crisis, and repeated attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea. The coalition has periodically targeted Houthi missile capabilities, arguing that the groups use ballistic rockets to strike Saudi territory and threaten maritime traffic. Hodeidah, a key port on the Red Sea, has been a focal point of fighting, with both sides accusing the other of endangering civilian vessels. International bodies have repeatedly called for the protection of humanitarian corridors, while the coalition has stressed the need to neutralise threats that could jeopardise those routes.

What happens next

Analysts expect the coalition to continue monitoring the Houthi missile infrastructure and to conduct further strikes if additional launch sites are identified. The United Nations and other humanitarian organisations will likely press for guarantees that port operations remain uninterrupted, while diplomatic channels may see renewed activity as regional powers seek to prevent further escalation that could impact global shipping lanes.

Summary
