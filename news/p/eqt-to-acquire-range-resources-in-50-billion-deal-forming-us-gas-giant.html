EQT to Acquire Range Resources in $50 Billion Deal, Forming US Gas Giant

EQT announced a $50 billion all‑stock purchase of Range Resources, a move that will combine two major players in U.S. gas production. The deal is set to create the country’s largest natural‑gas operator.

Latest developments

EQT, the Stockholm‑based private‑equity group, disclosed on Wednesday that it will acquire Range Resources in an all‑stock transaction valued at more than $50 billion. The agreement, reported by Reuters, will see EQT issue new shares to Range shareholders, effectively making the U.S. shale‑gas producer a subsidiary of the European firm. The combined entity is projected to generate over 2 billion cubic feet of gas per day, surpassing current U.S. leaders in output. The deal, which is subject to customary regulatory approvals and shareholder votes, is expected to close in the first half of 2027. Both companies have indicated that the transaction will be financed without cash, relying on the equity exchange to meet the purchase price. Market analysts cited by Seeking Alpha noted that the all‑stock structure reflects confidence in EQT’s balance sheet and its long‑term view of the U.S. gas market. The Financial Times highlighted that the merger will give EQT a foothold in the Permian Basin, a region that has driven much of the recent growth in U.S. natural‑gas production.

Why it matters

The creation of a single, larger gas producer could influence pricing dynamics across North America. With increased scale, the new entity may gain greater bargaining power with pipeline operators and downstream buyers, potentially affecting contract terms for utilities and industrial users. Additionally, the deal signals continued investor interest in fossil‑fuel assets despite broader trends toward renewable energy, suggesting that natural gas remains a critical bridge fuel in the United States' transition strategy. For consumers, the consolidation could lead to more stable supply but also raises questions about market competition and regulatory oversight.

Background

Range Resources, founded in 1993, has built a portfolio of assets primarily in the Marcellus and Utica shale plays, becoming one of the leading independent natural‑gas producers in the country. EQT, known for its diversified investments across sectors, entered the energy space in the early 2010s and has since expanded its portfolio to include midstream infrastructure and renewable projects. The U.S. natural‑gas market has experienced rapid growth over the past decade, driven by advances in hydraulic fracturing and a shift away from coal in electricity generation. However, the sector also faces challenges such as fluctuating commodity prices, environmental scrutiny, and the need for substantial capital to maintain drilling activity.

What happens next

The transaction now moves to the approval stage, where shareholders of both EQT and Range Resources must vote in favor of the merger. Following shareholder consent, the deal will undergo review by the U.S. Federal Trade Commission and other relevant regulators to assess antitrust concerns. If cleared, the combined company will begin integrating operations, aligning corporate governance, and setting a strategic plan for capital allocation across its expanded asset base. Observers expect the integration process to take several months, during which the firm will likely outline its approach to environmental, social, and governance (ESG) commitments.

Summary

- EQT will acquire Range Resources in an all‑stock deal valued over $50 billion. - The merger will create the largest natural‑gas producer in the United States. - Expected to close in early 2027 pending shareholder and regulatory approvals. - The combined entity will have significant influence over U.S. gas pricing and supply dynamics.
