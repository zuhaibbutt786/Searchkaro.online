Pakistani drama 'Gulbahar' sparks backlash over second‑marriage plot

The popular TV series Gulbahar has ignited a wave of criticism after a second‑marriage twist was introduced. Audiences and commentators say the plot undermines the show's earlier portrayal of women.

Latest developments

The drama series Gulbahar, which airs on a major Pakistani network, introduced a storyline in which the male lead takes a second wife. Within hours of the episode's broadcast, social media platforms were flooded with comments expressing disappointment and anger. Viewers accused the writers of reverting to clichéd tropes that they feel have been overused in local serials.

Online petitions calling for a revision of the plot have gathered thousands of signatures, and several prominent television critics have written pieces highlighting the perceived regression in the show's treatment of female characters. The network has yet to issue an official statement, but a spokesperson confirmed that the episode was part of a planned arc that will continue over the next few weeks.

Industry analysts note that the backlash is not limited to social media; rating agencies have reported a slight dip in viewership numbers for the episode in question. Advertisers, who typically invest heavily in high‑rating dramas, are reportedly reviewing their contracts pending further audience reaction.

Meanwhile, the drama's lead actress, who has been praised for her earlier performances, has remained silent on the controversy, focusing instead on her upcoming projects. The situation has also drawn attention from women's rights groups, who argue that the narrative reinforces outdated gender norms.

Why it matters

The controversy surrounding Gulbahar reflects a broader conversation about how Pakistani television portrays women. In recent years, audiences have shown a growing appetite for storylines that challenge traditional expectations, and many see the second‑marriage twist as a step backward.

When a popular series normalises polygamy without addressing its social implications, it can influence public perception, especially among younger viewers who look to media for cultural cues. Critics argue that such portrayals risk perpetuating stereotypes that limit women's agency.

The episode's reception also has economic implications. Television dramas are a major source of advertising revenue, and a sustained negative response could affect the network's financial performance. Moreover, the backlash may encourage producers to reconsider script choices, potentially leading to more nuanced storytelling in future productions.

Background

Gulbahar debuted earlier this year as a family drama centred on the lives of women in a small town, exploring themes of empowerment, education, and social mobility. The title, which translates to "spring flower," was chosen to symbolize renewal and hope for its female protagonists.

Initially, the series received praise for its strong female characters and for addressing issues such as early marriage and gender-based discrimination. Ratings were consistently high, and the show earned several nominations at national television awards.

However, the introduction of a second‑marriage plot aligns with a pattern observed in many regional serials, where male protagonists take additional wives as a dramatic device. While such storylines have historically attracted viewership, they are increasingly scrutinised by a more vocal audience that demands progressive narratives.

What happens next

Industry insiders suggest that the network may adjust the storyline in response to the mounting criticism, possibly by providing a more critical lens on the second marriage or by giving the affected female characters greater agency.

The producers have indicated that they are monitoring audience feedback closely and may hold a press briefing to address concerns. Until then, viewers will continue to watch the unfolding arc, and the next episode is expected to either deepen the controversy or attempt to mitigate it.
