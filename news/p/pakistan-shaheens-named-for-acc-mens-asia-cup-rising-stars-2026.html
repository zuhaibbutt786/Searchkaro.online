Pakistan Shaheens named for ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars 2026

The Pakistan Shaheens have unveiled their 50‑over squad for the ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars 2026. Abdul Samad will lead the side, with Maaz Sadaqat and Hasan Nawaz among the selected youngsters.

Latest developments

The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) issued a press release earlier this week confirming the 15‑member Pakistan Shaheens roster for the 2026 ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars tournament. The squad is set to compete in the 50‑over format and will represent the nation in the developmental wing of the competition, which runs later this year across several Asian venues.

Abdul Samad, a right‑hand batsman who has featured in domestic T20 leagues, has been appointed captain of the Shaheens. PCB officials highlighted his experience in high‑pressure matches and his ability to guide younger players through international exposure. Samad will wear the armband for the first time at a senior ACC event.

Among the newcomers, all‑rounder Maaz Sadaqat and fast‑bowling prospect Hasan Nawaz earned spots in the squad after impressive performances in the domestic circuit. Both players are expected to receive significant playing time, providing them with a platform to showcase their skills against peers from other Asian nations.

The complete squad also includes a blend of seasoned domestic performers and emerging talent, covering batting, pace, and spin departments. The team will commence a pre‑tournament camp in Lahore next week, focusing on fitness, fielding drills, and strategic planning ahead of the opening match against the host nation.

Why it matters

The selection of the Shaheens carries weight for Pakistan's long‑term cricketing strategy. By exposing young players to a multi‑nation tournament, the PCB aims to broaden the talent pool for future senior national duties. Successful performances could accelerate the transition of players like Sadaqat and Nawaz into the main Pakistan team, addressing gaps in the pace and all‑rounder categories.

For fans across Pakistan, the Rising Stars Asia Cup offers a glimpse of the next generation of cricketers. The tournament is broadcast on local sports channels, and strong showings are likely to boost viewership and commercial interest. Moreover, the experience gained by the squad under Samad's leadership may improve the overall depth of the country's cricketing resources, enhancing competitiveness in upcoming ICC events.

Background

The ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars competition was introduced to provide emerging cricketers from member nations a structured international platform. Unlike the senior Asia Cup, the Rising Stars edition focuses on 50‑over matches and limits squad ages to foster development. The inaugural edition took place in 2022, with India Emerging winning the title.

Pakistan has traditionally fielded a development side known as the Shaheens, a name that translates to "falcons" in Urdu, symbolising agility and ambition. The Shaheens have participated in previous ACC youth events, gaining valuable experience against regional rivals. Their involvement in the 2026 edition reflects PCB's commitment to nurturing talent beyond the under‑19 level, bridging the gap between junior cricket and full international play.

What happens next

The Shaheens will gather for a two‑week training camp in Lahore, followed by a series of practice matches against domestic sides. The PCB has scheduled a media tour to introduce the squad to fans ahead of the tournament's kickoff in September. Once the competition begins, Pakistan will face teams such as Nepal, United Arab Emirates, and Bangladesh Emerging in the group stage, with the aim of reaching the knockout rounds.
