State Bank clarifies rumors about the 10‑rupee note

Pakistan's central bank has issued a statement to counter misinformation about the 10‑rupee note. The clarification outlines the bank's policy and what it means for everyday transactions.

What is happening

In recent days, social media platforms in Pakistan have been flooded with posts claiming that the 10‑rupee banknote is being withdrawn from circulation. The posts often cite unnamed sources and use sensational language, leading many citizens to wonder whether the note will disappear from wallets and cash registers. In response, the State Bank of Pakistan (اسٹیٹ بینک) released an official clarification stating that there is no current plan to discontinue the 10‑rupee note. The bank emphasized that any decision to change the currency series would be communicated well in advance through formal channels, including the national press and the bank's website.

The clarification also addressed the origin of the rumors. According to the State Bank, the misinformation appears to have originated from a misinterpretation of a routine internal memo that discussed potential redesigns of certain denominations for security reasons. Such redesigns are common worldwide and do not imply immediate withdrawal. The bank reassured the public that existing 10‑rupee notes remain legal tender and will continue to be accepted for all transactions.

Media outlets such as BBC Urdu, Samaa TV, and Hum News Urdu have reported on the bank's statement, further reinforcing the official position. These reports note that the State Bank has not announced any timeline for a new series of the 10‑rupee note, and that the current stock of notes will remain in circulation until a formal phase‑out plan, if ever, is announced. The bank also encouraged citizens to verify information through its official communication channels before sharing unverified claims.

Background and context

Pakistan's currency system includes several denominations that have been periodically updated to incorporate new security features and to reflect changes in the economy. The 10‑rupee note, first introduced in the early 2000s, has undergone minor design tweaks but has remained a staple of everyday transactions. Historically, the State Bank has announced withdrawals of older series only after a clear transition period, allowing banks and businesses to adapt.

In the past, the State Bank has withdrawn higher denominations, such as the 500‑rupee note, following a comprehensive public awareness campaign. Those withdrawals were accompanied by official press releases, public service announcements, and coordination with commercial banks to ensure a smooth exchange process. The current rumor does not match this established pattern, as there has been no official notice, no public consultation, and no logistical preparation for a large‑scale recall of the 10‑rupee note.

What to know next

For now, citizens should continue to use the 10‑rupee note as normal. The State Bank has advised that any future changes to the denomination will be announced through its official website, press releases, and major news outlets. Individuals and businesses are encouraged to keep an eye on those channels rather than relying on unverified social media posts.

If a redesign or withdrawal were ever planned, the bank would likely provide a clear timeline, instructions for exchanging old notes, and a grace period during which both old and new notes would be accepted. Until such an announcement is made, the existing supply of 10‑rupee notes remains valid legal tender, and there is no immediate impact on cash transactions.
