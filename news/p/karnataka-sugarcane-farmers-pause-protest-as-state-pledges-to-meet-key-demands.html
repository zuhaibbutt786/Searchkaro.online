Karnataka sugarcane farmers pause protest as state pledges to meet key demands

Sugarcane growers in Karnataka have called off their strike for now, citing a state promise to fulfil two main demands. The move puts pressure on both state and central leaders to resolve a long‑standing pricing dispute.

Latest developments

The protest by sugarcane farmers in Karnataka entered a tentative lull on Monday after the state government announced a temporary suspension of the agitation. Officials said the decision rests on the shoulders of Agriculture Minister B.S. Joshi, who has been tasked with ensuring that two core demands of the farmers are met. The first demand concerns a revision of the Minimum Selling Price (MSP) for sugarcane, which growers argue should be raised to Rs 5,500 per ton. The second demand seeks a clear, time‑bound mechanism for the payment of arrears owed by sugar mills. In a statement released by the state, Joshi pledged to work with the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare to address these points within a stipulated period.

The farmers' union, led by senior activist K. Ramesh, welcomed the announcement but warned that any delay could reignite the strike. Over the past weeks, thousands of cultivators had blocked major highways and staged sit‑ins at sugar mills, demanding relief from what they describe as a crippling price squeeze. Their protest has drawn attention from national leaders, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who received a formal letter from Karnataka's Deputy Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar urging the central government to intervene and support the state's efforts to raise the MSP.

Shivakumar's letter, circulated to the Prime Minister's office, outlined five specific requests: an immediate increase in the MSP to Rs 5,500, a guarantee of timely payments to farmers, a review of the sugarcane procurement policy, the establishment of a grievance redressal cell, and financial assistance for small‑scale growers affected by the price gap. The Deputy Chief Minister emphasized that the current MSP of Rs 4,500 per ton leaves many cultivators operating at a loss, especially in drought‑prone districts such as Mandya and Mysore.

While the state has signalled willingness to meet the price demand, the central government has not yet issued a formal response. Analysts note that any change to the MSP will require coordination between the state agriculture department and the Ministry of Agriculture, which sets the national floor price for sugarcane. The outcome could set a precedent for other sugar‑producing states that face similar farmer unrest.

Why it matters

The sugarcane sector is a cornerstone of Karnataka's agrarian economy, employing over 1.5 million people across the state. A higher MSP would directly affect the livelihoods of small and marginal farmers who depend on sugarcane as their primary cash crop. Moreover, the dispute highlights the broader tension between state governments and the central authority over agricultural pricing policies, a recurring theme in India's federal structure.

For consumers, a rise in the MSP could translate into higher sugar prices, potentially impacting household budgets and the food processing industry. However, proponents argue that fair compensation for growers will stabilize production, ensuring a steady supply of raw material for sugar mills and preventing a collapse of the sector. The protest also underscores the political stakes for the ruling party in Karnataka, which faces criticism for perceived inaction on farmer issues ahead of upcoming state elections.

Background

Karnataka has long been one of India's leading sugarcane producers, with an annual output of roughly 30 million tonnes. The state's sugar industry operates under a procurement system where mills purchase cane at a price set by the government, known as the Minimum Selling Price. Over the past three years, the MSP has remained at Rs 4,500 per tonne, a figure that many growers claim does not reflect rising input costs, including fertilizer, labor, and water.

The discontent grew after a series of poor monsoons and a spike in production costs left many farmers with negative returns. In early 2024, the Karnataka Farmers' Association organized a series of rallies demanding a revision of the MSP and faster settlement of pending dues from sugar mills. The movement gained momentum when Deputy Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar intervened, sending a formal appeal to Prime Minister Narendra Modi to consider a national adjustment of the price floor.

The farmers' protest escalated in March when they blocked major transport routes, disrupting the movement of sugarcane to processing units. The state government responded with negotiations, but talks stalled over disagreements on the exact price level and the timeline for arrears clearance. The recent temporary suspension marks the first significant de‑escalation since the protests began.

What happens next

The next steps hinge on the state's ability to deliver on the promised price revision and arrears settlement. Agriculture Minister Joshi is expected to convene a meeting with senior officials from the Ministry of Agriculture within the next week to finalize the MSP adjustment. Simultaneously, the central government may issue a directive to sugar mills across the country to align their procurement rates with the new floor price.

If the state meets the farmers' demands promptly, the protest is likely to remain on hold. However, any perceived delay could trigger a resurgence of the strike, potentially affecting sugar production and market stability in the coming months.
