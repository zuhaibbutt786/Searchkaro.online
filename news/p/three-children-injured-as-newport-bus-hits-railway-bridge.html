Three children injured as Newport bus hits railway bridge

A school bus in Newport collided with a railway bridge, tearing off its roof and leaving three children with injuries. Emergency crews responded quickly and the incident is now under police investigation.

Latest developments

On Tuesday afternoon, a school bus travelling along Tregare Street in Newport struck a low railway bridge, causing the roof of the vehicle to be torn away. The impact injured three children, all of whom were taken to nearby hospitals for treatment of injuries described as non‑life‑threatening. Emergency services arrived within minutes, securing the scene and diverting traffic on the busy road. Police and the Rail Accident Investigation Branch have opened a joint inquiry to determine the cause of the collision. Early reports suggest that the bridge may have been lower than the bus's clearance, but officials have not confirmed any mechanical fault with the vehicle.

Why it matters

The crash raises immediate concerns about the safety of school transport routes across the United Kingdom. Parents rely on strict standards for vehicle height and route planning, and any deviation can have serious consequences for young passengers. The incident also highlights the need for regular inspections of railway infrastructure, particularly low bridges that intersect with road traffic. Local authorities have faced criticism in the past for delayed upgrades to ageing bridges, and this event may intensify calls for accelerated investment. Moreover, the injury of three children brings heightened public attention to emergency response times and the capacity of regional hospitals to manage sudden influxes of paediatric patients.

Background

Newport, a city in South Wales, has a network of roads that cross several railway lines, many of which were constructed in the 19th century. Over the years, a number of near‑miss incidents have been recorded where vehicles have struck bridge structures, prompting periodic safety reviews. The UK government introduced stricter vehicle‑bridge clearance guidelines in 2015, but implementation varies across local councils. In recent months, other school bus accidents have been reported in the region, though none involved a bridge collision of this severity. The Transport for Wales agency oversees public transport safety and works with Network Rail to monitor bridge heights, but resource constraints can delay remedial work.

What happens next

Police will continue to gather evidence, including CCTV footage from nearby businesses and statements from the bus driver and witnesses. The Rail Accident Investigation Branch will assess the structural integrity of the bridge and issue recommendations for any required modifications. Transport for Wales is expected to review the route used by the school bus and may temporarily suspend services on the affected road until safety can be assured. Parents have been advised to keep children at home until the investigation provides a clear picture of any ongoing risk. A formal report is likely to be published within the next six weeks.
